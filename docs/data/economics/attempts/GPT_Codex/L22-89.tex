\begin{abstract}
We give a constructive answer to the declared-model vertical-integration
question for arbitrary finite contracting networks with private information,
entry, partner changes and persistent investment. The strategic menus and
horizon are finite; primitive parameters vary over a specified real
semialgebraic domain. The output is an exact parameter-region description
for welfare dominance over every sequential equilibrium, explicit selected
welfare rules, and sharp joint welfare sets compatible with contract and
investment observations. Arbitrary Borel equilibrium-selection kernels
admit finite certificates: with $d$ observable categories and $k$ welfare
coordinates, $d+k$ equilibrium pairs suffice, and scalar conditional extrema
need only $d$. Common parameters and the physical economy remain fixed across
ownership regimes. A fully worked three-period economy has active private
entry costs, rematching, two-part contracts and persistent investment, with
both welfare signs and an exact observation-compatible interval. A separate
continuous Cournot benchmark gives a second exact sign frontier. We also
give finite-sample confidence sets for the entire identified welfare set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original target and the declared economic class}\label{sec:target}

The original L22-89 question~\cite{catalogue} fixes finite upstream and
downstream firm sets, consumer demand, positive differentiable production
costs, a contracting protocol, information and admissible entry/investment
decisions. A designated upstream--downstream pair integrates in regime
$a=1$; firms are independent in regime $a=0$. Technologies remain the same.
With equilibrium sets $\mathcal E_a(\theta)$, the strong welfare comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong}
 W_1(e_1;\theta)\ge W_0(e_0;\theta)
 \quad\text{for every }e_1\in\mathcal E_1(\theta),\
 e_0\in\mathcal E_0(\theta).
\end{equation}
When this fails, the question requests ranges under an explicitly stated
equilibrium-selection rule. Its fixed-link variant uses linear wholesale
prices; its dynamic-link variant permits rematching, private costs and
persistent investment over a finite horizon or a specified discount model,
and asks what contract/investment observations imply for welfare signs.
The source explicitly poses this within a declared model class, not over
every possible unencoded bargaining protocol. Lee, Whinston and
Yurukoglu~\cite[Section 5]{LWY} motivate precisely these additional margins.

We declare the following class before choosing parameters, observations or
welfare signs. A theorem uniform over its finite inputs is not a finite
library of precomputed economies.

\begin{definition}[Effective finite contracting template]\label{def:class}
An input contains a finite-horizon physical network decision tree, finite
private-state and shock labels, and finite menus of strategic contracts,
entry, investment, production commitments and partner changes. It specifies
information sets with perfect recall, costs, capacity and investment-stock
transitions, budgets, consumer preferences and outside options. Each terminal
history is a complete feasible physical allocation and resource ledger.

The parameter $\theta$ ranges over a supplied rational-semialgebraic set
$\Theta$. Every scalar primitive has a finite, single-valued
rational-semialgebraic graph on its domain. Nature probabilities are
nonnegative and sum to one. Conditional probabilities with a zero denominator
must have a specified completion; undefined $0/0$ is not an input.
Legal-action guards must agree at nodes in the same information set and
leave a nonempty action set. They may depend only on information available
to the actor. Each resulting legal game has perfect recall.

Regime 0 assigns controls, information and monetary profits to the separate
firms. Regime 1 assigns the designated pair's controls to their common owner,
with a specified perfect-recall information structure and the \emph{sum}
of their profits. Other firms keep their technological opportunities and
outside options. The common physical constraints and real costs are retained;
integration changes control and incentives, not the accounting definition
of welfare. Thus the input cannot be two unrelated games relabeled as a merger.

The equilibrium concept is sequential equilibrium in each actual-support
game. In each parameter stratum only Nature-impossible histories are omitted;
strategically off-path legal actions and histories remain. Consumer behavior,
production and rationing rules must implement the stated feasible allocation,
rather than assigning an unsupported arbitrary payoff table to a terminal.
\end{definition}

Menus may contain nonlinear and two-part contracts, with real-valued menu
terms varying continuously with $\theta$. The number of firms, menu entries,
private histories and dates is arbitrary but finite for each input.
Finite horizon alone does not imply finite strategic menus; their finiteness
is an explicit class choice. This covers every requested economic margin
simultaneously, rather than suppressing private information or rematching.
It does not claim to encode arbitrary measures over continuous strategic
actions, arbitrary type distributions or infinite-horizon games. The
continuous-price Cournot result in Section~\ref{sec:cournot} is separately
proved and is not said to follow from a finite-tree algorithm.

Continuous consumption is compatible with the class. For example, at a
terminal price vector one may supply a compact semialgebraic consumption set,
continuous semialgebraic quasilinear utility, and lexicographic utility-maximizing
tie breaking. Comparing all bundles and successively minimizing coordinates
gives a unique selected demand and its semialgebraic graph. An actual clearing
or rationing rule and feasible budgets are still required. Differentiable
positive production costs are evaluated on that physical consumption and
production; arbitrary terminal payoffs do not establish those economic facts.

The only previous L22-89 attempt~\cite{prior} solves the full homogeneous
fixed-link Cournot benchmark for any number of downstream firms. Its
equilibrium and strong welfare comparison are complete for that benchmark
and are credited in Section~\ref{sec:cournot}. It omits entry, investment,
private costs and rematching. Our use of a declared class does not make
another static benchmark a complete answer: the general theorem below
retains all those margins, and Section~\ref{sec:dynamic} makes them active.
Finite sequential-equilibrium descriptions and real quantifier elimination
are established tools~\cite{Graf,Basu}. Their use is not claimed as a new
algorithmic discovery. The selection reduction, exact observational outputs
and economic deductions are developed explicitly here.

\section{Resource accounting and the main output theorem}\label{sec:main}

At terminal history $z$ and date $t$, let $G_t$ be consumer gross utility,
$C_{ft}$ each firm's real production, investment and entry cost, $P_{ht}$
consumer payments and $T_{fgt}$ interfirm transfers. Consumer net utility
plus the firms' profits is
\[
 G_t-\sum_hP_{ht}
 +\sum_f\left(\text{consumer receipts}_f
            +\sum_g T_{gft}-\sum_gT_{fgt}-C_{ft}\right)
 =G_t-\sum_f C_{ft}.
\]
Every domestic transfer occurs once with each sign. Using the same declared
discount weights $d_t$, the terminal welfare is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare}
 w_z(\theta)=\sum_t d_t\bigl(G_t(z,\theta)-\sum_f C_{ft}(z,\theta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Combining owners does not change this identity. Internal transfers can alter
chosen physical actions; cancellation is not incentive irrelevance. Dividends
already counted in firm profits must not be added again through ownership
income. External losses require their stated social treatment rather than
automatic cancellation.

Let $b$ be behavioral strategies and $\mu$ beliefs. We write
$e=(b,\mu)$ and $\Gamma_a(\theta,e)$ for the exact sequential-equilibrium
predicate constructed in Section~\ref{sec:SE}. Terminal reach probabilities
give $W_a(\theta,e)=\sum_z\Prb(z\mid\theta,b)w_z(\theta)$.
At fixed $\theta$ this is continuous in the compact assessment space.

\begin{theorem}[Constructive welfare and identification answer]\label{thm:main}
For every valid input in Definition~\ref{def:class}, a finite exact algorithm
returns all of the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A quantifier-free semialgebraic description and a compatible finite
cell decomposition of the complete parameter region satisfying
\eqref{eq:strong}, retaining every sequential equilibrium in both regimes.
\item The minimum and maximum welfare graphs in each regime, and explicit
supported Borel selection kernels and their selected-welfare graphs.
Any prescribed finite-support selector family with supplied semialgebraic
atom and weight graphs and a fixed finite atom bound can instead be used
with its own restrictions.
\item Under a declared regime-separable finite observation law whose regime
sampling and terminal measurement/noise probabilities have supplied
rational-semialgebraic graphs, sharp joint
sets of parameters and selected welfare coordinates consistent with that
\emph{whole law}, when selection ranges over all supported Borel kernels.
For $d$ observable categories and $k$ welfare coordinates, membership has
a certificate using at most $d+k$ pairs of equilibria at one common
$\theta$. At fixed $\theta$, a scalar conditional extremum needs at most
$d$ pairs.
\item Incompatibility tests, all attainable scalar signs, and complete
scalar sets as finite unions of intervals and points, with open/closed
endpoints. Attained endpoints have witnesses; unattained finite endpoints
have witnesses within every positive rational tolerance. Infinite endpoints
are reported as such.
\end{enumerate}
The algorithm is uniform over arbitrary finite input sizes. No polynomial
runtime or practical bound on the resulting formula size is asserted.
Symbolic parameters may be left free. Numerical certificate extraction uses
rational or real-algebraic input values with finite exact encodings.
\end{theorem}

The observation and selection conventions are part of this theorem, not
conclusions inferred from data. A selector is drawn before private Nature
and chooses a genuine equilibrium of the common-prior game; its label may
be publicly known. A selector secretly conditioned on realized private types
would be a different model. The two regimes are joined only for marginal
observation laws and differences of welfare means. Joint cross-regime
counterfactual measurements or restrictions require separate constraints;
they are not silently introduced by a coupling.

\section{Exact equilibrium construction and existence}\label{sec:SE}

We give the construction to avoid requiring an equilibrium oracle. Its
ingredients are classical~\cite{KW,Graf}. Lift all primitive values to one
finite vector $c,u,w$, satisfying their supplied graph formulas, outside
the equilibrium-consistency quantifiers. At fixed $\theta$ these values
are unique. First enumerate the finitely many legal-action patterns and
form their legal game trees; then enumerate their Nature-support patterns.
Delete only a history whose root path includes a zero Nature factor;
retain every legal strategic action at each live node, including those
off the equilibrium path. The remaining
positive Nature probabilities still sum to one, with no renormalization.
Intersect each information set with the live histories and remove it only
if empty. Perfect recall is inherited. A tagged finite disjoint union, or
dummy zero coordinates, combines these strata without treating dummy
coordinates as actual beliefs.

On one stratum let $\mathcal I$ be the information sets and $A(I)$ their
nonempty action sets. Behavior and beliefs satisfy
\[
 b_{Ia}\ge0,\quad\sum_{a\in A(I)}b_{Ia}=1,\qquad
 \mu_{Ih}\ge0,\quad\sum_{h\in I}\mu_{Ih}=1.
\]
Perfect recall implies that no information set is visited twice on a path.
Let $r_h(c,b)$ be the product of Nature and behavioral probabilities from
the root to $h$, and $r_I=\sum_{h\in I}r_h$. For a completely mixed $b$,
all retained histories have positive probability. Its Bayes graph is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayes}
 \mathsf B(c,b,\mu):
 \quad b_{Ia}>0\ (I,a),\qquad
 r_I>0,\quad \mu_{Ih}r_I=r_h\ (I,h),
\end{equation}
together with the simplex constraints. In particular cross multiplication
does not admit a spurious $0=0$ posterior.

Consistency is the first-order real formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 \forall\rho>0\ \exists b',\mu':
 \mathsf B(c,b',\mu')\quad\text{and}\quad
 \|(b',\mu')-(b,\mu)\|_2^2<\rho^2.
\end{equation}
The same \emph{fixed} $c$ occurs inside and outside this formula. One
common witness satisfies all information sets at once, and witnesses are
not required to be equilibria. A consistent sequence supplies these
witnesses. Conversely choosing a witness at $\rho=1/j$, $j=1,2,\ldots$,
gives precisely a convergent completely mixed Bayes sequence of the fixed
game. Thus~\eqref{eq:consistency} is exact finite-game consistency, not
closure across changing parameters or independently selected posteriors.

For information set $I$ of player $i$ and a behavioral continuation
$\delta_i$, form the polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation}
 V_{iI}(c,u,b_{-i},\delta_i;\mu)
 =\sum_{h\in I}\mu_{Ih}
       \sum_{z\succeq h}u_{iz}
          \Prb(z\mid h,c,b_{-i},\delta_i).
\end{equation}
The inner probability is the suffix product using $\delta_i$ at all of
$i$'s later information sets and $b_{-i}$ elsewhere. Sequential rationality
requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SR}
 V_{iI}(b_i;\mu)\ge V_{iI}(\delta_i;\mu)
 \quad\text{for every }i,I,\delta_i.
\end{equation}
Suppressed fixed arguments are identical. This compares the whole
continuation, not only an initial-state best response. It is enough to
compare finitely many complete pure plans. Indeed~\eqref{eq:continuation}
is affine in each separate own information-set block; expanding the blocks
expresses any behavioral payoff as the convex combination of pure-plan
payoffs with weights $\prod_J\delta_{J,\pi(J)}$. The weights sum to one.
No repeated information-set variable occurs on a path, which is the needed
perfect-recall fact.

Define $\Gamma_a$ by the primitive graph constraints, the applicable
stratum, simplex conditions,~\eqref{eq:consistency} and~\eqref{eq:SR}.
Both directions follow from their definitions: $\Gamma_a$ holds exactly
for all sequential-equilibrium assessments of the actual-support game.
All its expressions are finite polynomials or supplied finite graph
formulas; $\rho$ is one real variable, not a quantifier over sequences.

\begin{lemma}[Nonempty compact equilibrium fibers]\label{lem:existence}
For every valid fixed parameter, each fiber of $\Gamma_a$ is nonempty
and compact. This does not assert joint closedness across support changes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here is the finite-agent construction using Nash's positive-gain map and
its Brouwer fixed-point argument~\cite[Theorem 1, p. 288]{Nash}.
At every information set require each action probability to be at
least $\varepsilon$, where
$0<\varepsilon<1/\max_I|A(I)|$. Give each information set a separate
decision agent, with its original player's expected payoff. Write
$b_{Ia}=\varepsilon+(1-|A(I)|\varepsilon)x_{Ia}$ for a simplex variable
$x_I$. Payoffs are affine in each agent's own block. This finite agent
game has a mixed Nash equilibrium: if $F_I$ is its payoff, put
$g_{Ia}(x)=\max(0,F_I(a,x_{-I})-F_I(x))$ and map
\[
 x_{Ia}\longmapsto\frac{x_{Ia}+g_{Ia}(x)}{1+\sum_b g_{Ib}(x)}.
\]
Brouwer gives a fixed point. If $G_I=\sum_b g_{Ib}>0$, its identity
$g_{Ia}=x_{Ia}G_I$ makes every supported action's gain positive,
contradicting the fact that their $x_I$-weighted average gain is zero.
Thus all $G_I=0$ and no agent can improve.

Let $b^\varepsilon$ be the resulting positive behavior and
$\mu^\varepsilon$ its Bayes beliefs. Every information set is reached
with positive probability, so agent optimality gives conditional local
optimality there among $\varepsilon$-constrained actions. We check that
it also gives full constrained continuation optimality. At an information
set $J$ of player $i$, perfect recall makes the product of $i$'s own past
action probabilities the same at every history in $J$. It cancels from
Bayes' rule. Thus its posterior, for fixed positive opponents' behavior
and Nature, is unaffected by changing its own positive strategy.
Moreover if $J$ follows an own information set $I$ on one path, all nodes
of $J$ remember $I$ and lie in that continuation.

Starting from any $\varepsilon$-constrained own continuation at $I$,
replace own decisions from latest to earliest by $b_i^\varepsilon$,
ordering them by the number of remembered own past decisions. At each
replacement, future own choices already agree with $b_i^\varepsilon$;
the conditional belief at that information set is still
$\mu_J^\varepsilon$ by cancellation. Its effect on payoff from $I$ is
the nonnegative reaching probability times its nonnegative local gain.
Unreachable or unrelated sets have no effect. Finitely many replacements
prove full constrained continuation optimality at every $I$.

Take $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ and a convergent subsequence of behavior
and Bayes beliefs in their compact product of simplexes. Its limit is
consistent. Approximate any proposed continuation $\delta$ by
$\delta^j_{Ia}=\varepsilon_j+(1-|A(I)|\varepsilon_j)\delta_{Ia}$.
Passing to the limit in the finite polynomial continuation comparisons
gives~\eqref{eq:SR}. This constructs a sequential equilibrium. If there
are no strategic information sets the empty assessment already suffices.

At fixed primitives the consistent assessments form the closure of the
Bayes graph, a closed subset of compact simplexes. The finitely many
pure-plan inequalities in~\eqref{eq:SR} are closed. Their intersection
is therefore compact, proving the lemma. The particular perturbed agent
equilibria used for existence do not restrict the arbitrary tremble
witnesses allowed in~\eqref{eq:consistency}.
\end{proof}

To see why fixing Nature matters, let its two states have probabilities
$p,1-p$. A player observes the state and enters with probabilities
$1/2$ in the first and $p^2$ in the second. As $p\downarrow0$, the
posterior of the first state after entry tends to one. At the fixed
parameter $p=0$, it is impossible and its posterior is zero whenever
entry is reached by strategic trembles. Closing a union over $p$ gives
the wrong beliefs. Our support convention and~\eqref{eq:consistency}
keep those operations distinct.

\section{All equilibria, explicit selection and finite certificates}\label{sec:selection}

Fixed-fiber compactness and continuity give attained values
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrema}
 L_a(\theta)=\min_{e:\Gamma_a(\theta,e)}W_a(\theta,e),\qquad
 U_a(\theta)=\max_{e:\Gamma_a(\theta,e)}W_a(\theta,e).
\end{equation}
The graph of $L_a$, for example, says there is an equilibrium with welfare
$l$ and every equilibrium has welfare at least $l$; the maximum is analogous.
The strong comparison~\eqref{eq:strong} is exactly $L_1\ge U_0$.
These formulas are inputs to the effective algorithm in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, not its final unevaluated answer.

For each $\theta$, lexicographically minimize the assessment coordinates
over the welfare-minimizer set to obtain $e_a^-(\theta)$, and similarly
obtain $e_a^+(\theta)$ over welfare maximizers. Repeated coordinate
minimization on nonempty compact sets gives unique points. Their graphs
are finite real formulas and hence semialgebraic. They are Borel: the
inverse image of each rational open box is a semialgebraic projection,
and countably many such boxes generate the topology. On the finitely
many support strata use their tagged assessment coordinates.

For any fixed semialgebraic function $\eta_a:\Theta\to[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-kernel}
 \kappa_a(\,\cdot\mid\theta)
 =(1-\eta_a(\theta))\delta_{e_a^-(\theta)}
          +\eta_a(\theta)\delta_{e_a^+(\theta)}
\end{equation}
is an explicit supported Borel probability kernel with welfare
$(1-\eta_a)L_a+\eta_a U_a$. Its weights must be fixed as part of the
comparison, not selected after seeing which welfare sign is desirable.
An arbitrary supplied non-effective kernel is not thereby a finite
algorithmic input. We can, however, treat the predeclared class of
\emph{all} supported Borel kernels exactly by the following theorem.

Fix $\theta$ and set
$X=\mathcal E_0(\theta)\times\mathcal E_1(\theta)$. Let
$o:X\to\Delta_d$ be the complete law of a declared finite-category
observation and let $w:X\to\R^k$ record target welfare means or
differences. Both maps are continuous in assessments. In the application
they are \emph{regime-separable}: for instance, observations containing
regime labels have probabilities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separable}
 o_{a,j}(e_0,e_1)=r_a P^a_j(e_a),\qquad
 w(e_0,e_1)=W_1(e_1)-W_0(e_0),\qquad r_0+r_1=1.
\end{equation}
Other coordinates may be any specified affine combinations of marginal
regime moments. Given marginal selectors $\kappa_0,\kappa_1$, their product
is a bookkeeping measure on $X$ with exactly these moments. Conversely any
measure on $X$ supplies its two marginals. Because of separability, replacing
it by their product leaves all the displayed quantities unchanged. No
cross-world dependence is restricted or inferred. For genuinely joint
pair observations this equivalence need not hold, so they are not included
without separately declaring a joint-selection model.

\begin{theorem}[Finite selection certificates]\label{thm:atoms}
For any Borel probability measure $\nu$ on $X$, its vector
$\int(o,w)\,d\nu$ has a representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atoms}
 \sum_{j=1}^{d+k} t_j\bigl(o(x_j),w(x_j)\bigr),\qquad
 t_j\ge0,\quad\sum_jt_j=1,\quad x_j\in X.
\end{equation}
Conversely each representation is attained by supported marginal
selection kernels. At fixed feasible observation law $p$ and $k=1$,
the attainable expected welfare values form a compact interval, whose
endpoints each have a representation with at most $d$ atoms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $T(x)=(o(x),w(x))$. Its compact image lies in the affine hyperplane
$\sum_i o_i=1$, of dimension at most $d+k-1$. A convex combination with
more than $d+k$ positive weights has an affine dependence $v\ne0$:
$\sum_jv_j=0$ and $\sum_jv_jT(x_j)=0$. Such $v$ has both signs.
Vary weights $t_j+s v_j$ until a first weight becomes zero; they remain
nonnegative with the same sum and moment. Iteration reduces every finite
combination to~\eqref{eq:atoms}. It follows that $\co T(X)$ is the
continuous image of the compact set $\Delta_{d+k}\times X^{d+k}$ and
is compact.

If $y=\int T\,d\nu$ were outside it, let $c$ be its closest point.
Comparison of squared distances on segments from $c$ to $z\in\co T(X)$
gives $(y-c)\cdot(z-c)\le0$. Integrate at $z=T(x)$ to get
$\|y-c\|_2^2\le0$, a contradiction. Thus the integral belongs to this
convex hull and admits the finite representation. Its atomic measure
gives the converse, followed by the marginal/separability argument.

For $k=1$, intersect the compact convex moment set with $o=p$ and project
onto its welfare coordinate. The result is a nonempty compact interval.
At either endpoint start with at most $d+1$ atoms. If all $d+1$ weights
are positive, the $d$-by-$(d+1)$ matrix of their observation vectors has
a nonzero null vector $v$. Its column sums imply $\sum v_j=0$.
Both small positive and negative weight changes preserve $p$. Optimality
in both directions forces $\sum v_j w(x_j)=0$. Increase one direction
until a weight vanishes; the endpoint is preserved with at most $d$ atoms.
\end{proof}

This is not an assumption that the true selector has finite support.
It is a representation of all its relevant observed and welfare quantities.
Nor is a finite certificate at one $\theta$ a measurability loophole:
extend its marginal atomic selectors to all other parameters by the
lexicographic default above. Changing a Borel kernel at a singleton
preserves Borel measurability, so the witness belongs to the predeclared
global class of supported Borel kernels.

\begin{proposition}[Affine observation certificates]\label{prop:dual}
At fixed $\theta$ and feasible $p$, let $U(p)$ be the conditional scalar
upper endpoint in Theorem~\ref{thm:atoms}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 U(p)=\inf\{\lambda\cdot p:\lambda\cdot o(x)\ge w(x)
                                      \text{ for every }x\in X\}.
\end{equation}
An intercept is included because the observation coordinates sum to one.
The infimum need not be attained. Replace $w$ by $-w$ for the lower endpoint.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrating any majorant proves weak duality. For $\varepsilon>0$, the
point $y=(p,U(p)+\varepsilon)$ is outside the compact convex moment set.
Let $c$ be its closest point and write $(b,t)=y-c$. Then
$(b,t)\cdot z\le s=(b,t)\cdot c<(b,t)\cdot y$ for every moment $z$.
Since $(p,U(p))$ is a moment, the strict separation implies $t>0$.
Thus the valid inequality $b\cdot o+t w\le s$ becomes a majorant
with $\lambda_j=(s-b_j)/t$, whose value at $p$ is less than
$U(p)+\varepsilon$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

For a boundary example take $X=[0,1]$, $o(x)=(1-x^2,x^2)$, $w(x)=x$
and $p=(1,0)$. The upper endpoint is zero. An attaining majorant would
have first coefficient zero and require $\lambda_2x^2\ge x$ for all
small positive $x$, which is impossible. Nevertheless
$\varepsilon+b x^2\ge x$ whenever $b\ge1/(4\varepsilon)$.
Thus strict sign bounds admit positive-margin affine certificates, while
weak boundary statements may need a limit or the exact atomic certificate.
We do not promise an attained finite dual multiplier in every case.

 \sum_jt_jo(\theta,e_{0j},e_{1j})=p,\qquad
 \sum_jt_jw(\theta,e_{0j},e_{1j})=z.

\section{A dynamic network with both welfare signs}\label{sec:dynamic}

We now solve a concrete member of the full class. This is not a substitute
for the arbitrary-template theorem. There are three dates, one upstream
firm $U$, incumbent $D_1$ and potential entrant $D_2$. One unit of either
downstream product requires one unit of $U$'s input; capacity is one unit
and one supplier relationship per date. Upstream unit cost is one.
$D_1$'s unit cost is three before investment and two afterwards;
$D_2$'s is $1/2$. These are linear differentiable technologies with
strictly positive marginal costs, unchanged by ownership.

A quasilinear household at each date values one unit of $D_1$'s product
at $v\in[6,7]$, one of $D_2$'s at five, and the outside option at zero.
Its numeraire endowment is at least ten. The protocol has binding posted
retail-price commitments $9/2$ and three, respectively. At most one
product is available; its strictly positive consumer surplus makes buying
the unit uniquely optimal. Allowing continuous quantity in $[0,1]$ and
linear gross utility gives the same demand. Thus actual demand and the
unit-capacity allocation clear, without a financing issue.

A common binding legacy contract requires $U$--$D_1$ delivery at date one
for input payment $3/2$. There is no strategic cancellation at that date,
in either regime. Its real welfare is $v-4$. After it, $D_2$ privately
observes an entry cost $f$: $f_L=1/2$ with probability $\pi$ and
$f_H=3/2$ with probability $1-\pi$, $\pi\in[0,1]$. The type is not
revealed. It publicly enters, paying the real sunk cost, or stays out.
Entry permits production in dates two and three. Then, after observing
entry, $D_1$ can pay $k\in[1/4,3/4]$ for a public irreversible investment
that reduces its cost at both remaining dates. All firms have sufficient
cash, for example ten, to finance every listed action.

At each remaining date $U$ chooses an eligible partner or no trade, and
offers that partner one of the prescribed two-part contracts:
\[
 D_1:\ (\text{unit price},\text{fixed fee})=(1,1/2),\qquad
 D_2:\ (1,1).
\]
The recipient accepts and receives the unit for production, or rejects
and there is no trade that date. The fixed fees are transfers, not real
costs. Entry makes a rematch from the legacy $D_1$ link to $D_2$ feasible.
In integration the joint owner controls $U$ and $D_1$ and maximizes their
combined profit, retaining the same capacity, menu and outside opportunities.
There is no discounting. The same physical economy is being compared;
the prices and finite bargaining menu are declared constraints, not
claimed solutions of unrestricted bargaining.

\begin{proposition}[Every-equilibrium dynamic frontier]\label{prop:dynamic}
Every sequential equilibrium of this economy has welfare
\begin{align}\label{eq:dynamic}
 W_1&=3v-10-k,\\
 W_0&=(v-4)+\pi\frac{13}{2}+(1-\pi)\{2(v-3)-k\},\nonumber\\
 \Delta:=W_1-W_0&=\pi(2v-25/2-k).\nonumber
\end{align}
The exact strong-dominance region is
$\pi=0$ or $2v\ge25/2+k$. For $\pi>0$ the strict inequalities give
strict benefit or harm in every equilibrium. In separation, low-cost
entry causes rematching and prevents investment; in integration both
types stay out and the incumbent invests. The foreclosure is sequentially
credible even after an off-path entrant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An entered $D_2$ strictly accepts: per-date profit is
$3-1/2-2=1/2$. Invested $D_1$ strictly accepts, earning
$9/2-2-3/2=1$. Uninvested $D_1$ is indifferent between accepting and
rejecting at zero profit; retain all its off-path ties.

In separation, if $D_2$ entered, $U$ strictly prefers its profit one
to the at-most-$1/2$ from $D_1$ and zero from no trade, at both dates.
$D_1$ cannot gain sales by investing and strictly avoids losing $k$.
If no entry occurred, investment gives $D_1$ the strictly positive
two-date profit $2-k$; not investing yields zero under every acceptance
tie. It therefore invests, and $U$ strictly supplies it at each date.
$D_2$ anticipates entry profit $1-f$, so its low type strictly enters
and its high type strictly stays out. Beliefs about the sunk $f$ do
not affect any subsequent payoff comparison.

Under integration an internal sale yields joint profit $1/2$ without
investment and $3/2$ with it; supplying entered $D_2$ yields one.
After entry, not investing gives two from supplying $D_2$ twice, while
investing gives $3-k>2$ from using $D_1$ twice. After no entry,
investment gives $3-k>1$, the uninvested two-date profit. Thus the
owner invests after either history and strictly uses its own division.
Either entrant would consequently lose its positive entry cost, so both
strictly stay out. Every on-path physical decision is thereby forced
in any sequential equilibrium. Harmless off-path acceptance ties and
consistent beliefs complete an equilibrium by Lemma~\ref{lem:existence};
they cannot alter the strict choices. At $\pi=0$ or one, omit only
Nature-impossible type nodes; the same calculations apply.

Two invested-$D_1$ periods have real welfare $H=2(v-3)-k$.
Two $D_2$ periods after low-cost entry have welfare
$2(5-1-1/2)-1/2=13/2$. Add the common date-one $v-4$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:dynamic}. Equivalently on the low-type branch integration
raises consumer gross value by $2(v-5)$, raises downstream real costs
by three, incurs $k$ and saves entry cost $1/2$. Upstream costs are
unchanged and domestic transfers cancel. This yields the same difference.
\end{proof}

At $(v,k)=(6,1/2)$ the change is $-\pi$, and at $(7,1/2)$ it is
$+\pi$. Thus favorable or adverse welfare is not manufactured by an
equilibrium-selection convention.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp contract/investment observation set]\label{cor:dynamic-data}
Suppose observations contain separated-regime entry, all partner and
contract-payment histories and the physical investment indicator, but
not $v$ or the real cost $k$. An exact compatible observation law identifies
$\pi=p\in[0,1]$ and has sharp welfare-change set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-id}
 \mathcal I_\Delta(p)=[-5p/4,5p/4].
\end{equation}
If $k$ is also observed, the set is $p[-1/2-k,3/2-k]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The entered, uninvested, rematched path has probability $\pi$ and the
no-entry, invested incumbent path has probability $1-\pi$.
All recorded numerical payments and quantities are fixed. They impose
no additional restriction on $v,k$ in their declared rectangle.
The affine factor $2v-25/2-k$ attains $-5/4$ at $(6,3/4)$ and
$5/4$ at $(7,1/4)$, and every intermediate value on their connecting
segment. Fixing $k$ gives the second interval. Integrated observations
alone do not identify $\pi$, since their physical path is always the
same; a sampling design using both regimes needs positive probability
of observing separation for the claimed identification.
\end{proof}

\section{Continuous fixed-link benchmark and a second sign frontier}\label{sec:cournot}

For comparison, the prior~\cite{prior} proves the homogeneous linear-price
benchmark with one upstream firm and $n\ge2$ Cournot downstream firms.
Write $a=A-c_u-c_d>0$ for linear inverse demand $A-Q$ and positive unit
costs. Separation chooses markup $h=a/2$ and gives
$Q_S=na/[2(n+1)]$. Integration with one downstream division has all
optimal $h\ge a/2$, forecloses the others and gives $Q_I=a/2$.
For completeness, the downstream first-order and boundary conditions
give $q_j=(a-h)/(n+1)$ in separation for $0\le h<a$, and zero
quantities for $h\ge a$. Under integration and $h<a/2$,
$q_1=[a+(n-1)h]/(n+1)$ and $q_j=(a-2h)/(n+1)$ for rivals;
above it only $q_1=a/2$ remains. Upstream separate profit is
$nh(a-h)/(n+1)$ for $0\le h\le a$ and zero for $h\ge a$,
while integrated profit below the threshold is
\[
 \frac{a^2+(n-1)(n+3)ah-(n-1)(n+3)h^2}{(n+1)^2},
\]
strictly increasing up to $a/2$, and constant $a^2/4$ thereafter.
These concave own-quantity conditions and all boundary cases determine
the unique quantities. Real welfare $aQ-Q^2/2$ gives the prior's exact
gain $a^2(2n+3)/[8(n+1)^2]>0$. This known result remains credited.

Here is a different full parametric calculation for unequal costs.
Let there be two downstream firms and put $a_i=A-c_u-c_{di}>0$.
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 r=a_2/a_1\in[3/2,5/2].
\end{equation}
Separate $U$ posts a common wholesale markup $h\ge0$; after integration
it posts $h$ only to $D_2$ and supplies its own division at real cost.
Quantities are simultaneous Cournot choices. The combined owner's
wholesale receipts from its rival are held fixed when it changes its
own simultaneous quantity. Bound $h$ on any compact interval containing
$[0,2a_2]$ and quantities on $[0,A]$; all computed optima lie inside
the latter upper bound. Pure subgame-perfect equilibrium is the declared
concept for this continuous-action benchmark.

\begin{proposition}[Asymmetric fixed-link frontier]\label{prop:cournot}
Every equilibrium in the stated class has separated quantities
$q_{1S}=0$, $q_{2S}=a_2/4$, and integrated quantities
\[
 q_{1I}=\frac{7a_1-2a_2}{10},\qquad
 q_{2I}=\frac{2(a_2-a_1)}5.
\]
The welfare difference is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cournot-gap}
 \Delta=\frac{a_1^2}{800}(129r^2-528r+524).
\end{equation}
It is negative precisely between
$r_-=(264-10\sqrt{21})/129$ and
$r_+=(264+10\sqrt{21})/129$, and nonnegative on the rest of
\eqref{eq:ratio}, including the two equality points.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When both separated firms produce, their unique first-order solution is
$q_1=(2a_1-a_2-h)/3$, $q_2=(2a_2-a_1-h)/3$.
For $h\ge2a_1-a_2$ only $D_2$ remains with
$q_2=(a_2-h)/2$ until $h=a_2$, after which both are zero.
If the first threshold is negative the one-firm branch starts at zero.
Strict concavity in own quantity and the zero-quantity derivative tests
justify these complete active-set cases.
The both-active upstream profit is at most $(a_1+a_2)^2/24$;
the one-active profit $h(a_2-h)/2$ has unique maximum $a_2^2/8$
at $h_S=a_2/2$, in that branch. Since $(1+r)^2<3r^2$ for
$r\ge3/2$, the latter strictly beats all both-active choices.

Under integration the both-active quantities are
$q_1=(2a_1-a_2+h)/3$, $q_2=(2a_2-a_1-2h)/3$.
For $r>2$ and $h\le a_2-2a_1$, only $D_2$ is active; for
$h\ge a_2-a_1/2$, only $D_1$ is active with $q_1=a_1/2$.
The boundary formulas agree. The middle-branch joint profit is
\[
 q_1^2+hq_2
  =\frac{(2a_1-a_2)^2+(a_1+4a_2)h-5h^2}{9}.
\]
Its unique maximum $h_I=(a_1+4a_2)/10$ is strictly between the
two relevant thresholds on~\eqref{eq:ratio}. The low only-$D_2$
branch is increasing up to its boundary, because
$a_2-2a_1<a_2/2$; the high only-$D_1$ branch is constant
$a_1^2/4$. Both equal the middle formula at the boundary and are
strictly below its interior maximum. This proves global optimality
and the displayed quantities, retaining all cases and off-path subgames.

Real welfare is $a_1q_1+a_2q_2-(q_1+q_2)^2/2$. Substitution yields
\[
 W_S=7a_2^2/32,\qquad
 W_I=(131a_1^2-132a_1a_2+76a_2^2)/200.
\]
Their difference is~\eqref{eq:cournot-gap}. Its quadratic has the
two stated roots, both strictly inside $[3/2,5/2]$, and positive leading
coefficient. The sign characterization follows. At $r=2$ the loss
is $-a_1^2/50$, despite higher total output: the integrated firm
revives its less efficient division and diverts output from its rival.
\end{proof}

There is also an exact same-data example. Fix $A=1$, $c_u=1/10$,
$c_{d2}=3/20$, so $a_2=3/4$, and let $a_1\in[3/10,1/2]$.
Every separated equilibrium has the same complete vector of wholesale
price $19/40$, retail price $13/16$, quantities $(0,3/16)$,
upstream profit $9/128$ and downstream profits $(0,9/256)$.
Thus these observations do not identify the inactive firm's cost.
At $a_1=3/8$ integration changes welfare by $-9/3200$; at $a_1=1/2$
it changes it by $89/12800$. More exactly its identified set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cournot-id}
 [-189/67072,\ 89/12800].
\end{equation}
Indeed the change as a function of $a_1$ is
$(524a_1^2-396a_1+1161/16)/800$, a convex quadratic with minimum
at $99/262$. The other endpoint $a_1=3/10$ gives $369/320000$;
continuity yields every value in~\eqref{eq:cournot-id}. This is a
sharp identified set for the explicitly declared observations, not a
claim about all possible data on real firm costs.

\section{Finite-sample inference for the full identified set}\label{sec:inference}

Declare independent market episodes, including independent equilibrium
selection before Nature in each episode, and a fixed finite observation
map with $d$ categories. Then the observations are iid with law
$p\in\Delta_d$. Dependence within one dynamic market is already included
in the full episode. A persistent hidden selector across all episodes
would be a different sampling experiment and is not silently treated
as iid. The primitive $\theta$ remains common across both regimes.

For rational $0<\alpha<1$, observed counts $n_j$ and $b=\alpha/(2d)$,
define the binomial tail polynomials
\[
 U_s(q)=\sum_{l=s}^n\binom nl q^l(1-q)^{n-l},\qquad
 L_s(q)=\sum_{l=0}^s\binom nl q^l(1-q)^{n-l}
\]
and the closed confidence set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-confidence}
 C_n=\{p\in\Delta_d:U_{n_j}(p_j)\ge b, 
                             L_{n_j}(p_j)\ge b\quad\forall j\}.
\end{equation}
There is no division at zero probabilities or extreme counts; the
displayed polynomials define all those boundaries.

\begin{theorem}[Honest coverage of observational welfare ambiguity]\label{thm:inference}
For every maintained law $p^*$,
$\Prb_{p^*}(p^*\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha$. The computable semialgebraic set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w-confidence}
 J_n=\{z:\exists p\in C_n,\ \exists\theta\in\Theta:
                                      \mathsf C(\theta,p,z)\}
\end{equation}
satisfies
$\Prb_{p^*}\{\mathcal I(p^*)\subseteq J_n\}\ge1-\alpha$.
Thus it simultaneously covers the entire identified set, not only
the value from a favorable equilibrium. Empty $J_n$ is a model
incompatibility outcome, not a vacuous favorable sign conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one cell $N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p_j^*)$.
$U_s(p_j^*)$ is nonincreasing in $s$. If the event
$U_{N_j}(p_j^*)<b$ is nonempty, it is $N_j\ge s_0$ for its least
such index and has probability $U_{s_0}(p_j^*)<b$.
The lower-tail failure has probability below $b$ by the analogous
monotonicity of $L_s$. A union bound over $2d$ events proves the first
claim without independence of the counts. Equality at the threshold
is retained. On $p^*\in C_n$, any certificate for any member of
$\mathcal I(p^*)$ is also a certificate in~\eqref{eq:w-confidence}
using the same common $\theta$, proving the second claim. Its finite
polynomial description follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:atoms} and
quantifier elimination, including the binomial inequalities.
\end{proof}

Scalar negative, zero and positive attainability tests in $J_n$ give
exact confidence-qualified sign statements. The entire set may be
disconnected; replacing it by an interval hull sacrifices sharpness.
No universal shrinking welfare width is claimed: even an exactly known
observation law can have the nonzero widths in~\eqref{eq:dynamic-id}
and~\eqref{eq:cournot-id}. This separates sampling uncertainty from
failure of identification.

In the dynamic example, sample separated-regime episodes and record
entry, partner changes and investment. The data reduce to a binomial count for
$\pi$. If its exact inversion interval is $[\pi_L,\pi_U]$, the
confidence identified union is exactly
$[-5\pi_U/4,5\pi_U/4]$. A positive lower bound $\pi_L$ cannot remove
the structural sign ambiguity. This is a worked calculation on a
declared observation model; no empirical dataset or held-out outcome
is fabricated.
