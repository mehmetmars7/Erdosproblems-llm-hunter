\section{Question and scope}
The catalogue asks for coupling and common-noise classes that restore strong uniqueness in mean-field games previously admitting several equilibria. Here we give a quantitative obstruction: a smooth bounded, translation-invariant terminal coupling on the circle has several strong equilibria for every intensity of ordinary additive common Brownian noise. This specifies a class that a positive regularization theorem must exclude. It does not exclude state-dependent vector fields or other population-noise models.

The source survey \cite{CD} explains the dimensional difficulty with ordinary common noise. The construction and calculations below are supplied here, without a novelty claim. We work on $\mathbb T=\R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$; rescaling gives the unit torus. Write $\ell(dx)=dx/(2\pi)$ and let $P_t$ be the heat semigroup with generator $\partial_{xx}$. In particular $P_t\cos x=e^{-t}\cos x$. Fix $T>0$, $\kappa>0$, and
\[
 F=0,\qquad G(x,\mu)=-\kappa\int\cos(x-y)\,\mu(dy),\qquad m_0=\ell.
\]
This coupling is smooth in $x$ and in its two Fourier moments. It is bounded by $\kappa$. It is nonmonotone: for a signed difference $\eta=\mu-\nu$,
\[
 \int[G(x,\mu)-G(x,\nu)]\eta(dx)
 =-\kappa\left[\left(\int\cos x\,d\eta\right)^2+
 \left(\int\sin x\,d\eta\right)^2\right].
\]
The player minimizes $\E[\int_0^T\alpha_t^2/2\,dt+G(X_T,\mu_T)]$.

\section{An explicit deterministic fixed point}
For $q\in[0,1]$, put $a=\kappa q/2$, $H_a(x)=e^{a\cos x}$, and
\[
 h_q(t,x)=P_{T-t}H_a(x),\qquad u_q(t,x)=-2\log h_q(t,x).
\]
These functions are smooth and $h_q$ is bounded away from zero. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_tu_q+\partial_{xx}u_q-\tfrac12(\partial_xu_q)^2=0,
 \qquad u_q(T,x)=-\kappa q\cos x.
\]
Let $X^q$ start uniformly and solve
\[
 dX_t^q=-\partial_xu_q(t,X_t^q)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB_t.
\]
Its drift is bounded and Lipschitz, so the equation has a unique strong solution. Denote its deterministic marginal law by $m_t^q$.

\begin{lemma}[Verification and terminal density]
For every progressively measurable control with finite expected quadratic energy, including controls adapted to additional independent Brownian motions,
\[
 \E\left[\int_0^T\tfrac12\alpha_t^2dt-\kappa q\cos X_T\right]
 =\int u_q(0,x)\ell(dx)
 +\tfrac12\E\int_0^T|\alpha_t+\partial_xu_q(t,X_t)|^2dt.
\]
The terminal density of $m_T^q$ with respect to $\ell$ is
\[
 f_q(y)=H_a(y)P_T\left[(P_TH_a)^{-1}\right](y).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to $u_q(t,X_t)$, use its displayed equation, take expectations, and complete the square. Bounded derivatives and finite energy justify localization followed by passage to the limit; the stochastic integral has mean zero.

For the density, let $p_t(x,y)$ be the heat kernel with respect to $\ell$. For fixed initial point $x$, define the transition density
\[
 p_{t-s}(x,y)\frac{h_q(t,y)}{h_q(s,x)},\qquad 0\le s<t\le T.
\]
The heat equation and semigroup identity show it integrates to one and obeys composition. Differentiating its action on a smooth test function at $t=s$ gives generator $\partial_{xx}+2(\partial_x\log h_q)\partial_x$, which is the displayed drift. Uniqueness of the diffusion therefore identifies this transition law. Set $s=0,t=T$ and average $x$ over $\ell$. Symmetry of the heat kernel gives the formula for $f_q$.
\end{proof}

Define $\Phi(q)=\int\cos y\,f_q(y)\ell(dy)$. Evenness implies $\int\sin y\,f_q(y)\ell(dy)=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit bifurcation condition]
If
\[
 \kappa>\frac{4}{1-e^{-2T}},
\]
then there is a $q_*\in(0,1)$ satisfying $\Phi(q_*)=q_*$. Both $m^0$ and $m^{q_*}$ are deterministic mean-field equilibria, and their terminal laws differ.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The density formula and positivity make $\Phi$ continuously differentiable. At $a=0$, $H_a=1+a\cos+O(a^2)$ uniformly, hence
\[
 P_TH_a=1+ae^{-T}\cos+O(a^2),\quad
 P_T[(P_TH_a)^{-1}]=1-ae^{-2T}\cos+O(a^2).
\]
Consequently $f_q=1+(\kappa q/2)(1-e^{-2T})\cos+O(q^2)$, so
\[
 \Phi(0)=0,\qquad \Phi'(0)=\frac\kappa4(1-e^{-2T})>1.
\]
Thus $\Phi(q)-q>0$ for all sufficiently small positive $q$. Since $f_1$ is a strictly positive density and $\cos y<1$ on a set of positive measure, $\Phi(1)<1$. The intermediate value theorem supplies $q_*$. At this fixed point the actual coupling is $G(x,m_T^{q_*})=-\kappa q_*\cos x$, so the verification lemma proves optimality and the diffusion supplies consistency. At $q=0$, the drift is zero and every marginal is uniform; its coupling is zero. Its cosine moment is zero, unlike that of $m_T^{q_*}$.
\end{proof}

This is a construction of actual equilibria, not merely nonzero solutions of a linearized equation. For example $T=1$ and $\kappa=5$ satisfy the strict inequality since $5(1-e^{-2})>4$.

\section{Transporting the equilibria by common noise}
Fix any $\sigma\in\R$ and an independent common Brownian motion $W$. Write $\tau_z\mu$ for translation of a law by $z$ modulo $2\pi$.

\begin{theorem}[No additive-noise threshold in this family]
Under the strict condition in Proposition 2.2, the game
\[
 dX_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t+\sigma dW_t
\]
with the above $F,G,m_0$ has at least two distinct strong equilibria, for every finite $\sigma$. More generally the same conclusion holds for finitely or countably many constant vector fields with square-summable coefficients. In particular, arbitrarily large additive common-noise variance does not restore the strong uniqueness requested in the catalogue.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For either deterministic equilibrium $m^q$, set
\[
 \mu_t^q=\tau_{\sigma W_t}m_t^q,\quad
 X_t=X_t^q+\sigma W_t,\quad
 \alpha_t=-\partial_xu_q(t,X_t-\sigma W_t).
\]
The control has finite energy and the conditional law of $X_t$ given the common noise is $\mu_t^q$, because $X^q$ depends only on $X_0,B$.

For a deviation $\alpha$, put $Z_t=X_t-\sigma W_t$. Then $dZ_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t$. The same subtraction is valid for controls depending on $W$; no conditional independence of a deviating state is presumed. Translation invariance gives, path by path,
\[
 G(X_T,\mu_T^q)=G(Z_T,m_T^q)=-\kappa q\cos Z_T.
\]
The verification lemma in the enlarged filtration proves the candidate optimal against every allowed deviation. For $q=0$ the terminal measure is uniform; for $q=q_*$ the absolute value of its first Fourier coefficient is $q_*>0$, invariant under translation. Thus the two flows on the same probability space differ. For countably many constants, their square-summable stochastic sum is a continuous Brownian translation with the summed variance, and the argument is identical.
\end{proof}

\section{What this does and does not establish}
The noise moves the center of the coordination pattern but never its magnitude. The nonuniqueness is therefore not removed by observing or conditioning on the common path. The example obeys bounded smooth couplings and ordinary conditional-law consistency; it does not insert an unexplained noise directly into the space of measures.

A positive theorem could still use state-dependent vector fields that change shape, or a coupling class excluding translation-invariant coordination. Establishing such a theorem uniformly over initial laws and horizons, with a game exhibiting deterministic multiplicity, remains open here. The calculation does not identify the exact deterministic bifurcation threshold below the sufficient condition, nor does it classify all equilibria.
