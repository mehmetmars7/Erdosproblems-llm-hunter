The unrestricted target is a complete allocation of arbitrary additive mixed items that is both EF1 and Pareto optimal. The one-deletion condition permits deleting either an item from the envied bundle or an item from the envying agent's own bundle:
\[
 u_i(A_i)\geq u_i(A_j),\quad\text{or}\quad
 u_i(A_i)\geq u_i(A_j\setminus\{g\}),\quad\text{or}\quad
 u_i(A_i\setminus\{g\})\geq u_i(A_j).
\]
The next result treats signed proportional common items together with arbitrary exclusive goods. It permits genuinely different valuations across agents, although not arbitrary item-dependent comparative advantages.

\begin{theorem}
Let $n\geq1$, let all utilities be additive, and partition the finite item set as $M=C\sqcup P$. Fix $c_i>0$. Each common item $g\in C$ has a real signed weight $w_g$ and value $u_{ig}=c_iw_g$. Each exclusive good $h\in P$ has a designated owner $o(h)$, value $u_{o(h),h}\geq0$, and value zero to every other agent. Then a complete EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation exists. Moreover, it maximizes the strictly positive weighted welfare $\sum_i u_i(A_i)/c_i$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First allocate only $C$. For a partition $B$ define the signed load $\ell_i=\sum_{g\in B_i}w_g$. Among the finitely many partitions choose one minimizing $\sum_i\ell_i^2$. Fix $i,j$ with $d=\ell_j-\ell_i>0$. If some positive item $g\in B_j$ has $w_g\geq d$, deleting it eliminates $i$'s envy. If some negative item $g\in B_i$ has $-w_g\geq d$, deleting it from $i$'s own bundle eliminates the envy.

Suppose neither kind of item exists. There must nevertheless be a positive item in $B_j$ or a negative item in $B_i$: otherwise $\ell_j\leq0\leq\ell_i$, contrary to $d>0$. A positive item of weight $a\in(0,d)$ can be moved from $j$ to $i$. This changes the objective by
\[
 (\ell_i+a)^2+(\ell_j-a)^2-\ell_i^2-\ell_j^2=2a(a-d)<0.
\]
A negative item of weight $-a\in(-d,0)$ can instead be moved from $i$ to $j$, producing exactly the same change. Both contradict minimality. Therefore $B$ is EF1 in the common signed weights, and multiplication by $c_i>0$ gives EF1 for each agent's evaluations.

Now give every exclusive good to its designated owner, obtaining $A$. For $i\neq j$, the added goods in $A_j$ have value zero to $i$, while those in $A_i$ have nonnegative value to $i$. Every no-envy inequality or deletion witness previously established for $B_i,B_j$ therefore still holds, including an own-bundle deletion witness: its deleted item lies in $C$, so the new exclusive goods remain in the own bundle.

For any complete allocation $D$, the common-item contribution to $\sum_i u_i(D_i)/c_i$ is the constant $\sum_{g\in C}w_g$. The contribution of an exclusive good $h$ is at most $u_{o(h),h}/c_{o(h)}$, attained in $A$. Thus $A$ maximizes that weighted welfare. Since all welfare weights $1/c_i$ are strictly positive, a Pareto improvement would strictly increase it, an impossibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the minimization argument does not prove the general case]
Moving a common item changes two normalized loads by equal and opposite amounts. This conservation gives the exact quadratic improvement $2a(a-d)$. With arbitrary $u_{ig}$, a transfer can lower total weighted welfare and change the two agents' loads by unrelated amounts. Minimizing a convex load objective then neither gives this EF1 contradiction nor certifies Pareto optimality. Exclusive goods avoid the issue because their allocation improves only the recipient's fairness inequalities and is independently welfare maximizing.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Source relationship and remaining gap.}
The survey~\cite{survey}, Section 4.1, Open Question 2, states the additive EF1-plus-PO question and discusses several settled special classes. The load argument here is an elementary restricted derivation, not a claim that the identical-valuation phenomenon is new. It supplies no polynomial-time algorithm for the quadratic minimization, and the existence question for general heterogeneous signed additive values remains unresolved by this note.
