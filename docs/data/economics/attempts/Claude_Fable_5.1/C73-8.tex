\begin{abstract}
The hand proof for this problem characterizes the bounded Borel payoffs whose one-sided-information repeated game has a value at every prior by a local convex certificate condition on the public history tree. This note reports a complete Lean~4 formalization of that proof against Mathlib: both directions of the characterization, the exact representation of the value gap, and the one external theorem the hand proof cites, the Borel superhedging duality of T'Joens and De Bock, which is proved here on finite alphabets for payoffs with values in $[0,1]$. The development has no \texttt{sorry}, no warnings, assumes nothing beyond Mathlib, and uses only the standard axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{The statement}

Fix nonempty finite sets $K$, $I$, $J$. Nature draws $k\sim p\in\Delta(K)$ and reveals it to the informed player. The players choose actions in $I$ and $J$ simultaneously and observe the pair; $\mathcal H$ is the set of finite histories and $\Omega=(I\times J)^{\N}$ the set of plays. Behavioural strategies are $\sigma:K\times\mathcal H\to\Delta(I)$ and $\tau:\mathcal H\to\Delta(J)$. For a Borel $F:K\times\Omega\to[0,1]$ put $\gamma_p(\sigma,\tau)=\mathbb E_{p,\sigma,\tau}F$, $\lv_F(p)=\sup_\sigma\inf_\tau\gamma_p$ and $\uv_F(p)=\inf_\tau\sup_\sigma\gamma_p$. A general bounded Borel $f$ is reduced to this case by a positive affine normalization, which preserves equality of the two values.

\begin{definition}[capital arrays at prior $p$]
Arrays $q(h)\in\Delta(K)$, $A_k(h),B(h)\in[0,1]$ for $h\in\mathcal H$ with $q(\varnothing)=p$ and:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(BF)] $q(hij)$ does not depend on $j$; write $\bar q_i(h)$.
\item[(UC)] for every $h$ there is $y\in\Delta(J)$ with $\sum_j y_j A_k(hij)\le A_k(h)$ for all $k,i$.
\item[(LC)] for every $h$ there is $w\in\Delta(I)$ with $q(h)=\sum_i w_i\bar q_i(h)$ and $\sum_i w_i B(hij)\le B(h)$ for all $j$.
\item[(UB)] $\liminf_t A_k(\omega|t)\ge F(k,\omega)$ for all $k$ and all $\omega$.
\item[(LB)] $\liminf_t B(\omega|t)\ge 1-G_q(\omega)$ for all $\omega$, where $G_q(\omega)=\liminf_t\sum_k q_k(\omega|t)F(k,\omega)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Conditions (UC) and (LC) are the two convex-hull intersections of the hand proof, written through their convex coefficients: a point of the convex hull of finitely many vectors is a convex combination of them.

\emph{Condition (C).} For every rational prior $p$ and every $n\ge1$ there are capital arrays at $p$ with $\sum_k p_kA_k(\varnothing)+B(\varnothing)\le1+1/n$.

\begin{theorem}[exact characterization]\label{main}
$F$ satisfies (C) if and only if $\lv_F(p)=\uv_F(p)$ for every $p\in\Delta(K)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[exact gap]\label{gap}
For every $p$, the infimum of $\sum_kp_kA_k(\varnothing)+B(\varnothing)-1$ over capital arrays at $p$ equals $\uv_F(p)-\lv_F(p)$.
\end{corollary}

This is the direction ``(C) implies a value'' of Theorem~\ref{main}, including the passage from rational priors to all priors.

\subsection*{The Borel superhedging duality}
\texttt{BorelDuality X} is Lemma~2 of the hand proof (T'Joens and De Bock \cite{TDB}, Theorems 2 and 13 with definition (3)) on the finite alphabet tree over $X$, for $[0,1]$-valued Borel payoffs: if each history $h$ carries a nonempty closed convex set $P_h$ of probability vectors and $W:\Omega\to[0,1]$ is Borel, then for every $\delta>0$ there is a bounded-below $M:\mathcal H\to\R$ with $\sum_z\pi_zM(hz)\le M(h)$ for all $\pi\in P_h$, $\liminf_tM(\omega|t)\ge W(\omega)$ for every $\omega$, and $M(\varnothing)<c+\delta$, where $c$ is the supremum of $\mathbb E W$ over the laws of play of policies whose one-step kernels lie in $P_h$. It is proved:
\begin{verbatim}
theorem borelDuality_of_fintype (X : Type*) [Fintype X] [Nonempty X] [DecidableEq X]
    [MeasurableSpace X] [DiscreteMeasurableSpace X] : BorelDuality X
\end{verbatim}
The proof follows the structure of \cite{TDB}. The game-theoretic upper expectation \texttt{ugame} is the infimum of initial capitals of hedges. The easy inequality $\mathbb E_Q W\le M(\varnothing)$ is Fatou's lemma. For finitary payoffs both sides equal the backward-induction value. The compatible kernels form a compact space, expectations of finitary payoffs are continuous in the kernels, and a compactness argument exchanges $\inf_n\sup_Q$ and $\sup_Q\inf_n$, which gives the duality for upper semicontinuous payoffs. The conditional upper expectation is a local supermartingale; a switching strategy that pockets the excess of the current hedge over the conditional upper expectation proves the game-theoretic form of L\'evy's zero-one law, and a truncated countable combination of such hedges gives continuity from below. Finally the hypograph of a Borel payoff is an analytic subset of $\Omega\times\R$ (Mathlib's \texttt{MeasurableSet.analyticSet}), hence the continuous image of Baire space; the fibre suprema over coordinatewise bounded subtrees, chosen recursively by continuity from below, give upper semicontinuous minorants whose upper expectation is within $\varepsilon$ of that of the payoff. This is the capacitability argument of Choquet, carried out directly on Baire space.

\subsection*{Necessity, the gap, and the characterization}
\begin{verbatim}
theorem exists_capital_of_duality (hdual : BorelDuality (I x J)) (F : Payoff K I J)
    {p} (hp : IsProb p) {d : R} (hd : 0 < d) :
    exists c : Capital F p,
      sum k, p k * c.A k [] + c.B [] - 1 < upperValue p F - lowerValue p F + 4 * d

theorem gap_eq' (F : Payoff K I J) {p} (hp : IsProb p) :
    (iInf c : Capital F p, (sum k, p k * c.A k [] + c.B [] - 1)) =
      upperValue p F - lowerValue p F

theorem condC_iff_value' (F : Payoff K I J) :
    CondC F <-> forall p, IsProb p -> lowerValue p F = upperValue p F
\end{verbatim}
Unicode binders are transliterated here (\texttt{exists}, \texttt{sum}, \texttt{iInf}, \texttt{forall}). The first is Section~7 of the hand proof, applied to the proved duality; the second is Corollary~\ref{gap}; the third is Theorem~\ref{main}. Neither of the last two has any hypothesis.

\section{Architecture of the formal proof}

The probabilistic content is reduced to one recursion formula: for the law of a policy, the expectation of a function of the history after $t+1$ steps equals the expectation, after $t$ steps, of its one-step average (\texttt{integral\_pre\_shift\_succ}, from Mathlib's \texttt{trajMeasure}). From it:
\begin{itemize}
\item Lemma~3 of the hand proof (capital can be bounded by one) is \texttt{clip}, proved on the tree by the descent-path argument.
\item Lemma~4 (Bayes flow) is proved in both directions. \texttt{PostData.ofBary} reconstructs $x$ from $(q,w)$, \texttt{PostData.ofStrategy} reconstructs $(q,w)$ from an arbitrary $x$, and the integrated form \texttt{postSum\_eq} shows that the $p$-mixture of state-wise expectations of any history functional equals that of its posterior-weighted average.
\item Identity (PI) is \texttt{integral\_Gq}. On the joint space $K\times\Omega$ the public histories generate a filtration; the posterior is identified with the conditional probability of the state (\texttt{condExp\_stInd}), Lévy's upward theorem gives almost-sure convergence, and the pull-out property gives $\mathbb E F=\mathbb E G_q$.
\item Inequalities (upper) and (lower) are \texttt{Capital.upperValue\_le} and \texttt{Capital.lowerValue\_ge}, through a Fatou lemma for $[0,1]$-valued processes with decreasing expectations.
\item The public law of $(x,\tau)$ is identified with the law of the policy $w\otimes\tau$ (\texttt{PostData.map\_shift\_law\_eq}) by comparing cylinder functionals; this is used to compute the upper expectation in the second application of the duality (\texttt{upperExp\_credB\_le}). The first application uses that the finitely many state components can be chosen independently (\texttt{sum\_upperExp\_le}).
\item The credal sets of both applications are shown nonempty, closed, convex and made of probability vectors (\texttt{Duality.lean}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope}

The formalization follows the hand proof, and the file \texttt{Exact.lean} removes the remaining presentational differences. The convex-hull forms of (UC) and (LC) are proved equivalent to the convex-coefficient forms, so the literal Definition~1 (\texttt{CapitalHull}) yields the same condition (C) (\texttt{condCHull\_iff}). A general bounded Borel payoff $f$ with $a\le f\le b$ is normalized to $F=(f-a)/(b-a)$; the values transform by the same positive affine map, so $f$ has a value at every prior iff $F$ satisfies (C) (\texttt{condC\_iff\_valueB}), and (C) does not depend on the chosen bounds (\texttt{condC\_normalize\_indep}). The duality is also proved for bounded nonnegative payoffs, the form of Lemma~2 (\texttt{borelDualityBdd\_of\_fintype}); the cited theorem covers bounded-below payoffs, which this problem does not use. No external theorem is assumed. The formal theorems do not provide a finite recognition algorithm, and the hand proof does not claim one.
