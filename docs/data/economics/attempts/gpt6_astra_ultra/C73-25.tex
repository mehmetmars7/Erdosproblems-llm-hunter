\section{Target and restriction}
The target concerns the exact finite-state common-noise master equation with the original potential costs unchanged. The corrected-cost minimizing-selection theorem does not answer it \cite[Section 4.3]{CD}. The primary paper explicitly introduces a correction to restore potential structure \cite[Sections 2 and 4]{CeD}. We derive that structural defect directly and prove a conditional minimizing-selection theorem with a specified boundary layer and an explicit missing feedback estimate.

Fix $d\ge3$, $T>0$, $m_0\in\operatorname{int}\Delta_d$, and smooth potentials $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$. Write $f_i=\partial_i\mathcal F$, $g_i=\partial_i\mathcal G$, $H_i(p)=\frac12\sum_{j\ne i}(p_i-p_j)_+^2$, and
\[
 b_i(m,p)=\sum_{j\ne i}[m_j(p_j-p_i)_+-m_i(p_i-p_j)_+],\quad
 A_{jk}(m)=m_j\delta_{jk}-m_jm_k.
\]
For a rate matrix $a$, $b_i(m,a)=\sum_{j\ne i}(m_ja_{ji}-m_i a_{ij})$ instead denotes its linear population drift. The deterministic action is
\[
 J(m,a)=\int_0^T\left[\frac12\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i a_{ij}^2+\mathcal F(m)\right]\dd t+\mathcal G(m(T)).
\]
Its admissible paths are absolutely continuous, start from $m_0$, obey $\dot m=b(m,a)$, and have finite weighted quadratic rate energy.

For clarity, the exact uncorrected regularized equation used below is
\begin{align*}
0={}&\partial_tU_i+\varepsilon A:D^2U_i
       +(b(m,U)+r^\varepsilon(m))\cdot DU_i-H_i(U)+f_i(m)\\
 &+2\varepsilon\sum_jm_j(\partial_iU_i-\partial_jU_i)
       +\sum_{j\ne i}\phi_\varepsilon(m_j)(U_j-U_i),
 \qquad U_i(T,m)=g_i(m),
\end{align*}
where $r_i^\varepsilon=\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)-m_i\sum_j\phi_\varepsilon(m_j)$.
An admissible solution is continuous on the closed time--simplex cylinder,
$C^1$ in time and $C^2$ in the interior, with bounded values and first
derivatives for each fixed $\varepsilon$, and locally bounded second
derivatives. Admissibility also requires the selected population SDE,
with drift $b(m,U^\varepsilon)+r^\varepsilon$ and covariance
$2\varepsilon A(m)\dd t$, to have a unique interior-valued solution.
These are assumptions on the regularization, not existence claims proved here.

\section{The precise diffusion mismatch}
\begin{proposition}\label{defect}
Work in an interior region where the boundary repulsion vanishes. Suppose a smooth scalar function $V$ solves the stochastic planner equation
\[
 0=V_t+\varepsilon\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}V_{jk}-\sum_jm_jH_j(DV)+\mathcal F(m).
\]
Let $U_i=\partial_iV$ for a fixed smooth extension. The component differences of the residual in the uncorrected game master equation are
\[
 R_i-R_d=\varepsilon(V_{ii}-V_{dd}),\quad i<d.
\]
This residual one-form need not be a gradient. For the polynomial $V(m)=m_1^2m_2$ in $d=3$, the diffusion-mismatch one-form on the chart $(m_1,m_2)$ is $2\varepsilon m_2\dd m_1$, whose exterior derivative is $-2\varepsilon\dd m_1\wedge\dd m_2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_i\left(\varepsilon A:D^2V\right)
 =\varepsilon A:D^2U_i+
 \varepsilon\left(V_{ii}-2\sum_jm_jV_{ij}\right).
\]
Also $D_p\sum_jm_jH_j(p)=-b(m,p)$, as follows by differentiating the positive-part squares. Thus differentiating the scalar planner equation produces all the deterministic terms of the game equation, and the extra diffusion term just displayed. The game instead contains
\[
 2\varepsilon\sum_jm_j(\partial_iU_i-\partial_jU_i)
 =2\varepsilon\left(V_{ii}-\sum_jm_jV_{ij}\right).
\]
Their difference is $\varepsilon V_{ii}$. The scalar equation is prescribed on the simplex, so take the tangent derivative $\partial_i-\partial_d$ of that equation; this yields exactly the asserted residual difference. All component representatives here use the fixed extension: an arbitrary population-dependent common shift of $U$ is not being declared a symmetry of the noisy equation. For the polynomial, $V_{11}=2m_2$, $V_{22}=V_{33}=0$; differentiation of the indicated one-form proves its nonzero curl. This polynomial illustrates the operator defect alone; it is not asserted to solve the scalar planner equation for arbitrary prescribed data.
\end{proof}

A factor $\varepsilon$ does not make this harmless without uniform Hessian control. Conversely, a nonzero finite-$\varepsilon$ curl does not prove failure of zero-noise minimizing selection. The proposition diagnoses a blocked argument, not a counterexample to the target.

\section{Verification by a smooth deterministic value}
Suppose, as an additional hypothesis, that a scalar $V\in C^1_tC^2_m([0,T]\times\Delta_d)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{HJB}
 V_t-\sum_i m_iH_i(DV)+\mathcal F(m)=0,\qquad V(T,m)=\mathcal G(m),
\end{equation}
with bounded indicated derivatives. Tangent derivatives, or any fixed smooth extension, give the same formulas below. Set $p=DV$ and
\[
 \mathcal B(m,a,p)=\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i\left[
 \tfrac12a_{ij}^2+a_{ij}(p_j-p_i)+\tfrac12(p_i-p_j)_+^2\right].
\]
\begin{lemma}\label{certificate}
For deterministic admissible paths,
\[
 J(m,a)-V(0,m_0)=\int_0^T\mathcal B(m,a,DV)\dd t\ge0.
\]
The feedback $a_{ij}=(p_i-p_j)_+$ attains equality, so $V(0,m_0)$ is the global minimum. For a continuous semimartingale population satisfying
\[
 \dd m=b(m,a)\dd t+r\dd t+\dd M,\qquad
 \dd\langle M_i,M_j\rangle_t=2\varepsilon A_{ij}(m_t)\dd t,
\]
with bounded rates and $m(0)=m_0$, its expected original action obeys the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{ito}
 \E J(m,a)-V(0,m_0)
 =\E\int_0^T\left[\mathcal B(m,a,DV)+DV\cdot r+\varepsilon A:D^2V\right]\dd t.
\end{equation}
For $a_{ij}=(U_i-U_j)_+$ one further has
\begin{equation}\label{square}
 0\le\mathcal B(m,a,p)\le\frac12\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i\big[(U_i-U_j)-(p_i-p_j)\big]^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The chain rule and \eqref{HJB} give the deterministic identity. For $q=p_i-p_j$,
$\frac12a^2-aq+\frac12q_+^2=\frac12(a-q_+)^2+a(-q)_+\ge0$, with equality at $a=q_+$. The feedback is bounded and Lipschitz in $m$, since $DV$ is Lipschitz and positive part is Lipschitz. Its forward ODE therefore has a unique simplex-valued solution, yielding an admissible equality case.

For the stochastic identity, apply It\^o's formula to $V(t,m_t)$, whose martingale part has zero expectation by boundedness of $DV$ and the quadratic variation. Substitute \eqref{HJB} and add the running costs to obtain \eqref{ito}. Finally put $z=U_i-U_j$, $a=z_+$. If $q,z\ge0$ the scalar gap is $(z-q)^2/2$. If $q,z\le0$ it is zero. If $q\ge0\ge z$, it is $q^2/2\le(z-q)^2/2$. If $z\ge0\ge q$, it is $z^2/2-zq\le(z-q)^2/2$. Multiplying by $m_i$ and summing proves \eqref{square}.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit boundary-layer removal estimate}
Let $\phi_\varepsilon$ be any admissible repulsion family for the exact game satisfying additionally
\begin{equation}\label{layer}
 0\le\phi_\varepsilon\le\kappa,\qquad
 \phi_\varepsilon(r)=0\text{ for }r\ge\delta_\varepsilon,\qquad
 \delta_\varepsilon\downarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is fixed. No rate relation between $\delta_\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon$ is imposed beyond the source's admissibility; that admissibility includes existence and interior uniqueness for each $\varepsilon$. The estimate below does not assert admissibility of every cutoff with \eqref{layer}.

\begin{lemma}\label{boundary}
Suppose $0\le a_{ij}^\varepsilon\le M_*$ uniformly. The repelled population has
\[
 r_i^\varepsilon(m)=\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)-m_i\sum_j\phi_\varepsilon(m_j),
\]
and the common-noise martingale covariance of Lemma~\ref{certificate}. Let $\mu=\min_i m_{0i}$, $C=(d-1)(M_*+\kappa)$ and $\delta_*=(\mu/2)e^{-CT}$. Then
\[
 \Pp\left(\min_{t\le T,i}m_i^\varepsilon(t)<\delta_*\right)
 \le\frac{32d\varepsilon T e^{2CT}}{\mu^2}.
\]
For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ with $\delta_\varepsilon<\delta_*$,
$\E\int_0^T\|r^\varepsilon(m_t^\varepsilon)\|_1\dd t=O(\varepsilon)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The total drift of coordinate $i$ is at least $-Cm_i$, because all incoming flows are nonnegative and its outgoing rate is at most $(d-1)(M_*+\kappa)$. Thus
\[
 m_i(t)\ge e^{-Ct}\left[m_{0i}+\int_0^te^{Cs}\dd M_i(s)\right].
\]
The bracket of $M_i$ is $2\varepsilon m_i(1-m_i)\dd t\le2\varepsilon\dd t$. Doob's inequality gives
$\E\sup_{t\le T}|\int_0^te^{Cs}\dd M_i|^2\le8\varepsilon T e^{2CT}$.
If these martingale suprema are at most $\mu/2$ for all $i$, every coordinate stays above $\delta_*$. Markov's inequality and a union bound give the claimed probability. On this good event all repulsions vanish for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. On its complement, $\|r\|_1\le2\sum_i\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)\le2d\kappa$. Multiplication by $T$ and the probability bound proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{A conditional selection theorem for unchanged costs}
\begin{theorem}\label{selection}
Assume \eqref{HJB}, \eqref{layer}, and admissibility of the exact uncorrected master solutions $U^\varepsilon$. Suppose their rates
$a^\varepsilon_{ij}=(U_i^\varepsilon-U_j^\varepsilon)_+$ are uniformly bounded by $M_*$, and along their population processes
\begin{equation}\label{missing}
 \E\int_0^T\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i^\varepsilon
 \left[(U_i^\varepsilon-U_j^\varepsilon)(t,m_t^\varepsilon)
 -(V_i-V_j)(t,m_t^\varepsilon)\right]^2\dd t\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Then the population laws are tight in $C([0,T],\Delta_d)$, and every weak subsequential limit is supported on globally minimizing law paths of the original deterministic action $J$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{boundary} removes repulsion in expected $L^1$ time norm. The bounded Hessian in \eqref{HJB} gives $|\varepsilon A:D^2V|\le C_V\varepsilon$. Combining \eqref{ito}, \eqref{square}, and \eqref{missing} therefore gives
$\E J(m^\varepsilon,a^\varepsilon)\to V(0,m_0)$.

Both original and repulsion drifts are uniformly bounded. The martingale has expected squared supremum $O(\varepsilon)$ in every coordinate. Thus $m^\varepsilon$ is the sum of a uniformly Lipschitz path starting at $m_0$ and a martingale tending to zero uniformly in probability. Arzel\`a--Ascoli and this approximation give tightness in the continuous-path space.

Here are details for passing the action to the limit without asserting that stochastic paths themselves are deterministic admissible paths. Adjoin the random occupation measures
$\Lambda^\varepsilon(\dd t,\dd a)=\dd t\,\delta_{a^\varepsilon(t)}(\dd a)$ on the compact set $[0,T]\times[0,M_*]^{d(d-1)}$. They have fixed first marginal $\dd t$ and hence form a compact set. Take a joint convergent subsequence with $m^\varepsilon$, and realize it with almost sure uniform convergence of paths and weak convergence of occupation measures. Such a realization is available because both spaces are Polish. Extract a further subsequence so that the vanishing martingale and integrated repulsion also vanish almost surely. Write the limit measure as $\dd t\,\pi_t(\dd a)$ and define $\bar a(t)=\int a\pi_t(\dd a)$. Passing to the integrated forward equations, using uniform path convergence and the continuity and linearity of $b(m,a)$ in $a$, gives $\dot m=b(m,\bar a)$ and $m(0)=m_0$.

The relaxed kinetic integral converges jointly because its integrand $\frac12\sum m_i(t)a_{ij}^2$ is continuous and bounded on the compact time-control domain after uniform path convergence. The potential terms converge as well. Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 J(m,\bar a)\le\lim J(m^\varepsilon,a^\varepsilon)
\]
on this realization. All actions are uniformly bounded, so expectations pass to the limit, giving $\E J(m,\bar a)\le V(0,m_0)$. The pair $(m,\bar a)$ is admissible, and Lemma~\ref{certificate} gives $J(m,\bar a)\ge V(0,m_0)$ for every realization. Equality therefore holds almost surely. Its law paths belong to the original minimizing set, which proves the support statement.
\end{proof}

The theorem keeps $f,g$ unchanged and supplies both tightness and a boundary-layer estimate. Its unproved hypotheses are substantial: a globally smooth deterministic value excludes shocks, and \eqref{missing} is exactly the rate identification that the uncorrected master equation does not obtain automatically from a potential representation. A bounded-value estimate alone supplies neither. Extending the certificate through singularities or proving \eqref{missing} for the full admissible class remains the first gap. No unconditional selection theorem or counterexample for the catalogue target is claimed.
