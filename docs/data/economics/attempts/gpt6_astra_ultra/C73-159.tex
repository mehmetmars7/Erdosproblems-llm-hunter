\section{Target and declared timing}
The catalogue deliberately makes the load-slack factor depend on routing,
buffers, feedback, and a learner class. Tardos \cite[Theorem 8]{Tardos}
gives a factor-three benchmark for its tiny-buffer learning model, with
additional individual arrival restrictions. We do not transfer that factor
to a different architecture. Instead we completely analyze one graph class
under explicit timing and give a counterexample to replacing window regret
by global regret.

Time is discrete. At the beginning of a round each nonempty source offers
one packet to a compatible server. A server already holding a packet
rejects offers; an empty server accepts one offer according to any
work-conserving priority rule. A server then independently succeeds with
its Bernoulli service coin, serving its held packet if any, including a
packet admitted in that round. Finally independent Bernoulli arrivals
join the source queues. Each server has a one-packet buffer. A packet
transferred to a buffer immediately leaves its source, and is counted in
exactly one place. Initial total backlog is finite.

\section{An exact factor for disjoint star components}
Partition sources into groups $I_1,\ldots,I_m$, with every source in $I_j$
compatible only with server $j$. Let server $j$ have success probability
$\mu_j\in(0,1]$, and write $a_j=\sum_{i\in I_j}\lambda_i$.
A learner has only one admissible action at a nonempty source, so every
external-regret bound holds with regret zero. The admission priority may
be arbitrary and history-dependent subject to accepting an offer whenever
the buffer is empty and offers exist.

\begin{theorem}[Exponential backlog bound and $c^*=1$]\label{stars}
Under this timing and graph, if $a_j<\mu_j$ for every $j$, then there are
constants $\theta_j>0$, $q_j<1$, and $B_j<\infty$ such that, for all $t$,
\[
 \E e^{\theta_j W_j(t)}\le
 q_j^t e^{\theta_j W_j(0)}+\frac{B_j}{1-q_j},
 \qquad W_j(t)=\sum_{i\in I_j}Q_i(t)+H_j(t).
\]
In particular strong stability holds for every allowed admission policy.
The open centralized stability region is exactly
$\{\lambda:a_j<\mu_j\text{ for every }j\}$, and the catalogue's load-slack
factor equals one for this model and learner class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the service step a server has a packet if and only if the pre-round
total $W_j(t)$ is positive. If its buffer is empty but the total is positive,
a source must have offered a packet and work conservation admits one.
Let $D_j(t)$ be its independent Bernoulli service coin and let
$A_j(t)$ be the sum of that round's arrivals in its group. Thus exactly
\[
 W_j(t+1)=\bigl(W_j(t)-D_j(t)\bigr)^++A_j(t).
\]
No routing choice or priority appears in this identity. For $w\ge1$,
conditional on the past and $W_j(t)=w$,
\[
 \E[e^{\theta W_j(t+1)}\mid\mathcal F_t]
 =e^{\theta w}q_j(\theta),\qquad
 q_j(\theta)=(1-\mu_j+\mu_j e^{-\theta})
 \prod_{i\in I_j}(1-\lambda_i+\lambda_i e^\theta).
\]
This smooth function has $q_j(0)=1$ and derivative
$q_j'(0)=a_j-\mu_j<0$. Choose sufficiently small $\theta_j>0$ with
$q_j=q_j(\theta_j)<1$. At $w=0$, the conditional exponential moment is
$B_j=\prod_{i\in I_j}(1-\lambda_i+\lambda_i e^{\theta_j})$.
For every state, therefore,
\[
 \E[e^{\theta_j W_j(t+1)}\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le q_j e^{\theta_j W_j(t)}+B_j.
\]
Iterating yields the displayed bound. Since
$w\le e^{\theta_j w}/\theta_j$, each first moment is uniformly bounded;
there are finitely many groups, proving strong stability.

Under any centralized policy, at most one Bernoulli-$\mu_j$ potential
service can be used per round in group $j$. Conservation of packets gives
$\E W_j(t)\ge W_j(0)+t(a_j-\mu_j)$, so rates with $a_j>\mu_j$ cannot
be strongly stable. No point with $a_j=\mu_j$ can be interior to the
stabilizable region: arbitrarily close rate vectors on its overloaded
side violate that necessary bound. The previous argument stabilizes every
strictly feasible point, proving the assertion about the open region.
For every centrally feasible $\lambda^0$, the same rates already stabilize
all learners and priorities in this class. The infimum over allowed
$c\ge1$ is consequently one.
\end{proof}
This result allows many competing sources per server, and priorities may
starve individual sources over particular finite intervals. The aggregate
bound nevertheless bounds every individual queue. It does not address
strategic server selection, because each source has one neighbor.

\section{Why global no-regret is insufficient}
The next construction keeps the same packet accounting and timing but has
one source and two compatible servers. The good server has $\mu_g=1$;
the bad server has $\mu_b=\varepsilon$, where
$0<\varepsilon<\lambda<1$. Start empty. At round $t$ define the learner's
counterfactual one-step payoff for an action to be the indicator that its
newly offered packet would be admitted and complete in that round,
holding current buffers and service coins fixed. Set both payoffs to zero
when the source is empty. This is an external-regret comparison on observed
states, not a counterfactual rerun of the whole queueing process.

The rule chooses the bad server in the deterministic windows
\[
 I_k=\{4^k,4^k+1,\ldots,4^k+2^k-1\},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,
\]
and the good server in every other round. Old buffered packets continue
to receive service attempts even if no new offer is sent to their server.

\begin{theorem}[Sublinear cumulative regret with unbounded expected backlog]
For every realized sequence of arrivals and service coins, the rule has
external regret through time $T$ at most $4\sqrt T$ relative to either
fixed server. Nevertheless
\[
 \sup_t\E[Q(t)+H_g(t)+H_b(t)]=\infty.
\]
The arrival rate $\lambda$ is centrally strictly feasible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The good server is empty at the beginning of every round: any packet it
accepts completes immediately. When the source is nonempty, choosing it
therefore yields payoff one, which no action can exceed. When the source
is empty all compared payoffs are zero. Consequently there is no positive
regret contribution in any good-server round. Every bad-server round
contributes at most one relative to either fixed action. If $4^k\le T$,
then $2^k\le\sqrt T$, and the sum of lengths of all windows started by
$T$ is less than $2^{k+1}\le2\sqrt T$ for the largest such $k$.
The announced more conservative bound follows, including times before
the first window. It is pathwise, hence holds in expectation and with
probability one.

During a window $I_k$, no new packet is sent to the good server, and it
starts and remains empty. The only departures are bad-server successes,
whose expected number is at most $\varepsilon 2^k$. Expected arrivals
in that window equal $\lambda 2^k$. Nonnegativity of its starting backlog
and packet conservation imply that total expected backlog just after the
window is at least $(\lambda-\varepsilon)2^k$. This tends to infinity.
Finally routing exclusively to the good server is a single-server system
with load $\lambda<1$, and Theorem~\ref{stars} proves its strong stability.
\end{proof}
Thus even a deterministic $O(\sqrt T)$ bound from the initial date gives
no time-uniform expected-backlog guarantee. Long bad windows can occur
arbitrarily late while occupying a vanishing fraction of elapsed time.
This example does not satisfy the catalogue's maintained uniform window
regret requirement; its purpose is to show why that requirement cannot
be silently dropped. It also makes the choice of counterfactual routing
payoff explicit, as required by the target.

\section{What remains}
The factor-one theorem solves only the specified disjoint-star architecture.
For graphs allowing strategic choice, determining $c^*$ still requires a
fixed learner class, a quantitative window event, and a stability argument
that uses that event under the chosen admission rules. Neither theorem
improves the published tiny-buffer factor-three bound. The cited Theorem 8
was checked in the full text on 9 October 2026: both total-load slack and
$\lambda_i<1/2$ are stated there. The proofs above are analytic; no
simulation is used.
