\section{Target, conventions, and scope}
Fix $T>0$ and the usual augmented natural filtration of a $d$-dimensional
Brownian motion $B$. The question concerns finite convex generators
$0\leq g(z)\leq |z|^2/2$ and solutions
\[
 dY_t=-g(Z_t)\,dt+Z_t\cdot dB_t,\qquad Y_T=\xi,
 \qquad \E e^{|\xi|}<\infty,
\]
for which $e^{|Y|}$ is of class (D). A solution has continuous adapted $Y$,
predictable $Z$, and $\int_0^T(|Z|^2+|g(Z)|)dt<\infty$ almost surely.
Class (D) means uniform integrability over all stopping times bounded by $T$.
No unstated expected-energy assumption is imposed.

We prove a comparison criterion using finite-entropy subgradient changes
of measure, and identify the endpoint cancellation that prevents the usual
entropy estimate from verifying that criterion. We also prove the complete
constant-coefficient entropic subcase. These are partial results; they do
not resolve the arbitrary convex endpoint question.

The source question and the distinction between one exponential moment,
strong convexity, and stronger moments were checked in
\cite[Problem 5.4]{FHT}. The relevant earlier results are
\cite{DHR11,DHR15}. Our elementary arguments are presented in full;
no novelty of their ingredients or exhaustive literature survey is claimed.

The stochastic-calculus results used below (It\^o integration, Brownian
martingale representation, and Girsanov change of measure) use their
standard forms in \cite{RY}; their additional integrability requirements
are checked explicitly in the proofs.

\section{Changing measure without losing class (D)}
For a density $D\geq0$ with $\E D=1$, write
$\Ent(D)=\E[D\log D]$, with $0\log0=0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{transfer}
Let $D_T>0$ almost surely, $\E D_T=1$, and $\Ent(D_T)<\infty$.
Set $D_t=\E[D_T\mid\F_t]$ and $d\Q=D_Td\Prb$.
If $e^{|Y|}$ is of class (D) under $\Prb$, then $Y$ is of class (D)
under $\Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditional Jensen gives
$D_\tau\log D_\tau\leq\E[D_T\log D_T\mid\F_\tau]$ for every
stopping time $\tau$. Since $x\log x\geq-1/e$, the positive parts
$(D_\tau\log D_\tau)^+$ are bounded by the conditional expectations of
$(D_T\log D_T)^+$ plus $1/e$. Conditional expectations of a fixed
integrable variable form a uniformly integrable family: for example,
truncate that variable at a constant and bound the remaining expectations
by its $L^1$ truncation error. Thus these positive parts are uniformly
integrable.

The elementary Young inequality $ab\leq e^b+a\log a-a$ for
$a>0$, $b\geq0$ shows
\[
 D_\tau|Y_\tau|\leq e^{|Y_\tau|}+(D_\tau\log D_\tau)^+.
\]
Consequently the products on the left are uniformly integrable under
$\Prb$. Also $\sup_\tau\Prb(|Y_\tau|>R)\leq C e^{-R}$, where
$C=\sup_\tau\E e^{|Y_\tau|}<\infty$.
Uniform integrability of the products therefore yields
\[
 \sup_\tau\E_\Q[|Y_\tau|\Ind_{\{|Y_\tau|>R\}}]
 =\sup_\tau\E[D_\tau|Y_\tau|\Ind_{\{|Y_\tau|>R\}}]\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is exactly class (D) under $\Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{subgrad}
For a finite convex $g$ with $0\leq g(z)\leq|z|^2/2$,
every $q\in\partial g(z)$ satisfies
\[
 |q|\leq2|z|,\qquad q\cdot z-g(z)=g^*(q)\geq\tfrac12|q|^2,
\]
where $g^*(q)=\sup_w(q\cdot w-g(w))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The subgradient inequality makes the supremum defining $g^*(q)$ attain
its value at $z$. The quadratic upper bound gives
$g^*(q)\geq\sup_w(q\cdot w-|w|^2/2)=|q|^2/2$.
If $z\ne0$ and $q\ne0$, apply the subgradient inequality at
$w=z+|z|q/|q|$ to obtain
$|z||q|\leq g(w)-g(z)\leq|w|^2/2\leq2|z|^2$.
At $z=0$, $g(0)=0$ and $q\cdot w\leq|w|^2/2$ for all $w$;
taking $w=\varepsilon q$ and sending $\varepsilon$ to zero gives $q=0$.
The cases $q=0$ need no further argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A finite-entropy sufficient condition]\label{conditional}
Let $(Y^1,Z^1)$ and $(Y^2,Z^2)$ be two solutions in the target class
with the same terminal value. Suppose, for $i=1,2$, there is a predictable
selection $q^i_t\in\partial g(Z^i_t)$ such that
\[
 D^i_t=\exp\left(\int_0^t q^i_s\cdot dB_s
                  -\tfrac12\int_0^t|q^i_s|^2ds\right)
\]
is a uniformly integrable martingale and $\Ent(D^i_T)<\infty$.
Then $Y^1,Y^2$ are indistinguishable and $Z^1=Z^2$
$dt\otimes\Prb$ almost everywhere.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The quadratic variations of the displayed stochastic integrals are finite
almost surely by Lemma~\ref{subgrad}; their terminal exponentials are
strictly positive. Under $d\Q_1=D^1_Td\Prb$, Girsanov's theorem makes
$B^1=B-\int q^1ds$ Brownian. For
$\Delta Y=Y^2-Y^1$, $\Delta Z=Z^2-Z^1$, we have
\[
 d\Delta Y=-A_tdt+\Delta Z\cdot dB^1,
 \quad A_t=g(Z^2)-g(Z^1)-q^1\cdot(Z^2-Z^1)\geq0.
\]
The finite-variation integral exists pathwise: the subgradient bound and
Cauchy--Schwarz control $\int |q^1||\Delta Z|$.
Lemma~\ref{transfer} makes $\Delta Y$ class (D) under $\Q_1$.
Localize the stochastic integral and the finite-variation integral so that
stopped conditional expectations are legitimate. The resulting inequality
is the supermartingale inequality. Let the localizing times increase to
$T$; class (D) gives $L^1(\Q_1)$ convergence of the stopped values,
so $\Delta Y$ is a true supermartingale. As $\Delta Y_T=0$,
$\Delta Y_t\geq0$ for each $t$. Reversing the roles of the two
solutions gives $Y^1_t-Y^2_t\geq0$. Equivalence of the measures and
continuity give simultaneous equality of the $Y$ components.
Subtracting the two semimartingale decompositions now shows that
$\int\Delta Z\,dB$ is a continuous finite-variation local martingale,
hence zero; its quadratic variation is $\int|\Delta Z|^2dt$, which
therefore vanishes.
\end{proof}

\section{The missing entropy estimate at the exact endpoint}
\begin{proposition}\label{entropy}
Let $(Y,Z)$ be any solution in the target class and let
$q_t\in\partial g(Z_t)$ be predictable. Choose stopping times
$\tau_n\uparrow T$ that bound $\sup_{t\leq\tau_n}|Y_t|$ and
$\int_0^{\tau_n}|Z_t|^2dt$ by deterministic constants, and put
$D^n=\mathcal E(\int_0^{\cdot\wedge\tau_n}q\,dB)_T$.
Then $D^n$ is a density with finite entropy and
\begin{equation}\label{entropybound}
 \Ent(D^n)\leq\E[D^nY_{\tau_n}]-Y_0.
\end{equation}
For every $r>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{gap}
 (r-1)\Ent(D^n)
 \leq \log\E e^{rY_{\tau_n}}-rY_0.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $e^{r|Y|}$ is class (D) for some $r>1$, then the
unstopped exponential is a uniformly integrable martingale with finite
terminal entropy. At $r=1$, the same calculation gives only
$0\leq\log\E e^{Y_{\tau_n}}-Y_0$ and supplies no entropy bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The stopping times can be formed by hitting levels of the continuous
processes $|Y|$ and $\int|Z|^2$; increase the bounds past $|Y_0|$.
By Lemma~\ref{subgrad}, $\int_0^{\tau_n}|q|^2\leq4n$ if the energy
cutoff is $n$. Bounded quadratic variation implies Novikov's condition
for every scalar multiple of $q$ and finite moments of the stopped
density. Under $d\Q_n=D^n d\Prb$, It\^o's formula for $\log D^n$
and the square-integrable stopped Brownian integral give
\[
 \Ent(D^n)=\tfrac12\E_{\Q_n}\int_0^{\tau_n}|q|^2dt.
\]
The stopped BSDE, with its bounded energy, also permits expectation under
$\Q_n$ and gives
\[
 \E_{\Q_n}Y_{\tau_n}-Y_0
 =\E_{\Q_n}\int_0^{\tau_n}(q\cdot Z-g(Z))dt
 \geq\tfrac12\E_{\Q_n}\int_0^{\tau_n}|q|^2dt.
\]
This proves \eqref{entropybound}. For any $r\geq1$, Jensen's inequality
under $\Q_n$ applied to $\log(e^{rY_{\tau_n}}/D^n)$ gives
\[
 r\E_{\Q_n}Y_{\tau_n}-\Ent(D^n)
 \leq\log\E e^{rY_{\tau_n}}.
\]
Combining proves \eqref{gap}. All expressions here are integrable because
the stopped $Y$ is bounded and the stopped density has finite moments.

When $r>1$ and the stated class-(D) assumption holds, its exponential
expectations are uniformly bounded, so \eqref{gap} bounds
$\Ent(D^n)$ uniformly. Since $x\log x$ is superlinear, this makes
$D^n$ uniformly integrable. Almost-sure convergence to the unstopped
terminal exponential follows from finite quadratic variation. Thus the
limit has expectation one, the unstopped positive local martingale is a
uniformly integrable martingale, and Fatou applied to $x\log x+1/e$
gives finite terminal entropy. Substitution of $r=1$ verifies the final
assertion exactly.
\end{proof}

This is an obstruction to this \emph{estimate}, not evidence of actual
nonuniqueness. At the endpoint the positive coefficient in front of the
entropy disappears. No inference from pathwise finite quadratic variation
to Novikov's condition has been made.

\section{Endpoint checks where the argument closes}
\begin{proposition}\label{bounds}
Every solution in the target class satisfies
\[
 \E[\xi\mid\F_t]\leq Y_t\leq\log\E[e^\xi\mid\F_t]
 \quad(0\leq t\leq T).
\]
Consequently, for deterministic terminal value $\xi=c$, the only solution
in that class is $Y_t=c$, $Z_t=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The drift of $Y$ is nonpositive and $Y$ is class (D); localization and
$L^1$ convergence of stopped values show it is a supermartingale.
It\^o's formula gives
\[
 d(e^Y)=e^Y\bigl(\tfrac12|Z|^2-g(Z)\bigr)dt+e^YZ\cdot dB.
\]
Its drift is nonnegative. Since $e^Y\leq e^{|Y|}$, it is a class-(D)
local submartingale and hence a submartingale, by the same stopped
conditional-expectation argument. Evaluating both inequalities at $T$
gives the bounds. For $\xi=c$ they coincide. Vanishing quadratic
variation in the BSDE then gives $Z=0$ almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The entropic family, including its zero-curvature endpoint]
For $0\leq a\leq1$ let $g_a(z)=a|z|^2/2$ and assume
$\E e^{|\xi|}<\infty$. There is exactly one solution in the target class.
For $a>0$ it is characterized by
\[
 Y_t=\frac1a\log\E[e^{a\xi}\mid\F_t],
\]
and for $a=0$ by $Y_t=\E[\xi\mid\F_t]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $a>0$, It\^o's formula makes $e^{aY}$ a local martingale for
any solution. It is dominated by $e^{|Y|}$, hence is a class-(D)
martingale. Its terminal value determines it, proving uniqueness of $Y$
and then of $Z$ by quadratic variation.

For existence, the Brownian martingale representation theorem represents
$M_t=\E[e^{a\xi}\mid\F_t]$ as $M_0+\int_0^tH_s\cdot dB_s$,
with a locally square-integrable integrand. The continuous nonnegative
martingale $M$ cannot hit zero: after a zero hitting time its conditional
terminal expectation is zero, contradicting $e^{a\xi}>0$.
Thus its continuous path has a strictly positive minimum on $[0,T]$
almost surely. Set $Y=a^{-1}\log M$ and $Z=H/(aM)$.
The martingale has finite quadratic variation on $[0,T]$, so
$\int|Z|^2dt<\infty$ almost surely. It\^o's formula verifies the BSDE.
Conditional Jensen, first for $x^{1/a}$ and then for $x^{-1/a}$, yields
\[
 e^{Y_t}\leq\E[e^\xi\mid\F_t],\qquad
 e^{-Y_t}\leq\E[e^{-\xi}\mid\F_t].
\]
Their sum is bounded by $2\E[e^{|\xi|}\mid\F_t]$, a class-(D)
martingale. This proves the required exponential class (D).
For $a=0$, use Brownian representation of $\E[\xi\mid\F_t]$;
conditional Jensen gives $e^{|Y_t|}\leq\E[e^{|\xi|}\mid\F_t]$.
Every other class-(D) local martingale with the same terminal value is
this martingale, completing that case.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact remaining question}
The general endpoint target remains unresolved by this attempt. It would
suffice to obtain the finite-entropy subgradient densities in
Theorem~\ref{conditional} from the original assumptions alone. Proposition
\ref{entropy} explains why importing the stronger-moment proof without
change fails. The entropic family and deterministic terminal values are
fully checked subcases, not a classification of all convex generators.
