\section{Short recap and the declared experiment}
The target is an honest confidence set for the entire moment-identified
set when relevance can vanish, invalidity is bounded, and the number of
instruments grows. We give a finite-sample construction requiring no
covariance inverse, establish explicit diameter bounds where relevance
is separated from zero, and prove two distinct obstructions to precision.
The construction is conservative and does not determine the efficiency
frontier for general norms, covariance structures, or adaptive instruments.

Identification-robust IV inference has a substantial existing literature.
The reviewed abstract of Andrews, Stock and Sun \cite{review} explicitly
covers weak-instrument inference and nonhomoskedastic settings. We do not
attribute the combined bounded-invalidity and adaptive-selection theorem
below to that review; its proof is supplied here.

Observe $n$ independent identically distributed triples $(Y,D,Z)$ with
$Z\in\R^k$, where $k=k_n$ is arbitrary. Fix instrument scaling before
sampling and assume known constants $A,B>0$ satisfy
\[
 |Z_jY|\leq A,\quad |Z_jD|\leq B\quad\text{almost surely},\quad j\leq k.
\]
Assume finite second moments for any structural equation under discussion.
Set $a=\E[ZY]$, $d=\E[ZD]$, and use the supplied invalidity norm
$\|\cdot\|_\infty$. The target is exactly
\[
 \mathcal B=\{b\in\R:\|a-bd\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon\}.
\]
For example each $b\in\mathcal B$ induces a structural residual $u=Y-Db$
whose moment invalidity is at most $\varepsilon$. This alone does not
establish the substantive causal interpretation of that equation.

\section{Uniform inversion without a first-stage division}
Let $\widehat a=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_iY_i$ and
$\widehat d=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_iD_i$. For $\gamma\in(0,1)$ put
\[
 t_Y=A\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4k/\gamma)}n},\qquad
 t_D=B\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4k/\gamma)}n},
\]
and define the random closed set
\[
 C=\{b\in\R:\|\widehat a-b\widehat d\|_\infty
                  \leq\varepsilon+t_Y+|b|t_D\}. \tag{1}
\]
Each side is continuous and piecewise linear in the scalar $b$; hence
membership and endpoints are measurable, including unbounded and empty cases.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample coverage of the whole set]
For every law satisfying the stated bounds,
$\Pr\{\mathcal B\subseteq C\}\geq1-\gamma$, uniformly in $k$, $d$,
and $\varepsilon$. The same assertion holds if (1) is imposed only on
any data-selected subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}$, using the same
full-dictionary thresholds. If $S$ is empty the resulting set is $\R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A mean of independent variables in $[-A,A]$ obeys
$\Pr(|\widehat a_j-a_j|>t)\leq2\exp[-nt^2/(2A^2)]$.
One proof centers the summands and bounds their moment generating
functions by $\exp(\lambda^2A^2/2)$: for a bounded variable the second
derivative of the centered log moment generating function is the variance
under exponential tilting, at most $(2A)^2/4$; integrate twice from zero.
Markov's inequality and optimization over $\lambda$ give the bound.
The analogous bound holds for $\widehat d_j-d_j$. A union bound over
the $2k$ coordinates gives probability at least $1-\gamma$ to the event
\[
 \mathcal A=\{\|\widehat a-a\|_\infty\leq t_Y,
                 \|\widehat d-d\|_\infty\leq t_D\}.
\]
On this one event, for every real $b$ simultaneously,
$\|\widehat a-b\widehat d-(a-bd)\|_\infty\leq t_Y+|b|t_D$.
Thus every member of $\mathcal B$ belongs to $C$. The conclusion already
holds for every coordinate on $\mathcal A$, and therefore for every
realization of a data-selected subset. No independence of the selector
and the observations is needed.
\end{proof}

This protects selection from a fixed dictionary of $k$ instruments.
It does not protect a newly optimized, unrestricted function of the same
outcomes: such a function was not included in the simultaneous event.
Nor may one replace $k$ by the smaller realized $|S|$ without another
argument. Selection only discards constraints here, so it cannot improve
the set's diameter relative to imposing all coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Observable informativeness certificate]
Let $s=\|\widehat d\|_\infty$. If $s>t_D$, every $b\in C$ satisfies
\[
 |b|\leq L:=\frac{\|\widehat a\|_\infty+\varepsilon+t_Y}{s-t_D},
 \qquad
 \diam C\leq\frac{2(\varepsilon+t_Y+Lt_D)}s. \tag{2}
\]
The diameter of an empty set is defined to be zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a coordinate $j$ with $|\widehat d_j|=s$. For $b\in C$,
$|b|s\leq|\widehat a_j|+\varepsilon+t_Y+|b|t_D$, proving the first
bound. For $b,c\in C$, subtract their two residuals at coordinate $j$:
$s|b-c|\leq2(\varepsilon+t_Y)+(|b|+|c|)t_D$.
Insert the first bound and take the supremum over pairs.
\end{proof}

\section{Expected diameter on an informative submodel}
For an expected-diameter comparison, specify a known $B_0>0$ and restrict
the law class to laws with $\mathcal B\subseteq[-B_0,B_0]$ and
$\|d\|_\infty\geq\kappa>0$. This is a substantive restriction on the
\emph{whole} identified set, not an unjustified truncation of weak-IV
confidence intervals. Let $C^0=C\cap[-B_0,B_0]$.

\begin{corollary}[Diameter bound and a sufficient growth condition]
If $t_D\leq\kappa/2$, then on $\mathcal A$,
\[
 \diam C^0\leq\min\left\{2B_0,
       \frac{4(\varepsilon+t_Y+B_0t_D)}\kappa\right\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \E\diam C^0\leq
 \frac{4(\varepsilon+t_Y+B_0t_D)}\kappa+2B_0\gamma. \tag{3}
\]
With $\gamma=\min(\alpha,n^{-2})$, informative vanishing expected
diameter follows if
$[\varepsilon+\sqrt{\log(kn)/n}]/\kappa\to0$, for fixed $A,B,B_0$.
Coverage remains at least $1-\alpha$ for every $n$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $\mathcal A$, $s\geq\|d\|_\infty-t_D\geq\kappa/2$.
For $b,c\in C^0$ the second inequality in the proof of (2) gives
$s|b-c|\leq2(\varepsilon+t_Y+B_0t_D)$. Divide by $s$, and also use
the deterministic $2B_0$ diameter bound. On the complement of
$\mathcal A$ use that deterministic bound and probability at most
$\gamma$, proving (3). The proposed sequence of thresholds has the stated
order and $t_D/\kappa\to0$. The coverage assertion follows from the
preceding theorem and the law-class assumption about $\mathcal B$.
\end{proof}

No claim of a necessary restriction on $k$ follows. The construction has
logarithmic dictionary dependence because it is simultaneous coordinatewise
inversion. Highly redundant instruments need not incur a corresponding
intrinsic loss; different covariance and norm classes require separate
frontiers. In particular a sample covariance matrix need not be invertible
for (1) to be valid, including when $k>n$.

\section{Two lower bounds and the zero-relevance boundary}
\begin{proposition}[Invalidity cannot be averaged away]
For any law with $a=0$ and $\|d\|_\infty=\kappa>0$,
$\mathcal B=[-\varepsilon/\kappa,\varepsilon/\kappa]$.
Any random set covering the whole $\mathcal B$ with probability at least
$1-\alpha$ has
$\E\diam C\geq(1-\alpha)2\varepsilon/\kappa$.
If $d=0$, then $\mathcal B=\R$ when $\|a\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon$,
and $\mathcal B=\varnothing$ otherwise. In the former case honest whole-set
coverage requires $C=\R$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $a=0$, $\|a-bd\|_\infty=|b|\kappa$, which gives the exact interval.
Its two endpoints must both belong to $C$ on the coverage event, so the
diameter there is at least their distance. Taking expectations proves
the bound. When $d=0$ the defining inequality is independent of $b$,
which gives the two stated cases. A subset of $\R$ containing all of
$\R$ equals $\R$, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sampling obstruction with bounded observations]
Fix $0<\kappa\leq1$, $B_0>0$, and $0<\alpha<3/8$. There exists a
one-instrument model satisfying $|ZY|\leq1$, $|ZD|\leq1$, normalized
$\E Z^2=1$, and exact validity $\varepsilon=0$, such that every confidence
set honest over this model obeys, at one of its laws,
\[
 \E\diam C\geq
 2h(1-2\alpha-1/4),\qquad
 h=\min\{B_0/2,1/(8\kappa\sqrt n)\}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus order $\min\{B_0,1/(\kappa\sqrt n)\}$ cannot in general be improved.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $Z$ be a fair random sign, $D=\kappa Z$, and $Y=ZV$, where $V$ is
an independent random sign with mean $\kappa b$, for $b\in[-h,h]$.
These laws have $d=\kappa$, $a=\kappa b$, and identified set $\{b\}$.
Writing $u=Y-Db$ gives $\E Zu=0$, and all variables are bounded.
Compare $b=h$ and $b=-h$. Put $x=\kappa h\leq1/(8\sqrt n)$.
The divergence between sign distributions with means $x$ and $-x$ is
$x\log((1+x)/(1-x))\leq4x^2$: indeed the logarithm is
$\int_{-x}^x(1+t)^{-1}dt\leq2x/(1-x)\leq4x$ for $x\leq1/2$.
Thus
$\KL(P_h^n,P_{-h}^n)\leq4nx^2\leq1/16$, and therefore
$\TV(P_h^n,P_{-h}^n)\leq\sqrt{1/32}<1/4$.
Here Pinsker follows from binary reduction and the curvature bound
$\operatorname{kl}(p,q)\geq2(p-q)^2$.
Honesty gives $P_h(h\in C)\geq1-\alpha$ and
$P_{-h}(-h\in C)\geq1-\alpha$. Transporting the second event to $P_h$
loses at most total variation. The union bound implies
$P_h(\{-h,h\}\subseteq C)\geq1-2\alpha-1/4$.
On this event the diameter is at least $2h$, proving (4).
\end{proof}

An asymptotically honest procedure yields the same conclusions with
$\alpha$ replaced by $\alpha+o(1)$ along these sequences. In particular
if $n\kappa_n^2$ stays bounded, even the zero-invalidity one-instrument
experiment need not permit shrinking confidence sets. The invalidity
obstruction and the sampling obstruction are separate: the former remains
with perfectly known population moments.

\section{Remaining questions and source limits}
The theorem supplies full-set coverage for bounded coordinates, any finite
dimension, and data-dependent coordinate deletion. It leaves unresolved
optimal covariance regularization under heavier tails, sharp dependence on
correlation and the chosen invalidity norm, instrument construction outside
a fixed dictionary, and an optimal expected-diameter frontier. Even in the
bounded class, (3)'s $\sqrt{\log k}$ factor has not been proved necessary.
The cited review was checked at its publisher abstract and bibliographic
record; no uninspected theorem of that review is used in these proofs.
