\section{Target and scope}
The target is the best worst-case expected Euclidean social-cost ratio over
all universally truthful planar facility mechanisms, uniformly in the
population size. Barak \cite[Sections 2--4]{Barak} establishes $4/\pi$ for
randomly rotated coordinate medians and leaves the unrestricted optimum
open. We give a self-contained upper-bound proof and a minimax reduction:
changing the report-independent distribution of axes cannot improve the
worst-case ratio of a fixed coordinate-median rule. This is a restriction
on a proposed direction of improvement, not a lower bound for every
universally truthful mechanism.

For $n\ge2$ set $k=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor$. The deterministic rule $C_n$
returns the $k$th order statistic in each of the two coordinates; in
particular it uses the lower median when $n$ is even. Let $R_\theta$
denote rotation through $\theta$, and define
\[
 C_{n,\theta}(x)=R_{-\theta}C_n(R_\theta x_1,\ldots,R_\theta x_n).
\]
All random angles below are independent of the reports. These maps are
Borel measurable since order statistics are continuous.

\section{Truthfulness and the average norm bound}
\begin{lemma}[The one-dimensional option interval]\label{clamp}
Fix $n-1$ real reports and an order rank $1\le k\le n$. The $k$th
order statistic after inserting a report $z$ is the projection of $z$
onto a fixed closed interval, allowing an infinite endpoint. Consequently,
for every true location $a$, its truthful output minimizes distance from
$a$ over all obtainable outputs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the other reports as $b_1\le\cdots\le b_{n-1}$, set
$b_0=-\infty,b_n=+\infty$, and inspect the three cases
$z\le b_{k-1}$, $b_{k-1}\le z\le b_k$, and $z\ge b_k$.
The output is respectively $b_{k-1},z,b_k$, with the evident interpretation
at infinite endpoints. This is exactly projection onto $[b_{k-1},b_k]$.
Projection is the closest point in that interval to $a$.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}\label{upper}
Every $C_{n,\theta}$ is truthful for Euclidean distance and unanimous.
For uniform $\Theta$ on $[0,2\pi)$, the resulting mechanism has finite
expected costs and satisfies, for every profile $x$,
\[
 \E\SC(x,C_{n,\Theta}(x))\le\frac4\pi\OPT(x).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the rotated coordinate system, Lemma~\ref{clamp} shows that a truthful
report weakly minimizes each of the two absolute coordinate errors.
Squaring and adding these two inequalities proves Euclidean truthfulness;
rotations preserve distances. Unanimity follows immediately.

Write $\|\cdot\|_1$ for the sum of absolute coordinates. Coordinate medians
minimize $\sum_i\|R_\theta x_i-z\|_1$: for a scalar coordinate, the slope
of the convex sum of absolute deviations changes from nonpositive to
nonnegative on the median interval. Thus, for a Euclidean minimizer $y^*$,
\begin{align*}
 \SC(x,C_{n,\theta}(x))
 &\le\sum_i\|R_\theta x_i-C_n(R_\theta x)\|_1\\
 &\le\sum_i\|R_\theta(x_i-y^*)\|_1.
\end{align*}
A minimizer exists because the social cost is continuous and tends to
infinity when $\|y\|_2$ tends to infinity. For every fixed vector $v$,
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\|R_\theta v\|_1\,d\theta
 =\|v\|_2\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
 (|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|)\,d\theta
 =\frac4\pi\|v\|_2.
\]
Integrating the preceding pointwise inequality proves the result and
finiteness. At unanimous profiles both sides are zero.
\end{proof}
This proof is a reconstruction of the standard random-projection norm
argument underlying the cited bound, not a claim of a new upper bound.

\section{A minimax obstruction to choosing better axes}
For a Borel probability measure $\nu$ on the circle define
\[
 W_n(\nu)=\sup_{x:\OPT(x)>0}
 \frac{\int\SC(x,C_{n,\theta}(x))\,d\nu(\theta)}{\OPT(x)}.
\]
Let $u$ be uniform angular measure. The next assertion does not assume
that $\nu$ has a density or finite support.
\begin{theorem}[Uniform angular mixing is minimax]\label{minimax}
For every $n\ge2$ and every $\nu$, $W_n(\nu)\ge W_n(u)$.
Consequently, for arbitrary population-dependent measures $(\nu_n)$,
\[
 \sup_{n\ge2}W_n(\nu_n)\ge\sup_{n\ge2}W_n(u).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a nonunanimous profile $x$ and let
$h_x(\theta)=\SC(x,C_{n,\theta}(x))/\OPT(x)$.
The identity
\[
 C_{n,\theta}(R_\varphi x)=R_\varphi C_{n,\theta+\varphi}(x)
\]
follows by multiplying the rotations. Both social cost and the optimum
are rotation-invariant, so the ratio on the rotated profile is
$\int h_x(\theta+\varphi)\,d\nu(\theta)$. Every such value is at most
$W_n(\nu)$. Integrate over uniform $\varphi$. Tonelli's theorem applies
to the nonnegative integrand, and translation-invariance of uniform
measure gives
\[
 W_n(\nu)\ge
 \int\!\int h_x(\theta+\varphi)\,du(\varphi)d\nu(\theta)
 =\int h_x(\psi)\,du(\psi).
\]
Taking the supremum over $x$ proves the first assertion, and taking the
supremum over $n$ proves the second.
\end{proof}
The argument applies to any fixed measurable base mechanism and its
rotation conjugates whenever its cost ratio is defined. Thus it is more
than a statement about discrete angle grids. Any strict improvement over
the uniform rotation guarantee must change the base family, use additional
mechanisms, or exploit structure other than reweighting absolute axes.

\section{The missing unrestricted lower bound}
For completeness, fixed report-independent mixtures of truthful rules
remain universally truthful by their definition as distributions over
seeds; Theorem~\ref{minimax} covers only mixtures of the displayed orbit.
It does not compare this orbit with a mixture of distinct base rules.
Nor does it allow the angle distribution to depend on reports: such a
choice requires a fresh incentive proof.

The cited source proves tightness of its random-rotation mechanism. We
have not reproved that lower-bound construction here, and we do not infer
from it that the global optimum is $4/\pi$. The precise remaining task
is a lower bound that applies to every deterministic truthful rule used
as a seed, or a universally truthful construction outside the above orbit
with a smaller uniform worst-case ratio. No finite computation is used.
