\section{A finite, experimentally interpretable restriction}
The general target is the identified set of selection kernels under a specified observation kernel, including common predictions in new games or contexts. We analyze a finite equilibrium dictionary and an experiment that repeatedly observes the same selected equilibrium. We derive a sharp fiber description, a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for identified predictions, an explicit repetition length guaranteeing identification, and simultaneous finite-sample uncertainty. We also show why a finite observation window cannot identify an unrestricted continuum mixture.

For each observed game and context, suppose a known list of $K$ candidate equilibria is specified. The list may be the entire equilibrium set when finite, or a maintained restriction. A session selects index $k$ with probability $q_k$, then produces an observation in a finite alphabet $\mathcal Z$ according to the known column vector $h_k$. Write $H=(h_1,\ldots,h_K)$ and $q\in\Delta_K$. The population observation law is $p=Hq$. These are maintained sampling and support assumptions, not implications of Nash existence. Historical selection proposals and noisy-information selection theorems motivate restrictions but do not identify this $q$ without observation assumptions \cite{hs,cvd}.

\section{Sharp fibers and identified predictions}
\begin{theorem}\label{fiber}
For the unrestricted finite-mixture family $q\in\Delta_K$, the sharp identified set is the polytope
\[
 Q(p)=\{q\geq0:Hq=p\}.
\]
Normalization is automatic because the columns of $H$ and $p$ sum to one. If $Q(p)$ is nonempty, let
$S=\{k:\text{some }q\in Q(p)\text{ has }q_k>0\}$.
For a given vector $\ell\in\R^K$, the target $\ell^\top q$ is identified at $p$ if and only if
\[
 \ell_S\in\operatorname{rowspan}(H_S).
\]
More generally, its sharp lower and upper bounds are the minimum and maximum of $\ell^\top q$ on $Q(p)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every generating mixture must satisfy $Hq=p$, and every nonnegative solution is a valid selection distribution producing $p$. This proves sharpness. The set is a closed subset of the compact simplex, so the extrema exist.

If $\ell_S=b^\top H_S$ for some $b$, then every feasible $q$ vanishes outside $S$, and $\ell^\top q=b^\top p$ is constant. Conversely, if $\ell_S$ is not in the row span, elementary finite-dimensional orthogonality gives $d\in\ker H_S$ with $\ell_S^\top d\ne0$. For each $k\in S$, choose a feasible mixture positive at $k$ and average these finitely many mixtures. The resulting $q^\circ$ is strictly positive on $S$. Since columns of $H_S$ sum to one, $H_Sd=0$ implies $\sum_{k\in S}d_k=0$. Hence for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, both $q^\circ\pm\epsilon d$, extended by zero off $S$, are feasible mixtures with different target values. This contradicts identification and proves necessity.
\end{proof}

For a held-out outcome with a maintained common-mixture rule and known prediction vector $\ell$, Theorem~\ref{fiber} applies directly. The common-mixture restriction across contexts must be stated. Without it, observations in one context put no restrictions on another context's freely varying selector.

\begin{corollary}[Observationally equivalent mixtures can predict identically]
Failure to identify $q$ does not preclude identifying $\ell^\top q$. If $H$ has dependent columns, any $\ell$ in its row space is still identified at every compatible population law. Conversely, an interior compatible mixture admits unidentified predictions for every $\ell$ outside that row space.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{fiber}; in the interior case $S=\{1,\ldots,K\}$. The first assertion follows from the same row-space representation without an interior assumption.
\end{proof}

\section{Repeated observations of one latent equilibrium}
Suppose each session retains its selected equilibrium and yields $L$ conditionally independent draws from $h_k$. Independence is conditional on the equilibrium; unconditional observations within a session are generally dependent. Let $H^{(L)}$ have columns $h_k^{\otimes L}$.

\begin{theorem}[A finite repetition design]\label{repeat}
If $h_1,\ldots,h_K$ are pairwise distinct, then $H^{(L)}$ has full column rank for every $L\geq K-1$. Thus $K-1$ repeated observations per session identify all mixture weights. If two columns are equal, no number of such repetitions distinguishes the weights within that equal-column class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There exists a vector $f\in\R^{|\mathcal Z|}$ such that the scalars $\theta_k=f^\top h_k$ are all distinct: each equality $f^\top(h_k-h_j)=0$ is a proper hyperplane, and a finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot fill the vector space. For completeness, take $f=(1,t,\ldots,t^{d-1})$ for $d=|\mathcal Z|$. Each pairwise difference becomes a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $d-1$. A finite set of their roots can be avoided by choosing an integer $t$.

From a session of length $L$, for each $r=0,\ldots,K-1$ observe the statistic $\prod_{s=1}^r f(Z_s)$, with empty product one. Its expectation conditional on $k$ is $\theta_k^r$. Thus a linear relation $\sum_k d_k h_k^{\otimes L}=0$ implies
$\sum_k d_k\theta_k^r=0$ for all $r=0,\ldots,K-1$. The Vandermonde matrix is nonsingular. One direct proof uses interpolation polynomials
$P_j(t)=\prod_{k\ne j}(t-\theta_k)/(\theta_j-\theta_k)$: taking the corresponding linear combination of the moment equations gives $d_j=0$. Hence all $d_k=0$, proving full column rank. Additional observations cannot reduce rank because marginalizing them recovers the length-$K-1$ law. Equal columns remain equal under every tensor power, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The theorem is about a shared latent equilibrium within a session. If the equilibrium is redrawn independently at every date, the session law becomes $(Hq)^{\otimes L}$ and repetition adds no population identification beyond $Hq$. This difference is experimentally substantive.

\section{A lower boundary for unrestricted equilibrium mixtures}
\begin{proposition}\label{continuum}
For every finite $L$, there is a finite game and two different selection distributions on its Nash equilibria that induce identical length-$L$ session laws under conditional independent play but different length-$(L+1)$ laws.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use a two-player game where player 1 has two actions, player 2 has one, and every payoff is zero. Every Bernoulli mixed action $\theta\in[0,1]$ of player 1 is a Nash equilibrium. Select $L+2$ distinct numbers $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{L+2}$ in $(0,1)$. The $(L+1)\times(L+2)$ matrix with entries $\theta_k^r$, $r=0,\ldots,L$, has a nonzero null vector $d$. Since its first row is all ones, $\sum_kd_k=0$, so $d$ has both positive and negative components. Let $s=\sum_{d_k>0}d_k=-\sum_{d_k<0}d_k>0$, and form two probability distributions with masses $d_k^+/s$ and $d_k^-/s$ on these equilibria.

They have identical moments through degree $L$. The probability of every binary sequence of length $L$ is a polynomial $\theta^r(1-\theta)^{L-r}$ of degree at most $L$, so the induced session laws coincide. Their degree-$(L+1)$ moments differ: otherwise the square Vandermonde matrix for degrees $0,\ldots,L+1$ would annihilate $d$, contradicting nonsingularity. In particular the probability of $L+1$ successive ones differs. This proves the assertion with a valid game and a fully specified observation kernel.
\end{proof}

This establishes a precise finite-window limitation, rather than claiming that no selection rule can be learned. A finite dictionary plus persistent sessions supplies exactly the additional restriction used in Theorem~\ref{repeat}.

\section{Uncertainty that includes estimating the selector}
Observe $B$ independent sessions under a fixed known finite observation matrix $H$ (possibly $H^{(L)}$). Let $d$ be its number of rows and $\widehat p$ the empirical observation law. For $0<\alpha<1$ set
\[
 r_B=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/\alpha)}{2B}},\qquad
 \widehat Q=\{q\in\Delta_K:\|Hq-\widehat p\|_\infty\leq r_B\}.
\]
\begin{theorem}\label{inference}
With probability at least $1-\alpha$, $\widehat Q$ contains the entire population fiber $Q(p)$. Consequently the minimum and maximum of any fixed $\ell^\top q$ over $\widehat Q$ cover its entire sharp identified interval. If $H$ has full column rank and $L_HH=I_K$ is a chosen left inverse, then
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_{\infty}(\widehat Q)
 \leq2\|L_H\|_{\infty\to\infty}r_B
\]
whenever $\widehat Q$ is nonempty.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each outcome cell the $B$ session indicators are independent Bernoulli variables. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give
$\Pr(\|\widehat p-p\|_\infty>r_B)\leq2d e^{-2Br_B^2}=\alpha$.
On the complementary event every $q$ with $Hq=p$ belongs to $\widehat Q$, proving coverage and the interval assertion. For any $q,q'\in\widehat Q$, the triangle inequality gives $\|H(q-q')\|_\infty\leq2r_B$. Multiplication by the left inverse proves the diameter bound.
\end{proof}

Poorly separated observation columns can make the inverse norm large even when population identification holds. This exposes an experimental precision issue: choosing sufficient repetition length does not itself guarantee useful sample size. An empty estimated set is a model-fit warning; the theorem bounds its probability under the maintained exact finite-mixture model. Held-out predictions using $\ell$ inherit the mixture confidence region rather than treating fitted weights as known.

\section{Source check and unresolved scope}

The finite model supplies a concrete experimental restriction and sharp prediction tests, rather than a universal behavioral selector.
