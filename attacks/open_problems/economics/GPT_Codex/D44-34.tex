\begin{abstract}
We give an exact algorithmic theorem for the entire specified
rational-semialgebraic design continuum of finite perfect-recall auctions.
Correlation, interdependence, monetary risk, private liquidity, and
endogenous entry and information acquisition are retained.
A fixed-game polynomial Bayes-graph closure encodes genuine sequential
equilibrium, with full continuation rationality and nonempty compact
assessment fibers. Classical real quantifier elimination then computes
the robust value against all environments and all sequential equilibria,
decides attainment, and constructs same-class algebraic
\(\varepsilon\)-mechanisms. We also characterize actual achievable
guarantees in a specified simpler format, including unattained threshold
cases. The complexity statement is polynomial in input bit precision
only when all compiled variable and degree bounds are fixed.
A nonlinear example has strictly active costly acquisition and a
\(1/16\) guarantee against the same budget-feasible class.
The algorithm is proved; a full all-friction CAD execution is not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The declared design continuum and information structure}\label{sec:model}
The canonical question expressly permits a stated environment collection
and feasible mechanism class. Here the input specifies finitely many
finite perfect-recall auction formats. A format records Nature's finite
type and payoff-state draws, each bidder's information partition,
entry, acquisition, report, bid and exit choices, physical outcomes,
and a seller-observable-history partition.
Mechanism parameters \(m\) range over the entire specified
semialgebraic domain \(D\), and environment parameters \(\omega\) over
a specified semialgebraic set \(\Omega\).
The initial nonemptiness tests below handle empty sets explicitly.
Compactness is not assumed.

All input formulas have rational coefficients.
Algebraic constants may instead be specified by polynomials and
isolating intervals.
Primitive Nature probabilities, values, budgets, costs, participation
benchmarks, and utility functions have finite semialgebraic graph
descriptions. Each graph must define a total, single-valued,
finite-valued function on its admitted domain.
An arbitrary relation, a missing graph point, or an undefined
\(0/0\) is not a valid primitive.
The class includes piecewise rational and algebraic functions; it does
not include arbitrary unrepresented utility functions.
Graph variables replace function notation when formulas are compiled.

The designer chooses \(m\) before the environment is selected.
At each fixed \(\omega\), the probabilistic and payoff specification
is common knowledge among bidders, while realized private signals and
liquidity remain private.
Seller-controlled transfers, allocations, and randomization depend only
on \(m\), the seller's actual observations, and its own random draws.
They coincide on seller-indistinguishable histories and cannot depend
on hidden Nature labels or unobserved \(\omega\).
Exogenous type and acquisition kernels may depend on the environment
and hidden states as specified by the experiment.
This does not grant the seller access to those states.

Monetary utility is nondecreasing on its declared monetary domain.
Its values are applied to the realized terminal monetary argument before
expectation. Terminal-utility graphs must be the specified compositions
of values, transfers, monetary utility, and costs.
Quasilinearity is not assumed.
Cash entry or acquisition costs belong in terminal monetary wealth and
in the cash account at their actual dates.
Nonmonetary disutilities, when declared, are subtracted outside monetary
utility; these conventions cannot be interchanged.
Seller-directed charges are included in seller payments, while payments
to outside parties are not seller revenue.

Realized allocations \(x_z\) belong to the finite feasible allocation
set \(X\). A finite lottery is represented by its Nature draw before
the terminal leaves.
With finite-valued seller cost \(C:X\to\R\), terminal seller profit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sellerleaf}
 r_z(m)=\sum_i\tau_{i,z}(m)-C(x_z).
\end{equation}
It has no direct dependence on hidden \(\omega\); the environment can
change which terminal history occurs.
The construction below is uniform in arbitrary finite input templates.
A finite input collection is a tagged finite union.
It does not turn an unspecified unbounded union of all finite trees
into one fixed finite-dimensional optimization problem.

\section{Legal games, true cash, and uniform input validity}\label{sec:validity}
Private-budget action availability, when present, is part of the actual
game family. A semialgebraic legality guard is identical at all nodes
of the acting bidder's information set.
Every live decision has an available action.
Split the parameter domain into its finitely many legality-guard
patterns before considering Nature support.
Each pattern determines a legal tree with its information partition;
perfect recall is checked there.
The pattern is determined by \((m,\omega)\), not chosen by a seller
who does not know the environment.
An available action remains in the game even if its equilibrium
probability is zero.

The hard-liquidity convention concerns actual feasible expenditure.
Every prefix of every Nature-supported legal history must leave
nonnegative cash, starting from the true budget \(b_i(\omega,s_i)\).
Actual deposits and cash payments are debited when made; refunds are
credited when received. A later refund cannot finance an earlier
unaffordable escrow deposit.
For the standard initial-budget account, cumulative net seller payments
plus declared nonnegative outside cash costs cannot exceed that budget.
In particular this account is required to imply, at every legal terminal,
\[
 \tau_{i,z}(m)+k_{i,z}^{\mathrm{outside}}(m,\omega)
 \le b_i(\omega,s_i(z)),\qquad
 k_{i,z}^{\mathrm{outside}}\ge0.
\]
Thus net seller payments themselves are bounded by the true budget.
False reports do not create cash or enlarge physically feasible moves.
Affordability-based menus are compatible with private liquidity when
the player knows and remembers its own cash; an actual deposit
implements the restriction without revealing the true budget to the seller.

These tests apply at every structurally live legal history, including
histories reached with zero probability in a proposed equilibrium.
They are not multiplied by equilibrium reach probabilities.
Testing payments only at an equilibrium cannot repair an incorrect
deviation set. Every legal deviation and every completely mixed legal
perturbation must already be physically feasible.
Alternative funding or account conventions require their own
explicitly proved terminal-payment upper envelope in the value argument
below. Terminal netting must not erase intermediate cash requirements.

Totality and functionality of graphs, proper Nature probabilities,
nonempty legal action sets, perfect recall, observation restrictions,
and monotonic monetary utility are uniform input hypotheses or
explicitly tested predicates.
For example, functionality means that two graph values at the same
argument are equal; totality is existence of a value at every admitted
argument. Both are first-order real conditions.
Nondecreasing utility is tested on its declared domain by the
corresponding two-argument implication.
An invalid graph or game cannot be hidden in a false antecedent that
makes an adverse environment disappear.

Participation is separately declared as ex ante or interim and is
required at every admitted equilibrium.
For positive-probability interim participation, a division-free form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interimIR}
 \sum_{z:s_i(z)=s_i}\Pr(z)\,u_{i,z}
 \ge \Pr(s_i)\,u_i^{\mathrm{out}}(\omega,s_i).
\end{equation}
Here \(u_{i,z}\) is actual terminal utility, and probabilities use the
true signal law. At a zero-marginal signal this expresses the declared
vacuous convention. Any additional condition at such a signal must
be specified separately; no posterior is invented for a null type.
Ex-post participation is a different restriction and is not substituted.

\begin{theorem}[Exact robust design in the declared class]\label{thm:main}
There is a finite symbolic algorithm on the stated rational-semialgebraic
input that detects an empty environment or robustly feasible design
class. Otherwise, under the standard payment cap above, or a supplied
and proved finite upper envelope, it computes the finite real-algebraic
value
\[
 V^*=\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_F}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}
          \inf_{e\in\cE(m,\omega)}\Rev(m,\omega,e),
\]
where \(\cE\) is the full set of genuine sequential-equilibrium
assessments of the declared legal actual-support game.
It decides attainment and, when attained, returns an algebraic
optimizer. For every rational \(\varepsilon>0\), it returns an
algebraic \(m_\varepsilon\) in the same feasible class with robust
revenue at least \(V^*-\varepsilon\).
For a specified semialgebraic subformat it also decides actual
\(\varepsilon\)-achievability and returns a witness when one exists.
\end{theorem}
The proof constructs the formulas and verifies their meanings in
Sections~\ref{sec:bayes}--\ref{sec:simple}; it invokes a complete
classical real quantifier-elimination algorithm, not an unspecified
oracle for the worst equilibrium.
No execution of that full CAD computation is claimed.

\section{Nature support and exact Bayes polynomials}\label{sec:bayes}
Work first with one fixed legal tree.
Perfect recall means that all nodes in one bidder information set have
the same sequence of that bidder's preceding information sets and
actions. In particular an information set is never revisited along a path.
Let \(p=(m,\omega)\). Lift Nature probabilities and terminal utilities
to vectors \(c,u\), with a supplied graph predicate \(\Graph(p,c,u)\).
They are uniquely determined by \(p\).
Rational functions use nonzero-denominator guards and multiplication
equations; an undefined conditional \(0/0\) must first be completed
semialgebraically or replaced by an unconditional signal draw.
The vectors \(c,u\) are fixed outside every tremble quantifier.

For each subset \(S\) of Nature edges, impose
\[
 c_e>0\quad(e\in S),\qquad c_e=0\quad(e\notin S),
\]
together with nonnegativity and unit outgoing sums at every Nature node.
These finitely many strata include boundary cases; empty ones may be
discarded. At fixed \(p\), exactly one support pattern applies.
A history is Nature-live when every Nature factor in its prefix is
positive. Remove only the other histories.
At a surviving Nature node retain the original positive probabilities,
which still sum to one.
At a surviving strategic node retain all its legal actions.
A strategic edge cannot introduce a zero Nature factor, and each
successor has a live terminal continuation by finiteness and the unit sums.

Replace information set \(I\) by its live intersection \(I^S\), omitting
it only if empty. Its available actions are unchanged.
Every prefix of a live history is live, so remembered own information
and actions are unchanged and perfect recall is preserved.
Assessments below refer to this actual-support game.
Strategically off-path legal histories remain.
An entirely Nature-impossible information set has Bayes denominator
zero under every strategic tremble; imposing ordinary Bayes beliefs
there while asserting nonemptiness would be inconsistent.
Assigning arbitrary such beliefs is an extra convention, not fixed-Nature
consistency. The explicit support convention removes that ambiguity.

On a stratum, let \(\mathcal I_S\) be the retained strategic information
sets, and write
\begin{align*}
 \mathsf B_S(\sigma):\quad&
   \sigma_{I,a}\ge0,\quad\sum_{a\in A(I)}\sigma_{I,a}=1
                    &&(I\in\mathcal I_S),\\
 \mathsf B_S^+(\sigma):\quad&
   \mathsf B_S(\sigma),\quad \sigma_{I,a}>0
                    &&(I\in\mathcal I_S,\ a\in A(I)),\\
 \mathsf M_S(\mu):\quad&
   \mu_{I,h}\ge0,\quad\sum_{h\in I^S}\mu_{I,h}=1
                    &&(I\in\mathcal I_S).
\end{align*}
Define the reach polynomial \(P_h(c,\sigma)\) as the product of
the probabilities along the root-to-\(h\) path, using \(c\) on Nature
edges and \(\sigma_{I,a}\) on strategic edges, with \(P_{\mathrm{root}}=1\).
Equivalently generate them recursively down the tree.
Set \(P_I=\sum_{h\in I^S}P_h\).
Every retained history, and hence every \(I\), has positive reach under
\(\mathsf B_S^+\). The exact Bayes graph is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bayes}
 \begin{split}
 \BG_S(c,\sigma,\mu):={}&
 \mathsf B_S^+(\sigma)\wedge\mathsf M_S(\mu)\\
 &{}\wedge\bigwedge_{I\in\mathcal I_S}
   \left[P_I(c,\sigma)>0\ \wedge\
    \bigwedge_{h\in I^S}\mu_{I,h}P_I(c,\sigma)=P_h(c,\sigma)\right].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The positive-denominator guards prevent a cross-multiplied \(0=0\)
from admitting spurious beliefs.
Each completely mixed strategic profile has exactly one Bayes belief
vector. In bookkeeping with original partially live sets, removed
nodes have belief zero. Empty sets have no assessment coordinates.
Different strata form a tagged finite disjoint union; common-dimensional
padding may use fixed dummy zeros, which are not beliefs at existing sets.

\section{Full continuation rationality}\label{sec:SR}
Fix player \(i\), its information set \(I\), and a behavioral continuation
\(\delta_i\) in the product \(\Delta_i\) of its retained action simplexes.
Components preceding \(I\) or never encountered later are irrelevant.
For \(h\in I^S\) and terminal descendant \(z\), let
\(T^i_{h,z}(c,\sigma_{-i},\delta_i)\) be the product along the suffix
from \(h\) to \(z\): Nature uses \(c\), other players use \(\sigma\),
and \(i\) uses \(\delta_i\).
Set it to zero if \(z\) is not descended from \(h\).
The exact conditional continuation payoff is the polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation}
 V_{i,I}(c,u,\sigma_{-i},\delta_i;\mu)
 =\sum_{h\in I^S}\mu_{I,h}
      \sum_{z\text{ descended from }h}
           u_{i,z}\,T^i_{h,z}(c,\sigma_{-i},\delta_i).
\end{equation}
There is no zero-probability conditioning: \(\mu_I\) is an explicit
part of the assessment and is fixed for this comparison.
All entry and information-acquisition choices in the tree are included.

Define \(\SR_S(c,u,\sigma,\mu)\) by requiring, for every \(i,I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SR}
 \forall\delta_i\left[
   \delta_i\in\Delta_i\ \Longrightarrow\
   V_{i,I}(c,u,\sigma_{-i},\sigma_i;\mu)
   \ge V_{i,I}(c,u,\sigma_{-i},\delta_i;\mu)\right].
\end{equation}
This is optimality of the entire continuation at every information
set. Other bidders' behavior is fixed, not their hypothetical responses
to the deviation. Neither beliefs nor admissible actions are silently
replaced.

\begin{lemma}[Finite complete-plan test]\label{lem:pure}
Condition~\eqref{eq:SR} is equivalent to the finite collection of
inequalities against all complete pure plans
\(\pi_i\in\Pi_i:=\prod_{J\text{ owned by }i}A(J)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Perfect recall prevents repetition of an information set on a path.
Thus \eqref{eq:continuation} is affine in each separate block
\(\delta_{i,J}\), keeping the other blocks fixed.
Successive expansion over simplex vertices gives, with the fixed
arguments of \(V\) suppressed,
\[
 V_{i,I}(\delta_i)
 =\sum_{\pi_i\in\Pi_i}
   \left[\prod_{J\text{ owned by }i}\delta_{J,\pi_i(J)}\right]
       V_{i,I}(\pi_i).
\]
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one.
Equivalently, drawing a complete plan by independent action choices
reproduces every behavioral continuation path probability.
Pure-plan inequalities therefore imply \eqref{eq:SR}; the reverse
implication is immediate. Further randomized choices over pure plans
are convex combinations and cannot improve the maximum.
\end{proof}
The number of inequalities may be exponential, but it is finite and
constructible. No one-step-deviation or PBE replacement is used.
Without perfect recall, repeated visits can destroy block affinity;
that extension is not claimed.

\section{Consistency at a fixed game and the exact equilibrium predicate}
\label{sec:consistency}
We use finite-game consistency in the sense of
Kreps--Wilson~\cite{KW1982}.
For assessments define
\[
 d^2((\sigma,\mu),(\sigma',\mu'))
 =\sum_{I,a}(\sigma_{I,a}-\sigma'_{I,a})^2
       +\sum_{I,h}(\mu_{I,h}-\mu'_{I,h})^2.
\]
The exact consistency condition is the following first-order formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 \Con_S(c,\sigma,\mu):=
 \forall r\left[
 r\le0\ \vee\
 \exists\sigma',\mu'\left(
 \BG_S(c,\sigma',\mu')\ \wedge\
 d^2((\sigma,\mu),(\sigma',\mu'))<r^2\right)\right].
\end{equation}
The same fixed \(c\) occurs throughout. Mechanism \(m\), environment
\(\omega\), and Nature probabilities do not vary.
One common \(\sigma'\) and its complete Bayes vector \(\mu'\) must
satisfy all information-set equations simultaneously.
No rationality or equilibrium condition is imposed on this witness.
No lower bound ties its action probabilities to \(r\); different
trembles may vanish at arbitrary relative rates.

\begin{lemma}[Exact consistency]\label{lem:consistency}
For a legitimate assessment, \eqref{eq:consistency} is equivalent to
being a limit of completely mixed strategic profiles and their
Bayesian beliefs in the same game.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A convergent sequence gives a witness for every \(r>0\) by taking a
sufficiently late term. Conversely, apply the formula at \(r=1/n\)
and choose one joint witness for each \(n\).
Every witness is completely mixed with all its Bayes beliefs in the
same game, and its assessment distance is less than \(1/n\).
It converges to the proposed assessment.
\end{proof}
Thus consistency is the closure of one fixed-\(c\) Bayes graph in
the finite-dimensional assessment space, not a closure across
environments or separately for different information sets.

On each legal support stratum define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SE}
 \begin{split}
 \SE_S(p,\sigma,\mu):={}&\mathcal P(p)\ \wedge\
 \exists c,u\bigl[
   \Graph(p,c,u)\wedge\operatorname{ValidChance}(c)
      \wedge\operatorname{Supp}_S(c)\\
 &{}\wedge\mathsf B_S(\sigma)\wedge\mathsf M_S(\mu)
      \wedge\SR_S(c,u,\sigma,\mu)\wedge\Con_S(c,\sigma,\mu)
 \bigr].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal P\) includes the stratum's fixed legality conditions.
The graph vector is quantified outside the tremble condition.
Taking a tagged finite disjunction, with the dummy-coordinate convention
above when necessary, gives \(\SE(p,e)\), \(e=(\sigma,\mu)\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact symbolic assessment set]\label{thm:SE}
At every admitted fixed parameter \(p\), formula~\eqref{eq:SE} describes
exactly all sequential-equilibrium assessments of its legal
actual-support game. The full parameter-assessment relation is
effectively rational-semialgebraic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A sequential equilibrium satisfies the behavior and belief simplexes,
the full continuation comparisons by \eqref{eq:continuation}, and
\eqref{eq:consistency} by its consistent sequence.
Its uniquely determined primitive vector supplies \eqref{eq:SE}.
Conversely the formula supplies a legitimate assessment, optimal
complete continuation at every information set, and one convergent
sequence of completely mixed profiles with their Bayes beliefs.
These are precisely sequential rationality and finite-game consistency.

For effectivity, all trees, support cases, information sets, actions,
and terminals are finite. Primitive graph constraints have supplied
rational polynomial coefficients.
Reach products, continuation payoffs, simplex constraints, positivity,
Bayes multiplication equations, and squared distances are polynomial.
Every quantifier ranges over finitely many real coordinates; the radius
is a real variable, not a quantifier over sequences.
The complete real-closed-field quantifier-elimination algorithm
therefore returns an equivalent quantifier-free formula in the free
variables \(p,\sigma,\mu\).
\end{proof}

Parameters kept free are variable values, not unencoded irrational
coefficients in the algorithmic input.
For individual rational or real-algebraic parameters, exact numerical
encodings give equilibrium descriptions and algebraic sample
assessments. No finite algorithm for arbitrary unencoded real constants
is asserted.
If a primitive graph is initially given by a first-order rational
formula, eliminate its quantifiers first; its totality and functionality
still must hold.
Graf, Engesser, and Nebel~\cite{GEN2024}, Definition~3 and
Sections~3.1--3.2, already develop exact polynomial representations
of sequential equilibrium. The direct fixed-game closure proof here
is supplied in full and carries no priority claim.

\section{Nonempty compact equilibrium fibers}\label{sec:existence}
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-game existence and compactness]\label{thm:fibers}
Every valid legal actual-support game has a nonempty compact set of
sequential-equilibrium assessments.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\), its legal support tree, and its primitive values \(c,u\).
No parameter varies in the argument.

First consider player \(i\)'s information set \(J\).
Perfect recall makes the product of its own previous behavioral
probabilities identical across all histories \(h\in J\).
For a completely mixed profile \(\gamma\), write
\[
 P_h(c,\gamma)
 =r_i(J;\gamma_i)\,r_{-i}(h;c,\gamma_{-i}).
\]
The common first factor is strictly positive and cancels in Bayes'
rule. Thus the belief at \(J\) depends on opponents and Nature, but
not on \(i\)'s completely mixed behavior.
If one of \(i\)'s information sets \(J\) follows another \(I\) on a
path, every node of \(J\) remembers \(I\), so \(J\) lies wholly in
the continuation from \(I\).
The number of preceding own decisions is constant within a set and
strictly increases along later own information sets.

Choose \(0<\epsilon<1/\max_I|A(I)|\), and impose a lower probability
\(\epsilon\) on each available strategic action.
Use one decision agent per information set, with its original
player's unconditional terminal utility as payoff.
Parametrize the constrained simplex by
\[
 \sigma_{I,a}=\epsilon+(1-|A(I)|\epsilon)x_{I,a},
 \qquad x_I\in\Delta(A(I)).
\]
Each agent payoff is continuous and affine in its own block because
its information set is not repeated on a path.
This is a finite normal-form mixed game.
Here is its standard Nash existence argument from Brouwer.
For agent \(A\), payoff \(H_A\), and its action vertex \(a\), set
\[
 g_{A,a}(x)=\max\{0,H_A(a,x_{-A})-H_A(x)\},\qquad
 T_{A,a}(x)=\frac{x_{A,a}+g_{A,a}(x)}
                   {1+\sum_b g_{A,b}(x)}.
\]
The continuous map \(T\) preserves the product of simplexes.
Brouwer's finite-dimensional fixed-point theorem supplies a fixed
point. If \(G_A=\sum_b g_{A,b}>0\), then
\(g_{A,a}=x_{A,a}G_A\), so every supported action has strictly
positive payoff gain. This contradicts affinity:
\[
 \sum_a x_{A,a}[H_A(a,x_{-A})-H_A(x)]=0.
\]
Thus \(G_A=0\) for every agent, proving Nash equilibrium of the
constrained agent game.

Let \(\sigma^\epsilon\) be its behavior and \(\mu^\epsilon\) its Bayes
beliefs. Every retained history has positive probability.
Changing only the decision agent at \(I\) changes its unconditional
payoff by the positive probability of reaching \(I\) times the
conditional continuation-payoff change there.
The prefix does not depend on the action at \(I\), since \(I\)
is not revisited. Consequently \(\sigma_I^\epsilon\) is conditionally
optimal among \(\epsilon\)-constrained local actions, with later play fixed.

We prove, rather than assume, the needed full-continuation conclusion.
Fix \(I\) of player \(i\), and any \(\epsilon\)-constrained continuation
\(\delta_i\). Starting with this deviation, replace \(i\)'s choices
from later to earlier information sets, in decreasing order of the
number of remembered own decisions, by \(\sigma_i^\epsilon\).
At a replacement at \(J\), all later own decisions already equal
\(\sigma_i^\epsilon\); opponents have always used
\(\sigma_{-i}^\epsilon\).
If \(J\) is outside the continuation from \(I\), the replacement has
no effect. Otherwise \(J\) lies wholly after \(I\), and the prefix
factorization proves that its current conditional belief equals
\(\mu_J^\epsilon\).
Further conditioning on having started at \(I\) changes nothing at
\(J\), since all its nodes remember \(I\).
The change in payoff from \(I\) is the nonnegative probability of
reaching \(J\) times the local payoff change, which is nonnegative
by the constrained local optimality just proved.
For \(J=I\), use \(\mu_I^\epsilon\) directly.
After finitely many replacements the continuation is
\(\sigma_i^\epsilon\). Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constrainedSR}
 V_{i,I}(c,u,\sigma_{-i}^\epsilon,\sigma_i^\epsilon;\mu^\epsilon)
 \ge
 V_{i,I}(c,u,\sigma_{-i}^\epsilon,\delta_i;\mu^\epsilon)
\end{equation}
for every constrained continuation.

Take \(\epsilon_n\downarrow0\). Compactness of the finite product of
behavior and belief simplexes gives a convergent subsequence
\[
 (\sigma^{\epsilon_n},\mu^{\epsilon_n})\longrightarrow(\sigma,\mu).
\]
It is consistent by Lemma~\ref{lem:consistency}.
For an arbitrary continuation \(\delta_i\), use the constrained
approximations
\[
 \delta_{J,a}^{(n)}
 =\epsilon_n+(1-|A(J)|\epsilon_n)\delta_{J,a}.
\]
They converge to \(\delta_i\).
Apply \eqref{eq:constrainedSR} and pass to the limit in the finite
polynomial \eqref{eq:continuation}.
This proves full sequential rationality for every \(i,I,\delta_i\).
If the game has no strategic information sets, the unique empty
assessment gives existence directly.

Finally, at fixed \(c,u\), consistency is a closed subset of the
compact assessment product, being the closure of its Bayes graph.
Sequential rationality is closed by the finite pure-plan weak
inequalities of Lemma~\ref{lem:pure}.
Their intersection is nonempty and compact.
\end{proof}
The special positive profiles in this existence proof are constrained
agent-game equilibria. They are not an extra restriction on the
witnesses in \eqref{eq:consistency}, which remain arbitrary completely
mixed profiles.
Compactness is a statement about each fixed game.
It implies neither a closed joint graph nor continuity across
Nature-support changes or discontinuous primitive graphs.

\section{Three exact consistency diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}
\subsection{Arbitrary off-path beliefs can fail}
Players 1 and 2 simultaneously choose Out or In, receiving \(0\)
for Out and \(-1\) for In regardless of subsequent play.
If someone enters, player 3 moves without seeing who entered.
Its information set has nodes Both, Only1, and Only2.
Action \(L\) pays it \(1\) at Both and \(0\) otherwise; action \(R\)
pays \(0\) at Both and \(1\) otherwise.
This has a finite perfect-recall representation.
Both early players strictly choose Out.
On-path Bayes equations alone permit belief one on Both and choice
\(L\), which is then a best reply. It is not sequentially consistent.
For completely mixed entry probabilities \(p,q\),
\[
 \mu(\mathrm{Both})
 =\frac{pq}{pq+p(1-q)+(1-p)q}
 =\frac{pq}{p+q-pq}\le\min\{p,q\}.
\]
As \(p,q\to0\), consistency forces belief zero on Both.
Every sequential equilibrium therefore prescribes \(R\).
Removing the strategically off-path information set would lose this
restriction.

\subsection{Different information sets need a common witness}
Let player 1 choose Out, \(L\), or \(R\), with Out terminal.
After \(L\) or \(R\), player 2 has an information set \(I\) with a
forced action, then an information set \(J\), still unable to
distinguish \(L\) from \(R\).
Perfect recall holds; payoffs may all be zero.
Every completely mixed profile gives the same \(L/R\) posterior at
both sets. When the limiting strategy chooses Out with probability
one, either posterior separately can approach any value, but their
limits must coincide.
Independent information-set witnesses would wrongly permit
\(\mu_I(L)=1\), \(\mu_J(L)=0\).

\subsection{Varying the parameter in the closure perturbs Nature}
Nature chooses \(A\) with probability \(p\), and \(B\) otherwise.
Player 1 observes the state and chooses Enter or Out; after entry
player 2 cannot distinguish the states.
For \(0<p<1\), use entry probabilities \(1/2\) at \(A\) and \(p^2\)
at \(B\), completed to fully mixed profiles.
The entry posterior of \(A\) is
\[
 \frac{p/2}{p/2+(1-p)p^2}\longrightarrow1\quad(p\downarrow0).
\]
At fixed \(p=0\), state \(A\) is Nature-impossible.
Entry at \(B\) is live, even if the limiting strategic probability
is zero, and strategic trembles force its posterior to be \(B\)
with probability one.
Closing over varying parameters gives the wrong assessment;
\eqref{eq:consistency} keeps this game fixed.

\section{Robust feasibility and the inner worst equilibrium}\label{sec:feasible}
Let \(e=(\sigma,\mu)\), and let \(\SE(m,\omega,e)\) be the exact
finite-disjunction formula above.
The expected seller profit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:revenue}
 \Rev(m,\omega,e)=\sum_z P_z(c,\sigma)\,r_z(m).
\end{equation}
Beliefs constrain equilibria but do not replace actual path
probabilities in this expression.
After primitive graphs are lifted, it is semialgebraic.
At fixed parameters it is a continuous polynomial in behavior.
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibers} makes the inner worst-equilibrium value an
attained minimum. Its value relation is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worstfixed}
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Worst}(m,\omega,v):={}&
 \exists e\,[\SE(m,\omega,e)\wedge\Rev(m,\omega,e)=v]\\
 &{}\wedge
 \forall e'\,[\SE(m,\omega,e')\Rightarrow
                           \Rev(m,\omega,e')\ge v].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular the universal clause includes unfavorable equilibria.

Write \(\mathcal F(m)\) for robust feasibility.
It includes \(D(m)\), uniform graph and game validity under every
\(\omega\in\Omega\), physical allocation and true-prefix-cash
constraints from Section~\ref{sec:validity}, and the declared
participation inequalities for every \(\omega\) and every \(e\)
satisfying \(\SE(m,\omega,e)\).
One may retain the redundant explicit check
\(\exists e\,\SE(m,\omega,e)\).
It is proved true for every valid game, rather than used as a convention
for an empty infimum.

All parts are finite first-order formulas.
Graph totality and uniqueness, probability constraints, legal-action
guards, observation equalities, and utility monotonicity are uniformly
verified hypotheses or quantified predicates.
The finite combinatorial recall tests are performed on the legal
templates. The remaining budget and allocation tests are imposed
on every structurally live legal history.
The participation conditions include the true-probability version of
\eqref{eq:interimIR} or the declared ex-ante alternative.
These predicates and their universal quantifiers are passed to the
same complete algebraic procedure; none is a free feasibility oracle.

The robust order is one design choice before all environments and
all equilibria:
\[
 \exists m\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega\quad
 \forall e\in\cE(m,\omega).
\]
Validity is required separately for every environment.
It cannot be hidden solely in an equilibrium implication with a false
antecedent. Legality and support patterns are functions of the actual
parameters, not extra favorable choices for the designer.
First decide whether \(\Omega\) and \(\{m:\mathcal F(m)\}\) are
nonempty. If either is empty, report that fact and no optimizer.

\section{A finite robust value without compactness assumptions}\label{sec:bounds}
Assume the environment and feasible design sets are nonempty.
For each feasible mechanism define
\[
 W(m)=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e:\SE(m,\omega,e)}
                       \Rev(m,\omega,e).
\]
No minimum over environments or maximum over mechanisms is presumed.
For fixed \(m\), its finitely many finite terminal seller profits
are independent of hidden \(\omega\). Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leafbounds}
 \min_z r_z(m)\le\Rev(m,\omega,e)\le\max_z r_z(m)
\end{equation}
for every environment and equilibrium.
In particular \(W(m)\) is finite even when \(\Omega\) is not compact.

Exact real-algebraic sample-point extraction, detailed below, gives
an algebraic feasible \(m_0\) and an algebraic \(\omega_0\in\Omega\).
The first point is extracted from the actual quantified feasibility
condition \(\mathcal F\), not merely from \(D\).
Set
\[
 L_0=\min_z r_z(m_0).
\]
Then \(V^*=\sup_{\mathcal F(m)}W(m)\ge W(m_0)\ge L_0\).
At the fixed environment \(\omega_0\), true terminal cash feasibility
for every feasible mechanism gives
\[
 \sum_i\tau_i\le\sum_i\max_{s_i}b_i(\omega_0,s_i).
\]
Nonnegative external cash costs only reduce the cash available for
seller payments. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:valuebound}
 V^*\le B_0:=
    \sum_i\max_{s_i}b_i(\omega_0,s_i)-\min_{x\in X}C(x)<\infty.
\end{equation}
Indeed every equilibrium at \(\omega_0\) satisfies this pointwise
upper bound, and the infimum over all environments is no larger.
For an alternative funding convention the same argument is used only
when its own finite, effectively specified uniform seller-profit
envelope has been supplied and proved.
Unbounded external funding is not included in the finite-value claim.
Thus \(V^*\) is a finite real number by explicit cash bounds, without
equilibrium continuity or strict-obedience assumptions.

\section{Exact value, attainment, and same-class approximations}\label{sec:optimization}
Define the lower-guarantee predicates
\begin{align}
 A(m,t)&:=\mathcal F(m)\ \wedge\
  \forall\omega,e\,
   [\,\Omega(\omega)\wedge\SE(m,\omega,e)
           \Rightarrow\Rev(m,\omega,e)\ge t\,],\label{eq:guarantee}\\
 B(t)&:=\exists m\,A(m,t).\label{eq:threshold}
\end{align}
Because all equilibrium fibers are nonempty,
\(A(m,t)\) means precisely \(\mathcal F(m)\) and \(W(m)\ge t\),
whether or not the inner environment infimum is attained.
The truth set of \(B\) is nonempty, downward closed, and has finite
supremum \(V^*\).
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Value}
 \begin{split}
 \Val(v):={}&
  [\,\forall t\ (B(t)\Rightarrow t\le v)\,]\\
 &{}\wedge
  [\,\forall\eta>0\ \exists t\ (B(t)\wedge t>v-\eta)\,].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
These are real quantifiers; positivity is an ordinary polynomial guard.

\begin{lemma}[The value formula]\label{lem:value}
Formula~\eqref{eq:Value} holds exactly for \(v=V^*\).
Its quantifier-free form defines a real-algebraic singleton.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first clause says \(v\) is an upper bound for the truth set of
\(B\). The second says that no smaller number is an upper bound.
These are exactly the defining properties of its finite supremum.
Apply complete real quantifier elimination to obtain an equivalent
Boolean combination of rational polynomial sign conditions in \(v\).
If none of its nonzero polynomials vanished at the unique solution,
all their signs would stay unchanged in a neighborhood.
The Boolean formula would then be constant there, contradicting
the singleton property. A nonzero rational polynomial therefore
vanishes at \(V^*\), making it real algebraic.
Real-root isolation and sign determination select the correct root.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact sample witnesses]\label{lem:sample}
A nonempty semialgebraic set over real-algebraic coefficients has a
constructible algebraic sample point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use an exact sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition.
In its first coordinate choose an appropriate algebraic root or
a rational point between adjacent roots.
Proceed successively in the chosen fiber.
Section values are algebraic over the field generated by the
preceding coordinates, hence algebraic over \(\Q\).
Open sectors, including unbounded ones, contain algebraic sample
points. The exact algorithm supplies their polynomials, isolating
data, and the cells to which they belong.
\end{proof}
This explains the extraction used in Section~\ref{sec:bounds}.
All statements invoke a complete classical algebraic algorithm with
the hypotheses verified in Theorem~\ref{thm:SE}; an uncomputed
worst-equilibrium oracle has not been introduced.

Exact attainment is decided by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:attainment}
 \exists m\,A(m,V^*).
\end{equation}
If true, Lemma~\ref{lem:sample} extracts an algebraic optimizer.
If false, the correct report is nonattainment.
For every rational \(\varepsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon}
 \exists m\,A(m,V^*-\varepsilon)
\end{equation}
is true by the definition of supremum and downward closure.
Extracting a point gives a mechanism in the original feasible
class satisfying
\[
 \inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e\in\cE(m_\varepsilon,\omega)}
       \Rev(m_\varepsilon,\omega,e)\ge V^*-\varepsilon.
\]
For any real \(\varepsilon>0\), existence follows by choosing a
smaller positive rational tolerance.
Exact algebraic coordinate names are the output convention.
Rational or decimal rounding is not part of this guarantee:
it may change tie incentives or violate an equality constraint.
No truthful-report revelation principle, equilibrium selection,
discretization continuity, or uniform strict-incentive margin is used.

\subsection{A rational-interval certificate alternative}
Exact value extraction is unnecessary for a requested approximation.
Choose rational \(L\le L_0\) and \(U\ge B_0\), and keep the witness
\(A(m_0,L)\).
Query \(B((L+U)/2)\). If true, extract a witness and replace \(L\)
by the midpoint. If false, replace \(U\) by it: downward closure
implies \(V^*\le U\), even when the supremum is unattained.
For positive initial width, after at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bisection}
 \max\left\{0,
   \left\lceil\log_2\frac{U_{\mathrm{initial}}-L_{\mathrm{initial}}}
                            {\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\}
\end{equation}
steps, \(U-L\le\varepsilon\).
Handle zero initial width separately with no queries.
The saved lower witness satisfies
\[
 L\le W(\widehat m)\le V^*\le U.
\]
Every query is decided by the same complete real-algebraic algorithm.
A checkable output contains the compiled formulas, exact mechanism
coordinates, certificates for \(A(\widehat m,L)\), the false threshold
decisions actually used, and the initial cash upper bound.
With exact \(V^*\), one can instead certify
\(A(\widehat m,V^*-\varepsilon)\) directly.

\section{Simpler formats, nonattainment, and threshold equality}\label{sec:simple}
Let \(S\) be a declared semialgebraic subformat of the same design
domain, inheriting the same environments, constraints, and equilibrium
correspondence. Actual \(\varepsilon\)-achievability is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple}
 \exists m\,[S(m)\wedge A(m,V^*-\varepsilon)].
\end{equation}
Elimination decides this sentence and sample extraction constructs
a witness when it is true.
It does not replace sequential equilibrium by a truthful or
designer-favorable equilibrium.

If publicly specified primitive data \(\xi\) vary semialgebraically,
construct \(\Val(\xi,v)\) by \eqref{eq:Value}.
Eliminating \(v,m\) from
\[
 \exists v,m\,[\Val(\xi,v)\wedge S(\xi,m)
                    \wedge A(\xi,m,v-\varepsilon)]
\]
returns a quantifier-free polynomial-sign criterion on
\((\xi,\varepsilon)\).
This is an effective exact characterization of the data on which
the specified simple format achieves the guarantee.
It permits explicit restrictions on prices, symmetry, messages,
rounds, and information acquisition.

If the simple class is nonempty, let \(V_S\) be its supremum.
The inequality \(V_S\ge V^*-\varepsilon\) is weaker at equality:
the supremum may be unattained.
A strict gap \(V^*-V_S<\varepsilon\) suffices.
At equality, actual attainment of that threshold in \(S\) is required.
Formula~\eqref{eq:simple} treats this boundary exactly.
Sections~\ref{sec:bounds}--\ref{sec:simple}, together with the
equilibrium construction, prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{example}[Prior tie-driven nonattainment]\label{ex:nonattainment}
This example is retained from the prior Pro attempt~\cite{PriorPro}.
A single bidder has known value \(1\), linear monetary utility, budget
\(1\), zero entry cost, and zero seller cost.
The environment set is a singleton and the design class consists of
posted prices \(p\in[0,1]\), followed by accept or reject.
For \(p<1\), acceptance is strictly optimal and worst-equilibrium
revenue equals \(p\).
At \(p=1\), every acceptance mixture is sequentially rational,
including certain rejection.
The unique decision information set has trivial Bayes belief, and
every such mixture is a limit of completely mixed strategies with
that belief. Thus all are sequential equilibria and worst revenue is zero:
\[
 W(p)=
 \begin{cases}p,&0\le p<1,\\0,&p=1.\end{cases}
 \qquad \sup_{p\in[0,1]}W(p)=1.
\]
Every mechanism meets the true budget and participation constraints.
The compact price domain has no robust optimizer.
For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), \(p=1-\varepsilon\) attains the
same-class bound \(V^*-\varepsilon\).
Moreover, for the subformat \(S=[0,a)\), \(0<a<1\), we have
\(V_S=a\) without attainment.
At \(\varepsilon=1-a\), the supremal inequality holds with equality,
but \eqref{eq:simple} is false.
\end{example}

\section{Imported algebraic algorithms and bit complexity}\label{sec:complexity}
We use complete real-closed-field quantifier elimination and algebraic
sample-point extraction, not a new CAD algorithm.
Basu~\cite{Basu2014}, Theorem~2.4 and Section~2.1.2 (PDF page~6),
give the standard CAD arithmetic complexity.
Theorem~2.27 of the pinned version (PDF page~16) gives effective
elimination with quantifier-block and free-variable counts, including
integer intermediate and output bit-size bounds.
The detailed underlying algorithm is cited there as reference~[25];
no claim of inspecting that entire monograph is made.

Let \(k\) be the total number of real variables in the fully compiled
formula, \(s\) its polynomial-predicate count, \(d_0\) the maximum
degree, and \(L\) the rational coefficient bit length.
These counts include legality and support cases, primitive-graph
variables, continuation and tremble variables, feasibility constraints,
all optimization quantifiers, and free public-data coordinates.
They are not merely the number of bidders or advertised price parameters.
General CAD has an arithmetic bound of the form
\[
 (s d_0)^{\,2^{O(k)}}.
\]
For the bit statement, use dense polynomial encoding and clear constant
rational denominators by positive integer multiples.
At fixed total variable and degree bounds, the effective-QE theorem's
operation counts are polynomial in predicate count and formula length,
and its intermediate integer lengths are polynomial in coefficient
length. Exact integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial bit cost
in the dense input size and the requested rational accuracy input length.
Algebraic witnesses are extracted by the exact sample-point construction
of Lemma~\ref{lem:sample}; the same fixed bounds control their descriptions.
The bisection alternative adds the number of calls in
\eqref{eq:bisection}.

This proves tractability at fixed \emph{compiled} structural size.
It proves no polynomial bound in unrestricted game size, no efficiency
claim as the structural dimension varies, and no strict
fixed-parameter-tractability claim.
All formulas are explicitly constructed from finite input before the
imported algorithm is applied.
A full CAD run for the all-friction family below has not been performed
or claimed.

\section{A nonlinear family with all frictions active}\label{sec:example}
Two bidders compete for one object, with zero seller cost.
Bidder \(i\) initially knows \((z_i,\beta_i)\).
Here \(z_i\in\{0,1\}\), while \(\beta_i\) independently selects an
initial cash budget \(1\) or \(2\), each with probability \(1/2\).
Budgets are independent across bidders and independent of the \(z\)'s.
The joint signal law is
\[
 \pi_\rho(0,0)=\pi_\rho(1,1)=\frac{1+\rho}{4},\qquad
 \pi_\rho(0,1)=\pi_\rho(1,0)=\frac{1-\rho}{4},
 \qquad \rho\in[1/4,1/2].
\]
All profiles have positive probability, and the signals are correlated.
If \(i\) wins, its monetary value is
\[
 v_i=\frac14+z_i+\frac32z_j.
\]
Thus the realized value depends on the other's true signal even after
allocation; this is genuine interdependence.
Entry costs \(1/32\) cash, and a further optional private signal costs
\(1/32\) cash, both paid to outside parties.
The acquired binary report of \(z_j\) has accuracy
\(\tau\in[9/10,19/20]\), with noises independent conditional on the
true signals. Both reports have positive probability in every state.

Bidder \(i\)'s monetary utility has
\(\eta_i\in[1/100,1/50]\) and is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:utilityexample}
 U_\eta(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  (1+4\eta)x+4\eta,&x<-2,\\
  x-\eta x^2,&-2\le x\le3,\\
  (1-6\eta)x+9\eta,&x>3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Values and first derivatives agree at the endpoints.
The derivative decreases from \(1+4\eta\) to \(1-6\eta>0\) on
the middle interval and is constant outside.
Hence the extension is globally increasing and concave and is
strictly concave on the relevant monetary outcomes.
It is rational-semialgebraic and \(U_\eta(0)=0\).
Terminal utility is applied to value received minus net seller payments
minus the actual external cash costs; these costs are not subtracted
again in utility units.

Use an independent fair public priority draw, and offer the object
serially at price \(1\), once to each bidder.
The calculations below condition on whichever bidder is drawn first.
An invited bidder may decline entry at no cost, or enter, optionally
buy the report, and accept or reject.
It never receives another offer.
Acceptance requires the actual affordable cash payment.
Seller revenue excludes the external fees.

\subsection{Strict acquisition by a low-signal, high-budget first bidder}
For the first bidder with \(z_i=0\) and budget \(2\), put
\[
 p=\Pr(z_j=1\mid z_i=0)=\frac{1-\rho}{2}\in[1/4,3/8].
\]
At \(\tau=9/10\), consider the complete continuation:
enter, acquire, and accept exactly after a positive report.
Its terminal monetary outcomes \(Z\) are
\[
 \frac{11}{16}\quad(z_j=1,+),\qquad
 -\frac{13}{16}\quad(z_j=0,+),\qquad
 -\frac1{16}\quad(-).
\]
The probabilities are respectively \(9p/10\),
\((1-p)/10\), and \(9/10-4p/5\).
Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:acquisitionmoments}
 \E Z=\frac34p-\frac{11}{80},\qquad
 \E Z^2=\frac{456p+89}{1280}.
\end{equation}
In particular \(\E Z\ge1/20\), while
\[
 \eta\,\E Z^2
 \le\frac1{50}\frac{260}{1280}<\frac1{200}.
\]
Therefore \(\E U_\eta(Z)>0\) throughout the parameter range.
For example, at \(\rho=1/3\), the two moments are
\(9/80\) and \(241/1280\).

The positive-report posterior is
\[
 r_+=\frac{\tau p}{\tau p+(1-\tau)(1-p)}\ge\frac34,
\]
and the negative-report posterior satisfies
\[
 r_-=\frac{(1-\tau)p}{(1-\tau)p+\tau(1-p)}\le\frac1{16}.
\]
The bounds follow by monotonicity of the likelihood odds in \(p,\tau\);
their extreme values occur at
\((p,\tau)=(1/4,9/10)\) and \((3/8,9/10)\), respectively.
After both fees are sunk, acceptance gives \(11/16\) or \(-13/16\);
rejection gives \(-1/16\).
At posterior \(r\), the utility difference is exactly
\[
 \frac34(2r-1)-\eta\,\frac{168-48r}{256}.
\]
For a positive report, its monetary part is at least \(3/8\), and
the absolute risk correction is at most
\(\eta(169/256)<1/50\), so acceptance is strict.
For a negative report, the monetary part is already negative and
the risk correction makes acceptance worse; rejection is strict.

Increasing \(\tau\) above \(9/10\) improves this policy's utility.
Its derivative with respect to \(\tau\) is
\[
 p[U_\eta(11/16)-U_\eta(-1/16)]
 +(1-p)[U_\eta(-1/16)-U_\eta(-13/16)]>0.
\]
This moves probability from a false acceptance to rejection, and
from rejection to a correct acceptance.
Thus the positive entry-and-acquisition payoff is uniform over
the entire stated accuracy range.

Without acquiring information, entry followed by acceptance has mean
monetary outcome
\[
 \frac32p-\frac{25}{32}\le-\frac7{32}.
\]
On all these outcomes \(U_\eta(x)\le x\), so its expected utility is
negative. Entering and rejecting yields \(U_\eta(-1/32)<0\);
mixing these choices cannot improve their expected-utility maximum.
Declining entry yields zero.
The strictly positive acquiring policy, whose report-contingent choices
are strict, therefore forces this type to enter and acquire in every
sequential equilibrium. Full continuation rationality at the first
invitation compares these complete policies using the true conditional
prior; no favorable off-path belief is assumed.
Once these actions are taken, both reports occur with positive probability
and their Bayesian continuation choices are the strict ones above.
Later bidder behavior cannot change this bidder's payoff because it
never receives another offer.

Its largest cash requirement is
\[
 1+\frac1{32}+\frac1{32}=\frac{17}{16}\le2.
\]
A budget-\(1\) bidder cannot fund price \(1\) even without acquiring,
since entry plus acceptance costs \(1+1/32>1\).
Acquisition does not create cash. Entry would then only incur a loss,
so this bidder strictly declines entry.
Liquidity therefore changes actual conduct.

\subsection{A worst-equilibrium same-class guarantee}
A first bidder with \(z_i=1\) and budget \(2\) has value at least
\(5/4\). It strictly enters and accepts without information:
entry and acceptance give strictly positive monetary surplus in
every state, and acceptance is optimal after any report.
Information cannot improve that already optimal decision and its
strictly positive cash cost reduces utility.
This type has probability \(1/4\).
Thus every sequential equilibrium in every displayed environment gives
seller revenue at least \(1/4\); later sale payments are nonnegative.

\begin{proposition}[All-friction same-class bound]\label{prop:ratio}
Let \(\mathfrak M_F\) be any declared feasible class for this environment
family that contains the price-\(1\) serial mechanism, uses the same
true initial budgets and prefix cash constraints, and has zero seller
cost. Its robust value satisfies
\[
 \frac14\le V^*\le4,
 \qquad W(m_{\mathrm{serial}})\ge\frac1{16}V^*.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound was proved for every equilibrium and environment.
Each of the two true budgets is at most \(2\).
Every legal terminal of every same-class mechanism therefore has
total net seller payments at most \(4\), independent of participation
or risk preferences. Hence \(V^*\le4\), and
\(W(m_{\mathrm{serial}})\ge1/4\ge V^*/16\).
\end{proof}
This uses the same feasible class on both sides; no independent-private-
values or ex-post-participation benchmark is substituted.
The information acquisition is strictly active on a positive-probability
type, changes its action with the report, and remains costly.
Correlation, interdependence, strict monetary risk aversion, and
conduct-changing private liquidity all coexist.

A continuous design family containing this mechanism lets the two
serial prices vary independently over \([3/4,5/4]\), and chooses the
public priority order with probability \(\lambda_0\in[1/4,3/4]\).
These are three real mechanism parameters, not a finite catalogue.
Each bidder has finitely many entry, acquisition, and accept/reject
choices. Initial private signals, budgets, and acquired reports remain
private, while the bidder remembers its own choices and observed
priority and refusals.
Accepting is permitted only when the actual cash payment can be made.
Cash-action legality changes at finitely many affine comparisons
between the offered price and remaining cash; the guards agree within
each information set.
The game family is consequently a finite union of rational
semialgebraic legal strata with perfect recall.
The environment parameters
\((\rho,\tau,\eta_1,\eta_2)\) vary over the stated rational intervals.
Nature laws, payoffs, and seller-independent cash costs all have
rational-semialgebraic descriptions.
The seller uses the announced prices and observed acceptances, not
hidden state labels or unknown environment parameters.

Interim participation holds in every equilibrium of this family.
At a bidder's first invitation, no entry guarantees zero for every
hidden state and every later play.
Sequential rationality gives nonnegative expected actual utility at
that information set.
Averaging over invitations and the public priority draw preserves
the inequality conditional on each initial private type.
No invitation incurs no cost.
Realized losses on some acquired-report branches are possible;
the condition is interim participation, not outcome-by-outcome ex-post IR.

\section{Scope, prior attempts, and verification status}\label{sec:scope}
The canonical formulation asks for a stated collection of environments
and a stated feasible mechanism class.
The result here covers the entire declared semialgebraic design
continuum for arbitrary finite input templates, including all legal
and Nature-support boundary strata.
It is not a finite catalogue or a discretized-price substitution.
It is also not a universal characterization across every unbounded
auction tree or arbitrary unrepresented monetary utility.
The environment specification is common knowledge within each
bidders' game; the seller's rules remain independent of hidden
environment and signal information except through actual observations.

The exact consistency condition keeps the mechanism and environment
fixed, uses one common completely mixed assessment witness, and imposes
no equilibrium requirement on that witness.
Full continuation comparisons, true liquidity, endogenous entry and
acquisition, and universal equilibrium participation and revenue are
retained. The finite robust bound comes from actual cash constraints,
not equilibrium continuity.
The value and witness formulas distinguish a supremum from an attained
maximum and actual simple-format achievability from an unattained
supremal threshold.
The explicit nonlinear family shows that the five frictions can be
simultaneously substantive, with a same-class approximation guarantee.

The prior Ultra attempt~\cite{PriorUltra} proves a restricted
harmonic posted-price guarantee with private values, free participation
and information, private budgets and monetary risk under stronger
outcome-by-outcome participation.
Its stated limitations include interdependence, costly entry and
acquisition, and the broader interim benchmark.
The prior Pro attempt~\cite{PriorPro} supplies the attributed
nonattainment example, equilibrium-selection obstructions, a
fixed-entry selected-truthful-equilibrium lottery program, and a
finite-catalogue bound conditional on supplied worst-equilibrium
enclosures.
Those results remain attributed and are not described as having
already computed the exact inner infimum required here.

The present proof explicitly constructs the full equilibrium and
feasibility formulas and applies an imported complete real-algebraic
algorithm to the entire stated design class.
Exact symbolic sequential-equilibrium computation and real quantifier
elimination have established precedents
\cite{KW1982,GEN2024,Basu2014}; no historical priority or new
general-purpose CAD algorithm is claimed.
The mathematical algorithm theorem is proved, but a full CAD execution
for the all-friction family, practical large-game performance, and
toolkit execution are not claimed.
The hand-checked example inequalities and consistency diagnostics do
not replace the general proof.
There is no Lean formalization or external-peer-review claim.
The full hand proof and its transcription have passed internal mathematical
and original-scope review; community review remains separate.
