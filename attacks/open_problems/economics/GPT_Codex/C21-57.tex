\begin{abstract}
For the original finite-state, observed longitudinal graph, we give
finite-sample estimation and simultaneous Euclidean confidence bounds for
the complete vector of hybrid-mechanism Shapley contributions. The outcome
regression is unknown and may involve overlapping sparse factors and a
singular, unknown-rank design. Fixed-quota conditional tables admit an
independent completion; a dimension-free bound for bounded Shapley games
then controls their joint error. Conditional least squares handles the
outcome using the same trajectories. Forward simulation gives polynomial
randomized additive accuracy without a graph-width restriction. We prove
matching regular-class rates, matching joint confidence-diameter lower
bounds, and explicit exponential information examples. Every count,
design, simulation and numerical failure is retained. The sharp rates
use the original convention of constants independent of sample size;
displayed dependence on all growing parameters is not asserted to be an
exact minimax formula in every transition regime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original question, model and contribution}\label{sec:model}

The canonical problem~\cite{catalogue}, motivated by the time-varying
mediation agenda of~\cite[Section 3.4.3]{agenda}, specifies a finite-state
longitudinal causal directed acyclic graph, $T$ times, treatments and
observed covariates, and $J$ ordered mediators at each time. All parents
are observed and each node has at most $r$ parents. Consistency,
sequential exchangeability and positivity identify the stipulated hybrid
interventions. Treatments follow a fixed sequence $a$. Each of the
$K=JT$ mediator mechanisms has active and reference kernels obtained
from its observable conditional law at two specified treatment values,
feasible at the generated history. The unknown bounded terminal mean
is a sum of interactions of order at most $r$ on a specified sparse graph,
and its total absolute value is at most one.

For $S\subseteq[K]$, let $Q_S$ be the resulting non-outcome trajectory
law, using active mechanisms exactly on $S$. For a uniform permutation
$\pi$, let $P_j(\pi)$ be the labels preceding $j$. The requested target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 v(S)=\E_{Q_S}m(H),\qquad
 \phi_j=\E_\pi[v(P_j(\pi)\cup\{j\})-v(P_j(\pi))].
\end{equation}
Here $H$ contains all non-outcome observations. Every permutation
telescopes, so $\sum_j\phi_j=v([K])-v(\varnothing)$. These are allocations
of the joint mechanism contrast; no natural-effect or cross-world
identification is substituted. The question asks for sharp estimation
and simultaneous inference from $n$ independent trajectories, polynomial
graph/overlap regimes, exponential obstructions, and ordering approximation
error. We use squared Euclidean loss and expected Euclidean confidence
diameter. The canonical definition calls a rate sharp when its constants
are independent of $n$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

\paragraph{Finite sparse means.}
Supply the graph and a finite known dictionary $f=(f_1,\ldots,f_p)^T$,
with $|f_l(h)|\le1$, spanning its specified local factor spaces. For example,
one indicator for every cell of every prescribed finite factor scope
always spans the original model, including overlapping factors. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean}
 \E[Y\mid H]=m(H)=f(H)^T\alpha,\qquad |m|\le1,
 \qquad |Y|\le B,\quad B\ge1.
\end{equation}
The bound on $m$ holds on observation and all required hybrid supports.
The coefficients are unknown, may be nonunique and may cancel; no separate
component bound or supplied outcome table is assumed. A supplied smaller
bounded basis may be used when that is the declared model. We count its
actual description and evaluation size. A large implicit factor table
does not become small merely because it is called sparse. The uniform
envelope $B$ is a class parameter; replace an actual smaller bound by one.
Section~\ref{sec:sharp} also treats the union with no uniform envelope.
Only the conditional mean in~\eqref{eq:mean}, not the full conditional
outcome distribution, is required to be parent-local.

\paragraph{Rows and support.}
Let $N$ count non-outcome random nodes whose conditional laws are estimated;
treatment assignment tables need not be estimated for a fixed intervention.
A source row is a parent address of the \emph{same} observed node, with
the stipulated source-treatment argument and the other generated parents.
Tables are node- and time-specific, even if true parameters happen to be
equal at different times. Distinct addresses of one node are disjoint
observed parent events. Let $\mathcal C$ contain the rows reached by some
true hybrid and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 \Prb\{\text{observed parents equal }c\}\ge\beta>0
 \quad(c\in\mathcal C),\qquad C=|\mathcal C|\le N/\beta.
\end{equation}
Irrelevant structural-zero rows are allowed. The procedure need not know
$\mathcal C$; it uses its displayed upper bound. Positivity of a treatment
decision alone does not imply~\eqref{eq:overlap} for its histories.

Set $\Sigma=\E[f(H)f(H)^T]$ and $R=\range(\Sigma)$. Terminal hybrid
positivity means that every generated outcome-parent state is
observationally supported. In particular it implies the compatibility
actually used below:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compat}
 f(H)\in R\quad Q_S\text{-almost surely for every }S.
\end{equation}
Indeed a function $f^T u$ with $u\in\ker\Sigma$ vanishes observationally
on every supported outcome-parent state and hence on every hybrid state.
This does not require absolute continuity of the \emph{entire} hybrid
trajectory. For example $M=A$ and a mean depending only on $M$ can support
the hybrid $A=1,M=0$ through its observed outcome-parent value $M=0$.
When $\Sigma$ has positive rank, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:design}
 \lambda_{\min}^{+}(\Sigma)\ge\lambda>0.
\end{equation}
The rank and range are unknown. Every fixed finite positive-rank design
has some such $\lambda$; quantitative uniform conclusions state it as
a class bound. Rank zero is addressed separately. Thus all finite
original graphs and finite $r$ are included; $r$ is not required to stay
fixed as the graph grows.

\paragraph{Context and distinction from the three prior attempts.}
All three complete repository attempts at the pinned commit in~\cite{prior}
were inspected. They already prove local-table bounds, simultaneous
intervals, a thin-graph rare-path lower bound, and polynomial exact
calculation under bounded elimination width. Version 2 adds small
mechanism-ancestor sets for supplied factors. Version 3 already learns
unknown finite-dictionary coefficients under a positive-definite Gram
bound and combines them with that exact calculation. Unknown-factor OLS
and fixed-graph root-$n$ exponents alone are therefore not new here.
The present argument supplies a dimension-free vector bound for kernel
estimation, unknown-rank identifiable quotients, matching vector-risk and
joint-diameter families, and additive computation for arbitrary width,
with all failure events and finite precision included. Sampling-based
Shapley error bounds are classical; see~\cite{sampling}. We prove the
particular bounds used here and do not claim priority for Monte Carlo.

\section{Estimator and complete finite-sample guarantees}\label{sec:main}

If $K=0$ or $p=0$, the target is the zero vector and is returned exactly;
the nontrivial formulas below assume $K,p\ge1$.
For a non-outcome table collection $\theta$, define the $K$-by-$p$ matrix
$A(\theta)$ whose $l$th column is the Shapley vector with outcome function
$f_l$. Then $\phi=A(\theta)\alpha$.

Suppose first $k=\lfloor n\beta/2\rfloor\ge1$. At each observed parent
address retain its first $k$ responses in a fixed trajectory order.
Use that empirical conditional law when the quota is met and a fixed
valid default otherwise. Store only observed addresses and look up rows
lazily. Denote the fitted tables by $\widehat\theta$ and their analytical
Shapley matrix by $\widehat A=A(\widehat\theta)$; the algorithm will not
compute this matrix exactly.

Let $X_{il}=f_l(H_i)$, $G=X^T X$, and $P_G$ be the orthogonal projector
onto the empirical range. Use the safe inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:estimator}
 D=\begin{cases}G^\dagger,&G-(n\lambda/2)P_G\succeq0,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad \widehat\alpha=DX^T Y,
 \qquad \widehat b=\|\widehat\alpha\|_1.
\end{equation}
The symbol $\dagger$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse. The test retains the
whole empirical inverse only when every positive empirical eigenvalue
meets the threshold. It is not a test of population rank.

For each of $M$ independent repetitions draw a uniform permutation.
Simulate its $K+1$ prefix hybrids forward under the fitted tables and
evaluate $\widehat m=f^T\widehat\alpha$ on each. Assign successive
differences to the switched labels, reusing each computed prefix value
in adjacent differences. Average the vectors to obtain $\widehat\phi_{MC}$.
Let $z$ be its Euclidean projection onto the ball $\mathcal B_5$ of radius
five centered at zero. This projection is useful for risk control; the
raw vector may also be reported.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:failures}
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_C&=\min\{1,(N/\beta)e^{-n\beta/8}\},&
 \delta_G&=\min\{1,p e^{-n\lambda/(8p)}\},\\
 \delta&=\delta_C+\delta_G,&
 L(a)&=1+\sqrt{2\log(1/a)}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[All-sample risk and honest simultaneous inference]\label{thm:main}
For every finite graph and unknown mean described in Section~\ref{sec:model},
with positive design rank and $k\ge1$, the ideal finite-index implementation
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk}
 \E\|z-\phi\|_2^2\le
 \frac{25}{k}+\frac{50B^2p}{n\lambda}
       +\frac{8B^2Kp}{\lambda M}+100\delta.
\end{equation}
For any $a_C,a_Y,a_M\in(0,1)$ such that
$\delta+a_C+a_Y+a_M\le a<1$, an honest joint confidence region is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confidence}
 \mathcal B_5\cap\{u:\|u-z\|_2\le R_n\},\qquad
 R_n=\frac{5L(a_C)}{\sqrt k}
       +B\sqrt{\frac{50p}{n\lambda}}L(a_Y)
       +2\widehat b\sqrt{\frac K M}L(a_M).
\end{equation}
Its coverage is at least $1-a$ for every $n$, and its diameter is at most
$\min(10,2R_n)$. If the deterministic budgets admit no positive split,
use the full ball $\mathcal B_5$. If $k=0$, use the zero point and that
ball. If the model has zero feature rank, its target and point estimator
are identically zero. Empirical rank zero alone does not justify a
singleton confidence region over an unknown-rank class.
\end{theorem}

The confidence region covers the entire vector on one event; its
coordinate projections are simultaneous intervals. We do not call their
maximum coordinate width minimax-optimal in every growing-dimensional
family. The raw simulated increments telescope to their matching
simulated endpoint contrast. Radial projection generally changes that
sample identity; it does not change the population identity in
\eqref{eq:target}. Section~\ref{sec:computation} adds finite-bit and
rounding errors to these exact mathematical bounds.

\section{A bounded-game lemma and independent row completion}\label{sec:tables}

\begin{lemma}[Euclidean Shapley bound]\label{lem:shapley}
For any finite set game with $|v(S)|\le b$, its Shapley vector satisfies
$\|\phi(v)\|_2\le5b$, independently of the number $K$ of labels.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$K=0$ is empty and $K=1$ has bound $2b$. For $K\ge2$, fix
$\|u\|_2=1$ and put $U=\sum_j u_j$. For $|S|=s$ with $0<s<K$,
the coefficient of $v(S)$ in $u^T\phi(v)$ is
\[
 \frac{K\sum_{j\in S}u_j-sU}{s(K-s)\binom K s}.
\]
This follows by separating additions to $S\setminus\{j\}$ and
subtractions at $S$ in the permutation formula. The empty/full
coefficients contribute $2|U|/K$ in absolute value. A uniform $s$-subset
has mean sum $sU/K$ and variance
\[
 \frac{s(K-s)}{K(K-1)}\left(1-\frac{U^2}K\right).
\]
One obtains this by expanding indicator covariances, using
$\Prb(j\in S)=s/K$ and $\Prb(i,j\in S)=s(s-1)/(K(K-1))$.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the absolute coefficient sum at size $s$ by
$\sqrt{K/(K-1)}\sqrt{1-U^2/K}/\sqrt{s(K-s)}$.
The function $[x(K-x)]^{-1/2}$ is convex on $(0,K)$: its second derivative
is $[x(K-x)]^{-3/2}+3(K-2x)^2/[4\{x(K-x)\}^{5/2}]>0$.
Midpoint comparison on $[s-1/2,s+1/2]$ therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{s=1}^{K-1}\frac1{\sqrt{s(K-s)}}
 \le\int_0^K\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(K-x)}}=\pi.
\]
With $t=U/\sqrt K$, the full coefficient sum is at most
\[
 \frac{2|t|}{\sqrt K}+\pi\sqrt{\frac K{K-1}}\sqrt{1-t^2}
 \le\sqrt{\frac4K+\frac{\pi^2K}{K-1}}
 \le\sqrt{2+2\pi^2}<5.
\]
Multiply by $b$ and take the supremum over unit $u$.
\end{proof}

We will use two elementary probability facts. If a variable has range
length $c$, its log moment generating function, after centering, is at
most $t^2c^2/8$. Indeed its tilted variance is at most $c^2/4$:
for any variable in $[a,b]$, $(V-a)(b-V)\ge0$ bounds its variance by
$(b-a)^2/4$; integrate the second derivative of the log moment generating
function twice. Revealing independent inputs of a function with
single-input changes bounded by $c_i$ and applying this fact to its Doob
increments gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bd}
 \E e^{t(F-\E F)}\le e^{t^2\sum_i c_i^2/8},\qquad
 \Prb\{F>\E F+s\}\le e^{-2s^2/\sum_i c_i^2}.
\end{equation}
Also a binomial count with mean $nq$ obeys
$\Prb\{Z<nq/2\}\le e^{-nq/8}$. For example Markov applied to
$e^{-(\log2)Z}$ gives the stronger exponent
$-nq(1-\log2)/2$, using $1-q+qe^{-t}\le\exp(q(e^{-t}-1))$.

\begin{lemma}[Completion of fixed-quota tables]\label{lem:completion}
There are mutually independent completed $k$-sample arrays, each with
its true source-row law, whose fitted hybrid games agree with the actual
quota estimator on an event $E_C$ satisfying $\Prb(E_C^c)\le\delta_C$.
On $E_C$, every fitted hybrid support is contained in its true support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a deficient row retain all available responses and append independent
draws from its true conditional law until there are $k$. These extra
draws are a proof coupling and are not available to the estimator.
Condition on the observed earlier nodes of all trajectories in a
topological ordering, as well as earlier auxiliary draws. Parent addresses
and selected trajectory indices for the present node are fixed.
The observed responses have independent row laws by factorization of
the observed DAG and independence across trajectories. After completion,
every row has exactly $k$ independent draws, and the joint conditional
product law is independent of everything conditioned upon. Induction
over nodes proves independence of all completed arrays. It does not
assert independence of ordinary varying-count empirical tables.

Let $E_C$ mean that every row in $\mathcal C$ meets quota. Binomial
tails and a union bound give
$\Prb(E_C^c)\le\min\{1,C e^{-n\beta/8}\}\le\delta_C$; independence of counts
is unnecessary. On this event, completed and actual tables coincide
at all relevant rows. Their responses lie in true conditional supports.
In a generated path a first departure from true hybrid support would
need either an earlier unsupported parent path or an out-of-support
response at a relevant row. Neither is possible. This induction proves
both support containment and agreement for every hybrid, without
enumerating coalitions. Defaults at irrelevant rows cannot be the first
cause of a departure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product-empirical vector bound]\label{lem:product}
Using the true bounded mean $m$ with the completed non-outcome arrays
gives a vector $\widetilde\phi$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \E\|\widetilde\phi-\phi\|_2^2\le25/k,\qquad
 \Prb\{\|\widetilde\phi-\phi\|_2>5L(a)/\sqrt k\}\le a.
\end{equation}
On $E_C$, $\widetilde\phi=\widehat A\alpha$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $U$ contain one independent draw from each relevant true row.
For each $S$, generate a path $H_S(U)$ by reading the appropriate draw
at each node. Each node-specific row is read at most once along a path.
Thus $\E m(H_S(U))=v(S)$. Sharing $U$ between different hybrids is
only a coupling of already identified marginals, not a causal assumption
about one subject's incompatible counterfactuals. The pathwise game is
bounded by one on its true supports. Its Shapley vector $g(U)$ has norm
at most five by Lemma~\ref{lem:shapley}, and $\E g(U)=\phi$.

The completed plug-in vector is the product-empirical average of $g$,
choosing one of the $k$ entries independently in every row. Independently
permute the $k$ entries in each row, match entries into $k$ diagonal
tuples, and average $g$ on those tuples. Conditional on the arrays, the
expectation of this diagonal average equals the product-empirical vector.
Unconditionally the $k$ tuples are independent with the true product law,
even after fixing their permutations. Jensen and the variance of an
iid average give the squared bound in~\eqref{eq:product}.

For that iid average the expected error norm is at most $5/\sqrt k$,
and replacing one tuple changes it by at most $10/k$. Equation~\eqref{eq:bd}
bounds its exponential norm moment. Conditional Jensen for
$x\mapsto\exp(t\|x\|_2)$ transfers the same upper bound to the
product-empirical average. Markov optimization gives the second bound
in~\eqref{eq:product}. The last assertion follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:completion}. No dimension or output-coordinate union
appears in this argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Unknown regression on the identifiable feature quotient}\label{sec:regression}

For $u\in\ker\Sigma$, $\E(f^Tu)^2=0$. Taking a finite basis of this
kernel proves $f\in R$ observationally. Compatibility~\eqref{eq:compat}
does the same for true hybrids. Hence $A\ker\Sigma=0$. Fitted support
containment on $E_C$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annihilate}
 \widehat A\ker\Sigma=0\quad\text{on }E_C.
\end{equation}
Thus coefficient nonuniqueness is harmless for both targets on that event.

We import precisely the following matrix Chernoff inequality
from~\cite[Theorem 1.1]{tropp}: independent positive semidefinite
$d$-dimensional matrices $V_i$ with $\|V_i\|\le R_0$ and
$\mu=\lambda_{\min}(\sum_i\E V_i)$ satisfy
\[
 \Prb\left\{\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum_iV_i\right)
          \le(1-t)\mu\right\}
 \le d\left[\frac{e^{-t}}{(1-t)^{1-t}}\right]^{\mu/R_0},
 \quad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Restrict $f(H_i)f(H_i)^T$ to the fixed population space $R$.
These matrices are independent, have norm at most $p$, and their
expected sum has minimum eigenvalue at least $n\lambda$ there.
At $t=1/2$ the bracket is at most $e^{-1/8}$. Consequently, with
probability at least $1-\delta_G$, the event $E_G$ holds that
$G|_R\succeq(n\lambda/2)I_R$. Every sampled feature already lies in
$R$, so on $E_G$, $\range(G)=R$ and~\eqref{eq:estimator} uses the
pseudoinverse on exactly that space. The method does not know $R$.

Condition on all non-outcome data, denoted $\mathcal D_H$. The errors
$\epsilon_i=Y_i-f(H_i)^T\alpha$ are conditionally independent and
centered, with variance at most $B^2$ and range length at most $2B$.
Their absolute values need not be at most $B$. On $E=E_C\cap E_G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noise}
 \widehat A(\widehat\alpha-\alpha)
   =\sum_i w_i\epsilon_i,\qquad
 w_i=\widehat A G^\dagger f(H_i),\qquad
 V=\sum_i\|w_i\|_2^2=\tr(\widehat A G^\dagger\widehat A^T).
\end{equation}
The equality uses $G^\dagger G G^\dagger=G^\dagger$.
The exact conditional squared error is
$\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(Y_i\mid H_i)\|w_i\|_2^2$, at most $B^2V$.
Every column of $\widehat A$ is a bounded-feature game, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:shapley} implies $\|\widehat A\|_F^2\le25p$.
On $E_G$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leverage}
 V\le\frac{50p}{n\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Conditional mean norm is at most $B\sqrt V$. A change of $Y_i$ changes
the noise norm by at most $2B\|w_i\|_2$. Thus~\eqref{eq:bd} gives
a conditional error radius $B\sqrt V L(a_Y)$, with failure at most
$a_Y$. The deterministic bound~\eqref{eq:leverage} avoids exact
computation of $\widehat A$. It is an upper bound, not a claim that
every poorly conditioned feature direction affects this target.

The safe inverse also ensures on \emph{every} sample that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 \|\widehat\alpha\|_2\le B\sqrt{2/\lambda},\qquad
 |\widehat m(h)|\le\widehat b\le B\sqrt{2p/\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Indeed every accepted nonzero singular value of $X$ is at least
$\sqrt{n\lambda/2}$, while $\|Y\|_2\le B\sqrt n$; rejection gives zero.
This controls even fallback histories. No global projection of the
sparse regression function onto a difficult constraint set is used.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Conditional on all data, each simulated increment vector $W$ has mean
$\widehat A\widehat\alpha$ and norm at most $2\widehat b\sqrt K$.
Correctness follows by forward generation of each prefix and then
averaging the uniform permutation. Prefix simulations may be independent
or share a correctly implemented random coupling. Independent repetitions
give conditional variance at most $4\widehat b^2K/M$ for their average.
Its mean error norm is at most $2\widehat b\sqrt{K/M}$ and replacing
one repetition changes that norm by at most $4\widehat b\sqrt K/M$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:bd} gives the last radius in~\eqref{eq:confidence}.

On $E$, decompose the raw error into
\[
 (\widehat A\alpha-\phi)
 +\widehat A(\widehat\alpha-\alpha)
 +(\widehat\phi_{MC}-\widehat A\widehat\alpha).
\]
The first term is a function of $\mathcal D_H$ and equals the completed
error there. The second is centered conditional on $\mathcal D_H$;
$E$ also depends only on $\mathcal D_H$. The last is centered conditional
on all data. Therefore all cross terms vanish after multiplication by
$\ind_E$ and expectation. Lemma~\ref{lem:product},
\eqref{eq:leverage} and~\eqref{eq:envelope} bound the three squared
terms by $25/k$, $50B^2p/(n\lambda)$ and $8B^2Kp/(\lambda M)$.
Projection onto a convex set containing $\phi$ cannot increase distance
to $\phi$. On $E^c$ both projected point and target have norm at most
five, so squared error is at most $100$. This proves~\eqref{eq:risk}.

The three norm tails and $\Prb(E^c)\le\delta$ give~\eqref{eq:confidence}
by the triangle and union bounds. Projection again cannot increase
distance to the true target. If $\Sigma=0$, observational and hybrid
features vanish, $m=0$, and every computed coefficient is zero; this
proves the rank-zero assertion. An unknown positive population direction
can be absent from the sample, which explains the retained $\delta_G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local outcome tables without irrelevant-cell coverage]\label{cor:table}
If $m$ is an unknown table of its observed parents, let $\beta_Y>0$
lower-bound only parent cells used by a true hybrid. Use ordinary cell
means and zero for absent cells. Then~\eqref{eq:risk} improves to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:table-risk}
 \frac{25}{k}+\frac{50B^2}{n\beta_Y}+\frac{4B^2K}{M}
 +100(\delta_C+\delta_Y),\qquad
 \delta_Y=\min\{1,\beta_Y^{-1}e^{-n\beta_Y/8}\}.
\end{equation}
In~\eqref{eq:confidence} use $50/(n\beta_Y)$ instead of $50p/(n\lambda)$,
$B$ instead of $\widehat b$, and $\delta_Y$ instead of $\delta_G$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use disjoint cell indicators as the dictionary. For independent signs $s_l$,
the function $\sum_l s_l f_l$ is bounded by one. Thus
$\|\widehat A\|_F^2=\E_s\|\widehat A s\|_2^2\le25$.
There are at most $1/\beta_Y$ relevant cells. Except on probability
$\delta_Y$, each has count at least $n\beta_Y/2$. On $E_C$ every fitted
hybrid uses only these cells, so all other columns of $\widehat A$ vanish.
Conditional cell-mean centering and~\eqref{eq:noise} then give
$V=\sum_c\|\widehat A(:,c)\|_2^2/N_c\le50/(n\beta_Y)$.
Irrelevant rare cells, including unobserved ones, contribute zero and
cannot force a global inverse rejection. Every cell mean is bounded by
$B$, so the simulation bound is $4B^2K/M$. The preceding proof applies.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp risk and simultaneous confidence diameter}\label{sec:sharp}

For clarity, an honest region has a measurable membership graph and
covers every member of its stated class with probability at least $1-a$.
Randomized procedures are allowed; adjoining their independent seed
changes neither KL divergence nor total variation. We will repeatedly use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bern-kl}
 \KL(\operatorname{Ber}(u),\operatorname{Ber}(v))
 \le\frac{(u-v)^2}{v(1-v)},\qquad
 \TV(P,Q)\le\sqrt{\KL(P,Q)/2}.
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows from $\log x\le x-1$. For the second,
apply the log-sum inequality to an event attaining the total variation
and its complement. The resulting binary divergence is at least twice
the squared difference of the event probabilities, since its second
derivative in the first argument is $1/[u(1-u)]\ge4$ and its value and
first derivative vanish at equality. The log-sum inequality itself is
Jensen for $x\log x$. These arguments also follow by limits at zero masses.

\begin{proposition}[Source coverage and fixed-class sharpness]\label{prop:scalar}
There is a binary indegree-one family within the original problem with
minimax squared risk at least $c\min\{1,(n\beta)^{-1}\}$ and honest
expected joint diameter at least
$c_a\min\{1,(n\beta)^{-1/2}\}$ for every fixed $a\in(0,1)$.
Consequently Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives sharp $n^{-1}$ risk and
$n^{-1/2}$ diameter over fixed quantitative classes containing this
nondegenerate family. These lower bounds are not assertions for every graph.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $A\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\beta)$, $0<\beta\le1/4$,
$M\mid A=0\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)$,
$M\mid A=1\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+t)$, $|t|\le1/4$, and $Y=M$.
Treatments are randomized, both source rows and both mediator values are
supported, and the sole contribution is $t$. Additional independent dummy
mechanisms may be added. The $n$-sample KL between $t$ and $s$ is at most
$(16/3)n\beta(t-s)^2$, and that to $t=0$ is at most $4n\beta t^2$.
Choose $t=\pm\Delta$ with
$\Delta=\min\{1,(n\beta)^{-1/2}\}/32$. Their total variation is less
than $1/4$. Nearest-target testing turns squared error below $\Delta^2$
into correct testing. The average testing error is at least
$(1-\TV)/2\ge3/8$, proving the risk lower bound.

For the diameter put $h=1-a$ and
$b=h\min\{1,(n\beta)^{-1/2}\}/16$. Every $|t|\le b$ has sample law
within $h/2$ of the zero law. Honesty therefore implies under that same
zero law probability at least $h/2$ of containing each point $t$ of
the target line. Fubini gives expected intersection length at least
$hb$; a subset of a line has length no greater than its diameter.
This proves the lower bound for every $a<1$, without a restriction
$a<1/2$. For fixed finite parameters, $M\ge nK$ makes
\eqref{eq:risk} $O(n^{-1})$ and~\eqref{eq:confidence}
$O(n^{-1/2})$ in deterministic diameter, using~\eqref{eq:envelope}.
The failure exponentials vanish faster than $1/n$.
\end{proof}

More generally the same argument applies to a class containing a smooth
one-dimensional family with sample KL to its center bounded by $cn t^2$
and a nonzero target derivative: project onto that derivative, on a small
interval where the projected derivative stays bounded away from zero,
and integrate along that target coordinate. This explicitly witnessed
nondegeneracy is needed for a graph-specific positive lower bound.
Known dummy targets, identical mechanism laws or known constant means
can instead have exactly zero risk.

\begin{theorem}[Matched sparse-regression risk and joint diameter]\label{thm:paired}
For each $d\ge1$ and $0<\kappa\le1/4$, there is a fixed original-model
class with $K=2d$, $p=d+1$, $\beta=\kappa$ and
$\lambda\asymp\kappa$ whose minimax squared risk and honest expected
joint Euclidean diameter, when $n\kappa\ge d^2$, are respectively
\[
 \asymp\frac{p}{n\lambda},\qquad
 \asymp_a\sqrt{\frac{p}{n\lambda}}.
\]
The statement permits known non-outcome kernels, so its lower bounds
also apply when they must be learned. The diameter conclusion holds
for every fixed $a\in(0,1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $T=1$, $J=2d$, $A\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\kappa)$. Conditional on
$A=1$ the mediators are independent fair bits, and conditional on $A=0$
they are independent Bernoulli$(\kappa)$ bits. The active/reference laws
are those two laws. Put $Z_j=M_{2j-1}-M_{2j}$, and use dictionary
$(1,Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)$. Conditional means of distinct $Z_j$ vanish under
either treatment, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-gram}
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sigma^2 I_d),\qquad
 \sigma^2=\kappa/2+2\kappa(1-\kappa)^2,\qquad
 13\kappa/8\le\sigma^2\le5\kappa/2.
\end{equation}
Also $\sigma^2\le13/32<1$, so $\lambda=\sigma^2$.
Let the fixed coefficient class be $\|b\|_1\le1/4$, and let
$Y\mid H$ be Bernoulli with mean $1/2+\sum_j b_jZ_j$.
This is a sum of unary terms, bounded between $1/4$ and $3/4$.
Its outcome has $2d$ parents; take the original bound $r\ge2d$.
Every hybrid configuration is supported under observed $A=1$.
All causal assumptions are realized by independent structural draws.
With $\gamma=1/2-\kappa\ge1/4$, direct evaluation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-target}
 \phi_b=\gamma\sum_j b_j(e_{2j-1}-e_{2j}),\qquad
 \|\phi_b-\phi_c\|_2^2=2\gamma^2\|b-c\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The non-outcome law is fixed. Equation~\eqref{eq:bern-kl} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-kl}
 \KL(P_b^n,P_0^n)\le4n\sigma^2\|b\|_2^2
        \le10n\kappa\|b\|_2^2,\qquad
 \KL(P_b^n,P_c^n)\le\tfrac{16}3n\sigma^2\|b-c\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Take $b_j=\Delta s_j$, $s_j\in\{-1,1\}$, with
$\Delta=1/\lceil32\sqrt{n\kappa}\rceil$.
Since $n\kappa\ge d^2$, $d\Delta\le1/32$ and all alternatives lie in
the same fixed coefficient class. Neighboring signs have KL at most
$5/96<1/16$ and total variation less than $1/4$. A wrong decoded sign
of the pair contrast costs at least $2(\gamma\Delta)^2$ squared error.
Pairing the uniform sign alternatives along each bit yields average
error probability at least $3/8$ for that bit. Summing gives risk at
least $(3/4)d\gamma^2\Delta^2\ge c d/(n\kappa)$.

For a matching upper on this known-kernel family use
\[
 \widehat b_j=\frac1{n\sigma^2}\sum_iZ_{ij}(Y_i-1/2).
\]
It is unbiased, and $|Y_i-1/2|=1/2$ gives
$\E\|\phi_{\widehat b}-\phi_b\|_2^2\le
\gamma^2d/(2n\sigma^2)$. Markov gives an honest Euclidean ball of
radius $\sqrt{\gamma^2d/(2an\sigma^2)}$, proving the diameter upper.

For its lower let $h=1-a$ and first suppose
$d\ge D_a=\lceil32\log(2)/h\rceil$. A greedy packing of the sign cube
with Hamming distance greater than $d/4$ has size $m\ge e^{d/8}$.
Indeed the radius-$\lfloor d/4\rfloor$ ball occupies at most $e^{-d/8}$
of that cube: for $V\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/2)$,
$\E e^{-(V-d/2)}=\cosh(1/2)^d\le e^{d/8}$ and Markov proves the
claim; greedy removed balls cover the cube. Use these signs with
$\Delta_a=1/\lceil32\sqrt{n\kappa/h}\rceil$.
Their target separation is at least
\[
 s_n=\gamma\Delta_a\sqrt{2d}
       \ge\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{132}\sqrt{\frac d{n\kappa}}.
\]
For a uniform packing index, its mutual information with the data is
at most the average KL to $P_0^n$, at most $5hd/512$ by
\eqref{eq:paired-kl}. This follows by subtracting the nonnegative
KL of the mixture law from that average. For any decoder, conditional
entropy is at most $\log2+\Prb(\text{error})\log m$: reveal first
whether the decoder is correct and, if not, one of at most $m-1$ labels.
Since $\log m\ge d/8$, every decoder has error at least
\[
 1-\frac{I+\log2}{\log m}\ge1-21h/64=a+43h/64.
\]
Decode by the first packing target in the proposed confidence region.
Coverage and diameter less than $s_n$ imply a correct answer. Therefore
the average probability of diameter at least $s_n$ is at least $h/2$,
and worst expected diameter is at least $hs_n/2$.

For $1\le d<D_a$ use the continuum $b=t e_1$ with
$|t|\le h/(16\sqrt{n\kappa})$. Its law is within $h/2$ of $P_0^n$
by~\eqref{eq:paired-kl} and~\eqref{eq:bern-kl}. The line-segment
argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} gives expected diameter at
least $\sqrt2h^2/(64\sqrt{n\kappa})$, hence at least
$\sqrt2h^2\sqrt{d/(n\kappa)}/(64\sqrt{D_a})$.
This covers every dimension and every fixed $a<1$.
\end{proof}

The population target-aligned regression trace in this family is exactly
$2d\gamma^2/\sigma^2$, of order $p/\lambda$. It is not a lower bound
for a target that annihilates the corresponding directions. The condition
$n\kappa\ge d^2$ is material: a dense sign box must fit the bounded
total-mean class. No formula $\min\{1,p/(n\lambda)\}$ for all other
joint regimes is inferred. For fixed $d,\kappa$, the theorem establishes
precisely the canonical sharp $n$-rates. Under uniform asymptotic coverage,
apply the lower proofs with $a+\eta<1$ for all sufficiently large $n$;
the canonical liminf--inf condition supplies that finite-sample coverage.

\paragraph{The unrestricted unions.}
If qualitative positivity is the only uniform restriction, choose
$\beta_n$ of order $n^{-2}$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}. Risk and
honest diameter cannot shrink uniformly. Lemma~\ref{lem:shapley} gives
the matching constant risk upper with the zero estimator and constant
diameter upper with $\mathcal B_5$. Likewise, with fixed non-Y overlap
but no uniform outcome envelope, take a mediator with active/reference
probabilities $3/4,1/4$ and an outcome on $\{-B,B\}$ with mean $tM$,
$|t|\le1/4$. Its target is $t/2$, while sample KL to $t=0$ is uniformly
$O(nt^2/B^2)$ for $t\in[-1/4,1/4]$. Choose $B/\sqrt n$ sufficiently
large that all these laws lie within $(1-a)/2$ of the zero law.
Integrating transferred coverage over this fixed target segment gives
a positive constant diameter lower for every $a<1$; two separated
targets give the risk lower. Individual boundedness therefore
does not silently supply a uniform noise bound.

\section{Polynomial computation and its full error budget}\label{sec:computation}

The data pass stores at most $nN$ observed address records. No Cartesian
product of possible parent values is initialized. For rational data,
features and $\lambda$, the inverse rule~\eqref{eq:estimator} is an
exact rational algorithm. Choose a rational full-column-rank basis $U$
of the empirical range and compute
\[
 P_G=U(U^TU)^{-1}U^T,\qquad
 G^\dagger=U(U^TGU)^{-1}U^T.
\]
Both are zero in rank zero. The second identity follows by representing
$G$ on its range and checking $GG^\dagger=G^\dagger G=P_G$.
An exact rational positive-semidefiniteness test for
$G-(n\lambda/2)P_G$ suffices. Symmetric elimination either pivots on a
positive diagonal and reduces to its Schur complement, encounters a
negative diagonal, or has a zero diagonal with a nonzero off-diagonal
entry, which certifies a negative two-dimensional quadratic value.
An all-zero residual is accepted. These are polynomial rational
operations; determinants and minors bound their reduced bit lengths
polynomially in input length. No exact irrational eigenvector encoding
is assumed.

Each simulation repetition uses $K+1$ topological traversals and dictionary
evaluations. It draws only from stored empirical responses or explicit
defaults. Uniform permutations use Fisher--Yates; uniform finite indices
can be drawn by rejection from fair bits in expected polynomial time.
Thus runtime is polynomial in $n,M,K,N,p$, graph/address and dictionary
description/evaluation length, and data bit length. In particular no
elimination-width or mechanism-ancestry restriction is needed.

For a strict worst-case bit budget, let $D_0$ bound the number of finite
uniform choices and $s_0=\max(n,K,1)$ bound their sizes. Replace each
choice from $s$ possibilities by $\lfloor sU/2^b\rfloor$ for a fresh
uniform $b$-bit integer $U$. Its total variation distance from uniform
is at most $s/2^b$. Stepwise maximal coupling, also for adaptive row
queries, couples the entire algorithm to the ideal one except on
probability at most $D_0s_0/2^b$. Choose $b$ to make this at most
$\eta_{\rm bit}$. Add $\eta_{\rm bit}$ to the confidence failure and
$100\eta_{\rm bit}$ to the projected risk. Fixed deterministic defaults
need no extra draws.

Radial projection of a rational vector uses a single square root. Bisection
with rational upper bounds on its norm gives an inward rational projection
within Euclidean error $\rho>0$, in polynomial bit length and
$\log(1/\rho)$. For a zero vector use zero. Add $\rho$ to the radius;
if $R_*$ bounds the ideal risk with the bit allowance, the final risk is
at most $(\sqrt{R_*}+\rho)^2$ by the $L^2$ triangle inequality.
Logarithms and square roots in reported radii may similarly be rounded
up with a declared error tolerance. These are numerical error budgets,
not unreported exact-real operations.

For prescribed additive error $\varepsilon$ and failure $a$, it suffices
to make the three terms in~\eqref{eq:confidence} small and require, for
example,
\[
 n\beta\ge8\log\frac{4N}{\beta a},\qquad
 n\lambda\ge8p\log\frac{4p}{a}.
\]
With a fixed positive failure split, this requires polynomial sample and
simulation counts if $N,K,p,B,\beta^{-1},\lambda^{-1}$, the input
description/evaluation size, $\varepsilon^{-1}$ and $\log(1/a)$ are
polynomial in $J,T$. Bounded alphabets and interaction order, with a
polynomial number of factor scopes, ensure polynomial explicit
dictionary size. These are sufficient conditions; they are not necessary
for a graph whose target ignores its difficult directions. For a fixed
class, $M\ge nK$ and suitably small bit/rounding tolerances preserve the
sharp statistical rates.

\section{Exponential information and computation distinctions}\label{sec:exponential}

\begin{proposition}[Thin-graph rare paths, credited to the priors]\label{prop:rarepath}
For fixed $0<\kappa<1$, bounded-indegree binary longitudinal graphs
can require exponentially many observations: a family has squared risk
at least $c\min\{1,(n\kappa^T)^{-1}\}$ and honest diameter at least
$c_a\min\{1,(n\kappa^T)^{-1/2}\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use independent $A_t\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\kappa)$ and update
$H_t=H_{t-1}A_t$ from $H_0=1$. Earlier mechanisms are irrelevant fair
bits. The final mediator has probabilities $3/4$ under $A_T=1$ and
$1/4$ under $A_T=0$. Set the terminal Bernoulli mean to
$1/2+tH_TM_T$, $|t|\le1/4$. Under all-one treatment the sole contribution
is $t/2$. All nodes have at most two parents, the mean has interaction
order two, and every required hybrid state is supported. Observations
depend on $t$ only on $H_T=M_T=1$, of probability
$(3/4)\kappa^T$. The KL/testing and continuum calculations in
Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} apply with that probability. The risk
construction already appears in all three prior versions~\cite{prior};
it is included to complete, not to reassign credit for, this regime.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Constant source coverage, exponential feature information]\label{prop:feature}
There is an original-model family with all non-Y source-parent masses
at least $1/2$ and a directly evaluable two-feature mean for which
minimax risk and honest expected joint diameter are, for every $n$,
\[
 \asymp\min\{1,2^d/n\},\qquad
 \asymp_a\min\{1,\sqrt{2^d/n}\},\qquad d\ge2.
\]
Its outcome interaction order and indegree are $d+1$; they are not
uniformly bounded in this example.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $T=d+1$, $J=1$, and independent fair treatments $A_1,\ldots,A_{d+1}$.
At time $j+1$ observe $Z_j$ generated from $A_j$, with success
probability $1-1/d$ when $A_j=1$ and $1/d$ when $A_j=0$.
This respects covariates before the next treatment. All structural
draws are independent conditional on their displayed parents. The
first $d$ mediator mechanisms are dummy fair bits. The final mediator
$M$ has probabilities $3/4,1/4$ at $A_{d+1}=1,0$. Observationally
$Z_1,\ldots,Z_d,M$ are independent fair bits. Put
\[
 F=\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^d Z_j\Bigr)M,\quad
 q=\Prb(F=1)=2^{-d-1},\quad
 Y\mid H\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+tF),\quad |t|\le1/4.
\]
The known basis $(1,F)$ is evaluated in $O(d)$ operations and its
coefficient $t$ is unknown. This is a subfamily of the specified finite
factor model, so giving its simpler form to the learner strengthens a
lower bound. Every non-Y parent address has mass $1/2$ or one.
All generated outcome-parent configurations have positive observational
probability. Take $r\ge d+1$, as the original finite but unrestricted
parameter allows. Under all-one treatment put $r_d=(1-1/d)^d$.
Only the last mechanism matters, giving
\[
 \phi_t=c_dt e_K,\qquad c_d=r_d/2,\qquad 1/8\le c_d<1/(2e).
\]
For the inequalities, $x\log(1-1/x)$ increases for $x\ge2$, since
its derivative is $\log(1-1/x)+1/(x-1)>0$; its limit is $-1$.
The feature Gram is
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&q\\q&q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 7q/9\le\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\le q.
\]
Indeed its determinant is $q(1-q)$, its maximum eigenvalue lies in
$[1,1+q]$, and $q\le1/8$. The target-aligned trace equals
$c_d^2/[q(1-q)]$, so this small eigenvalue is relevant to the target.

The sample KL to $t=0$ is at most $4nqt^2$, and between $t,s$ at
most $(16/3)nq(t-s)^2$. Taking
$t=\pm\min\{1,(nq)^{-1/2}\}/32$ proves the risk lower by testing.
For the upper, $(nq)^{-1}\sum_i F_i(Y_i-1/2)$ is unbiased for $t$
with variance at most $1/(4nq)$. Clip it to $[-1/4,1/4]$ and multiply
by $c_de_K$; its risk is at most the smaller of a constant and
$c_d^2/(4nq)$.
For the diameter lower, integrate honesty under the zero law over
$|t|\le(1-a)\min\{1,(nq)^{-1/2}\}/16$, exactly as before.
The lower is at least $(1-a)^2\min\{1,(nq)^{-1/2}\}/128$.
Markov for the clipped estimator, intersected with the known target
segment, gives the matching upper at every fixed $a$.
\end{proof}

Introducing observed AND accumulators into the last example would bound
indegree but create rare non-Y parent cells. It would not preserve the
constant-$\beta$ claim. The two exponential examples exhibit distinct
ways information becomes scarce and do not conflate their assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[A genuine growing-graph coverage transition]\label{prop:empty}
With $O(d)$ binary nodes, indegree at most two and source-cell mass at
least $\beta\le1/4$, a class has minimax risk at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:emptylower}
 \frac14\left[1-\{1-(1-2\beta)^n\}^d\right].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let unknown bits $\theta_i\in\{0,1\}$ determine
$Z_i=\theta_i A_i$, where $A_i$ are independent Bernoulli$(2\beta)$
treatments. Take an independent fair observed bit $R$, form
$H_0=R$, $H_i=H_{i-1}\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} Z_i$, and
$W=H_d\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} R$. Use dummy earlier mechanisms and a
final independent fair treatment whose mediator $M$ has active/reference
probabilities $3/4,1/4$. Set $Y=M\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} W$.
The construction can be ordered by putting $Z_i,H_i$ before the next
treatment. Each node has at most two parents and the outcome mean is
$M+W-2MW$. Under all-one treatment the sole effect is
$\frac12(-1)^{\sum_i\theta_i}$.

The mask $R$ makes each $H_{i-1}$ fair and independent of $Z_i$.
Every relevant update-parent cell has mass at least $\beta$. If $h$
unknown bits equal one, $\Prb(W=1)=[1-(1-4\beta)^h]/2$ is either
zero or at least $2\beta$; $R$ is independent of $W$, so parent cells
of the final unmasking, and then of $Y$, have positive mass at least
$\beta$. Other required rows have mass at least $\beta$ as well.
Structural-zero irrelevant cells are allowed and every hybrid history
is supported by the observational all-one treatment sequence.

Give the $\theta_i$ independent fair prior bits. Bit $i$ is revealed
exactly when some observed $A_i=1$. If all its $n$ assignments are zero,
the bit remains conditionally fair: the other observed variables are
functions of the observed $Z_i,R,M$ and add no information about it.
If any bit is unseen, the target sign is fair and its conditional variance
is $1/4$. Missing-bit events are independent with probability
$(1-2\beta)^n$. Bayes risk, and hence minimax risk, gives
\eqref{eq:emptylower}. Thus $n\beta$ substantially below $\log d$
can force constant risk; not every count failure term is a union-bound
artifact. No exact formula for the whole transition window is claimed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Ordering error.}
The $K/M$ term for naive independent-ordering averages cannot generally
be removed. Take $K\ge2$ independent fair treatments and mediators with
reference success $\varepsilon=1/(4K^2)$ and active success
$1-\varepsilon$. Binary parity accumulators have indegree two, and let
$Y=(-1)^{\text{final parity}}$. All relevant non-Y parent cells have
mass at least $1/4$ (some initial ones are larger). Writing
$c=1-2\varepsilon$, the exact game is $v(S)=c^K(-1)^{|S|}$ and
$c^K\ge1-1/(2K)\ge1/2$. Each exact ordering vector has squared norm
$4c^{2K}K$; its mean is zero for even $K$ and has every coordinate
$-2c^K/K$ for odd $K$. The exact squared error of its $M$-average is
$4c^{2K}K/M$ for even $K$ and
$4c^{2K}(K-1/K)/M$ for odd $K$. This is a statement about that
sampling procedure, not about every analytic or variance-reduced method.

\paragraph{Exact integration versus additive accuracy.}
There is also a direct reduction for exact rational values. Given a
Boolean circuit with $G$ binary gates and uniform inputs, flip each gate's
correct output independently with probability $z$. Its output probability
is a polynomial of degree at most $G$ in $z$: condition on the input and
all gate-error bits. Exact values at $G+1$ distinct rational
$z\in[1/3,2/3]$ allow rational interpolation at zero and recover the
satisfying-assignment fraction. All gate conditional probabilities lie
in $[1/3,2/3]$. Any specified combination of at most $r$ earlier parent
bits has probability at least $(1/3)^r$, by successive conditioning
and the same bound for conditional mixtures; independent fair inputs
also satisfy it. Attach an independent mediator with active/reference
success $3/4,1/4$ and outcome equal to its product with the circuit
output. Its single contribution is half that output probability.
Consequently a polynomial exact-value oracle would solve counting
satisfying assignments by polynomially many calls. This reduction is
an exact-computation obstruction conditional on counting complexity;
it is not an unconditional exponential-time lower bound and does not
conflict with the additive simulator of Section~\ref{sec:computation}.

\section{Scope, verification and reproducibility}\label{sec:verification}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:table} address every finite
specified graph, all finite factor scopes and unknown coefficients,
including structural zeros, singular unknown rank, arbitrary outcome
parent unions and all sample sizes. Quantitative $\beta,\lambda,B$
give explicit uniform strata; their unquantified unions have the
nonshrinking conclusion in Section~\ref{sec:sharp}. A known zero target
needs no positive lower bound. Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:paired} prove matching regular-class rates and actual
informative joint diameter, in the original $n$-sharp sense. Polynomial
sufficient graph/overlap/input conditions, exponential information
families and the complete simulation/precision budget answer the other
bundled questions. The statistical procedure never substitutes a
cross-world effect for~\eqref{eq:target}.

The bounds do not assert a necessary-and-sufficient complexity label for
every graph, an exact joint minimax formula for every varying
$(n,N,p,\beta,\lambda)$, optimal maximum coordinate width, unknown graph
discovery, infinite dictionaries or an unconditional exponential exact
computation theorem. The original fixed finite graph and its explicit
definition of a sharp $n$-rate are retained. These distinctions also
credit the priors with the fixed-class rates implied by their own
bounds; their actual achievements are not erased by an unresolved label.

The significant imported matrix concentration statement is specified
with all its hypotheses in Section~\ref{sec:regression}. Its primary
PDF, the original agenda, the canonical statement and all three priors
at commit \texttt{738965af403ff4375a58450fd971c43a817843bc} were
consulted on 10--11 October 2026. The scalar concentration, testing,
packing, entropy and interpolation arguments used here are given in
the proof. Literature searching was targeted, not exhaustive; no claim
of worldwide novelty is made.
