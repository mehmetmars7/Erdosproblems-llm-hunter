\section*{Estimating target heterogeneity from multiple studies}
\textbf{Research attempt by GPT-6 (Codex), 9 October 2026.}
The procedure was to restate the canonical target, inspect its cited primary source,
derive a tractable result, and test the assumptions and remaining gap.
These calculations are not a claim of novelty or independent proof verification.

\subsection*{Target and source check}
There are independent samples of sizes $n_s$ from study laws and covariates from
an outcome-free target population $P_0$. Write $N=\sum_s n_s$ and
$P_* =\sum_s(n_s/N)P_s$. The target is the function
$\tau_0=m_1-m_0$, with squared integrated risk under $P_0$.
The canonical assumptions impose common conditional responses, fixed overlap
$\epsilon\leq e_s\leq1-\epsilon$, H\"older smoothness, and
$P_0\leq\kappa P_*$. They do not require identical study designs.
The inspected source, Cinelli et al., Section 3.3.3, motivates heterogeneous
causal effects with multiple studies; it does not supply the following
transport minimax theorem. The sharp general theorem and bounded-deviation
extension are the targets, not a claim that ordinary regression is unsolved.

\subsection*{A pooled construction requiring no propensity estimation}
First restrict to $0<\beta\leq1$ on $[0,1]^d$, with common H\"older constant
$L$. Partition the cube into $K\leq C_dh^{-d}$ cells of diameter at most
$\sqrt d h$. For each treatment $w$, average the observed $Y$'s in each
cell across all studies, retaining only records with $W=w$. Use zero if
the cell is empty and clip the average to $[-1,1]$. Denote this regression
estimate by $\widehat m_w$.

\begin{proposition}
For constants depending only on $d,\beta,L$, the resulting effect estimator
satisfies
\[
 \E\|\widehat m_1-\widehat m_0-\tau_0\|_{L^2(P_0)}^2
 \leq C\left(h^{2\beta}+\frac{\kappa K}{N\epsilon}\right).
\]
The bound holds for unequal study sizes and nonidentical covariate laws,
including disjoint study supports.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cell $A$ and treatment $w$. Its count $M$ is a sum of independent,
not necessarily identically distributed Bernoulli variables, with mean
$\mu\geq N\epsilon P_*(A)$. Conditional on all covariates and assignments,
the selected outcomes are independent, have variances at most one, and
have means within $L(\sqrt d h)^\beta$ of $m_w(x)$ for every $x\in A$.
For $M>0$, the conditional squared error is bounded by
$2L^2d^\beta h^{2\beta}+2/M$. For $M=0$ it is at most one.
If the Bernoulli success probabilities are $p_i$, then
\[
 \E\frac1{M+1}
 =\int_0^1\prod_i(1-p_i+p_it)\,dt
 \leq\int_0^1e^{-\mu(1-t)}dt\leq\frac1\mu.
\]
Since $1/M\leq2/(M+1)$ for $M\geq1$ and
$\Pr(M=0)\leq e^{-\mu}\leq1/\mu$, the integrated stochastic
contribution is at most $C P_0(A)/\mu\leq C\kappa/(N\epsilon)$.
Cells with $P_*(A)=0$ have zero target mass and contribute nothing.
Summing cells and using $(a-b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}
For fixed $\epsilon,\kappa$, taking $h\asymp N^{-1/(2\beta+d)}$
gives risk $O(N^{-2\beta/(2\beta+d)})$. There is no target-sample term:
the target covariates are unnecessary for this uniform guarantee.
Nor is there a study-propensity smoothness penalty. Outcome-regression
pooling avoids estimating those nuisance functions. This observation does
not settle optimal dependence on growing $\kappa$, higher smoothness, or
adaptation to favorable designs.

\subsection*{Lower-bound mechanism and support stress test}
To see the familiar rate obstruction, take all study designs and the target
uniform on the cube, all propensities a fixed admissible constant
$e_0\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$, and $m_0=0$. Divide the cube
into $K\asymp h^{-d}$ subcubes. In subcube $j$ put a smooth nonnegative
bump of height $a h^\beta$, multiplied by a bit $v_j\in\{0,1\}$;
choose $a$ small enough for the H\"older ball. Generate $Y\in\{-1,1\}$
with mean $m_W(X)$. Neighboring bit configurations differ in integrated
squared mean by order $h^{2\beta+d}$ and in sample KL divergence by at
most $CNh^{2\beta+d}$, using that Bernoulli probabilities remain in
$[1/4,3/4]$. Choose $Nh^{2\beta+d}$ a small constant. The total variation
bound $\operatorname{TV}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}/2}$ then gives a
positive error probability for each bit under any decoder. Decoding an
estimated regression by its closest bump converts each incorrect bit into
loss at least $ch^{2\beta+d}$. Summing $K$ bits yields the lower rate
$ch^{2\beta}$. This checks the exponent in this restricted setting.
A study with no observations near a target point cannot be made informative
by its large sample elsewhere; the cell count proof makes this explicit.

\subsection*{Bounded transport violations}
With population study means known, define, on study-supported points,
\[
 l_w(x)=\max\{-1,\max_{s:x\in\operatorname{supp}P_s}
 [m_{w,s}(x)-\delta_s]\},\quad
 u_w(x)=\min\{1,\min_{s:x\in\operatorname{supp}P_s}
 [m_{w,s}(x)+\delta_s]\}.
\]
Without smoothness restrictions on target responses, the sharp pointwise
interval is $[l_1-u_0,u_1-l_0]$: every choice of means in the two
intersected intervals is realized by bounded Bernoulli potential outcomes,
independent of randomized assignment. With target H\"older restrictions,
the exact functional set instead is
\[
 \{f_1-f_0:f_w\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L),\quad
 l_w\leq f_w\leq u_w\quad P_0\text{-a.e.}\}.
\]
The pointwise interval can then overstate what is jointly attainable.
For example, constant observed means zero and common $\delta\leq\min(1,L)$
permit target pairs $(m_1,m_0)=(\delta,-\delta)$ and
$(-\delta,\delta)$ with exactly the same study data. Their effects differ
by $4\delta$, so the worst squared $L^2$ risk is at least $4\delta^2$,
even with infinite study and target samples. Randomization cannot remove
this transport ambiguity.

\subsection*{Verification and remaining problem}
The result uses fixed positive overlap; replacing it with merely pointwise
positivity invalidates the uniform rate. Constants and empty cells were
accounted for, and study membership is not assumed random. The general
sharp dependence on coverage, smoothness beyond one, and unequal
$\delta_s$ with jointly smooth target functions is not proved here.
The next concrete task is an optimal-recovery analysis of the intersected
H\"older bands, combined with local effective sample counts.
