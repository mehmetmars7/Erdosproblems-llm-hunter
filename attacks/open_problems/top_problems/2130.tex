The least prime factor $p(n)$ is defined for $n\geq2$. The nonprime terms in the first sum are composite integers, omitting the undefined $n=1$ term. The second sum, as written in the source, is not restricted to composites. \sref{2130}{1} \sref{2130}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $p(n)$ denote the least prime factor of $n$. There is a constant $c>0$ such that $ \sum_{\substack{n<x\\ n\textrm{ not prime}}}\frac{p(n)}{n}\sim c\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^2}. $ Is it true that there exists a constant $C>0$ such that $ \sum_{x\leq n\leq x+Cx^{1/2}(\log x)^2}\frac{p(n)}{n} \gg 1 $ for all large $x$? \sref{2130}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

What are the global asymptotics and short-interval lower bounds for sums of the least prime factor divided by the integer itself?
