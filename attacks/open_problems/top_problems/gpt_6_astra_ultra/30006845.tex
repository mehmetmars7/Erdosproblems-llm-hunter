\section*{\texorpdfstring{Secret key from all entangled states}{Secret key from all entangled states}}\label{30006845}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a bipartite state $\rho_{AB}$, a secret-key protocol applies local quantum operations and public classical communication to $n$ copies. Eve may hold a purification and the public transcript. An $\ell_n$-bit key is secure when the final state approaches, in trace distance,
\[
 2^{-\ell_n}\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_n}}|zz\rangle\langle zz|_{K_AK_B}\otimes\rho_E.
\]
The distillable key $K_D(\rho)$ is the supremum of asymptotic rates $\liminf\ell_n/n$ with error tending to zero. The question is whether $K_D(\rho)>0$ for every entangled bipartite state, where entangled means not separable into a convex mixture of product states. Secret classical key and maximally entangled quantum pairs are distinct output resources; their distillation rates must not be identified by definition.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17007}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every bipartite entangled state contain a strictly positive asymptotic rate of secret classical key extractable by local operations and public communication?
\subsection{Research attempt}
\textbf{The resource and the quantifiers.}
The output is secret classical correlation decoupled from Eve and the public
transcript.  It is not a collection of Bell pairs.  The 2026 revision of [S1]
studies key cost and related formation quantities; in its discussion, absence
of entangled zero-key states is an assumption for certain conclusions, not a
proved theorem covering this question.  A formation cost or an entanglement
witness is not itself a distillation protocol.  This attempt proves positivity
for pure entangled states, proves zero key for finite-dimensional separable
states, and isolates why the usual obstruction to entangled-pair distillation
does not settle the mixed-state key question.

All finite-dimensional matrix arguments below use the standard model with
arbitrary local ancillas and authenticated public communication.  They do not
assume that Eve lacks a purification.  Any additional infinite-dimensional
technical extension would require its own continuity and approximation
arguments and is not claimed here.

\textbf{A direct positive-rate protocol for a pure entangled state.}
Let
\[
 |\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^r\sqrt{\lambda_i}\,|i\rangle_A|i\rangle_B,
 \qquad \lambda_i>0,\quad\sum_i\lambda_i=1,
\]
with $r\geq2$.  Since $AB$ is pure, every purification factors as
$|\psi\rangle_{AB}|e\rangle_E$.  Choose two Schmidt coefficients,
$\lambda_1,\lambda_2$, and put $\mu=\min(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)>0$.
Alice applies the successful filter
\[
 F=\sqrt{\mu/\lambda_1}|1\rangle\langle1|
   +\sqrt{\mu/\lambda_2}|2\rangle\langle2|,
\]
with zero action on the remaining basis vectors.  Since $F^*F\leq I$, this
is a valid local measurement branch, completed by the failure operator
$(I-F^*F)^{1/2}$.  Its success probability is $2\mu$, and the normalized
successful state is $(|11\rangle+|22\rangle)/\sqrt2$.
Alice publicly announces which copies succeed.  On independent copies the
number of successes is binomial.  For any $0<R<2\mu$, the probability of
fewer than $\lfloor Rn\rfloor$ successes tends to zero by the law of large
numbers.  Keep that many successes and measure them in the Schmidt basis.
The output bits are uniform, equal for Alice and Bob, and independent of Eve
and the success transcript.  On the rare failure event output an arbitrary
fixed string; its probability bounds the trace-distance error.  Hence
$K_D(\psi)\geq2\mu>0$ after taking the supremum over $R<2\mu$.
The same two-coefficient argument applies to a pure state with more Schmidt
coefficients whenever the indicated Schmidt decomposition and filter exist.

\textbf{A stronger pure-state rate without identifying key and entanglement.}
For finite Schmidt rank, both parties can measure every copy immediately in
the Schmidt basis.  Their resulting length-$n$ strings are identical and
have law $\prod_{j=1}^n\lambda_{i_j}$, still independent of Eve.  Publicly
announce the type $t=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$.  Conditional on $t$, the common string
is uniform over a type class of size
\[
 N_t=\frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_r!}.
\]
The law of large numbers and the elementary Stirling estimate imply that,
with probability tending to one,
\[
 \log_2N_t\geq n(H(\lambda)-\varepsilon),\qquad
 H(\lambda)=-\sum_i\lambda_i\log_2\lambda_i.
\]
Fix $M=2^{\lfloor n(H(\lambda)-2\varepsilon)\rfloor}$.
On each such type class, order its strings and keep the first
$M\lfloor N_t/M\rfloor$ strings.  Partition these equally among $M$ labels.
The rejected fraction is less than $M/N_t\leq2^{-n\varepsilon}$.
Given the publicly announced type and acceptance, the label is exactly
uniform.  Both parties compute the same label locally.  Atypical types and
rejections have vanishing total probability, so this proves
\[
 K_D(\psi)\geq H(\lambda)>0.
\]
This is directly a classical-secret-key construction.  No equality between
distillable key and distillable entanglement for general mixed states was
used or inferred.  The protocol's rate may depend on the state, as the
question allows.  Both parties retain any unused local measurement records
in their laboratories; the protocol does not hand a copy of the raw secret
string to Eve.

\textbf{Why a finite-dimensional separable state has no secret key.}
Write a separable state as a finite convex combination of pure product states,
\[
 \rho_{AB}=\sum_jp_j|a_j\rangle\langle a_j|
                         \otimes|b_j\rangle\langle b_j|.
\]
Eve may hold the purification
$\sum_j\sqrt{p_j}|a_j\rangle|b_j\rangle|j\rangle_E$ and measure the
orthogonal label register.  Conditional on $J=j$, the two parties' state is
a product.  For $n$ copies use the sequence of such labels.

Under any finite-round local protocol, conditional on Eve's labels and the
public transcript, the parties' states remain a product.  This follows by
induction over messages: a local instrument changes only one tensor factor,
and conditioning on its announced outcome normalizes only that factor.
Private outcomes can remain in that party's local register.  Local private
randomness is likewise part of a local tensor factor.  Thus, after final key
measurements, $K_A$ and $K_B$ are independent conditioned on
$W=(J,\text{public transcript})$.

For each $w$, write their conditional laws as $p_z(w)$ and $q_z(w)$.
The probability of agreement is
$\sum_zp_z(w)q_z(w)\leq\max_zp_z(w)$.
Eve can guess Alice's most probable value, so after averaging
\[
 P_{\rm guess}(K_A\mid W)\geq\mathbb P(K_A=K_B).
\]
If the output is within trace distance $\delta$ of an ideal equal uniform
key on $M$ values independent of Eve, contractivity under measurement gives
\[
 \mathbb P(K_A=K_B)\geq1-\delta,\qquad
 P_{\rm guess}(K_A\mid W)\leq M^{-1}+\delta.
\]
Hence $1-\delta\leq M^{-1}+\delta$.  With $\delta\to0$, a growing key
alphabet is impossible.  The argument extends to limits of finite-round
protocols by trace-distance continuity of these probabilities.  Security
against a purification implies security against the particular Eve
measurement used here, so weakening Eve in the proof creates no loophole.
This establishes $K_D=0$ for this finite-dimensional separable class and
clarifies why the question begins at entanglement.

\textbf{Private states show what the secret output actually needs.}
Let $A'B'$ be auxiliary local registers, called the shield, let $\sigma$ be
a state on them, and let $U_i$ be unitaries on the joint shield.  The private
state form is
\[
 \gamma=\frac1d\sum_{i,j=1}^d|ii\rangle\langle jj|_{AB}
                         \otimes U_i\sigma U_j^*.
\]
It is positive with trace one: purify $\sigma$ as $|\chi\rangle_{A'B'E}$,
and use the purification
$d^{-1/2}\sum_i|ii\rangle(U_i\otimes I_E)|\chi\rangle$.
Measuring $A,B$ in the displayed basis gives the same uniform label $i$.
Eve's conditional state is
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{A'B'}(U_i\otimes I_E)
 |\chi\rangle\langle\chi|(U_i^*\otimes I_E)
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{A'B'}|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|,
\]
independent of $i$.  Thus the state supplies $\log_2d$ secret bits per copy
(or the corresponding asymptotic binary rate).  The twisting unitaries need
not be local operations across $A':B'$; their appearance describes the given
state and does not claim that arbitrary such states can be prepared by LOCC.
Nor is the shield handed to Eve.  Eve holds a purification of all registers
retained by Alice and Bob, exactly as required by the security definition.

\textbf{The PPT obstruction stops at entangled pairs.}
Partial transpose positivity is preserved by LOCC: a product Kraus branch
$A\otimes B$ sends $\rho^{\Gamma_B}$ to
$(A\otimes\overline B)\rho^{\Gamma_B}(A^*\otimes B^T)$, and sums and
conditional normalization preserve positivity.  Tensor products also preserve
it.  If $\tau$ is PPT on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$, then its overlap
with the maximally entangled state satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\tau\Phi_d)
 =\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}(\tau^{\Gamma_B}F)\leq\frac1d,
\]
where $F$ is the swap operator and $F\leq I$.  This prevents convergence to
Bell pairs from PPT inputs and proves zero distillable entanglement for them.
The target ideal \emph{classical} key state
$d^{-1}\sum_i|ii\rangle\langle ii|$ is itself separable and PPT.
The overlap argument therefore has no analogous obstruction to that target
with decoupling from Eve.  The source discusses known positive-key bound
entangled examples; this separation is not merely terminological.  A proof
that a mixed state cannot yield Bell pairs is insufficient to show $K_D=0$.

\textbf{The unresolved mixed-state step.}
For a mixed entangled state, Eve can correlate with every proposed measurement
outcome.  A witness detecting entanglement supplies a separating linear
functional, whereas a key protocol must simultaneously give high agreement,
uniformity, and decoupling after public communication on arbitrarily many
copies.  No direct implication between those tasks has been proved above.
Likewise, a particular one-way key lower bound being nonpositive only shows
that this chosen protocol fails to certify a rate.  It is not an upper bound
on all multi-copy, two-way protocols.  To disprove the conjecture one would
need an entangled state and a valid zero upper bound for the regularized
operational quantity; to prove it one needs a positive protocol for every
mixed entangled state.  Neither follows from a finite-copy search.

\textbf{Verification and outcome.}
The offline diagnostic checks the two-Schmidt-coefficient filter exactly for
a rational example, balanced type-class partitions by exact integer counting,
the conditional-independence guessing inequality for rational distributions,
and Bell partial-transpose eigenvalues numerically.  It also checks a small
twisted private-state purification's conditional Eve marginals numerically.
These checks validate the explicit subcases and resource distinction.  The
general positive-rate assertion for arbitrary mixed entangled states remains
unresolved by this attempt.
