\section*{Rendezvous on a line}\label{3447}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3447 · OPG-581 · Miscellaneous · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Two players start distance two apart on a line, at global coordinates
$0$ and $2$, at time zero. Neither knows the direction of the other
or has a shared orientation. A strategy is a probability distribution
$\mu$ on continuous paths $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ with $f(0)=0$
and $|f(t)-f(s)|\le|t-s|$; thus speed is at most one. Paths describe
displacement in a player's local coordinates before any meeting.

In the symmetric model both players independently sample $f,g$
from the same distribution $\mu$. Their local orientations
$\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{-1,1\}$ are independent fair signs,
independent of the path samples. Define
\[
 T=\inf\{t\ge0:\epsilon_1 f(t)=2+\epsilon_2g(t)\},
 \qquad R=\inf_\mu\mathbb E[T],
\]
with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$ for a missed meeting. The problem is
to determine $R$ exactly. The infimum is over measurable strategy
distributions for which the hitting-time expectation is defined,
allowing the value infinity.
No shared random bits, preassigned distinct roles, or communication
before meeting is allowed. Both players know the separation and
start simultaneously. The source's additional proposed value is
$R=17/4$ in this normalization. Rescaling initial separation would
rescale time, so the specified distance two is essential.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the smallest expected meeting time for two indistinguishable players distance two apart on an unoriented line, using the same independently randomized strategy?

\paragraph{Keeping the symmetric model explicit.}
Both players must independently sample from the same
distribution. Assigning one player to wait and the
other to search would change the problem. The source
[S2] reports bounds much sharper than the elementary
ones proved below and proposes $17/4$. Our purpose
is to construct a fully admissible baseline strategy
and prove a model-specific lower bound, then identify
why this strategy cannot attain the proposed value.
All times use initial separation two.

\paragraph{Independent excursions give a finite bound.}
Each player samples an infinite sequence of independent
fair signs. In round $j$, lasting two time units,
the player walks one unit in the locally chosen
direction and then returns to its initial position.
This describes a continuous speed-one path sampled
in advance, hence a permitted distribution $\mu$.
Multiplying its fair signs by a fixed random local
orientation still gives independent fair global
directions in each round. The two players both
move towards one another with probability $1/4$,
meeting exactly one unit into that round. If both
move in the same global direction, their separation
stays two. If both move away, their separation
increases and then returns to two; they do not meet.
Every failed round resets both positions.
If $J$ is the number of failures before the first
success, then
$\Pr(J=j)=(3/4)^j/4$, $T=2J+1$, and
\[
                 \mathbb E T=2\frac{3/4}{1/4}+1=7.
\]
In particular the probability of never meeting is
zero. This proves $R\le7$, without using distinct
roles, a shared compass or shared random bits.

\paragraph{A lower bound from four orientations.}
Fix any two sampled 1-Lipschitz paths $f,g$ starting
at zero and condition on them. The four orientation
pairs are equally likely. In a pair $(a,b)$, a
meeting at time $s$ means $af(s)-bg(s)=2$.
No meeting is possible before time one, by the
speed bound on relative displacement.
Two orientation pairs differing only in the second
sign cannot both meet before time two: if their
meeting times are $s\le t$, adding their equations
and using the Lipschitz bounds gives
\[
 4=a(f(s)+f(t))+b(g(t)-g(s))\le s+t+(t-s)=2t.
\]
The same conclusion for pairs differing only in
the first sign follows by adding equations to
bound $f(s)-f(t)$ and $g(s)+g(t)$ instead.
If both signs differ, the same 2-Lipschitz relative
path must take values $2$ and $-2$; their hitting
times differ by at least two. Thus at most one
orientation pair can have met by any time $t<2$.
For $1\le t<2$, conditional meeting probability
is therefore at most $1/4$. Averaging over the
sampled paths and integrating survival probabilities
proves, also for strategies of infinite expectation,
\[
             \mathbb E T\ge1+\frac34=\frac74.
\]
This lower bound is intentionally weaker than the
published bounds; it is a self-contained consequence
of the stated orientation model.

\paragraph{Optimizing this restricted excursion family.}
Replace the unit excursion by one of fixed length
$a$. For $a\ge1$, a successful round still meets
at time one, while every failure lasts $2a$.
The expectation is $1+6a$, minimized at $a=1$.
For $a<1$, even simultaneous inward motion can
close a distance of at most $2a<2$; every round
returns to the starting configuration, so meeting
never occurs. No member of this fixed-amplitude
independent-round family attains $17/4$.
Improving it requires changing the path distribution,
rather than optimizing that single amplitude.

\paragraph{Verification and final status.}
The root script solves the piecewise-linear meeting
equations for all four direction pairs and several
rational amplitudes. It checks the finite geometric
sum with its exact remaining tail. These calculations
verify the strategy's expectation, not an optimal
strategy theorem. The proved interval is
$7/4\le R\le7$; determining the exact infimum
remains unresolved by this attempt.
