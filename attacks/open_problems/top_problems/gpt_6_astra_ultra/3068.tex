\section*{Saturated $k$-Sperner Systems of Minimum Size}\label{3068}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3068 · OPG-59928 · Combinatorics · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite set $X$, write $2^X$ for its power set. A family
$\mathcal F\subseteq2^X$ is $k$-Sperner if it contains no strict chain
$A_0\subsetneq A_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq A_k$ of $k+1$ members.
It is saturated when it is $k$-Sperner and adjoining any missing set
$S\in2^X\setminus\mathcal F$ creates such a chain. Thus saturation is
maximality under inclusion of families, not largest possible cardinality.
Let $\operatorname{sat}_k(n)$ be the smallest cardinality of a saturated
$k$-Sperner family when $|X|=n$.

The question asks whether its exponential growth for sufficiently large
ground sets has an exponent strictly greater than $1/2$. A precise
quantified form of the source's asymptotic assertion is: do there exist
$c>1/2$, a function $n_0:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$, and nonnegative real
numbers $\eta_k\to0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $k$ and all
$n\ge n_0(k)$,
\[
 \operatorname{sat}_k(n)\ge 2^{(c-\eta_k)k}?
\]
One may increase $n_0(k)$ to ensure $n\ge k$ without altering the problem.
The error term is controlled by $k$ uniformly throughout the permitted
range of $n$, rather than separately for individual ground-set sizes.

The requested improvement is in the exponential rate. A multiplicative
factor polynomial in $k$ in front of $2^{k/2}$ does not by itself give a
constant $c>1/2$. Both the absence of a chain and the creation of a chain
by every possible addition are essential to the minimum-size problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For large ground sets, must every saturated family with no chain of length $k+1$ have exponential size with rate strictly above one half?
\subsection{Research attempt: a saturated construction and a counting barrier}
\paragraph{Scope and literature check.}
GPT-6 Astra Ultra, 1 October 2026. Saturated means inclusion-maximal
among families with no $(k+1)$-chain; it does not mean a family of
maximum size. Morrison--Noel--Scott [S3] give constructions smaller
than the formerly proposed $2^{k-1}$. Martin--Veldt [S4], published
in 2025, improve the asymptotic lower bound to $\sqrt{k}2^{k/2}$
in the relevant large-ground-set regime. That polynomial factor does
not give a fixed exponent greater than $1/2$. We verify an elementary
construction and examine why a natural counting argument stops there.

\paragraph{1. A complete saturation certificate with $2^{k-1}$ sets.}
Let $k\ge2$, choose $Y\subset X$ with $|Y|=k-2$, and put
$R=X\setminus Y$, assuming $R\ne\varnothing$. Define
\[
 \mathcal F=\{A:A\subseteq Y\}\ \cup\
 \{A\cup R:A\subseteq Y\}.
\]
These two parts are disjoint, so $|\mathcal F|=2^{k-1}$.
A chain contained in one part has at most $k-1$ members. A chain
that uses both parts has a last lower member $A$ and a first upper
member $B\cup R$, where $A\subseteq B\subseteq Y$. Its length is at most
\[
 (|A|+1)+(|Y|-|B|+1)\le |Y|+2=k.
\]
Hence $\mathcal F$ is $k$-Sperner.

Now let $S\notin\mathcal F$. Necessarily $S\cap R$ is a nonempty
proper subset of $R$; put $A=S\cap Y$. A maximal chain from
$\varnothing$ to $A$ contributes $|A|+1$ lower members. Follow it
by $S$, then by a maximal chain from $A\cup R$ to $X$, contributing
$|Y|-|A|+1$ upper members. All inclusions at $S$ are strict. The
result has $|Y|+3=k+1$ members. This proves saturation against
every possible addition, rather than merely against typical subsets.
When $|R|=1$ there are no missing sets; the family is the whole
Boolean lattice on $k-1$ points, and the same conclusion is valid.

\paragraph{2. A lower bound for the antichain route.}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a saturated antichain on an $n$-element set,
with $a\le|A|\le n-b$ for every $A\in\mathcal A$. Every subset
$S$ is comparable to at least one member of $\mathcal A$: otherwise
it could be added while preserving the antichain condition.
Choose $S$ by independent fair inclusion of the $n$ elements. For
fixed $A$, the probabilities of $A\subseteq S$ and $S\subseteq A$
are $2^{-|A|}$ and $2^{-(n-|A|)}$, respectively. Their intersection
is $S=A$, of probability $2^{-n}$. Therefore the union bound proves
\[
 1\le\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
 \left(2^{-|A|}+2^{-(n-|A|)}-2^{-n}\right)
 \le|\mathcal A|(2^{-a}+2^{-b}),
\]
and consequently $|\mathcal A|\ge(2^{-a}+2^{-b})^{-1}$.
For $a=b=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ this is only
$2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1}$. No choice of a polynomial multiplier
changes the limiting base-two exponent divided by $k$.

\paragraph{Verification and first remaining gap.}
The construction's chain bound explicitly counts a possible jump from
the lower copy to the upper copy; treating those copies as unrelated
antichains would be wrong. A direct Boolean-lattice check for
$k=2,3,4$ and $|X|=k+1$ verified the maximum chain length and
every missing-set witness. The antichain estimate is not asserted for
an arbitrary $k$-Sperner family: extracting an antichain with controlled
ranks is a separate issue. Even on that route the estimate charges
overlapping comparability events separately. Improving their overlap
accounting by an exponential factor, uniformly in $k$, is the first
unproved step towards the requested exponent.
