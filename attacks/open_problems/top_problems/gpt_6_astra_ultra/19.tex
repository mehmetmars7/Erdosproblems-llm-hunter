\section*{\texorpdfstring{Unconditional Separation of Quantum and Classical Bounded-Error Polynomial Time (BQP != BPP)}{Unconditional Separation of Quantum and Classical Bounded-Error Polynomial Time (BQP != BPP)}}\label{19}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Both classes decide total binary languages with error at most $1/3$ on every input and polynomial running time. The class $\mathsf{BPP}$ uses classical random bits; $\mathsf{BQP}$ uses uniform quantum computation over a fixed effective universal gate set. The target is
\[
 \exists L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*:\quad L\in\mathsf{BQP}\ \text{and}\ L\notin\mathsf{BPP}.
\]
The assertion is unconditional and unrelativized: a separation only in the presence of an oracle or under a conjectured hardness assumption does not meet it.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539796300921}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one prove, without computational assumptions, that quantum computers efficiently solve some decision problem beyond all efficient randomized classical computers?
\subsection{Research attempt}
\paragraph{Scope, source check, and process.}
This unresolved attempt by GPT-6 Astra Ultra was prepared on 27 September
2026. The strategy was to test whether explicitly structured interference
supplies a total decision-language separation, while auditing the conversion
from sampling and oracle results. All circuits are uniform and use fixed
effective gates. Error bounds must hold on every input of the resulting
language, including malformed encodings, which may be rejected explicitly.

The classical simulation inclusion $\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}$ is
compatible with the target: fair bits are obtained by Hadamard gates and
classical gates by reversible computation with ancillas. What is missing is
one total language escaping \emph{all} polynomial-time randomized deciders.
The original Bernstein--Vazirani reference [S1] supplies the model and
relativized context. The CCC 2025 work [S2] concerns sampling and functional
tasks with its stated assumptions and parameters. Mantri's September 2026
paper [S3] explicitly concerns oracle separations in a Fourier hierarchy.
Neither is treated here as the unrelativized total-language separation.

The concrete interference family below lies inside the efficiently simulable
stabilizer setting studied in [E1]. Its exact probability formula and sampler
are derived here to expose why a large superposition and cancellation do not
alone provide classical hardness. This calculation is an illustrative
special case, not a new stabilizer-simulation theorem.

\paragraph{Attack: quadratic phases between two Fourier layers.}
Let $G$ be a simple graph on $n$ labeled vertices, with adjacency matrix
$A\in\mathbb F_2^{n\times n}$, so $A$ is symmetric and has zero diagonal.
Define
\[
 q(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G),\,i<j}x_i x_j\pmod2,
 \qquad D_q|x\rangle=(-1)^{q(x)}|x\rangle.
\]
The circuit
\[
                 H^{\otimes n}D_qH^{\otimes n}|0^n\rangle
\]
has $O(n^2)$ gates: each edge contributes a controlled-$Z$ phase. Such gates
have fixed finite decompositions in a universal effective basis, or may be
included in that fixed basis. All gates and their order are generated from
the input graph in polynomial time. Before the final Hadamards the state has
exactly $2^n$ nonzero amplitudes and can be entangled.

On measurement, the amplitude and probability of $y$ are
\begin{equation}\label{a19:fourier}
 \alpha_y=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_2^n}(-1)^{q(x)+y\cdot x},
 \qquad p_y=|\alpha_y|^2.
\end{equation}
The sum appears exponentially large. Squaring it and changing variables
gives a much smaller description of the full distribution.

\paragraph{Lemma 19.1: an exact affine-support formula.}
Put $K=\ker A$ and $r=\operatorname{rank}A$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a19:distribution}
 p_y=
 \begin{cases}
  2^{-r},&y\cdot z=q(z)\text{ for every }z\in K,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The support is a nonempty affine subspace of dimension $r$. It is computable,
and its uniform distribution is exactly samplable, in classical polynomial
time.

\emph{Proof.} The polarization identity is
\[
                   q(x+z)+q(x)+q(z)=x^{\mathsf T}Az.
\]
Expand the square in \eqref{a19:fourier} and substitute $x'=x+z$:
\[
 p_y=2^{-2n}\sum_z(-1)^{q(z)+y\cdot z}
                   \sum_x(-1)^{x^{\mathsf T}Az}.
\]
If $Az\ne0$, choose a coordinate where it is $1$. Pair each $x$ with $x$
plus the corresponding unit vector; the inner summands cancel. If $Az=0$,
all $2^n$ inner summands equal $1$. Therefore
\[
                 p_y=2^{-n}\sum_{z\in K}(-1)^{q(z)+y\cdot z}.
\]
For $u,z\in K$, polarization gives $q(u+z)=q(u)+q(z)$; also $q(0)=0$.
Thus $q$ restricted to $K$ is a linear functional. The last sum is $|K|$
if that functional equals $z\mapsto y\cdot z$, and otherwise is zero by
pairing with an element on which they disagree. As $|K|=2^{n-r}$, this
proves \eqref{a19:distribution}.

Every linear functional on $K$ extends to one on $\mathbb F_2^n$: extend
a basis of $K$ to a full basis and assign arbitrary values to the added
basis vectors. Every such full functional has the form $z\mapsto y\cdot z$.
Hence the support is nonempty, with one independent equation for each basis
vector of $K$, and has dimension $r$.

Compute a kernel basis by Gaussian elimination, evaluate $q$ on each basis
vector, and solve those linear equations for $y$. Obtain a particular
solution $y_0$ and a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ for their homogeneous solutions.
Output $y_0+\sum_i B_i v_i$ for independent fair bits $B_i$. The map from
the $r$ bits to the affine subspace is bijective, so this is the exact
required distribution. Every arithmetic operation is over $\mathbb F_2$,
and all storage and work are polynomial in the graph encoding.
\hfill$\square$

An easily missed detail is the affine shift $q|_K$. For a triangle,
$K=\{000,111\}$ and $q(111)=1$, so the output is uniform on the four
odd-parity strings, not the even-parity subspace. For the empty graph,
$K=\mathbb F_2^n$ and the output is always $0^n$. For a single edge on
two vertices, $A$ is invertible and all four outputs have probability $1/4$.
These cases test both interference cancellation and the normalization.

\paragraph{Consequence for attempted decision separations.}
Suppose an input $x$ determines such a graph in polynomial time and a
polynomial-time predicate $R(x,y)$ is applied to its measured output. The
classical sampler above, followed by $R$, reproduces the quantum acceptance
probability \emph{exactly}. If those probabilities satisfy the total BQP
gap on all inputs, the same procedure is a BPP decider for the language.
Consequently this entire proposed source of separating decision problems
fails, despite exponential state support and nontrivial interference.
Adding arbitrary nonquadratic phases changes the calculation: the derivative
of the phase need no longer be linear in $x$, and the inner sum need not
reduce to the kernel test. No polynomial simulator for that generalization
is proved here, but failure of this formula is not a classical lower bound.

\paragraph{A cubic stress test: losing the formula still need not give hardness.}
Take $q(x)=x_1x_2x_3$. Its sign function is $1$ at seven inputs and $-1$
at $111$. The Walsh sum is therefore $6$ at $y=000$. At every nonzero
$y$, the Walsh sum of the constant function is zero, so changing the one
sign gives $-2(-1)^{y_1+y_2+y_3}$. The output probabilities are
\[
                 p_{000}=9/16,\qquad p_y=1/16\quad(y\ne000).
\]
This full-support, nonuniform distribution cannot have the form in
Lemma 19.1. Nevertheless it has an exact constant-time classical sampler:
draw four fair bits, map nine of the sixteen outcomes to $000$, and map
the other seven bijectively to the seven nonzero strings.

For $k$ disjoint triples, let the phase polynomial be the sum of these
$k$ cubic monomials. Both Hadamard layers and the phase operator factor
across triples. Consequently the measured distribution is the product of
the $k$ displayed distributions and is sampled with $4k$ fair bits and
linear work. This family has $2^{3k}$ output strings of positive probability
and falls outside the quadratic calculation, yet it remains efficiently
classically samplable. Nonquadratic degree is therefore not a hardness
criterion by itself. A plausible next candidate must also address coupling
between blocks and prove an algorithm-independent lower bound, rather than
merely invalidate one Gaussian-elimination formula.

There is also a quantitative version of the sampling-to-decision implication.
If a classical polynomial-time sampler approximates each circuit's output
distribution within total variation distance $1/12$, the probability of any
specified accept event changes by at most $1/12$. A BQP gap of $2/3$ versus
$1/3$ becomes at least $7/12$ versus at most $5/12$. On $145$ independent
runs, majority voting fails with probability at most $36/145<1/3$ by
Chebyshev's inequality: the sample mean has variance at most $1/(4\cdot145)$
and its expectation is separated from $1/2$ by at least $1/12$.
Thus such uniform approximate sampling would already suffice for BPP.
The converse inference from a sampling hardness claim to a particular total
decision-language lower bound requires an additional argument.

\paragraph{Stress test: compiling an oracle can reveal its secret.}
Simon's original separation [E2] is explicitly an oracle result. To test a
naive conversion to explicit circuit inputs, fix a nonzero
$s\in\mathbb F_2^n$ and an index $j$ with $s_j=1$. Define
\[
                         f_s(x)=x+x_j s.
\]
This linear map satisfies
$f_s(x)=f_s(x')$ precisely when $x'=x$ or $x'=x+s$. Indeed its kernel
contains $0,s$, and solving $v+v_js=0$ shows these are its only elements.
Thus it has the usual two-to-one hidden-XOR collision structure.

Yet a circuit description of this particular family can explicitly record
$j$ and the bits of $s$, using $O(n)$ gates to implement the displayed
formula. A classical algorithm reading that description recovers $s$
immediately. Even oracle access to this restricted linear family permits
learning its whole matrix with $n$ standard-basis queries, so it is not the
hard arbitrary-label oracle distribution used in Simon's lower bound.
This example does not refute that lower bound. It shows exactly why merely
choosing an efficient family satisfying the same collision promise does not
transport the lower bound to algorithms reading circuit descriptions.

A valid unrelativized separation would need an explicitly uniform family
with a hardness proof against every randomized algorithm allowed to inspect
its representation. Hiding the description behind a query interface changes
the input model. Listing the full oracle table changes the input length;
a runtime polynomial in the table length can be exponential in the number
of oracle-address bits without contradicting BPP.

\paragraph{Stress test: a promise cannot be filled in by a threshold rule.}
Suppose a quantum procedure distinguishes promised yes- and no-inputs with
probabilities at least $2/3$ and at most $1/3$. Declaring every other input
a yes-instance exactly when its acceptance probability is greater than
$1/2$ defines a total set, but need not give that set a bounded-error quantum
decider. On newly assigned inputs the probability may be $1/2$ or differ
from it by an exponentially small amount.

For a concrete gap test, generate $m+1$ fair bits and accept when the first
bit is $1$ or when all bits are $0$. The probability is
$1/2+2^{-(m+1)}$. Sampling this output polynomially often does not turn its
comparison with a fair coin into a bounded-error test: couple a Bernoulli
sample of bias $1/2+\delta$ to a fair bit with disagreement probability
$\delta$. For $R$ independent samples, the transcripts have total variation
distance at most $R\delta$ by the union bound. Constant distinguishing
advantage therefore requires exponentially many samples when
$\delta=2^{-(m+1)}$. This is an obstruction to the proposed generic repetition
wrapper, not a lower bound for all algorithms analyzing those simple circuits.

\paragraph{Verification, remaining gap, and outcome.}
The accompanying exact checks enumerate every simple graph on up to five
vertices, compute all integer Walsh sums in \eqref{a19:fourier}, and compare
their squared probabilities with \eqref{a19:distribution}. They check the
triangle shift, normalization, and all hidden-XOR fibers through dimension
six. No empirical timing comparison is used to infer an asymptotic separation.

\textbf{UNRESOLVED.} The main checked result is a complete classical sampler
for a structured interference family, together with three explicit scope
obstructions: approximate sampling can already simulate decision, oracle
compilation may expose structure, and a threshold rule need not preserve a
total bounded-error gap. A successful positive continuation needs one total
uniform BQP language and an unconditional lower bound against all randomized
polynomial-time deciders for that same language. A negative continuation
would need a general classical simulation far beyond the quadratic-phase
family. Neither has been obtained; the restricted calculations carry no
claim of historical novelty.
