\section*{Combinatorial covering designs}\label{3058}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3058 · OPG-762 · Combinatorics · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $v,k,t$ be integers with $1\le t\le k\le v$, and let
$V=\{1,\ldots,v\}$. A $(v,k,t)$ covering is a family
$\mathcal B\subseteq\binom Vk$ of $k$-element subsets, called blocks,
such that each $T\in\binom Vt$ is contained in at least one block.
Repeated copies of a block provide no additional coverage and are omitted.
The covering number is
\[
 C(v,k,t)=\min\left\{|\mathcal B|:
 \forall T\in\binom Vt\ \exists B\in\mathcal B\ (T\subseteq B)\right\}.
\]
Relabelling $V$ does not change this number. Coverage is by containment,
not by intersection of a prescribed size, and a $t$-set may occur in
several different blocks.

Determine $C(v,k,t)$, preferably through useful formulas, recurrences or
sharp bounds across parameter families, and construct coverings attaining
the claimed values. Counting incidences between blocks and $t$-sets gives
the elementary benchmark
\[
 C(v,k,t)\ge
 \left\lceil\frac{\binom vt}{\binom kt}\right\rceil.
\]
If the unrounded ratio is attained, every $t$-set occurs in exactly one
block; divisibility and structural restrictions can prevent this.
The boundary cases include $C(v,k,k)=\binom vk$ and $C(v,v,t)=1$.

The request is a general extremal and constructive problem, rather than
a single universal equality suggested by the counting bound. A finite
exhaustive search defines an algorithm in principle; the intended advance
is a mathematically useful exact determination or improved construction
and lower-bound method.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the smallest family of $k$-subsets covering every $t$-subset of a $v$-element set, and how can optimal families be constructed?
\subsection{Research attempt: point links, alteration, and exact test families}
\paragraph{Scope and literature check.}
GPT-6 Astra Ultra, 1 October 2026. The prompt asks for a genuine attempt
with an adversarial check, rather than a claimed solution. We work with
distinct blocks and containment coverage. The original page [S2] cites
Schönheim's point-link lower bound and construction work. The authors'
paper [S3] develops constructions; Gordon's maintained repository [S4]
distinguishes an exhibited covering from a proof of optimality. The
arguments below recover elementary bounds and exact subfamilies, without
claiming improvement over those sources.

\paragraph{1. A stronger lower bound from every point.}
Let $b=|\mathcal B|$ and $b_x=|\{B\in\mathcal B:x\in B\}|$.
For $t\ge2$, deleting $x$ from those blocks gives a
$(v-1,k-1,t-1)$ covering: any $(t-1)$-set outside $x$ extends with $x$
to a target $t$-set. Consequently
\[
 kb=\sum_{x\in V}b_x\ge v C(v-1,k-1,t-1),\qquad
 C(v,k,t)\ge\left\lceil\frac vk C(v-1,k-1,t-1)\right\rceil.
\]
Iterating, with $C(v,k,1)=\lceil v/k\rceil$, gives nested ceilings.
The last equality follows by the singleton incidence lower bound and
partitioning the vertices into groups of size at most $k$, extending
each group to a $k$-set. Integer rounding inside the recurrence can
strictly strengthen rounding the final ratio only.

\paragraph{2. A quantified random construction with repair.}
Write $N=\binom vt$ and $b_0=\binom kt$. A uniformly random $k$-set
contains any fixed $t$-set with probability $b_0/N$. Select $m$ blocks
independently with replacement. The expected number $U$ of uncovered
targets is
\[
 \mathbb E U=N(1-b_0/N)^m\le N\exp(-mb_0/N).
\]
For each uncovered target choose any containing $k$-set and add it.
Remove duplicates. The repaired family has size at most $m+U$, so
some outcome proves
\[
 C(v,k,t)\le\left\lfloor m+N\exp(-mb_0/N)\right\rfloor.
\]
The floor is legitimate because the optimum is an integer bounded by
the real expectation. For $b_0>1$, taking
$m=\lceil (N/b_0)\log b_0\rceil$ gives an upper bound at most
$(N/b_0)(1+\log b_0)+1$. This is an existence construction with a
repair procedure, not a proof that a random sample is always good.

\paragraph{3. Two exact checks.}
For $k=v-1$, every block is the complement of one omitted point. If
the distinct omitted points form $D$, a target $T$ is uncovered exactly
when $D\subseteq T$. Hence coverage holds exactly when $|D|>t$,
and $C(v,v-1,t)=t+1$ for $1\le t\le v-1$.
For $(v,k,t)=(7,3,2)$, use vertices $\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z$ and
the seven translates of $\{0,1,3\}$. The six ordered differences of
this triple are $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each once. Therefore each ordered pair
of distinct vertices occurs in one translated block, and every unordered
pair is covered. The incidence lower bound is $21/3=7$, proving optimality.

\paragraph{Verification and first remaining gap.}
The random calculation uses containment, not intersection. Its repair
step is possible because $t\le k\le v$. The point-link argument
requires $t\ge2$ and explicitly supplies its $t=1$ base. The two exact
families match their lower bounds; a local enumeration checked the 21
pairs in the seven-block construction. Outside such families, neither
the nested ceilings nor alteration proves equality: the former omits
compatibility between links, while the latter pays for overlaps and
repairs. Closing that gap uniformly would require additional structure.
