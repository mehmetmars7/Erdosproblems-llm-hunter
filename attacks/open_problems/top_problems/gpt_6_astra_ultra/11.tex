\section*{\texorpdfstring{The abc Conjecture of Masser and Oesterle}{The abc Conjecture of Masser and Oesterle}}\label{11}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer $n$, its radical is $\operatorname{rad}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p$, with $\operatorname{rad}(1)=1$. A triple $a,b,c>0$ with $a+b=c$ and $\gcd(a,b)=1$ is automatically pairwise coprime. The conjecture is
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_\varepsilon>0\ \forall a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\qquad
 \begin{cases}a+b=c,\\\gcd(a,b)=1\end{cases}
 \Longrightarrow c<C_\varepsilon\operatorname{rad}(abc)^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]
The constant depends on $\varepsilon$ only. A counterexample must defeat every constant for some fixed positive $\varepsilon$; one unusually large triple does not suffice.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00025-026-02616-5}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the size of a coprime additive triple always be controlled, up to any small exponent loss, by its distinct prime factors?
\subsection{Research attempt}

\paragraph{Quantifiers, literature, and direction of attack.}
Put $R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)$, and retain positive, pairwise coprime
$a,b,c$ with $a+b=c$. The assertion asks for a constant depending only
on $\varepsilon$, simultaneously for all prime supports and exponents.
A high-quality example $c>R$ does not contradict it. The catalogue's
disputed-claim status and sources [S2]--[S4] are retained; this notebook
neither assumes a claimed proof nor purports to adjudicate those arguments.
Scholze--Stix [S3] is a specific critique of an existing approach, not a
proof that every possible approach must fail.

The polynomial analogue suggests a sharply testable route. In the
Mason--Stothers theorem, differentiation removes one multiplicity at every
root, and additivity identifies several Wronskians. Snyder [E1] gives a
short proof of that established theorem. Pasten [E2] develops arithmetic
derivatives tailored to one equation and proves a precise relation between
small nondegenerate derivatives and $abc$. These are existing ideas. The
present work first computes the exact defect for the \emph{fixed} ordinary
arithmetic derivative, proves the resulting restricted radical bound, and
then tests how allowing adjustable prime weights changes the obstruction.

\paragraph{The fixed derivative and the additive defect.}
For $n>0$ define
\[
 D(n)=\sum_{p\mid n}v_p(n)\frac np,
 \qquad D(1)=0.
\]
This integer-valued function satisfies $D(mn)=mD(n)+nD(m)$, as follows
by adding the prime valuations. It is not additive. Define
\[
 \Delta=D(a)+D(b)-D(c),\quad W=aD(b)-bD(a),\quad
 A=\frac a{\operatorname{rad}(a)},\ B=\frac b{\operatorname{rad}(b)},\
 C=\frac c{\operatorname{rad}(c)}.
\]
The letters $A,B,C$ here denote integers, not polynomials or unspecified
constants. Since $a,b,c$ are pairwise coprime, so are $A,B,C$.
For each $n$, the integer $n/\operatorname{rad}(n)$ divides $D(n)$:
every summand $v_p(n)n/p$ contains all prime factors of that divisor.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{ra:11:congruences}
 AB\mid W,\qquad
 W=aD(c)-cD(a)+a\Delta,\qquad C\mid W-a\Delta.
\end{equation}
In particular, because $(a,C)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{ra:11:exact-defect}
 \gcd(C,W)=\gcd(C,\Delta),\qquad
 C\mid W\ \Longleftrightarrow\ C\mid\Delta.
\end{equation}
Thus the missing factor in the polynomial analogy is an explicit
congruence. It cannot be supplied merely by applying the product rule.

\textbf{Lemma 11.1 (a radical estimate with the defect retained).}
Suppose $W\ne0$, and put
$J=C/\gcd(C,\Delta)$, with $\gcd(C,0)=C$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ra:11:defect-bound}
 c\le R J\,\frac{\log c}{\log2}.
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.}
The integer $AB\gcd(C,\Delta)$ divides $W$, by
\eqref{ra:11:congruences}--\eqref{ra:11:exact-defect} and pairwise
coprimality. Since $W$ is nonzero, this divisor is at most $|W|$.
Also, using $p\log p\ge2\log2$ for every prime $p$,
\[
 \frac{D(n)}n=\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{v_p(n)}p
       \le\frac{\log n}{2\log2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{abc}{RJ}=AB\gcd(C,\Delta)
       \le |W|
       \le ab\frac{\log a+\log b}{2\log2}
       \le ab\frac{\log c}{\log2}.
\]
Divide by $ab>0$ to obtain \eqref{ra:11:defect-bound}.
The logarithm is natural throughout.\hfill$\square$

This proves the desired near-linear estimate on the concrete subclass
where $W\ne0$ and $C\mid\Delta$: there $J=1$.
To check the exponent carefully, fix $\varepsilon>0$ and set
$\alpha=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$. The elementary maximum of
$(\log x)x^{-\alpha}$ on $x\ge1$ is $1/(e\alpha)$.
Thus the lemma gives
\[
 c^{1-\alpha}\le\frac{R}{e\alpha\log2},\qquad
 c\le\left(\frac1{e\alpha\log2}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}
                      R^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]
Enlarging the constant yields the strict inequality in the catalogue.
This is a proved restricted result, with a uniform constant on that
subclass. There is no proof that every triple belongs to it.

\paragraph{Exact examples and a failure of the naive argument.}
For $(a,b,c)=(1,8,9)$, one has $D(8)=12$, $D(9)=6$,
$\Delta=6$, $C=3$, and $W=12$. Here $R=6<c=9$, and $J=1$.
The triple is therefore a nontrivial test of the restricted result.
For $(1,80,81)$, however,
\[
 D(80)=176,\quad D(81)=108,\quad \Delta=68,
 \quad (A,B,C)=(1,8,27),\quad W=176.
\]
The required factor $C=27$ does not divide $W$; indeed $ABC=216>176$.
Hence the unqualified claim $abc/R\le|W|$, obtained by copying the
polynomial proof, is false for this explicit coprime additive triple.
The corrected lemma retains $J=27$ and survives this test.
This is a counterexample to a proposed intermediate argument, not to
the $abc$ conjecture.

\paragraph{Degeneracy is also an arithmetic issue.}
The condition $W\ne0$ cannot simply be deleted. There is an exact
description of its failure. Write
$a=\prod_{p\mid a}p^{e_p}$ and $b=\prod_{q\mid b}q^{f_q}$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{ra:11:zero-class}
 W=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 p\mid e_p\text{ for every }p\mid a,\\
 q\mid f_q\text{ for every }q\mid b,\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{p\mid a}e_p/p=\sum_{q\mid b}f_q/q.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
To prove the forward implication, multiply
$D(a)/a=D(b)/b$ by the product $P$ of all primes dividing $ab$.
Reduction modulo a prime $p\mid a$ leaves only the term
$e_pP/p$, so $p\mid e_p$. The argument on the other side is identical.
The remaining equality is already the displayed derivative identity.
Conversely, the three conditions immediately give $W=0$.
Empty prime supports are allowed; thus $(1,1,2)$ is included.

For example $a=4$, $b=27$, $c=31$ gives
$D(a)/a=D(b)/b=1$ and $W=0$. More generally
$(a,b)=(2^{2t},3^{3t})$ has $W=0$ for every positive integer $t$.
These are valid coprime additive triples after setting $c=a+b$,
but no radical estimate for their sums follows from a zero Wronskian.
Their existence blocks a universal proof using this one fixed derivative;
it does not imply that their radical ratios are large.

\paragraph{Allowing prime weights: a known framework, an explicit test.}
Let $z_p\in\mathbb Z$ be zero outside the primes dividing $abc$, and
define
\[
 D_z(n)=\sum_{p\mid n}v_p(n)\frac np z_p,
 \qquad H(z)=\max_{p\mid abc}|z_p|.
\]
These are Pasten's arithmetic derivatives [E2], expressed in coordinates.
Impose $D_z(a)+D_z(b)=D_z(c)$ and require
$W_z=aD_z(b)-bD_z(a)\ne0$. Theorem 3.3 of [E2] gives
\begin{equation}\label{ra:11:pasten}
 c\le R H(z)\frac{\log c}{\log2}.
\end{equation}
The divisibility calculation above is exactly the local mechanism, now
with zero additive defect. This bound and the general derivative strategy
are established work; they are not being claimed as new progress on $abc$.

For the failed example $(1,80,81)$, the additivity equation is
\[
 160z_2+16z_5=108z_3,
 \quad\hbox{equivalently}\quad40z_2+4z_5=27z_3.
\]
The weights $(z_2,z_3,z_5)=(3,4,-3)$ give
$D_z(80)=D_z(81)=432$, $W_z=432$, and $H(z)=4$.
The required divisor $216$ now divides $W_z$.
Moreover height four is minimal: reducing the equation modulo four forces
$4\mid z_3$, while nondegeneracy here requires $z_3\ne0$ because
$W_z=D_z(80)=108z_3$. A height below four is therefore impossible.
This is an exact optimization for one triple, rather than a numerical
lattice guess or an asymptotic statement.

Existence alone is inexpensive. Except at $(a,b)=(1,1)$, interchange
$a,b$ if necessary so $b>1$, choose $p\mid b$ and $r\mid c$, and set
\[
 U=bv_p(b)/p,\quad V=cv_r(c)/r,\quad g=\gcd(U,V),
 \qquad z_p=V/g,\quad z_r=U/g,
\]
with all other weights zero. Their supports are disjoint by coprimality.
Then $D_z(a)=0$, $D_z(b)=D_z(c)=UV/g>0$, so $W_z\ne0$.
But this construction only gives
$H(z)\le c\log c/(2\log2)$. Substitution in
\eqref{ra:11:pasten} cancels the useful factor $c$ and does not prove
the conjecture. The obstruction has moved from existence to size.

There is a separate short-vector trap. In the model lattice
$x_1+x_2+Mx_3=0$, the nonzero vector $(1,-1,0)$ has height one.
If the required nondegeneracy is $x_3\ne0$, every acceptable vector
has height at least $M/2$, since
$|x_1|+|x_2|\ge M|x_3|$. This is not asserted to be the lattice of an
actual triple; it disproves the abstract inference that finding a short
nonzero vector automatically finds a short vector outside a forbidden
hyperplane. The Wronskian condition must remain in any lattice estimate.

\paragraph{A precise sufficient next statement.}
One possible stronger-than-necessary target is: for every $\delta>0$
there is $K_\delta$ such that every coprime triple with $c>R$ admits
additive weights with $W_z\ne0$ and $H(z)\le K_\delta c^\delta$.
Together with \eqref{ra:11:pasten}, this would imply the conjecture.
Indeed use $\log c\ll_\delta c^\delta$ and choose
$2\delta=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$; then
$c^{1-2\delta}\ll_\delta R$, giving exactly exponent $1+\varepsilon$.
Triples with $c\le R$ already satisfy the target after enlarging the
constant. No equivalence between this particular formulation and $abc$
is claimed. Pasten's more refined equivalence has its own exponents and
exceptional configurations [E2, Section 4]. The elementary construction
above falls well short of even this sufficient target.

\paragraph{What a disproof search would have to establish.}
For a fixed $\varepsilon>0$, define the normalized ratio
$Q_\varepsilon(a,b,c)=c/R^{1+\varepsilon}$.
A disproof requires an unbounded sequence of these ratios with the same
$\varepsilon$. Maximizing the quality $\log c/\log R$ over one finite
box does not provide that sequence, nor does allowing $\varepsilon$ to
shrink separately for every record triple. The defect estimate gives a
more directed necessary condition on a hypothetical counterexample
sequence with $W\ne0$:
\[
 J\ge\frac{c\log2}{R\log c}
       =Q_\varepsilon\,\frac{R^\varepsilon\log2}{\log c}.
\]
Thus a candidate family must escape the class with small defect factor,
or repeatedly fall into the exactly described zero-Wronskian class.
This inequality is a filter, not a construction: a large $J$ is
compatible with a perfectly ordinary radical ratio. The same caution
applies to the minimum weighted height; failure to find small weights
in a bounded search does not prove they do not exist outside that box.
The height-four example avoided this problem by a modular lower bound
matching an explicit vector. A useful computational extension would
retain such certificates of minimality and organize difficult triples
by their divisibility obstruction, rather than reporting large quality
values alone. No family meeting the required unbounded-ratio condition
was obtained here.

\paragraph{Finite verification.}
The local standard-library script exhausts all $608294$ coprime triples
with $1\le a\le b$ and $c\le2000$. It verifies
\eqref{ra:11:congruences}, \eqref{ra:11:exact-defect}, and the full
equivalence \eqref{ra:11:zero-class} by exact integer arithmetic.
There are three zero-Wronskian triples in that range. Among the other
triples, $467698$ satisfy $C\mid\Delta$; most are easy because $C=1$.
Exactly seven of these also have $c>R$, including $(1,8,9)$,
$(1,48,49)$, and $(1,512,513)$.
Exhaustion of the bounded weight boxes independently confirms the
height-four minimum for $(1,80,81)$; the modular argument above explains it.
No finite count is used to infer a density or a universal bound.
Reproduction files are \texttt{checks.py} and \texttt{results.json} under
\texttt{\_Local/top\_problems/continuation\_2026\_09\_27/analytic/}.

\paragraph{First gap and outcome.}
The fixed-derivative calculation proves the radical bound with its exact
defect factor, and hence the full $\varepsilon$-family of inequalities
on the specified subclass. It also classifies zero Wronskians and gives
an explicit counterexample to the uncorrected polynomial analogy.
The first universal gap is controlling the defect or finding sufficiently
small additive weights while preserving $W_z\ne0$. The example and the
lattice stress test show that neither requirement follows from formal
differentiation or from a nonzero lattice vector alone.

\textbf{Outcome: UNRESOLVED.} This is a checked restricted estimate and
an obstruction analysis within an established derivative approach. It
does not prove $abc$, disprove it, or certify a disputed proof. Direct
derivation, primary-source comparison, and exact finite tests were used;
no formal proof assistant or independent expert review was involved.
