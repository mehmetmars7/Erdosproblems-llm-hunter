\section*{Inscribed Square Problem}\label{3408}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3408 · OPG-757 · Topology · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Jordan curve in the plane is the image $\Gamma=f(S^1)$ of a
continuous injective map $f:S^1\to\mathbb R^2$, where $S^1$ is the
unit circle. No differentiability, rectifiability, or finite-length
condition is assumed. Four distinct points form a square if they
can be listed as
\[
             a,\quad a+u,\quad a+u+v,\quad a+v,
\]
where $u,v\in\mathbb R^2$, $u\cdot v=0$, and
$\|u\|=\|v\|>0$. The dot product and norm are Euclidean.

The conjecture asserts that every Jordan curve contains such four
points. Formally, for every $f$ as above there are $a,u,v$ satisfying
these conditions and
$\{a,a+u,a+u+v,a+v\}\subseteq f(S^1)$. Only the vertices need lie
on the curve; the sides are not required to be subsets of it or
of its bounded complementary component.
The square must have positive side length, excluding a collapsed
limit configuration. The statement does not prescribe the cyclic
order of the four vertices along a parametrization of the curve.
A proof for smooth or polygonal curves resolves only those
subclasses unless it also controls degeneration in passing to
arbitrary continuous Jordan curves.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every simple closed continuous plane curve pass through all four vertices of some nondegenerate square?
