\section*{Artin's Conjecture on Primitive Roots}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 029; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that each integer other than $-1$ or a square is primitive modulo at least $c_a x/(\log x)^2$ primes between $x$ and $2x$ for large $x$; In manuscript 2 (family 029; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that a fixed finite list of positive prime bases simultaneously has full multiplicative order for at least $cx/(\log x)^2$ primes in a dyadic interval, conditional on four specified companion inputs.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 029; 2026-10-04), cubic theta coefficients under Kummer twists give a Hecke zero-free comparison. Controlled prime predecessors in fixed progressions are then sieved against complete-splitting obstructions, retaining enough primes on which the base has full multiplicative order.

For manuscript 2 (family 029; 2026-10-04), uniform splitting estimates remove bad order divisors from a marked family in a progression. Rough-number counts and separation of the least prime factor eliminate balanced composites, leaving the simultaneous prime conclusion under those inputs.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}

The simultaneous-prime-base paper also retains four explicit assumptions. General rank-one torus formulations over other number fields exceed the integer-base setting.
