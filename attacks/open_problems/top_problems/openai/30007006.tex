\section*{Self-Ext vanishing over symmetric algebras}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A finite-dimensional symmetric algebra is claimed to carry a nonprojective finite module without positive self-extensions, refuting Tachikawa second-conjecture rigidity.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Take \(k=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2)\), with three algebraically independent indeterminates. The source claims a finite-dimensional symmetric unital algebra \(A\) and a nonprojective finite-dimensional left module \(M\) satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^i_A(M,M)=0\qquad(i>0).
\]
For every extension field \(K/k\), put \(A_K=K\otimes_kA\), \(M_K=K\otimes_kM\), and \(\Gamma_K=\operatorname{End}_{A_K}(A_K\oplus M_K)^{\mathrm{op}}\). The homological corollary claims that its minimal injective resolution has only projective terms but infinite injective dimension; also a nonzero simple \(S\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^i_{\Gamma_K}(S,\Gamma_K)=0\qquad(i\ge0).
\]
It supplies a projective-injective Wakamatsu tilting module that is not tilting. The finitistic corollary provides a left module \(C_n\), finite-dimensional as a vector space, for which \(\operatorname{pd}_{\Gamma_K}C_n=n\) for each \(n\ge1\), hence both left finitistic dimensions are infinite.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
The construction strengthens complete-resolution vanishing on both sides of degree zero before passing to a trivial extension. A transfer calculation then produces a symmetric algebra and preserves the nonprojective module together with its positive Ext vanishing.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The main theorem gives a finite-dimensional symmetric algebra over F\_2(q,H\_1,H\_2) and a finite-dimensional nonprojective module with all positive self-Ext zero. lean/docs/199.md directly documents this Tachikawa main counterexample; later homological consequences are unnecessary. Within these assumptions, this is a claimed negative answer to the stated question.
