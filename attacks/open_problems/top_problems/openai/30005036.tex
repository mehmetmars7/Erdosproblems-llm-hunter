\section*{Kaplansky's Conjectures for Group Rings}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A finitely presented torsion-free group is claimed to have nonzero elements $a,c\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $ac=0$, despite admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying complex; A finite characteristic-two field and a finitely presented group are claimed to produce a scalar one-sided inverse pair; the resulting shift is injective without being onto and the group is nonsofic; For a particular odd prime $p$, the authors claim a one-sided inverse defect over a field with $p^4$ elements, together with a nonsurjective injective shift; Another construction claims a torsion-free, finitely presented $G$ for which $\mathbb F_2[G]$ contains $a,b$ satisfying $ab=1$ and $ba\ne1$; The Bass trace restriction is claimed for complex group rings of arbitrary discrete groups, implying triviality of scalar idempotents for torsion-free groups over characteristic-zero domains; For every discrete group, the trace of an idempotent matrix over $\ell^1(G;\mathbb C)$ is claimed to vanish outside a finite collection of finite-order conjugacy classes. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For some torsion-free, finitely presented group \(G\) admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying space, the source claims
\[
 \exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2[G]\setminus\{0\},\qquad\alpha\beta=0.
\]
The coefficients are in the field with two elements. This is a zero-divisor example, without a claim here about integral units.

\paragraph{Manuscript 2.}
Take a finite field \(K\) with \(\operatorname{char}K=2\). The constructed group \(G\) is finitely presented and has an element of odd prime order. Two finite group-ring sums meet
\[
 a_{\mathrm{out}},b_{\mathrm{out}}\in K[G],\qquad
 a_{\mathrm{out}}b_{\mathrm{out}}=1,\qquad
 b_{\mathrm{out}}a_{\mathrm{out}}\ne1.
\]
Its specification uses terminating finite-set and finite-field computations. Unlike the torsion-free companion, this particular group contains torsion.

\paragraph{Manuscript 3.}
Define the integers
\[
 m=\binom{1200}{600},\qquad
 p=\min\{h\in\mathbb Z:h>1,\ h\mid(m!)^2+1\}.
\]
The claimed example has \(p\) an odd prime, \(|K|=p^4\), and a specified finitely generated group \(G\) containing torsion. Its finite sums \(a,b\in K[G]\) satisfy
\[
 ab=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad ba\ne1.
\]
This is an example in a specified odd characteristic, rather than a theorem for each odd prime.

\paragraph{Manuscript 4.}
The construction supplies a torsion-free, finitely presented group \(G\), with a finite two-dimensional \(K(G,1)\), and elements of its characteristic-two group algebra satisfying
\[
 a,b,c\in\mathbb F_2[G],\qquad ab=1,\quad ac=0,\quad c\ne0,\quad ba\ne1.
\]
In particular, \(a\ne0\) and \(e=ba\) obeys \(e^2=e\), \(e\notin\{0,1\}\). Thus this same example supplies failures of direct finiteness, absence of zero divisors, and triviality of scalar idempotents. These assertions concern \(\mathbb F_2[G]\); the separate integral-unit assertion is not a consequence.

\paragraph{Manuscript 5.}
For an arbitrary discrete group \(G\), let \(\operatorname{HS}_G\) be the Hattori--Stallings trace, whose value at a conjugacy class sums the coefficients on that class. For every \(x\in K_0(\mathbb C[G])\),
\[
 |g|=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{HS}_G(x)([g])=0.
\]
The torsion-free corollary states that, for every \(q\ge1\) and idempotent \(e\in M_q(\mathbb C[G])\), its identity-coefficient trace equals the rank of its augmentation matrix:
\[
 \tau_{G,q}(e)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}(\epsilon_{G,q}(e)).
\]
For such \(G\), any commutative unital domain \(R\) of characteristic zero satisfies
\[
 e\in R[G],\ e^2=e\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The characteristic-zero qualification does not cover positive-characteristic group rings.

\paragraph{Manuscript 6.}
For any discrete group \(G\), any integer \(q\ge1\), and any idempotent matrix \(e\in M_q(\ell^1(G))\), write \(\tau_C(e)\) for the sum, on a conjugacy class \(C\), of the coefficients of its diagonal trace. The claim is that some finite set \(F\) of finite-order conjugacy classes satisfies
\[
 C\notin F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\tau_C(e)=0.
\]
Equivalently, the \(\ell^1\) Hattori--Stallings trace has image in the algebraic direct sum on torsion conjugacy classes. No claim about arbitrary positive-characteristic fields follows from this complex Banach-algebra statement.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Finite labeled graphs encode supports whose product coefficients cancel modulo two. A random-label counting argument excludes planar path arrangements; cone surgeries turn that exclusion into asphericity and root separation, so the coefficients remain nonzero in a torsion-free group. Incidence data and polynomial pairings first give a defective inverse pair of matrices. A geometric group-embedding construction preserves the defect, and character packing realizes the matrix algebra inside a larger group algebra; dual convolution gives the cellular-automaton consequence.

The construction uses translation representations of finite groups: a high-degree subspace is killed on one side and a weighted pairing supplies the other annihilation. Finite group identifications and a quadratic field tower package these local relations into the scalar inverse pair; the claim concerns the specified prime rather than all odd characteristics. An unequal-side parity pattern supplies the one-sided inverse relation in labeled graphs. Weighted counts handle repeated edge traversals, and a separator reduces arbitrarily large planar arrangements to bounded path collections; cone attachment and root separation then preserve the inverse defect while proving torsion-freeness.

An idempotent is encoded by finite cyclic path configurations whose weighted deletion relations generate cycles. Exterior differential forms and a cyclic Pfaffian identity detect forbidden conjugacy support; trace-rank arguments then turn the complex trace conclusion into the stated idempotent consequence. Analytic idempotent correction first replaces the matrix, up to similarity, by one with exponential decay. Weighted cyclic configurations are then treated with smooth spatial summaries, multiscale time averages, and an ordered-kernel estimate; one class-independent cutoff proves actual finite support rather than only absolute summability.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The torsion-free characteristic-two example refutes both the zero-divisor and scalar-idempotent assertions. The characteristic-two and odd-characteristic examples also refute unrestricted direct finiteness, with the former torsion-free paper giving a stronger counterexample of that kind. The selected papers do not settle the bundle's unit clause. The Bass results establish characteristic-zero trace and scalar-idempotent conclusions; these coexist with the positive-characteristic counterexamples.
