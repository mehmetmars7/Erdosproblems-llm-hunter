\section*{Kaplansky's Conjectures for Group Rings}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A finitely presented torsion-free group is claimed to have nonzero elements $a,c\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $ac=0$, despite admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying complex; A finite characteristic-two field and a finitely presented group are claimed to produce a scalar one-sided inverse pair; the resulting shift is injective without being onto and the group is nonsofic; For a particular odd prime $p$, the authors claim a one-sided inverse defect over a field with $p^4$ elements, together with a nonsurjective injective shift; Another construction claims a torsion-free, finitely presented $G$ for which $\mathbb F_2[G]$ contains $a,b$ satisfying $ab=1$ and $ba\ne1$; The Bass trace restriction is claimed for complex group rings of arbitrary discrete groups, implying triviality of scalar idempotents for torsion-free groups over characteristic-zero domains; For every discrete group, the trace of an idempotent matrix over $\ell^1(G;\mathbb C)$ is claimed to vanish outside a finite collection of finite-order conjugacy classes. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Finite labeled graphs encode supports whose product coefficients cancel modulo two. A random-label counting argument excludes planar path arrangements; cone surgeries turn that exclusion into asphericity and root separation, so the coefficients remain nonzero in a torsion-free group. Incidence data and polynomial pairings first give a defective inverse pair of matrices. A geometric group-embedding construction preserves the defect, and character packing realizes the matrix algebra inside a larger group algebra; dual convolution gives the cellular-automaton consequence.

The construction uses translation representations of finite groups: a high-degree subspace is killed on one side and a weighted pairing supplies the other annihilation. Finite group identifications and a quadratic field tower package these local relations into the scalar inverse pair; the claim concerns the specified prime rather than all odd characteristics. An unequal-side parity pattern supplies the one-sided inverse relation in labeled graphs. Weighted counts handle repeated edge traversals, and a separator reduces arbitrarily large planar arrangements to bounded path collections; cone attachment and root separation then preserve the inverse defect while proving torsion-freeness.

An idempotent is encoded by finite cyclic path configurations whose weighted deletion relations generate cycles. Exterior differential forms and a cyclic Pfaffian identity detect forbidden conjugacy support; trace-rank arguments then turn the complex trace conclusion into the stated idempotent consequence. Analytic idempotent correction first replaces the matrix, up to similarity, by one with exponential decay. Weighted cyclic configurations are then treated with smooth spatial summaries, multiscale time averages, and an ordered-kernel estimate; one class-independent cutoff proves actual finite support rather than only absolute summability.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Theorem 1.1 refutes the zero-divisor clause over F\({}_{2}\). It does not determine the other clauses of the bundle. The scalar counterexample refutes unrestricted direct finiteness. Its group contains torsion, so it does not determine the bundle’s torsion-free clauses. Theorem 1.1 refutes the torsion-free zero-divisor and idempotent clauses via ac=0 and e=ba; it also refutes unrestricted direct finiteness. It does not settle the bundle’s separate integral/field unit assertion. The Bass trace theorem implies the characteristic-zero scalar-idempotent clause for torsion-free groups. The positive-characteristic cases and the other independent bundled conjectures are not determined. This entry records partial support; complete coverage of the question is not established by this catalogue.
