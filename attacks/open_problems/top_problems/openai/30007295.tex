\section*{Approximation paving for every MASA}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Every self-adjoint element is claimed to admit strong approximants bounded by three times its norm, norm-pavable over any MASA with O(\(\varepsilon^{-6}\)) pieces and error relative to each approximant's norm. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every $0<\varepsilon<1$, OpenAI claims an integer $R(\varepsilon)\le C_{\mathrm{ap}}\varepsilon^{-6}$ for a universal constant, working for every von Neumann algebra $M\subset B(H)$, maximal abelian $A\subset M$, self-adjoint $x\in M$, finite $F\subset H$ and $\delta>0$. There are self-adjoint $y\in M$, $a\in A$ and a unit partition of $R(\varepsilon)$ projections $p_i\in A$ such that
\[
\|y\|\le3\|x\|,\quad\|(y-x)\xi\|<\delta\ (\xi\in F),\quad
\|a\|\le\|y\|,\quad\left\|\sum_ip_iyp_i-a\right\|\le\varepsilon\|y\|.
\]
Zero partition projections are allowed. This is paving of a strong-operator approximation $y$, not norm paving of the original $x$; no expectation hypothesis is imposed.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 300, 2026-09-25): Buffered determinant polynomials give measurable one-sided matrix pavings. For the normalizer algebra these transfer through measured equivalence relations, while random diagonal phases treat diffuse complementary kernels. Separable reduction and a three-corner assembly remove the expectation hypothesis; norming vectors and clipping retain the required normalization for the actual approximants.
