\section*{Hyperinvariant subspaces for quasinilpotent Hilbert-space operators}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Each separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space supports, according to the paper, a nonzero norm-quasinilpotent operator with no proper nonzero closed hyperinvariant subspace and a transitive commutant. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 293, 2026-09-27): Quadratically weighted bilateral shifts have explicitly classified invariant tails, and the construction organizes them over a 2-adic probability base. A bounded commuting transport moves these tails backward, forcing an impossible strict descent for any hyperinvariant projection field. Uniform estimates on powers establish quasinilpotence of the entire operator, not just of individual fibers.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Manuscript 1 (family 293, 2026-09-27): The problem selects the mathematical statement summarized here, including its stated hypotheses and parameter range. The cited theorem gives a claimed counterexample to that assertion in full.

\subsection{Formal verification}
For family 293, the scope page documents the following selections. The rotation selection gives quasinilpotent weighted operators with no proper invariant projection in the associated finite factor. The backward-intertwiner selection gives a proper strong-closed transitive commutant on each separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space; Brown-measure and product variants are documented separately. The documented statement covers the main claim of manuscript 1. Its selected declaration also covers the clause used for this problem.
