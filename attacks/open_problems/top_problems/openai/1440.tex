\section*{Ryser's Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Ryser obstruction at extension-field ranks}: The first manuscript claims intersecting $r$-partite, $r$-uniform hypergraphs with covering number $r$ at ranks $r=s^n+1$ in the specified large-prime and odd-degree range; a single such example contradicts Ryser’s proposed intersecting-case bound.

\textit{Balanced Ryser counterexamples}: For each sufficiently large prime $q$, the companion paper claims a uniform hypergraph of rank $q+1$, also with $q+1$ parts, whose edges intersect pairwise and whose covering number is $q+1$; each part has exactly $q+1$ vertices that lie in an edge.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} There is $s_0$ such that for every prime $s\ge s_0$ with $s\equiv2\pmod3$, all sufficiently large odd $n$ yield $q=s^n$ and a finite intersecting $(q+1)$-partite $(q+1)$-uniform hypergraph $H$ with $\tau(H)=q+1$. Intersecting means every two edges meet; $\tau$ is the smallest vertex-set size meeting every edge. Hence $\nu(H)=1$ and $\tau(H)>q\nu(H)$. The same parameters give an $(q+1)$-edge-colored complete graph requiring exactly $q+1$ monochromatic trees to cover its vertices, also when trees are replaced by connected monochromatic subgraphs.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} For every sufficiently large prime $q$, the paper claims an intersecting $(q+1)$-partite $(q+1)$-uniform hypergraph with parts $V_i$ satisfying \[|V_i|=q+1\ (1\le i\le q+1),\qquad\tau(H)=q+1.\] Every vertex belongs to an edge. Since the matching number is $1$, this refutes $\tau\le(r-1)\nu$ at $r=q+1$. Its complete-graph corollary supplies a $(q+1)$-edge-coloring requiring exactly $q+1$ monochromatic trees, or connected monochromatic subgraphs, for a vertex cover.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Ryser obstruction at extension-field ranks}: Affine-plane points define ordinary edges, and modified exceptional edges are added to defeat small covers. Incidence savings force any near-minimal ordinary cover to resemble a pencil of lines. Random deletions preserve the required intersections while the exceptional labels make every candidate pencil cover too expensive.

\textit{Balanced Ryser counterexamples}: Tuples of constituent lines provide many candidates for exceptional edges, with conflicts tracked by a graph. Conditional selection retains compatible candidates while controlling degrees, codegrees, and local prescription probabilities. Pencil-concentration estimates rule out small covers, and a deterministic reduction turns a successful finite line configuration into the balanced hypergraph.

\textit{Balanced Ryser counterexamples}: An intersecting r-partite r-uniform example has matching number one and cover number r, contradicting tau<=(r-1)nu even without any balance restriction.
