\section*{Finding matching upper and lower bounds for k-sets and halving lines}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Planar halving-line saving}: The claimed number of halving pairs in a sufficiently large even planar configuration with no three collinear is $O(n^{4/3-\varepsilon})$ for one absolute $\varepsilon>0$. The constants are existential.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For an even-sized planar point set $P$, with no three points collinear, let $h(P)$ count pairs whose line leaves $(|P|-2)/2$ points in each open halfplane. The claimed constants $\epsilon>0,C<\infty,n_0$ are absolute and satisfy \[h(P)\le C|P|^{4/3-\epsilon}\quad(|P|\ge n_0).\] The all-ranks theorem also bounds by $CN^{4/3-\epsilon}$ the number $S_k(P)$ of switches of the $k$ points minimizing $y-sx$ as $s$ ranges from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, for every rank $0\le k\le N$ of a generic $N$-point configuration. This power improvement does not give matching sharp bounds at every rank. Here the all-ranks genericity conditions prohibit three collinear points, repeated first coordinates, equal slopes from different point pairs, and any proper intersection of two point-pair segments lying on a third such segment. Its parameters range over $N\ge1$ and $0\le k\le N$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Planar halving-line saving}: Rotating rank sweeps track membership changes in the lower point set, including points that later return. Complete bipartite exchange graphs and convex-chain decompositions control crossings of intervals with high activity. Near saturation yields limiting rank-coordinate measures with an impossible concentration pattern; a little-oh packing improvement at one fixed scale then contracts the recurrence.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The claimed power saving gives a sharper planar halving-line upper bound, accompanied by generic sweep bounds at all ranks. It does not supply matching lower bounds for the $k$-set counts or halving-line counts requested here.
