\section*{Lück's Determinant Conjecture for Discrete Groups}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. An integral group-ring matrix is claimed to be rationally invertible while its Fuglede--Kadison determinant lies strictly between $0$ and $1$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} For some finitely generated discrete group \(G_D\), integer \(n\ge1\), and matrix
\[
 A\in M_n(\mathbb Z[G_D]),\qquad A\text{ invertible over }\mathbb Q[G_D],
\]
the operator induced by the left regular action \(T_A\) on \(\ell^2(G_D)^n\) has Fuglede--Kadison determinant
\[
 0<\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)<1.
\]
Here \(\mathcal N(G_D)\) is the group von Neumann algebra and the logarithmic spectral integral defining the determinant is finite and negative. The characteristic-two direct-finiteness result quoted in the proof is an input, not an additional determinant hypothesis. The convention is $(L_g\xi)(h)=\xi(g^{-1}h)$, and the matrix trace $\tau_n$ is unnormalized. For the normalized spectral law $\mu_A$ of $T_A^*T_A$,
\[
 \log\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)=\tfrac12\tau_n(\log(T_A^*T_A))=\tfrac n2\int\log t\,d\mu_A(t).
\]

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
A characteristic-two inverse defect is lifted using finite-coordinate projections in a wreath-product setting. These projections detect a selected coefficient, and a tracial logarithmic derivative applied to block shears calculates a determinant below the conjectured integral lower bound.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Theorem 1.1 gives an allowed integral square group-ring matrix with a finite strictly negative logarithmic spectral integral; normalization of matrix trace multiplies that integral by a positive factor and does not change its sign. Within these assumptions, this is a claimed negative answer to the stated question.
