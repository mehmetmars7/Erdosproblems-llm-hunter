\section*{The Zariski Multiplicity Problem}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Manuscript 1 (family 059, 2026-09-27): Two isolated hypersurface germs in four complex variables are claimed to have the same ambient topological type while their multiplicities are four and five. Manuscript 2 (family 059, 2026-09-24): Two isolated weighted-homogeneous hypersurface germs in a common finite dimension divisible by eight are claimed to be ambiently homeomorphic despite having multiplicities two and three.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} OpenAI claims reduced convergent holomorphic germs $f_4,f_5:(\mathbb C^4,0)\to(\mathbb C,0)$ for which zero is an isolated critical point in each case, such that
\[
\operatorname{ord}_0f_4=4,\qquad\operatorname{ord}_0f_5=5,\qquad
(\mathbb C^4,V(f_4),0)\cong(\mathbb C^4,V(f_5),0)
\]
by a homeomorphism germ of ambient pairs. The multiplicity $\operatorname{ord}_0f$ is the least total degree appearing among nonvanishing terms of its power series. The equivalence is embedded topology, despite distinct multiplicities.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} For some finite integer $N>3$ divisible by eight, OpenAI claims reduced real-coefficient polynomials $f_1,f_2\in\mathbb R[z_1,\ldots,z_N]$, each weighted homogeneous for positive rational weights and with an isolated critical point at zero, satisfying
\[
\operatorname{ord}_0f_1=2,\qquad\operatorname{ord}_0f_2=3,\qquad
(\mathbb C^N,V(f_1),0)\cong(\mathbb C^N,V(f_2),0)
\]
by an ambient-pair homeomorphism germ. Weighted homogeneous means $f(t^{a_1}z_1,\ldots,t^{a_N}z_N)=t^df(z)$ for positive integers $a_i,d$. The existence assertion does not specify a fixed small ambient dimension.

Manuscript 1 (family 059, 2026-09-27): Convergent equations in analytic charts give a finite-map lattice calculation that matches the integral Seifert forms. The nonspherical open-book classification on the seven-sphere identifies the embedded links, and the ambient cone construction transfers that identification to the zero-set germs.
