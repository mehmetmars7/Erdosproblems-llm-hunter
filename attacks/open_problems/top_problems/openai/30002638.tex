\section*{Gromov Macroscopic Dimension Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Pointwise scalar curvature and width}: The paper claims dimension-only control of the full fibers of a continuous map from any complete scalar-positive $n$-manifold to an $(n-2)$-complex, for $n\ge4$; it also gives the stated macroscopic-dimension consequence on universal covering spaces when the closed base admits a positive-scalar-curvature metric.

\textit{Spectral width in dimension three}: A complete three-manifold satisfying $-4\Delta+\mathrm{Scal}\ge\lambda>0$ is claimed to map continuously to a graph with full fiber diameter at most $500/\sqrt\lambda$; no global bounded-geometry condition is assumed.

\textit{Higher-dimensional spectral width}: The spectral lower bound $-4\Delta+\mathrm{Scal}\ge1$ is claimed to imply uniform continuous $(n-2)$-width for every complete $n$-manifold with $n\ge4$; the bound uses the initial metric rather than an auxiliary conformal distance.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every $n\ge4$ there is $C_n<\infty$ such that a connected complete smooth boundaryless $n$-manifold with $\operatorname{Scal}_g\ge1$ has a continuous map $f:M\to K$, where $K$ is a simplicial complex with the usual polyhedral topology and $\dim K\le n-2$, with \[\sup_{y\in K}\operatorname{diam}_g f^{-1}(y)\le C_n.\] Entire fibers, rather than their individual components, are bounded. For smooth Riemannian $(M,g)$ of dimension $n\ge4$, assume $M$ is closed and connected and $\operatorname{Scal}\ge\sigma^2>0$, the corollary gives $\operatorname{FillRad}(M,g)\le C_n/(2\sigma)$. A separate stated corollary covers all closed positive-scalar-curvature dimensions $n\ge2$: the universal-cover geometry obtained by lifting that metric has continuous macroscopic dimension at most $n-2$.

 The filling radius uses the integral fundamental class when the manifold is orientable and its mod-two fundamental class otherwise.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} Let $(M^3,g)$ be connected, complete, smooth and without boundary. If $\lambda>0$ and \[\int_M(4|\nabla\phi|^2+\operatorname{Scal}_g\phi^2)\,dV_g\ge\lambda\int_M\phi^2\,dV_g\quad(\phi\in C_c^\infty(M;\mathbb R)),\] the claim supplies a simplicial complex $K$ satisfying $\dim K\le1$ and a map $f:M\to K$ which is continuous with $\operatorname{diam}_g f^{-1}(y)\le500/\sqrt\lambda$ for every fiber. Compactness, orientability, spin and bounded geometry are not required.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 3.} For $n\ge4$, a connected complete smooth boundaryless $(M^n,g)$ satisfying \[\int_M(4|\nabla\phi|^2+\operatorname{Scal}_g\phi^2)\,dV_g\ge\int_M\phi^2\,dV_g\quad(\phi\in C_c^\infty(M;\mathbb R))\] is claimed to have a continuous $f:M\to K$ with $\dim K\le n-2$ and all fiber diameters at most $C_n$, depending only on $n$. Thus its Urysohn $(n-2)$-width, the infimum of these uniform fiber-diameter bounds, is at most $C_n$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Pointwise scalar curvature and width}: A separated net supplies labeled inner and outer balls in the original metric. Compatible walls and inserted cylinders are constructed while a nearby collapse map keeps track of those original positions. The final walls have no intersection of $n-1$ different labels, so their sign coordinates define an $(n-2)$-dimensional map; shared coordinates place the entire fiber in one uniformly bounded original ball.

\textit{Spectral width in dimension three}: A positive supersolution is converted into a drift-weighted scalar inequality without changing the original distance. Weighted stable surfaces inherit a two-dimensional inequality, whose geodesic index estimate bounds their intrinsic diameter. Local minimization and smoothing in compact regions then organize these surfaces into a graph-valued decomposition with a uniform bound on each complete fiber.

\textit{Higher-dimensional spectral width}: A positive supersolution produces a signed drift with a weighted curvature bound. An auxiliary graph metric and updated drift add a quadratic reserve even when the original drift is unbounded. The subsequent stretching and wall construction uses that reserve to preserve curvature while reducing intersection multiplicity, yielding fibers bounded in the original geometry.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The universal-cover corollary explicitly claims continuous macroscopic dimension at most $n-2$ for every closed positive-scalar-curvature manifold in all dimensions $n\ge2$. The quantitative and spectral width theorems give additional complete-manifold bounds; they do not restrict that corollary to one dimension.
