\section*{Difference Sets Containing Squares}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Polynomial differences at prime arguments}: For a fixed integer polynomial with positive leading term and a unit root modulo every modulus, the paper claims a fixed power saving for sets avoiding its nonzero values at prime inputs as differences; both the exponent and constant may depend on the polynomial.

\textit{Degree-uniform intersective differences}: For an intersective polynomial with a positive leading term and degree $k\ge2$, the claimed bound for difference-avoiding subsets is $O_h(N^{1-c_k})$; the exponent depends only on $k$, although the implicit constant depends on the polynomial.

\textit{Square-difference-free power saving}: The manuscript claims absolute positive constants $c,C$ with $|A|\le C N^{1-c}$ whenever $A\subseteq[1,N]$ has no nonzero square difference; the improvement is a fixed power of $N$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} An integer polynomial $h$ is prime-intersective if each positive modulus $q$ has a root $r$ with $\gcd(r,q)=1$ and $h(r)\equiv0\pmod q$. If $\deg h\ge2$ and its leading coefficient is positive, then the claim supplies $c_h>0,C_h\ge1$ such that \[(A-A)\cap\{h(p):p\text{ prime}\}\subseteq\{0\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|A|\le C_hN^{1-c_h}\] for every integer $N\ge1$ and $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Both constants may depend on the polynomial.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} For each degree $k\ge2$ there is $0<c_k<1$ such that every intersective $h\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of degree $k$ with positive leading coefficient has constants $C_h\ge1,N_h$ satisfying \[(A-A)\cap h(\mathbb N_{>0})\subseteq\{0\},\quad A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\},\quad N\ge N_h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|A|\le C_hN^{1-c_k}.\] Intersective means having a root modulo every positive integer. The saving exponent depends only on the degree; the prefactor and threshold may depend on all coefficients.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 3.} The claimed square-difference bound gives absolute $c>0,C<\infty$ with \[|A|\le CN^{1-c}\] for every $N\ge1$ and $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ having no distinct $a,b$ with $|a-b|=m^2$ for any positive integer $m$. Equivalently every larger subset contains a nonzero square difference.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Polynomial differences at prime arguments}: The progression-compatible polynomial-difference method is adapted to a distribution supported on prime arguments. Root normalizations retain the required coprimality through successive arithmetic progressions. Prime-distribution estimates from the companion Dirichlet zero-free result supply the local comparison needed to contract the density-dominating multilinear quantity.

\textit{Degree-uniform intersective differences}: Local roots are normalized until their first nonzero Taylor coefficients are units, without changing their multiplicities. Auxiliary polynomials are chosen to remain compatible under passage to progressions. A multilinear density lower bound and a distribution-comparison argument then produce contraction with numerical choices controlled by the degree rather than the coefficients.

\textit{Square-difference-free power saving}: A probability law on tuples of residues over many primes defines a nonnegative multilinear quantity dominating density. A rational Fourier lift places the indicator into this residue model. Comparison with shorter intervals works for every residue choice, and a positive fraction of negligible choices makes the quantity contract by a fixed power.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Polynomial differences at prime arguments}: The polynomial h(x)=(x-1)\textasciicircum{}2 is prime-intersective (the root one is coprime to every modulus). A set avoiding all square differences avoids h(p) for primes, so the theorem gives the recorded power saving, with positive exponent depending only on this fixed polynomial.

\textit{Degree-uniform intersective differences}: Taking the intersective polynomial h(x)=x\textasciicircum{}2 gives the required power saving for square-difference-free sets; the theorem treats every intersective polynomial and permits a degree-only exponent.

\textit{Square-difference-free power saving}: The square paper gives |A|<=C N\textasciicircum{}(1-c) when no nonzero square difference occurs. The general paper specializes to h(t)=t\textasciicircum{}2; the prime companion specializes to h(t)=(t-1)\textasciicircum{}2, which has the unit root 1 modulo every q and forbids only the squares (p-1)\textasciicircum{}2, hence also applies to square-difference-free A. In each case constants can be absorbed by decreasing the positive exponent for sufficiently large N, meeting the on-site fixed power-size threshold.

The claimed result implies the stated question and also supplies the additional strength described above.
