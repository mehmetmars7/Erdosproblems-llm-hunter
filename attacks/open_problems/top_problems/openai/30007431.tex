\section*{The inverse-first-power universal integral-means spectrum}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Inverse first-power means}: OpenAI announces a universal growth exponent below $1/4$ for circular means of the inverse derivative of normalized injective holomorphic disk maps. Applied to bounded maps, this gives a strict inequality at the spectrum parameter $-1$, contrary to Kraetzer’s proposed value.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} Write $\mathcal S=\{f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C:\ f\text{ holomorphic and injective},\ f(0)=0,\ f^\prime(0)=1\}$ and $M_p(f,r)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^\prime(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta$. The claim is that some $0<\epsilon<1/4$ and finite $C$, independent of $f$ and $r$, satisfy \[M_{-1}(f,r)\le C(1-r)^{-1/4+\epsilon}\quad(f\in\mathcal S,\ 1/2\le r<1).\] In particular the bounded-image subclass has spectrum $B_b(-1)=\sup_f\limsup_{r\uparrow1}\log M_{-1}(f,r)/\log(1/(1-r))<1/4$. The theorem bounds this exponent; it does not determine its exact value.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Inverse first-power means}: Affine rebasings of upper-half-plane maps define a positive averaging operator. Hypothetically excessive growth produces a scale-compatible weighted probability law and identities for logarithmic-derivative moments. Critical points of a target potential are paired geometrically; their signed Jacobians have an incompatible expected sign, and the equality case is separately eliminated.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The uniform estimate on all normalized univalent maps implies $B_b(-1)<1/4$ for bounded maps. Affine normalization changes an integral mean only by a constant factor, leaving its limiting growth exponent unchanged, so the disk-to-disk equality $B_b(-1)=1/4$ in this question is claimed false.
