\section*{Barker-sequence length conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Circulant Hadamard orders and Barker lengths}: The manuscript claims that real circulant Hadamard matrices occur only at orders one and four. Its further consequence restricts Barker sequences longer than one to the seven lengths $2,3,4,5,7,11,13$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} A real circulant sign matrix $H\in\{-1,1\}^{n\times n}$ with $HH^{\mathsf T}=nI$ is claimed to exist exactly for $n\in\{1,4\}$. The Barker corollary gives an aperiodic sign sequence $(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})$ with $|\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}a_ja_{j+k}|\le1$ for every $1\le k<n$, at an integer length $n>1$, exactly when \[n\in\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}.\] Thus the longer-length impossibility assertions are proved according to the manuscript; they are not counterexamples to those assertions.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Circulant Hadamard orders and Barker lengths}: The first row is written in an integral cyclic group ring, where orthogonality becomes a scalar norm equation. Cyclotomic coefficient divisibility first reduces the order to $4u^2$ with odd $u$. An alternating product of character evaluations over the prime components is then followed through descent, ruling out $u>1$; standard correlation reductions give the Barker consequence.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The Barker corollary classifies every length $n>1$, of either parity, as possible exactly for $2,3,4,5,7,11,13$. Hence its claimed result proves that every sign sequence of length greater than thirteen has a nonzero-shift aperiodic autocorrelation of magnitude greater than one.
