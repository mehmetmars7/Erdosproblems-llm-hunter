\section*{Thomas–Fermi asymptotics for growing atomic ionization number}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. Removing $m$ electrons from a full Coulomb atom is claimed to cost asymptotically $a_{\mathrm{TF}}m^{7/3}$ when $m\to\infty$ and $Z/m\to\infty$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} On the full two-spin-state antisymmetric continuum space, set
\[
 H_{Z,N}=\sum_i(-\Delta_i/2-Z/|x_i|)+\sum_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|^{-1},
 \quad E_Z(N)=\inf\sigma(H_{Z,N}),\quad I_m(Z)=E_Z(Z-m)-E_Z(Z).
\]
For integer \(Z>m\ge1\), the claimed joint asymptotic is
\[
 I_m(Z)/m^{7/3}\to a_{\mathrm{TF}}>0
 \qquad\text{whenever }m\to\infty,\ Z/m\to\infty.
\]
The same coefficient appears in the iterated upper and lower limits with \(Z\to\infty\) first at fixed \(m\), then \(m\to\infty\). It is the Thomas--Fermi ionization coefficient for kinetic normalization \(\tfrac3{10}(3\pi^2)^{2/3}\int\rho^{5/3}\), characterized by \(\lim_ZI_m^{\mathrm{TF}}(Z)=a_{\mathrm{TF}}m^{7/3}\). No fixed-\(m\) quantum limit is asserted.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Positive-price particle sectors and an exterior cutoff isolate the outer electrons. Conditional screening comparison continues a lower barrier, identifies the limiting field and charge, and then returns to fixed particle sectors to recover the Thomas--Fermi coefficient.
