\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.9}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{A smooth three-space immersion obstruction}: A smooth positive surface metric is claimed to have no isometric immersion into $\mathbb R^3$ on any neighborhood of the origin. All its derivatives at the origin nevertheless agree with the Euclidean metric.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} There exists a positive-definite smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $(-1,1)^2$ such that for every open neighborhood $U$ of $(0,0)$ there is no $C^\infty$ immersion $F:U\to\mathbb R^3$ satisfying \[F^*(dx_1^2+dx_2^2+dx_3^2)=g|_U.\] Consequently smooth local isometric embeddings also fail for this metric.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{A smooth three-space immersion obstruction}: The necessary Darboux equation for an immersion height is obstructed on a sequence of small square patches. A candidate height with an appropriate Hessian component would violate the patch estimate. The metric is arranged to produce such a height from any hypothetical local immersion, while the patch amplitudes vanish fast enough to preserve smooth flatness at the origin.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{A smooth three-space immersion obstruction}: A smooth positive-definite metric with no immersion on any neighborhood of its origin also has no embedding there, exactly negating the universal local smooth embedding assertion. Local immersion/embedding equivalence is explicitly part of our definition.

The claimed counterexample lies within the question’s hypotheses and contradicts its universal conclusion.
