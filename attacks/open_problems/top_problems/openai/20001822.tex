\section*{Finite traceless-Ricci budget and Ricci-flat bubbles under bounded scalar curvature}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Scalar-bounded Ricci-flow singularity}: In sufficiently high dimension, the paper claims closed Ricci flows whose full curvature blows up at a finite maximal time even though scalar curvature stays bounded; in one fixed high dimension, arbitrarily large power-law blowup exponents are possible.

\textit{Four-dimensional Ricci-flow extension}: The paper claims smooth continuation of a Ricci flow on a closed four-manifold through any finite time at which scalar curvature stays uniformly bounded; the continuation occurs on the same manifold.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} There exist an integer $q\ge10$, $T>0$ for which a smooth solution of $\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}_g$ exists on $M=S^2\times S^{q+1}$, a connected closed manifold for $0\le t<T$ with \[\sup_{M\times[0,T)}|\operatorname{Scal}_{g(t)}|<\infty,\qquad\lim_{t\uparrow T}\max_M|\operatorname{Rm}_{g(t)}|=\infty.\] Its initial metric is smooth and $T$ is the finite maximal smooth existence time. This example is in dimension $q+3$, rather than four.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} On a closed connected smooth four-manifold consider a Ricci flow $g(t)$ whose initial metric and time evolution are smooth for $0\le t<T<\infty$. The claimed implication is \[\sup_{M\times[0,T)}|\operatorname{Scal}_{g(t)}|<\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists\epsilon>0:\ g(t)\text{ extends smoothly to }[0,T+\epsilon).\] In particular at a finite maximal four-dimensional existence time, $\limsup_{t\uparrow T}\max_M\operatorname{Scal}_{g(t)}=+\infty$. No corresponding extension conclusion in arbitrary dimension is asserted.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Scalar-bounded Ricci-flow singularity}: Two sphere factors are warped over an interval, with one factor collapsing at the endpoints and the other shrinking at the singular time. The parabolic scale approximates a Ricci-flat cone, while a smaller cap scale controls smoothness at the pole. One-sided cone comparison, logarithmic-slope monotonicity, and scalar estimates keep scalar curvature bounded without suppressing the smaller-scale full-curvature blowup.

\textit{Four-dimensional Ricci-flow extension}: Outermost concentration scales produce buffered time patches and finite Ricci-flat bubble trees. Parabolic annular estimates and a time maximal function convert spacetime Ricci energy into the static weighted error of a companion elliptic theorem. Comparing positive functional variation with static smallness forces the root energy to vanish, contradicting the selected change of concentration scale.
