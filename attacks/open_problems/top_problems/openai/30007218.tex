\section*{Local noninteracting ensemble representation of Coulomb ground densities}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A three-electron two-nucleus molecule is claimed to have a density outside all noninteracting ground-state ensembles for a single real spin-independent local Kohn--Sham potential in the stated class.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Set \(D=10^{200}\), \(e_z=(0,0,1)\), and take the source's finitely specified positive integer charge \(Z\). For the three-electron Coulomb molecule
\[
 H_C=\sum_{i=1}^3\left(-\tfrac12\Delta_i-
 \frac Z{|r_i-(D/Z)e_z|}-\frac Z{|r_i+(D/Z)e_z|}\right)
 +\sum_{i<j}|r_i-r_j|^{-1},
\]
the source claims a normalized absolute ground state \(\Psi\) in the \(S_z=1/2\) sector. For every real scalar local potential \(v\in L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\), the noninteracting Hamiltonian \(H_s(v)=\sum_i(-\Delta_i/2+v(r_i))\) has no trace-one ground-space ensemble \(\Gamma\) with
\[
 n_\Gamma=n_\Psi\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
The densities are spin-summed, with integral three, and the same scalar potential acts on both spin components.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
A controlled small-coupling Coulomb limit isolates the two lowest spatial orbitals. Density and occupation constraints forced by a hypothetical noninteracting representation conflict with the interacting ground-state structure; exact finite charge selection makes the obstruction a genuine finite molecule.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The explicit three-electron, two-equal-nucleus S\_z=1/2 Coulomb density admits no local noninteracting ground ensemble in the admissible class V. This is an admissible counterexample to the universal representability question. Within these assumptions, this is a claimed negative answer to the stated question.
