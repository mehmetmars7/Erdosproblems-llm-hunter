\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.2}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A specific Artin group on 116 generators is claimed to have no proper cocompact isometric action on a proper CAT(0) space; A hyperbolic group with a finite classifying complex is claimed to admit no proper and cocompact isometric action with proper complete CAT(0) target. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} The source constructs a finite, connected, aspherical simplicial complex \(K\) of dimension two. In its universal cover, a length-\(\ell\) combinatorial loop admits a triangular disk diagram with at most \(C\ell\) faces, for one finite constant \(C\). Nevertheless,
\[
 \pi_1K\text{ has no proper cocompact isometric action on a proper complete CAT}(0)\text{ space}.
\]
The linear filling estimate makes \(\pi_1K\) word-hyperbolic. Within the homotopy class of \(K\), no simplicial complex with finitely many simplices admits a geodesic length metric compatible with its topology and locally CAT(0); the claim also excludes such a locally CAT(-1) metric.

\paragraph{Result 2.} For an Artin matrix \(M\) on 116 generators with off-diagonal entries only in \(\{2,3,\infty\}\), the constructed Artin group \(A_M\) is claimed to have no action that is simultaneously isometric, proper, and cocompact on any nonempty proper CAT(0) metric space. In quantified form,
\[
 \nexists X,\ A_M\curvearrowright X:\quad
 X\text{ proper CAT}(0),\quad\text{action proper and cocompact}.
\]
The metric space is required to be proper, and the exclusion is independent of dimension.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Stable translation lengths and centralizer characters impose quantitative braid constraints. Six asymptotic sectors and labeled triangular diagrams yield a local winding count incompatible with those constraints; a finite Artin matrix realizes the contradictory configuration.

Nested incidence tables produce finite relations with a linear disk-filling bound. Conditional sampling creates a limiting configuration of controlled energy, and its geometric obstruction contradicts any proposed CAT(0) action; the finite aspherical model preserves the topological finiteness claim.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Combined with its Salvetti model the example obstructs proper cocompact actions on proper targets only. The record explicitly permits noncocompact actions and nonproper targets, so it is not a full refutation. The example has a finite two-dimensional K(G,1), but the obstruction concerns only geometric (proper cocompact) actions on proper complete targets. The record explicitly permits a larger class of actions and spaces. This entry records partial support; complete coverage of the question is not established by this catalogue.

\subsection{Formal verification}
For family 254, the scope includes finite-rank Salvetti contractibility, the 116-generator Artin CAT(0) counterexample, and arbitrary parabolic intersections with closure consequences. Each has its own Comparator statement. The listed Comparator files are \texttt{ArtinCAT0.lean}, \texttt{ArtinParabolicIntersections.lean}, \texttt{HarmonicArtin.lean}. Named declarations include \texttt{OAI.ArtinCAT0.main}, \texttt{OAI.HarmonicArtin.salvetti\_cover\_contractible}. For family 257, the documented construction gives a finite aspherical hyperbolic group model without a geometric proper-complete CAT(0) action. The stronger dimension-two and all locally CAT(0) model claims are excluded. The listed Comparator files are \texttt{HyperbolicObstruction.lean}. Named declarations include \texttt{OAI.HyperbolicObstruction.main}. This site has not executed the Lean code. The scope descriptions do not certify the remaining manuscript arguments.

\subsection{Status caveat}
OpenAI attributes these manuscripts to work by an internal model. Its release cautions that arguments without formalization can contain mistakes. This catalogue has not independently checked the proofs; the labels report the mathematical claims attributed to OpenAI.
