\section*{Stable Coordinate Conjecture for Complex Polynomials}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Manuscript 1 (family 047, 2026-09-23): An explicit complex affine fourfold is claimed to become affine five-space after one stabilization while itself failing to be affine four-space, with stable-coordinate and nontrivial affine-fibration consequences. Manuscript 2 (family 049, 2026-10-05): A degree-five polynomial in four complex variables is claimed to become a coordinate after adjoining one variable while failing to be a coordinate originally, despite every fiber being affine three-space.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} In $P=\mathbb C[p,s,u,F,J]$, define
\[
x=s^2+u^3+p^2F,\qquad H=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-p^2J^2-pu,\qquad A=P/(H).
\]
OpenAI claims that $A$ is integral of dimension four and for an independent variable $w$,
\[
A[w]\simeq\mathbb C^{[5]},\qquad A\not\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}.
\]
The notation $\mathbb C^{[j]}$ denotes a polynomial algebra in $j$ variables. It also claims that $H$ is a coordinate of $P[w]$ but not of $P$, where coordinate means an element belonging to a polynomial coordinate system. These assertions give one-variable cancellation and stable-coordinate counterexamples in the indicated dimensions.
Writing $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, the affine-fibration corollary claims that
\[
\pi:X\to\mathbb A^1,\quad x\mapsto p(x),\qquad
\rho:\mathbb A^5\to\mathbb A^2,\quad z\mapsto(p(z),H(z))
\]
are smooth and surjective with relative dimension three; every fiber over a base point $y$ is $\mathbb A^3_{\kappa(y)}$, yet neither map is locally a product with $\mathbb A^3$ in the Zariski topology. Thus the examples satisfy faithful flatness and geometric integrality of the fibers.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} In $R=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, set
\[
Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3,\qquad f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr).
\]
OpenAI claims a complex-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, and no such automorphism of $R$. It further claims that every hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)$, $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, is isomorphic to affine three-space but its embedding in affine four-space cannot be straightened to a coordinate hyperplane by a polynomial automorphism.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 047, 2026-09-23): A locally nilpotent derivation and an explicit inverse polynomial change give the cylinder isomorphism. To obstruct cancellation, a valuation along the exceptional parameter computes the associated graded affine modification; the resulting rigidity distinguishes the original coordinate ring from a polynomial algebra.

Manuscript 2 (family 049, 2026-10-05): Two hypersurface presentations and determinant-preserving matrix changes identify the polynomial ring and the distinguished parameter. An exponential of a locally nilpotent derivation gives the stabilized coordinates, while a parameter filtration and the rigidity of its associated bundle obstruct an unstabilized coordinate system.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Manuscript 1 (family 047, 2026-09-23): The primary manuscript's explicit stable-coordinate corollary gives a five-variable complex polynomial which becomes a coordinate after one new variable but was not a coordinate before it. That single instance disproves the problem all-n stable-coordinate implication.

Manuscript 2 (family 049, 2026-10-05): The main theorem gives a polynomial in four complex variables which an automorphism sends to x1 after adjoining one variable, but no original-ring automorphism does so. This is precisely a stable-coordinate counterexample.
