\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.109}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Brennan integrability}: The announced integrability range for a conformal map onto the disk is $4/3<s<4$ for the area integral of the $s$th derivative power. The paper also identifies the inverse-square integral-means exponent for the schlicht class as one.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every $\epsilon>0$ there is a finite $C_\epsilon$ such that all injective holomorphic $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$ and $f^\prime(0)=1$ satisfy \[\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^\prime(re^{i\theta})|^{-2}\,d\theta\le C_\epsilon(1-r)^{-1-\epsilon}\quad(1/2\le r<1).\] The supremum over these maps of the limiting logarithmic growth exponent is exactly $1$. If $W\subset\mathbb C$ is simply connected, its spherical boundary has at least two points, and $\varphi:W\to\mathbb D$ is conformal and bijective, then \[\int_W|\varphi^\prime|^s\,dA<\infty\quad(4/3<s<4).\] Equivalently $\int_{\mathbb D}|f^\prime|^t\,dA<\infty$ for every injective holomorphic $f$ and $-2<t<2/3$. These are strict intervals, with no endpoint-integrability assertion. The sharp-endpoint corollary exhibits the Koebe map $k(z)=z/(1-z)^2$, for which $\int_{\mathbb D}|k^\prime|^{-2}\,dA=\int_{\mathbb D}|k^\prime|^{2/3}\,dA=\infty$, so both Brennan endpoints fail for its inverse.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Brennan integrability}: The proof represents inverse-square derivative growth by a positive operator acting on normalized half-plane maps. An eigenmeasure is averaged over translations and scales to produce a weighted rebasing law. Moment identities from this law are compared with a pairing of critical points of target potentials, ruling out any growth exponent larger than one; change of variables then gives the stated derivative integrability.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Brennan integrability}: The manuscript’s result bounds M\_\{-2\} by C\_epsilon(1-r)\textasciicircum{}(-1-epsilon); Hayman 6.109 asks I\_\{-2\}=sqrt(M\_\{-2\})=O((1-r)\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)) without any epsilon loss. The exponent is reached only up to loss and the full no-loss bound is not proved.
