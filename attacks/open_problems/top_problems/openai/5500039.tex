\section*{Distances among Point Sets in \$\textbackslash{}mathbb\{R\}\textasciicircum{}2\$ and \$\textbackslash{}mathbb\{R\}\textasciicircum{}3\$}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Planar unit-distance power saving}: The unit-distance manuscript claims an absolute exponent $\beta<4/3$ bounding the number of unordered unit pairs by $C n^\beta$; the point configuration is otherwise arbitrary.

\textit{Higher-dimensional distinct distances}: For fixed $d\ge3$, the claimed distance lower bound for $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb R^d$ is $c_d n^{2/d}$; the constant depends only on dimension, with no vanishing subpower factor.

\textit{Almost all planar pins}: For any fixed positive $\varepsilon$, the claimed number of pins determining fewer than $n^{1-\varepsilon}$ nonzero distances is $o(n)$ uniformly over planar $n$-point sets; this is an almost-all-pins conclusion rather than an exact linear lower bound at every pin.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Planar unit-distance power saving}: A near-extremal incidence sequence is transferred to algebraic configurations and analyzed using distance identities over a negligible-height coefficient field. After differentiating those identities, valuations distinguish the individual radicands and allow independent square-root sign changes. One unused sign change contradicts the differentiated system; a separate exclusion of popular unit circles handles the possible square-class failure, preventing approach to exponent $4/3$.

\textit{Higher-dimensional distinct distances}: Polynomial interpolation and Hilbert-function estimates track concentration at several degree scales. Geometry of skew-form evaluation flats gives sparse-cone and rich-motion bounds, with exceptional algebraic families treated explicitly. These incidence estimates are combined with the distance reduction in a way that retains a fixed multiplicative constant rather than only the exponent up to a small loss.

\textit{Almost all planar pins}: An extremal pair graph is extracted from large distance fibers while retaining nonconcentration within those fibers. Real-closed-field transfer moves finite configurations into number fields without altering their distance relations. Factorization into two complex coordinates and the product formula balance local closeness partitions across absolute values, giving an incompatibility with the assumed abundance of large fibers.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Planar unit-distance power saving}: The bound u(n)<=C n\textasciicircum{}beta with beta<4/3 is an improved planar upper bound, not the conjectured n\textasciicircum{}(1+o(1)) order, an exact extremal value, or the other bundled dimension/distance questions.

\textit{Higher-dimensional distinct distances}: The lower bound c\_3 n\textasciicircum{}(2/3), together with the grid upper bound, settles the order of the three-dimensional distinct-distance minimum; the planar distinct-distance and unit-distance maximum clauses remain open.

\textit{Almost all planar pins}: The theorem supplies n\textasciicircum{}(1-epsilon) distinct distances from almost every planar pin and therefore globally, but not the sharp planar order or the other three-dimensional and unit-distance clauses.
