\section*{Maker winning strategy for Snaky on the infinite square lattice}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Snaky within 21 Maker claims}: The paper claims a Maker strategy for Snaky using at most 21 Maker moves against every legal Breaker response. It can operate on a fixed 251-cell region inside a $17\times17$ square.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} On the initially empty board $\mathbb Z^2$, Maker moves first and each player then claims one unowned cell per turn. For \[S=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(3,1),(4,1)\},\] targets are $t+RS$ with $t\in\mathbb Z^2$ and $R$ a signed permutation matrix, so rotations and reflections but no scaling are allowed. The claim supplies one globally legal Maker policy which, against every legal Breaker continuation, owns an entire target within at most $21$ Maker claims. It therefore asserts a winning strategy for the ordinary unbiased Snaky game.
