\section*{GPY lower bounds and distances between Gaussian primes}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The selected manuscript concerns walks through Gaussian primes with a fixed bound on each step. Fix $D<\infty$. Put an edge between distinct Gaussian primes $z,z'$ when $|z-z'|\le D$. OpenAI asserts a bound $B_D$, depending only on $D$, for the cardinality of each connected component. An infinite path of distinct primes with that step bound is consequently excluded. This is related to Gaussian-prime spacing, but is not a claimed GPY tuple-count estimate or a construction of close prime pairs.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For $D<\infty$, form $G_D$ on all Gaussian primes in $\mathbb Z[i]$, joining distinct vertices when their Euclidean distance is at most $D$. The claimed uniform bound is
\[
 \forall D<\infty\ \exists B_D<\infty\ \forall\text{ connected components }C\subseteq G_D:\quad |C|\le B_D.
\]
Axis primes are included. Consequently a sequence of distinct Gaussian primes with every successive distance at most $D$ has at most $B_D$ terms; the bound does not depend on its starting point.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 028; 2026-09-26), geometric sampling of a hypothetical long walk produces many residue differences. Shared continuation data create an entropy budget incompatible with avoiding zero residues, yielding a finite periodic sieve barrier and hence the uniform component bound.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The problem asks for an asymptotic or correct-order GPY count of prime tuples and for an adaptation that produces small distances between Gaussian primes. A bound on the sizes of bounded-step components does not give either count or adaptation: infinitely many close pairs can lie in distinct finite components. The manuscript addresses infinite walks, which are not a clause of this question. Its result is related background rather than a partial solution of the displayed problem.
