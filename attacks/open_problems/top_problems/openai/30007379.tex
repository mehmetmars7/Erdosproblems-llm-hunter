\section*{The exact low-temperature transfer gap of the planar O(4) model}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: The proposed large-$\beta$ $O(4)$ mass is $32e^{\pi/4-1/2}\sqrt\beta e^{-\pi\beta}(1+o(1))$ in original lattice time units; The full square-lattice $O(4)$ transfer gap is claimed to be positive at every finite coupling and comparable to $\sqrt\beta e^{-\pi\beta}$ at large $\beta$. Together they give a claimed affirmative answer in the matching scope.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} For the nearest-neighbor square-lattice \(O(4)\) model, spins lie in \(S^3\) and the periodic Gibbs density is proportional to \(\exp(\beta\sum_{\langle xy\rangle}\sigma_x\cdot\sigma_y)\), each bond counted once. In the periodic local limit, let \(T_\beta\) be the full Osterwalder--Schrader one-time-step transfer operator and \(\Omega_\beta\) its constant-function vacuum. Define
\[
 m_{\mathrm{lat}}(\beta)=-\log\|T_\beta|_{\Omega_\beta^\perp}\|.
\]
The exact claimed asymptotic is
\[
 \lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{e^{\pi\beta}}{\sqrt\beta}m_{\mathrm{lat}}(\beta)
 =32e^{\pi/4-1/2}.
\]
The gap includes every sector generated by local observables, not only spin correlations.

\paragraph{Result 2.} For the homogeneous nearest-neighbor square-lattice \(O(4)\) model, the source claims a unique periodic local limit for sufficiently large \(\beta\), and full transfer gap
\[
 c\sqrt\beta\,e^{-\pi\beta}\le\operatorname{gap}(\beta)
 \le C\sqrt\beta\,e^{-\pi\beta},\qquad\beta\ge\beta_0,
\]
with \(0<c<C<\infty\). Fixed dyadic \(L\), terminal coupling \(H\), and integers \(N_0,M_0\) give couplings \(\beta_N\) whose exact \(N\)-step transform ends at \(H\), satisfying
\[
 \frac{\log2}{M_0L^N}\le\operatorname{gap}(\beta_N)\le\frac{\log8}{L^N}\quad(N\ge N_0).
\]
The preliminary result uses \(\Xi(\beta)=\exp[\pi\beta+C\sqrt{(1+\beta)\log(2+\beta)}]\). For unpinned free subgraphs, or rectangular tori with sides at least \(C\Xi\), couplings \(0\le\beta_e\le\beta\), and local Lipschitz observables at distance \(d\), it bounds covariance by \(C(1+\beta+|A|+|A'|+d)^{20}L_FL_Ge^{-cd/\Xi}\). It also gives
\[
 0\le4\log Z_\beta(n,n)-\log Z_\beta(2n,2n)
 \le C(1+\beta)^pn^p e^{-cn/\Xi(\beta)}
\]
for even \(n\ge C\Xi(\beta)\). Spins lie in $S^3$ with normalized spherical measure, and the Gibbs density is proportional to $\exp(\sum_e\beta_e\sigma_x\cdot\sigma_y)$. Reflection positivity of the periodic limiting state supplies a vacuum $\Omega$ and positive one-step translation contraction $T_\beta$, with $T_\beta\Omega=\Omega$. The gap convention is
\[
 \operatorname{gap}(\beta)=-\log\|T_\beta|_{\Omega^\perp}\|.
\]
For a local observable $F$ with support $A$, define $L_F=\|F\|_\infty+\max_{x\in A}\operatorname{Lip}_x(F)$, using round-sphere distance in that coordinate while all others are held fixed. The separation $d$ is maximum-norm distance between supports, with periodic distance on tori. The preliminary estimates hold for every $\beta\ge0$; $Z_\beta(n,w)$ is the homogeneous torus partition function. The constants $L,H,N_0,M_0$ above are selected once, with $H$ sufficiently large.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Odd and even observable sectors express the full gap through ratios of twisted partition functions. A Brownian regulator permits an exact auxiliary spectral calculation, and regulator comparison transports its leading constant back to the square-lattice model.

Quaternionic rotation and current identities drive the scale-by-scale stiffness decrease. A massive block expansion provides two-sided gap bounds, and comparison of polynomial local-observable sectors ensures the spin estimate controls the entire transfer gap.
