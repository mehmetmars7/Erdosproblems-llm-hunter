\section*{Parabolic subgroup intersections in Artin groups, including Euclidean type}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a mathematical result relevant to this question in the selected manuscript. Every intersection, including an infinite intersection, of parabolic subgroups in a finite-rank Artin group is claimed parabolic.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(A_M\) be the Artin group of a finite Coxeter matrix on \(S\); \(A_X\) denotes its standard parabolic subgroup for \(X\subseteq S\). For arbitrary \(X,Y\subseteq S\) and \(g,h\in A_M\), the claim is
\[
 \exists Z\subseteq S,\ k\in A_M:\qquad
 gA_Xg^{-1}\cap hA_Yh^{-1}=kA_Zk^{-1}.
\]
Every family of parabolics has the same intersection as some subfamily of size at most \(|S|\), so arbitrary intersections are parabolic and every subset has a least parabolic closure. The algorithmic corollary takes \(M,X\) and words \(u,v\) and decides
\[
 v\in uA_Xu^{-1};
\]
choosing \(X=\varnothing\) yields a uniform word-problem algorithm. If the diagram is irreducible and has an infinite label, the additional corollary gives acylindrical hyperbolicity and, for every proper parabolic \(P\), a \(g\) with \(P\cap gPg^{-1}=\{1\}\).

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Relative categorical layers track positive divisors and caps. Two-point uniqueness and a relative frame control null layers, while harmonic heights produce connected minima and bounded positive-height representatives; these give the parabolic carrier of the intersection.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The all-finite-Coxeter-matrix theorem makes intersections of arbitrary families of parabolic subgroups parabolic. Affine Artin groups are included, so its conclusion covers the Euclidean case and is stronger than the requested pairwise statement.
