\section*{Nuclear Dimension after Jiang–Su Stabilization}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Finite nuclear dimension is claimed to characterize Jiang--Su stability for separable nuclear algebras lacking elementary ideal subquotients; moreover, Jiang--Su stabilization always lowers nuclear dimension to at most one. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For a separable nuclear complex $C^*$-algebra $A$ with no ideal subquotient that is nonzero and elementary $I/J\simeq\mathcal K(H)$, OpenAI claims
\[
\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}A<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A\simeq A\otimes_{\min}\mathcal Z\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}A\le1.
\]
Nuclear dimension is defined by finite-dimensional completely positive approximations with order-zero color decomposition; $\mathcal Z$ is the Jiang--Su algebra. The conditions are also claimed equivalent to either a canonical or an abstract isomorphism of $\operatorname{Cu}(A)$ with $\operatorname{Cu}(A\otimes_{\min}\mathcal Z)$. Without the subquotient restriction it asserts $\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}(A_0\otimes_{\min}\mathcal Z)\le1$ for every separable nuclear $A_0$. Separately, for every nonzero unital characterless $D$ it claims a finite maximal tensor power containing two full orthogonal positive elements; full means generating the whole algebra as a closed ideal.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 291, 2026-09-23): Localized order-zero approximations control ranks even when traces are infinite on some ideals. A supporting map and comparison yield two-color approximations that survive stabilization and hereditary passage. Conversely, Gaussian sign tensors and pure-state excision construct two full orthogonal central elements from the absence of characters, supplying the absorption criterion.
