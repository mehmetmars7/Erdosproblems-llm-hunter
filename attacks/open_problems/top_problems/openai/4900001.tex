\section*{Arnold and Arnold–Givental conjectures}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Degenerate Arnold fixed-point obstruction}: A Hamiltonian map of the complex quadric threefold is claimed to have exactly three fixed points although its critical number and rational cup-length lower bound are four; at least one fixed point is degenerate; additionally, \textit{Ordinary Morse-number obstruction}: The paper claims nondegenerate Hamiltonian maps on closed twelve-dimensional symplectic manifolds with fewer fixed points than their ordinary Morse numbers; the shortfall can be made arbitrarily large and all associated orbit loops are contractible.

\textit{Stable Morse obstruction from torsion}: The first stable-Morse companion claims a simply connected closed Kähler example with sixteen fewer nondegenerate Hamiltonian fixed points than its stable Morse number; every associated orbit is contractible.

\textit{Stable Morse deficits in dimension twenty-two}: In fixed real dimension 22, the paper claims simply connected Kähler examples with stable Morse number $80+1968m$ and exactly $80+1952m$ nondegenerate Hamiltonian fixed points for each $m\ge1$; hence the deficit is unbounded.

\textit{Sharp cyclic Floer count below stable Morse}: The manuscript claims a simply connected Kähler manifold of dimension 3332 with 1,872,232 nondegenerate Hamiltonian fixed points and stable Morse number 1,872,264; its minimal Chern number is one, and the cyclic integral Floer lower estimate is exactly attained by that count.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Degenerate Arnold fixed-point obstruction}: A finite-order Hamiltonian map is perturbed by the flow of an invariant extension of a function on its fixed locus. A power identity excludes new fixed points elsewhere, and symmetric criticality identifies the remaining fixed points with critical points on that locus. A suitably chosen function on the quadric surface then supplies the three-point count. \textit{Ordinary Morse-number obstruction}: Finite groups with increasing group-generator number but bounded augmentation-ideal generation create a gap between ordinary and stabilized Morse counts. A relative twelve-dimensional handle construction realizes a small critical-point model while preserving quadratic collars. Local Hamiltonian realization on a closed product transfers that reduced count to nondegenerate fixed points.

\textit{Stable Morse obstruction from torsion}: Integral homology at two distinct primes gives a stable-Morse lower bound sensitive to the degree-wise torsion generator counts. A Hamiltonian involution on a blown-up Kähler surface has four fixed components. An invariant small perturbation realizes Morse functions on those components, and the resulting product count lies below the stable-Morse benchmark. \textit{Stable Morse deficits in dimension twenty-two}: Torsion of different prime orders is distributed across degrees so that taking products changes the maximum generator multiplicity. Blowups and product constructions realize the needed homology while keeping the dimension fixed. An involution with controlled fixed components and an invariant Morse perturbation give the explicit stable-Morse and Hamiltonian counts.

\textit{Sharp cyclic Floer count below stable Morse}: The construction distinguishes degree-wise integral torsion counts from their cyclic reduction modulo twice the Chern number. Complex blowups and a Hamiltonian finite-order action realize the desired topology and fixed components. A controlled invariant perturbation computes the nondegenerate count exactly, showing that equality in the cyclic bound can coexist with a stable-Morse deficit.
