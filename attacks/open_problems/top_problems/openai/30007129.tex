\section*{Worst-case symmetric translative covering density}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 092; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that in every sufficiently large dimension, some symmetric convex body has translative covering density at least an absolute multiple of $n\log n$, ruling out a uniform linear bound.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$, define its translative covering density by
\[
 \theta_T(K)=\inf_{\substack{X\subset\mathbb R^n\text{ locally finite}\\K+X=\mathbb R^n}}
 |K|\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{|X\cap[-R,R]^n|}{(2R)^n}.
\]
The covering centers are not required to form a lattice. The claimed lower bound is
\[
 \exists c>0\ \exists n_0\ \forall n\ge n_0\ \exists K_n=-K_n:
 \quad\theta_T(K_n)>c n\log n,
\]
with $c,n_0$ absolute and every $K_n$ a full-dimensional convex body.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 092; 2026-09-23), a random body is tested against a finite family obtained by rounding periodic covering patterns. Cap-intersection and Poisson-witness estimates make successful low-density coverage too unlikely for any pattern; periodization extends the obstruction to all coverings.
