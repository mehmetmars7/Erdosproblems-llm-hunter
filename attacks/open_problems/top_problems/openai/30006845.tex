\section*{Secret key from all entangled states}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A bipartite state with local dimension ten is claimed entangled yet to have zero distillable private key under the specified unrestricted-copy, two-way local-instrument protocols.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} An explicitly specified entangled state \(\rho\) on \(\mathbb C^{10}\otimes\mathbb C^{10}\) is claimed to have no nonzero product vector in its range and distillable key \(K_D(\rho)=0\). For every \(n\ge1\) and admitted completed local/public-classical bit-output protocol on \(\rho^{\otimes n}\), let \(\tau_{K_AK_BE'}\) be its normalized output, retaining the purification and full public transcript in \(E'\). The quantitative claim is
\[
 \left\|\tau-\tfrac12\sum_{i=0}^1|i,i\rangle\langle i,i|\otimes\sigma_{E'}\right\|_1\ge1/5
 \quad\text{for every state }\sigma_{E'}.
\]
Protocols use no initially shared private resource beyond the input; local auxiliaries and private randomness are allowed, and countable completed processes obey the source's reference-compatibility conditions. The conventional halved trace distance is therefore at least \(1/10\).

\subsection{Outline of the argument}

This refutes the universal positive-key assertion by a single state. Within these assumptions, this is a claimed negative answer to the stated question.
