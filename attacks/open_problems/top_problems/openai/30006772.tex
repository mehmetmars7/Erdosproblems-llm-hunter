\section*{Arnold Conjecture on Hamiltonian Fixed Points}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Degenerate Arnold fixed-point obstruction}: A Hamiltonian map of the complex quadric threefold is claimed to have exactly three fixed points although its critical number and rational cup-length lower bound are four. At least one fixed point is degenerate.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For the complex quadric $Q^3=\{[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^4:\sum_{i=0}^4z_i^2=0\}$ with restricted Fubini--Study symplectic form, the claim constructs a smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphism $\phi$ with \[\#\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)=3<4=\operatorname{Crit}(Q^3)=\operatorname{cuplength}(Q^3;\mathbb Q).\] At least one fixed point is degenerate. Here $\operatorname{Crit}$ minimizes over all smooth functions, including degenerate ones; the cup-length convention is the longest nonvanishing product of positive-degree classes, with one added to that count.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Degenerate Arnold fixed-point obstruction}: A finite-order Hamiltonian map is perturbed by the flow of an invariant extension of a function on its fixed locus. A power identity excludes new fixed points elsewhere, and symmetric criticality identifies the remaining fixed points with critical points on that locus. A suitably chosen function on the quadric surface then supplies the three-point count.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Degenerate Arnold fixed-point obstruction}: The quadric is a closed connected symplectic six-manifold and has exactly three fixed points while Crit equals four for arbitrary smooth functions. Degenerate fixed points are permitted by the precise site definition, so this is a direct counterexample.

The claimed counterexample lies within the question’s hypotheses and contradicts its universal conclusion.
