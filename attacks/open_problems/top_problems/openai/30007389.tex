\section*{Critical SK mixing lower bounds from realized Gibbs states}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. At SK criticality, typical fixed equilibrium starts are claimed to remain far from equilibrium for every time $o(n^{2/3})$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} For independent standard Gaussian $J_{ij}$, $i<j$, with $J_{ji}=J_{ij},J_{ii}=0$, use the zero-field SK measure
\[
 \pi_{n,\beta}^J(x)=Z^{-1}\exp\left(\frac\beta{\sqrt n}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}x_ix_j\right),\qquad x\in\{-1,1\}^n.
\]
Continuous heat-bath dynamics refreshes every site at rate one; a discrete step refreshes one uniformly selected site, including attempts that retain its spin. At $\beta=1$, let $d_t^{\mathrm{ct}}(J,x)$ and $d_k^{\mathrm{dt}}(J,x)$ be the transition-law total-variation distances from $\pi$ for a fixed realized initial state $x$. Every deterministic sequence $t_n=o(n^{2/3})$ and integer sequence $k_n=o(n^{5/3})$ satisfy
\[
 \pi\{x:d_{t_n}^{\mathrm{ct}}(J,x)>1/4\}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1,\qquad
 \pi\{x:d_{k_n}^{\mathrm{dt}}(J,x)>1/4\}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1.
\]
Probability here concerns the Gaussian interactions; the Gibbs sample is fixed before evaluating its distance.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Edge-scale resolvent traces and Gaussian conditioning produce a slow critical projection of a Gibbs sample. Overlap and dynamical comparison estimates show that this projection cannot decorrelate on a smaller timescale, giving a total-variation obstruction for most sampled starting states.
