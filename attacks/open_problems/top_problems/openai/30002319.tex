\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a mathematical result relevant to this question in the selected manuscript. Uniform honeycomb walks of every sufficiently large length are claimed to have diameter scale $3/4$, while local counts and covers simultaneously have exponent $4/3$, with a polynomial failure bound.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(\gamma\) be uniform among length-\(n\) self-avoiding honeycomb-lattice walks from a fixed vertex, with unrestricted endpoint. Write \(D(\gamma)\) for Euclidean diameter, \(M_\gamma(z,s)\) for the number of visited vertices in the closed radius-\(s\) ball at \(z\), and \(\mathcal N_\gamma(s)\) for the minimum number of such balls covering the visited set. For every \(\delta,k>0\), the source supplies \(C_{\delta,k}<\infty\) and \(n_0\), so every \(n\ge n_0\) has an event of probability at least \(1-C_{\delta,k}n^{-k}\) on which
\[
 n^{3/4-\delta}\le D(\gamma)\le n^{3/4+\delta},
\]
\[
 n^{-\delta}\min\{n,s^{4/3}\}\le M_\gamma(z,s)\le n^\delta\min\{n,s^{4/3}\},
\]
\[
 n^{-\delta}(1+ns^{-4/3})\le\mathcal N_\gamma(s)\le n^\delta(1+ns^{-4/3}).
\]
The latter bounds hold simultaneously for all visited \(z\) and every real \(1\le s\le n\).
