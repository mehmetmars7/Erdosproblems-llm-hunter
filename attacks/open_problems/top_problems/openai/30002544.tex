\section*{Enumeration of Graphs with Prescribed Degree Sequences}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 131; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that the lazy simple-undirected switch chain mixes for every graphical degree sequence in at most $2n^8$ steps at distance $1/4$, with an exact expected-polynomial sampler.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
Let $d$ be any graphical degree vector on $[n]$, $n\ge4$, and $\Omega_d$ all simple labeled graphs with that degree vector. The chain stays with probability $1/2$; otherwise it uniformly chooses four vertices and one of six ordered pairs of different perfect matchings on them, replacing the first by the second only when its two edges are present and both new edges absent. With uniform stationary law $\pi_d$, the claimed bounds are
\[
 \min\{t:\max_G\|P_d^t(G,\cdot)-\pi_d\|_{\rm TV}\le1/4\}\le2n^8,
 \qquad\operatorname{gap}(P_d)\ge[24n^2\binom n4]^{-1}
\]
when $|\Omega_d|>1$. A further claimed sampler returns an exactly uniform graph from $\Omega_d$, using unbiased bits, terminating almost surely with expected bit cost polynomial in binary encoding length of $d$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 131; 2026-09-25), pair resampling operators are compared through triple and disjoint-pair variance estimates. A global operator inequality gives the spectral gap, and decomposition into individual switches transfers it to the specified lazy chain; exact residual correction yields uniform sampling.
