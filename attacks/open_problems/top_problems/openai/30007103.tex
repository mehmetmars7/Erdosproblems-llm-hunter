\section*{Sharp logarithmic exponents for fixed off-diagonal Ramsey numbers}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Ramsey logarithms for clique size at least six}: For every fixed $s\ge6$, the manuscript claims $r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}$; the separate parameter $s=5$ is treated by another manuscript.

\textit{The logarithmic exponent of r(5,t)}: The companion manuscript claims $r(5,t)=t^4/(\log t)^{3+o(1)}$; the lower estimate allows any arbitrarily small extra logarithmic power, while the matching upper estimate has power three.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every fixed integer $s\ge6$, the claimed bounds on the Ramsey number $r(s,t)$ are \[\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2+\epsilon}}\le r(s,t)\le C_s\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2}}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ t\ge t_0(s,\epsilon)).\] Here $r(s,t)$ is the least order forcing a clique of size $s$ or an independent set of size $t$. In particular $((s-1)\log t-\log r(s,t))/\log\log t\to s-2$. This paper covers $s\ge6$.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} The complementary fixed-clique-size result claims an absolute $C$ with \[\frac{t^4}{(\log t)^{3+\epsilon}}\le r(5,t)\le C\frac{t^4}{(\log t)^3}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ t\ge t_0(\epsilon)).\] Therefore $(4\log t-\log r(5,t))/\log\log t\to3$. Together with the separately cited $s\ge6$ manuscript, this covers every fixed $s\ge5$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Ramsey logarithms for clique size at least six}: Random incident point--hyperplane flags define an ordered graph in projective dimension $s-1$. Linear independence forbids the target clique, while independent sequences satisfy an asymmetric consistency rule. Sparse-incidence descriptions, validation samples, and dimensional projection give short encodings of such sequences, contradicting the entropy needed for a long independent set.

\textit{The logarithmic exponent of r(5,t)}: Random flags in projective four-space produce a graph without five-cliques. Two marking scans and a small set of representative flags bound the information in a purported long consistent sequence. Geometric incidence analysis controls exceptional subspace rectangles, causing its upper entropy bound to conflict with the entropy forced by occurrence in the original random stream.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The two selected manuscripts together cover the full fixed-size range $s\ge5$: one proves the displayed bounds for $s=5$, and the other for each $s\ge6$. The upper constant depends only on $s$, while the sufficiently-large-$t$ threshold may depend on $s$ and the positive lower-bound loss $\epsilon$.
