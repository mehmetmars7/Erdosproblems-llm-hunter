\section*{Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that zeta, Dirichlet functions, and finite-order Eisenstein-field Hecke families admit a zero-free region with boundary $11/12$; In manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), the asserted result is that zeta, Dirichlet $L$-functions, and finite-order Hecke $L$-functions for $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ have no zeros with real part greater than $7/8$, with poles excluded; In manuscript 3 (family 003; 2026-10-01), the asserted result is that an absolute positive constant bounds $(1-\beta)\log q$ away from zero for real zeros of primitive nonprincipal real Dirichlet characters of conductor $q\ge3$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05), twisted M\"obius sums are enlarged to a family before Poisson summation. A recursive estimate for the dual mean square, followed by inversion of cube factors, supplies the saving used to exclude a reciprocal-function singularity.

For manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), smoothed character sums are estimated in two ways: directly by large-sieve and recursive moment bounds, and through a Mellin transform involving the reciprocal of an $L$-function. Poisson summation and sextic residue characters align the two expressions so that their savings force continuation of the reciprocal.

For manuscript 3 (family 003; 2026-10-01), a hypothetical exceptional zero forces a pronounced prime bias. Interpolation on a projected integer box produces a weighted conjugate determinant whose Archimedean size conflicts with its divisibility at primes selected by that bias.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
This problem requires every nontrivial zero to have real part $1/2$. The two half-plane papers exclude zeros only beyond $7/8$ or $11/12$, leaving the intermediate strip untreated. The real-character result gives a conductor-dependent gap near one, rather than the critical-line assertion.
