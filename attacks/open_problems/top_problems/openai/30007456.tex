\section*{Memory–precision lower bounds for exact Gaussian regression}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that finite messages on a spherical image of a cube reveal at most order $dt$ information after $t$ Gaussian blocks of order $d$, even when nested spread-restoring cells are added; In manuscript 2 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that retaining at most $Ad^2$ bits between exact standard Gaussian measurements requires at least $c_A d\log(1/\varepsilon)$ observations for constant-probability angular accuracy on a uniform sphere; In manuscript 3 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that a finite message from exact Gaussian data has bounded conditional information relative to an independently revealed projection, yielding the stated low-memory precision lower bound; In manuscript 4 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that controlled local mass on the sphere gives projection-moment and positive-cap domination estimates, which imply the stated low-memory noiseless-regression sample lower bounds; In manuscript 5 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that replacing the Gaussian rows that selected a finite message with independent rows changes residual conditional information by at most order $d$ under the stated entropy and dimension bounds; In manuscript 6 (family 140; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that a uniform-sphere learner with $o(d^2)$ persistent bits needs at least $2^{-16}d\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$ exact Gaussian measurements for the stated success level at small angular error.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
Let $S$ be uniform on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^d$, independent of successive $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$, and observe exact labels $\langle x_t,S\rangle$. A jointly measurable one-pass learner has at most $2^{M(d)}$ persistent states, data-independent initial/shared randomness, fresh transition randomness, and prescribed finite horizon $T(d)$; discarded observations cannot be revisited, and terminal output uses only state, index and fresh randomness. If $M(d)=o(d^2)$ and angular accuracy $0<\varepsilon(d)\le1/10$ has success probability at least $2/3$, the claimed consequence is
\[
 T(d)\ge c d\log(1/\varepsilon(d))
\]
with one absolute $c>0$; it holds eventually along each allowed memory sequence, with a sequence-dependent dimension threshold. This corollary alone treats subquadratic memory, rather than every fixed multiple of $d^2$.

\paragraph{Manuscript 2.}
Let $S$ be uniform on $S^{d-1}$, independent of rows $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$ and the learner's data-independent randomness. A jointly measurable one-pass learner sees exact labels $\langle x_t,S\rangle$, has at most $2^M$ persistent states, uses only current observation/index and fresh randomness in transitions, cannot revisit discarded data, and stops by a prescribed deterministic finite horizon $T$. Its unit output depends only on terminal state, index and fresh randomness. The claim states
\[
 \forall A>0\ \exists c_A>0,d_A:\quad
 d\ge d_A,\ M\le Ad^2,\ 0<\varepsilon\le1/10,
 \Pr[\arccos\langle\widehat s,S\rangle\le\varepsilon]\ge2/3
 \ \Rightarrow\ T\ge c_A d\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]
The entire range of each fixed quadratic-memory budget is covered, not only $o(d^2)$.

\paragraph{Manuscript 3.}
In dimension $d$, take an independent uniformly distributed unit signal $S$, independent Gaussian rows $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$ and exact labels $\langle x_t,S\rangle$. A measurable one-pass learner retains at most $2^{M(d)}$ states, has data-independent shared/initial randomness, uses only the current row and label in transitions, and stops at a prescribed finite horizon $T(d)$; its terminal output cannot revisit observations. If $M(d)=o(d^2)$ and $0<\varepsilon(d)\le1/10$, the claimed implication is
\[
 \Pr[\arccos\langle\widehat S,S\rangle\le\varepsilon(d)]\ge2/3
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(d)\ge c d\log(1/\varepsilon(d))
\]
for absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $d$. Probability includes the signal, samples and all learner randomness, with a dimension threshold allowed to vary with the chosen memory sequence.

\paragraph{Manuscript 4.}
Let $S$ be uniform on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^d$, independent of exact Gaussian observations $(x_t,\langle x_t,S\rangle)$, $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$. The one-pass learner is jointly measurable, has at most $2^M$ persistent states, data-independent initial/shared randomness, fresh transition randomness and prescribed finite horizon $T$; output uses only terminal state, index and fresh randomness. The claimed absolute $c>0,d_0$ give
\[
 d\ge d_0,\ M\ge0,\ 0<\varepsilon\le1/10,\quad
 \Pr[\text{angular error}\le\varepsilon]\ge2/3
 \ \Rightarrow\quad T\ge\frac{c d\log(1/\varepsilon)}{1+M/d^2}.
\]
Every finite memory budget is allowed, and dependence on memory is explicit.

\paragraph{Manuscript 5.}
With a uniform spherical signal $S\in\mathbb R^d$ and independent exact observations $(x_t,\langle x_t,S\rangle)$ for $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$, consider measurable one-pass learners retaining at most $2^{M(d)}$ states and having data-independent initial/shared randomness and prescribed finite horizon $T(d)$. Discarded observations cannot be revisited; output uses terminal state and fresh randomness. For $M(d)=o(d^2)$ and $0<\varepsilon(d)\le1/10$, the claimed implication is
\[
 \Pr[\text{angular error}\le\varepsilon(d)]\ge3/5
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(d)\ge c d\log(1/\varepsilon(d))
\]
for absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $d$. The necessary dimension cutoff can vary with the chosen memory sequence, and success probability includes all learner randomness.

\paragraph{Manuscript 6.}
Consider the jointly measurable one-pass model with at most $2^{M(d)}$ persistent states, data-independent initial/shared randomness, exact independent rows $x_t\sim N(0,I_d)$ labeled $\langle x_t,S\rangle$, uniform independent unit signal $S$, and a prescribed deterministic finite horizon $T$. Discarded observations cannot be revisited, and terminal output is determined by state, index and fresh randomness. For every sequence $M(d)=o(d^2)$, a sufficiently large dimension threshold is claimed such that all $0<\varepsilon\le1/10$ obey
\[
 \Pr[\arccos\langle\widehat s,S\rangle\le\varepsilon]\ge2/3
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\ge2^{-16}d\log_2(1/\varepsilon).
\]
The dimension threshold depends only on the memory sequence.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 140; 2026-09-27), a dyadic cell is chosen to restore geometric spread. Exact projection density estimates bound one message's information, while the entropy of selecting that cell pays for the apparent localization gain; a weighted inverse-distance potential closes the block recurrence. For manuscript 2 (family 140; 2026-09-27), continuation-success mass is localized into controlled spherical balls. Multirow projection moments bound the gain from one block of exact equations, and a backward recurrence propagates the terminal small-cap cost through the finite memory states.

For manuscript 3 (family 140; 2026-09-27), resampling posterior signals preserves the signal--message law while introducing replica correlation. Equal-label fibers are evaluated with affine-volume normalization, and a Gaussian test cancels that volume; total-correlation chain rules then bound the information carried between blocks. For manuscript 4 (family 140; 2026-09-27), exact frame densities are integrated using local ball-mass control. Positive sums of cap measures dominate the propagated law, and radial or Riesz estimates bound successive blocks; forward and backward arguments keep their stopping and terminal accuracy costs explicit.

For manuscript 5 (family 140; 2026-09-27), mixed moments compare shared-row and fresh-row projection densities. A critical radius aligns mass-level charges so the density contributions cancel, and counterfactual independent kernels transfer the remaining information bound to a finite-state streaming recurrence. For manuscript 6 (family 140; 2026-09-27), conditioning a batch on its observed projection leaves a random residual subsphere. Radius-weighted cap estimates control the success mass on that subsphere, and an affine-invariant dimension budget limits how quickly successive batches can reach the terminal cap.
