\section*{The Graphic Kahn–Kalai Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{The second Kahn--Kalai bound}: The claimed graph containment threshold is at most $C p_{\mathrm E}(n,H)(1+\log_2 |E(H)|)$ for every nonempty graph $H$ fitting inside the host vertex set. The constant is universal.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For a finite simple $H$ with $h=e(H)\ge1$ and at most $n\ge2$ vertices, let $p_c(n,H)=\inf\{p:\mathbb P(G(n,p)\supset H)\ge1/2\}$ and $p_E(n,H)=\inf\{p:\mathbb E N(G(n,p),F)\ge1/2\text{ for every }F\subseteq H\}$, where $N$ counts noninduced copies. The claimed edge-dependent bound is \[p_c(n,H)\le\min\{1,\,2048e^{50}p_E(n,H)(1+\log_2h)\}.\] It implies $p_c(n,H)\le6144e^{50}p_E(n,H)\log_2n$. Since $h\le\binom{|V(H)|}{2}$, the stronger edge-dependent statement also supplies one universal constant times $p_E(n,H)\log|V(H)|$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{The second Kahn--Kalai bound}: A maximizing-core selection creates nested subgraphs whose added-edge distributions have controlled conditional probabilities. Copies of these cores form a tree of disjoint increment labels with geometrically changing sizes. Bayesian resampling compares planted and posterior labels, and iteration across the tree turns the spread control into the logarithmic containment bound.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The claimed estimate uses $1+\log_2 e(H)$, which is bounded by a universal multiple of $\log|V(H)|$ for every graph with an edge. It therefore implies the requested graph-expectation-threshold bound with one universal constant; edgeless graphs are contained trivially.
