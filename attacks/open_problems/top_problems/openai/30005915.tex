\section*{The Graphic Kahn–Kalai Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{The second Kahn--Kalai bound}: The claimed graph containment threshold is at most $C p_{\mathrm E}(n,H)(1+\log_2 |E(H)|)$ for every nonempty graph $H$ fitting inside the host vertex set. The constant is universal.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{The second Kahn--Kalai bound}: A maximizing-core selection creates nested subgraphs whose added-edge distributions have controlled conditional probabilities. Copies of these cores form a tree of disjoint increment labels with geometrically changing sizes. Bayesian resampling compares planted and posterior labels, and iteration across the tree turns the spread control into the logarithmic containment bound.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{The second Kahn--Kalai bound}: The sharper edge-count theorem gives p\_c(n,H)<=A p\_E(n,H)(1+log\_2 e(H)). Since e(H)<=binom(v(H),2), this is at most K p\_E log v(H) for one universal K on graphs with an edge. The empty graph has trivial containment probability one. Thus it reaches the question log v(H) factor rather than only log n.
