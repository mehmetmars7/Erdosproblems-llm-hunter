\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 120; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that general simple graphs admit a uniform randomized algorithm producing a maximum matching in $(n+m)^{1+o(1)}$ time on every path with success probability at least $2/3$, also yielding prescribed-degree subgraphs.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For each finite simple general graph supplied as adjacency lists (a boundary array and two neighbor occurrences per undirected edge) $G=([n],E)$, put $m=|E|$, $p=n+m$ and let $\nu(G)$ be its maximum matching size. One uniform randomized word-RAM algorithm is claimed with absolute constants $C\ge1,c_0\ge2$ and a nonincreasing $\eta(p)\ge0$ tending to zero, using words of $\lceil c_0\log_2(n+2)\rceil$ bits, such that
\[
 T(G)\le Cp^{1+\eta(p)}\quad\hbox{on every random path},\qquad
 \Pr[\text{output is a matching of size }\nu(G)]\ge2/3.
\]
The matching is explicitly output as an edge list, and the graph need not be bipartite.
