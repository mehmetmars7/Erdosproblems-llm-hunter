\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For an irreducible $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of degree $d\ge4$, put $k=d-2$ and $\rho_f(p^k)=|\{a\bmod p^k:f(a)\equiv0\pmod{p^k}\}|$. Assume no prime has its $k$th power dividing every value of $f$, equivalently $\rho_f(p^k)<p^k$ for every prime. The claimed asymptotic is
\[
 |\{1\le n\le X:f(n)\text{ is }k\text{-free}\}|
 =c_{f,k}X+o_f(X),\qquad
 c_{f,k}=\prod_p(1-\rho_f(p^k)/p^k)>0.
\]
The theorem proves the range $4\le d\le8$, and its cited all-degree corollary extends the assertion to all $d\ge4$. No primitivity or leading-coefficient sign requirement is imposed; the quartic case is squarefreeness.
