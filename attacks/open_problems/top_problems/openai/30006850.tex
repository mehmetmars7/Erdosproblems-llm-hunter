\section*{Unrestricted Linear-Circuit Lower Bound for the Discrete Fourier Transform}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 130; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that exact complex DFTs at every length admit a deterministic arithmetic algorithm with cost $O(n(\log n)^{1-10^{-13}})$, including scalar preparation and indexing in the stated model; In manuscript 2 (family 130; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that unrestricted complex linear circuits compute exact Fourier transforms with size $o(n\log n)$ along an unbounded sequence of lengths.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
Let $F_n=(e^{2\pi i jk/n})_{0\le j,k<n}$. The model counts exact complex field operations, preparation of input-independent scalars, and integer/address operations on $O(\log(n+2))$-bit words. A specified root of unity of an effectively computable order $D_*<1024n^3$ is supplied; no finite-precision or bounded-coefficient restriction is imposed. Define
\[
 m_*=10^6,\quad W_*=2^{71},\quad\Delta=6871402692000000,\quad
 \theta=\log_{m_*}(m_*-\Delta/W_*)<1.
\]
One deterministic algorithm is claimed to compute $F_nx$ exactly for every $n\ge1$, $x\in\mathbb C^n$, in
\[
 O(n(\log n)^{\theta}(\log\log n)^{4-\theta})
 =O(n(\log n)^{1-10^{-13}})=o(n\log n)
\]
time asymptotically, including schedule and scalar construction. The same model, with general field multiplication for pointwise products, computes the linear convolution $c_k=\sum_{r+s=k}a_rb_s$ of two length-$n$ vectors within this cost. The latter is a bilinear arithmetic algorithm, rather than an input-linear circuit.

\paragraph{Manuscript 2.}
Let $F_n=(e^{2\pi i jk/n})_{0\le j,k<n}$ and let $L(n)$ be the minimum number of exact complex linear gates computing $F_nx$, where an addition $u+v$, subtraction $u-v$ or multiplication $\lambda u$ by an input-independent scalar each costs one gate; intermediate values can be reused arbitrarily, allowing unrestricted input-independent coefficients, arbitrary intermediate registers and no coefficient-preparation charge. The claim is
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{L(n)}{n\log_2n}=0.
\]
Equivalently, for every $c>0$ and every $N_0\ge2$, there is some $n\ge N_0$ and an exact circuit using fewer than $cn\log_2n$ gates. This gives a subsequence bound in the unrestricted linear-circuit model, not an every-length bit-time algorithm.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 130; 2026-09-25), a fixed two-coordinate network supplies a tensor-axis saving. Exact-width Toeplitz and Fourier factorizations preserve borrowed coordinates, while coprime working lengths, CRT permutations, and chirp convolution extend the saved transform to every requested length.

For manuscript 2 (family 130; 2026-09-25), a finite improvement over the standard tensor-axis matrix-call count is amplified in tensor powers. A price-separation argument guarantees such a saving, and an exact-width Fourier factorization transfers it while charging diagonal scalings and auxiliary operations.
