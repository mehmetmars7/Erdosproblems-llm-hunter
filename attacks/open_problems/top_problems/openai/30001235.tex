\section*{The Lex-Plus-Powers Betti Number Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: In characteristic zero, the Eisenbud--Green--Harris Hilbert-function comparison is claimed for ideals containing a regular sequence, using a new commuting-form model over division coefficients; For characteristic-zero regular sequences, the proposed lex-plus-powers ideal simultaneously preserves the Hilbert function and bounds every graded Betti number above. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(F\) have characteristic zero, \(S_F=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), and \(I\) contain a homogeneous regular sequence of length \(1\le c\le n\) with degrees \(2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_c\). The claimed monomial ideal \(J\) contains \(P_F=(x_1^{a_1},\ldots,x_c^{a_c})\) and satisfies
\[
 \dim_F(S_F/J)_d=\dim_F(S_F/I)_d\qquad(d\ge0).
\]
It may be selected as lex-plus-powers: outside \(P_F\), each degree uses the lexicographically greatest \(\dim I_d-\dim(P_F)_d\) monomials, with \(x_1>\cdots>x_n\). The construction theorem over \(\mathbb C\), for a full regular sequence \(f_i\), additionally supplies a finite-dimensional central division algebra \(\Delta\) over a finitely generated extension \(k/\mathbb C\) and a commutative graded algebra \(k[x]\subseteq A\subseteq\Delta[x]\), finite over \(k[x]\), generated by commuting linear forms. Its distinguished forms satisfy
\[
 t_i^{a_i}=c_if_i+\sum_{\ell<i}B_{i\ell}f_\ell+\sum_{d>i}C_{id}t_d,
\]
with \(c_i\in k^\times\), \(B_{i\ell}\in A_{a_i-a_\ell}\), \(C_{id}\in A_{a_i-1}\); all monomials in the \(t_i\) form a left \(\Delta\)-basis of \(\Delta[x]\).

\paragraph{Result 2.} For a characteristic-zero field \(F\), a homogeneous ideal \(I\subseteq S_F=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) containing a regular sequence of degrees \(2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_c\), \(1\le c\le n\), put \(P_F=(x_1^{a_1},\ldots,x_c^{a_c})\). In degree \(d\), define \(J_d\) by adjoining to \((P_F)_d\) the lexicographically greatest \(q_d=\dim I_d-\dim(P_F)_d\) monomials outside \(P_F\). The source claims \(J=\bigoplus_dJ_d\) is an ideal with
\[
 \dim_F(S_F/I)_d=\dim_F(S_F/J)_d\quad(d\ge0),\qquad
 \beta^{S_F}_{u,j}(S_F/I)\le\beta^{S_F}_{u,j}(S_F/J)
 \quad(u\ge0,\ j\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Here \(\beta_{u,j}\) are graded Betti numbers. The additional generators of \(I\) have no degree or number restriction. The main complex-field theorem is the full-length Artinian case \(c=n\).

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
The central construction replaces variables by commuting linear forms with coefficients in a division algebra while retaining an ordered polynomial basis. Triangular identities model the regular sequence by powers; monomial compression gives the Artinian comparison, and reduction plus coefficient descent handles shorter sequences.

The division-coefficient commuting forms retain enough filtration information to compare minimal free resolutions, beyond merely counting graded dimensions. The Artinian Betti comparison is then transported through the established regular-sequence reduction and descent to arbitrary characteristic-zero coefficient fields.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The first manuscript's characteristic-zero corollary constructs a lex-plus-powers ideal with the required Hilbert function for every regular-sequence length $1\le r\le n$. The second manuscript's characteristic-zero corollary also gives all the graded Betti inequalities for that same ideal, including $r<n$. Thus the selected claims cover the full characteristic-zero branch. The question allows fields of positive characteristic as well, which these corollaries do not treat; the overall match remains partial.
