\section*{A dimension-free quadratic bound for the canonical Foulkes–Howe map}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. The canonical Foulkes--Howe map is claimed onto whenever $a\geq2$ and $b\geq a(a-1)$, uniformly in the underlying dimension.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(V\) be any finite-dimensional complex vector space. Using averaging over permutations to identify symmetric powers with invariant tensors, multiplication in \((\operatorname{Sym}V)^{\otimes a}\) defines the canonical Foulkes--Howe map
\[
 \mu_{a,b,V}:\operatorname{Sym}^b(\operatorname{Sym}^aV)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^a(\operatorname{Sym}^bV).
\]
The asserted dimension-independent sufficient bound is
\[
 a\ge2,\quad b\ge a(a-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu_{a,b,V}\text{ surjective}.
\]

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
An annihilator formulation reduces surjectivity to vanishing of symmetric multilinear forms. Two successive differential procedures first free a slot from common factors and then eliminate the remaining common power; injectivity of directional differentiation supplies the quadratic degree condition.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The paper defines mu\_\{a,b,V\} by multiplication after the averaging identifications, exactly the stated canonical map, and proves surjectivity for all a>=2, b>=a(a-1) and finite-dimensional complex V. lean/docs/210.md documents this exact map/range. Within these assumptions, this is a claimed affirmative answer to the stated question.
