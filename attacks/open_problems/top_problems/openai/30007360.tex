\section*{A nonlinear-capacity criterion for four-state reconstruction on arbitrary trees}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. On any bounded-degree deterministic tree, four-state ferromagnetic reconstruction is claimed equivalent to positive $L^3$ capacity for resistances $\lambda^{-2|e|}$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(T\) be an infinite tree with a distinguished root; each vertex has at most \(B<\infty\) children. On this tree, broadcast four uniform states through \(P_\lambda(j|i)=\lambda\mathbf1_{i=j}+(1-\lambda)/4\), \(0<\lambda<1\). Let \(a_\infty\) be the limiting expected total-variation advantage of the root posterior from level observations. For a nonnegative conserved flow \(\theta\), with root mass \(|\theta|\), define
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}_{3,\lambda}(T)=\sup\left\{|\theta|:
 \sup_{\xi\in\partial T}\sum_{e\in\xi}(\lambda^{-2|e|}\theta(e))^2\le1\right\}.
\]
The source claims
\[
 a_\infty(T,\lambda)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Cap}_{3,\lambda}(T)>0.
\]
The edge depth is the depth of its child endpoint.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
A cubic posterior-information saving is converted to a nonlinear flow inequality by changing variables. Reconstruction then telescopes into a positive admissible flow; conversely a scaled flow specifies noisy terminal observations whose accumulated information loss remains controlled.
