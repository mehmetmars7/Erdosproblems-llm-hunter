\section*{Uniqueness of admissible periodic hard-sphere Boltzmann evolution}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Periodic Boltzmann nonuniqueness}: The manuscript claims two global renormalized hard-sphere Boltzmann solutions on $\mathbb T^3\times\mathbb R^3$ with the same initial density; they satisfy the stated local-mass, total-momentum, energy, and entropy conditions, with strong $L^1$ continuity and integrable gains and losses on an initial interval.

\textit{Boltzmann nonuniqueness with local conservation}: The refinement claims two distinct global entropy solutions for one periodic hard-sphere initial datum while preserving mass, momentum, and kinetic energy locally; polynomially weighted gain and loss integrability and strong $L^1$ continuity hold on bounded time intervals.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} On $\Omega=\mathbb T^3_x\times\mathbb R^3_v$, consider $(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)F=Q(F,F)$ with $a=(v-v_*)\cdot\omega$, $v^\prime=v-a\omega$, $v_*^\prime=v_*+a\omega$ and $Q=\int_{\mathbb R^3\times S^2}|a|(F^\prime F_*^\prime-FF_*)\,dv_*\,d\omega$. The claim gives a nonnegative datum $F_0$ of bounded velocity support and finite $\int_\Omega F_0(1+|v|^2+|\log F_0|)$ with two distinct global admissible solutions. These are weakly $L^1$ continuous against bounded tests, retain bounded mass, energy and absolute entropy on finite intervals, conserve local mass and total momentum, and obey global energy and relative-entropy dissipation inequalities at every time. For $M=(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-|v|^2/2}$ and every $C^1$ renormalization $\beta$ with $\beta(0)=0$, $|\beta^\prime(z)|\le C_\beta/(1+z)$, they solve $(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)[M\beta(F/M)]=\beta^\prime(F/M)Q(F,F)$ distributionally, with the separate renormalized gains and losses integrable. For some initial time interval shared by the two evolutions, continuity holds in the strong $L^1$ norm and their unrenormalized gains and losses are integrable. Local momentum and energy conservation are not assumptions of this first solution class.

\paragraph{Mathematical claim 2.} On $\Omega=\mathbb T^3_x\times\mathbb R^3_v$, consider $(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)F=Q(F,F)$ with $a=(v-v_*)\cdot\omega$, $v^\prime=v-a\omega$, $v_*^\prime=v_*+a\omega$ and $Q=\int_{\mathbb R^3\times S^2}|a|(F^\prime F_*^\prime-FF_*)\,dv_*\,d\omega$. One datum $F_0\ge0$ is claimed to have bounded velocity support, finite mass, energy and absolute entropy, yet admit two global solutions differing on a positive-measure set. Both belong to $C([0,\infty);L^1(\Omega))$, obey the renormalized equation for every $\beta\in C^1([0,\infty))$ with $\beta(0)=0$ and $|\beta^\prime(z)|\le C/(1+z)$ relative to the Maxwellian, and satisfy both the global energy bound and the full relative-entropy dissipation inequality at every $t\ge0$. Each solution is nonnegative, takes initial value $F_0$, and for every finite $T$ has \[\sup_{0\le t\le T}\int_\Omega F(t)(1+|v|^2+|\log F(t)|)\,dx\,dv<\infty,\qquad\int_0^T\int_\Omega(1+|v|^3)F\,dx\,dv\,dt<\infty.\] The second condition ensures that the local fluxes below are integrable. In addition \[\partial_t\int F\psi\,dv+\nabla_x\cdot\int vF\psi\,dv=0\quad(\psi=1,v_1,v_2,v_3,|v|^2/2)\] distributionally with the prescribed initial traces. For every finite $T$ and $k\ge0$, \[\int_0^T\int_\Omega(1+|v|^2)^{k/2}(Q^+(F,F)+Q^-(F,F))\,dx\,dv\,dt<\infty.\] Thus the stronger nonuniqueness claim retains exact local mass, momentum and energy conservation.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Periodic Boltzmann nonuniqueness}: Two cutoff approximation families separate a dormant hot component from one activated by a seed of vanishing mass. Velocity-weight caps provide uniform integrability and permit removal of the annular and collision cutoffs. A rescaled branching argument prevents an apparent cap hit from disappearing into negligible mass, proving that the admissible limits differ despite sharing the same initial datum.

\textit{Boltzmann nonuniqueness with local conservation}: Kernel estimates and carefully arranged cold jets select short-time hot-component approximations. A global continuation bound and weighted compactness preserve collision terms and each local conservation law. Dilation and branching produce a nonzero rescaled limit with a finite horizon, which separates the two limiting solutions rather than merely their approximations.
