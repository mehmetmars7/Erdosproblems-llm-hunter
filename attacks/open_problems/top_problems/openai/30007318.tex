\section*{The optimal one-third stability exponent for compact-target Brenier maps}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Sharp one-third Brenier stability}: The paper claims a uniform $L^2$ stability estimate with exponent $1/3$ for quadratic optimal maps from a uniform convex-body source to targets in a fixed compact set. A three-atom family shows that larger exponents, including the proposed square-root estimate, fail.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} Fix $d\ge2$, a compact convex $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interior, uniform source probability $\rho=\mathbf1_Kdx/|K|$, and nonempty compact target set $Y$. For the quadratic-cost Brenier maps $T_\mu$ to arbitrary probability measures $\mu$ on $Y$, the claimed uniform stability is \[\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le C(K,Y)W_2(\mu,\nu)^{1/3}.\] Here $W_2$ minimizes the root-mean-square distance over couplings. Atomic, singular and absolutely continuous targets are all permitted. The exponent is asserted optimal: no larger exponent works uniformly, even with $K=Y=[-1,1]^d$ and three-atom targets.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Sharp one-third Brenier stability}: In the obstruction, tilting one plane in a three-plane maximum switches a thin source cell between separated target slopes while preserving its mass. For the upper estimate, semi-discrete cell calculus tracks cell masses and first moments as slopes and intercepts vary. The central moment comparison controls the map difference, and approximation extends the estimate to arbitrary compactly supported targets.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Sharp one-third Brenier stability}: The source is the uniform measure of one fixed convex body, and all targets share one compact support bound. Atomic targets are allowed; the three-atom construction rules out every exponent above $1/3$ in this uniform setting.
