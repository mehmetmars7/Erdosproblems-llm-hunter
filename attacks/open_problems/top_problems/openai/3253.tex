\section*{List Hadwiger Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Linear list-coloring in clique-minor size}: The paper claims a universal constant $C$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C h(G)$ for every finite nonempty graph. The factor is independent of the graph and its order.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every finite nonempty simple graph $G$ there is an absolute integer $C\ge1$, independent of $G$, such that \[\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C h(G),\qquad h(G)=\max\{t:K_t\text{ is a minor of }G\}.\] The list chromatic number is the least $k$ for which every assignment of $k$ available colors per vertex permits a proper coloring. Consequently $\chi(G)\le Ch(G)$. The constant-factor bound does not imply the conjectured coefficient $1$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Linear list-coloring in clique-minor size}: For small minor-free graphs, a random global partition of colors gives each vertex many eligible bins. Stable-set sampling repeatedly reduces fractional chromatic complexity among eligible vertices. If the reduction failed, separator and connectivity arguments would assemble disjoint clique models into the excluded minor; the resulting small-graph bound feeds the global list-coloring argument.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The claimed universal estimate $\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le Ch(G)$ implies $\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C(t-1)$ whenever $K_t$ is excluded as a minor. This covers the stated linear List Hadwiger question with a universal constant, rather than requiring the coefficient-one variant.
