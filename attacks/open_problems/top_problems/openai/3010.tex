\section*{Kirby Problem 5.3}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
A faithful continuous action of a second-countable locally compact group on any connected finite-dimensional topological manifold is claimed to force the group to be Lie, resolving Hilbert--Smith in every finite dimension. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For every finite integer $n\ge1$, OpenAI asserts that a Hausdorff second-countable group $G$, locally compact, with a faithful action on a connected topological $n$-manifold $M$. Require $M$ to be Hausdorff, second-countable and without boundary, and the action map $G\times M\to M$ to be continuous. Under these conditions $G$ is a Lie group. Its $p$-adic exclusion theorem says
\[
\ker(\mathbb Z_p\curvearrowright M)\ne\{0\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_p=\varprojlim_k\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z,
\]
for every prime $p$ and every such jointly continuous action; therefore some finite quotient already determines the action. Faithfulness means trivial global kernel, and does not mean free action.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 304, 2026-09-23): Witt groups of sheaves generated by continuous polyhedral maps acquire a fixed integral signature lattice through cutoff localization and doubling comparisons with semialgebraic objects. A hypothetical p-adic action produces equally valued classes in arbitrarily large orbit batches. Their rational values would have unbounded p-power denominators, contradicting the lattice fixed before the batch size is chosen.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Manuscript 1 (family 304, 2026-09-23): The Hilbert–Smith bullet is proved in every finite manifold dimension, but the second bullet asks the stronger homology-Z-set assertion for Cantor-group actions on arbitrary ENRs, which is not proved.
