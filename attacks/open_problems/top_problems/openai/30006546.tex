\section*{Optimal Covering Density of Euclidean Balls}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 092; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that every convex body in dimension at least two admits a single-lattice covering of density at most an absolute multiple of $n\log n$; In manuscript 2 (family 092; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that in every sufficiently large dimension, some symmetric convex body has translative covering density at least an absolute multiple of $n\log n$, ruling out a uniform linear bound.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 092; 2026-09-23), a hierarchy of branching lattice blocks supplies usable anchors. Folded Gaussian sampling and simultaneous shears cover a finite collection of horizontal patterns, after which a final small-hole removal produces an exact covering by one lattice.

For manuscript 2 (family 092; 2026-09-23), a random body is tested against a finite family obtained by rounding periodic covering patterns. Cap-intersection and Poisson-witness estimates make successful low-density coverage too unlikely for any pattern; periodization extends the obstruction to all coverings.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The universal upper bound gives order $n\log n$ for balls as a special case. The matching worst-case lower bound uses constructed bodies rather than Euclidean balls, so the optimal asymptotic ball-covering density is not determined.
