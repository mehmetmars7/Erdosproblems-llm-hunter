\section*{Baum–Connes Conjecture for Torsion-Free Groups}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
Manuscript 1 (family 285, 2026-09-23): For one finitely generated group without torsion, the coefficient-free reduced assembly map annihilates an infinite-order degree-zero K-homology class; this is claimed to refute rational injectivity. Manuscript 2 (family 285, 2026-09-23): OpenAI claims the existence of a nontrivial scalar projection inside the reduced C*-algebra of a finitely generated torsion-free group, contradicting the Kadison--Kaplansky assertion.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Manuscript 1 (family 285, 2026-09-23): The construction embeds a torus subgroup with an integral cohomology detector while arranging disappearance of its Bott class analytically. Operators assembled on almost disjoint Cayley-graph sheets retain controlled spectral supports; estimates for the leftover forest permit the required homotopies in the reduced completion. A finite witness then restricts the construction to a finitely generated subgroup.

Manuscript 2 (family 285, 2026-09-23): A magnetic tower model produces a separated spectral band carrying a matrix projection of positive trace below one half. Graph sampling and small-cancellation recovery preserve this band after the model is represented on the group, with intersection losses bounded by trace estimates. A free-product compression converts the matrix projection into a scalar one while retaining torsion-freeness.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Manuscript 1 (family 285, 2026-09-23): The infinite-order degree-zero assembly-kernel class for a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group directly refutes the universal coefficient-free reduced injectivity and isomorphism assertions, with no coefficient or maximal-completion substitution.

Manuscript 2 (family 285, 2026-09-23): The nonintegral scalar projection class lies outside the coefficient-free reduced assembly image, so surjectivity and hence the universal isomorphism assertion fail; torsion freeness is used only in the projection-conjecture paper.
