\section*{Measure-zero singular set of stationary integral varifolds}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Codimension-one varifold singular bound}: For a stationary integral varifold of dimension $m$, the refinement claims a singular-set Hausdorff bound of $m-1$; the Euclidean ambient dimension can be any larger dimension, and the assertion begins at $m=1$.

\textit{Almost-everywhere varifold regularity}: For Euclidean stationary integral varifolds of dimension $m$, the initial paper claims that singular points carry no $m$-dimensional Hausdorff measure; off this exceptional set there are smooth minimal neighborhoods with one constant integer multiplicity; a separate argument handles round spheres.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Codimension-one varifold singular bound}: Smooth reference graphs are fitted at prescribed flat scales, with controlled frequency intervals covering transitions between scales. Centered heights are normalized so that their second moments remain nonzero while their first moments vanish. The limiting height and small mean-curvature errors provide the dimension-reduction estimate needed to sharpen the earlier almost-everywhere result.

\textit{Almost-everywhere varifold regularity}: Integer multiplicity, height estimates, and heat-contact comparisons are combined in a signed-excess induction. Packing of witness scales closes the estimates when sheets merge. A center chosen from minimal fits and multiplicity defects gives inward doubling, forcing the residual height to vanish and the exceptional support to have zero relevant Hausdorff measure.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Codimension-one varifold singular bound}: A singular set of Hausdorff dimension at most m minus one has zero m-dimensional Hausdorff measure, for every positive dimension and codimension. The varifold stationarity, integrality and open smooth-neighborhood regularity conventions agree with our definition.

\textit{Almost-everywhere varifold regularity}: The main theorem is exactly zero m-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the singular support of an arbitrary stationary integral m-varifold, with all positive m and codimensions and no additional stability, minimization or multiplicity restriction.

The claimed result implies the stated question and also supplies the additional strength described above.
