\section*{Kaplansky's Conjectures}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A finitely presented torsion-free group is claimed to have nonzero elements $a,c\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $ac=0$, despite admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying complex; Another construction claims a torsion-free, finitely presented $G$ for which $\mathbb F_2[G]$ contains $a,b$ satisfying $ab=1$ and $ba\ne1$; The Bass trace restriction is claimed for complex group rings of arbitrary discrete groups, implying triviality of scalar idempotents for torsion-free groups over characteristic-zero domains; For every discrete group, the trace of an idempotent matrix over $\ell^1(G;\mathbb C)$ is claimed to vanish outside a finite collection of finite-order conjugacy classes. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Finite labeled graphs encode supports whose product coefficients cancel modulo two. A random-label counting argument excludes planar path arrangements; cone surgeries turn that exclusion into asphericity and root separation, so the coefficients remain nonzero in a torsion-free group. An unequal-side parity pattern supplies the one-sided inverse relation in labeled graphs. Weighted counts handle repeated edge traversals, and a separator reduces arbitrarily large planar arrangements to bounded path collections; cone attachment and root separation then preserve the inverse defect while proving torsion-freeness.

An idempotent is encoded by finite cyclic path configurations whose weighted deletion relations generate cycles. Exterior differential forms and a cyclic Pfaffian identity detect forbidden conjugacy support; trace-rank arguments then turn the complex trace conclusion into the stated idempotent consequence. Analytic idempotent correction first replaces the matrix, up to similarity, by one with exponential decay. Weighted cyclic configurations are then treated with smooth spatial summaries, multiscale time averages, and an ordered-kernel estimate; one class-independent cutoff proves actual finite support rather than only absolute summability.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Theorem 1.1 refutes the zero-divisor clause over F\({}_{2}\). It does not determine the other clauses of the bundle. Theorem 1.1 refutes the torsion-free zero-divisor and idempotent clauses via ac=0 and e=ba; it also refutes unrestricted direct finiteness. It does not settle the bundle’s separate integral/field unit assertion. The Bass trace theorem implies the characteristic-zero scalar-idempotent clause for torsion-free groups. The positive-characteristic cases and the other independent bundled conjectures are not determined. This entry records partial support; complete coverage of the question is not established by this catalogue.
