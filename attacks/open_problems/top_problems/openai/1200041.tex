\section*{Some Questions — Question 41}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A finitely generated amalgam is claimed to have free probability-preserving actions of different costs, refuting fixed price.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let \(A=F(a,b_1,\ldots,b_{99})\), \(w=ab_1ab_2\cdots ab_{99}a\), \(J=\langle b_1,\ldots,b_{99},w\rangle\), and
\[
 \Gamma=A*_J(J\times\langle t\rangle),\qquad\chi(a)=1,\quad\chi(b_i)=\chi(t)=0.
\]
Let \(X\) be the free conull part of the Bernoulli shift \([0,1]^\Gamma\), and \(Y_M=X\times\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\) with action \((x,r)g=(xg,r+\chi(g))\). Both carry their natural probability measures. Set \(K=e^{96}96^{99}/95^{95}\), choose \(0<\alpha<1/200\) with \(K\alpha^3<1/2\), and put \(\eta=\alpha/100\). The asserted orbit-equivalence-relation costs satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Cost}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X)\ge1+\eta,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cost}(\Gamma\curvearrowright Y_M)\le1+99/M\quad(M>100).
\]
An integer \(M>\max\{100,99/\eta\}\) therefore separates the costs of two essentially free probability-preserving actions of the same group.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Finite-height extensions of the amalgam yield graphings with costs approaching one. A relative graphing-compression inequality and Bernoulli finite-permutation models give a separate cost lower bound, supported by a planar word lemma; the two action constructions therefore have unequal costs.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The witness is one group with finite generators carrying two essentially free actions preserving probability. Their costs lie in disjoint ranges, so the cost cannot be independent of the action as requested. This is presented as a claimed negative answer.
