\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} Let $P_j(L_1,\ldots,L_m,z)$ be finitely many real-holomorphic functions, bounded on products $Q(A,c)=\{L:\operatorname{Re}L>A,\ |\operatorname{Im}L|<c(\operatorname{Re}L)^2\}$ near a finite ordinary-parameter point $z_0$. For each length and recursively extracted coefficient require expansions \[P\sim\sum_{d\in D_i}e^{-dL_i}\sum_{k=0}^{k_d}L_i^kP_{dk}(L_{\widehat i},z),\] where $D_i\subset[0,\infty)$ is locally finite, $k_d<\infty$, $k_0=0$, and removal of terms $d\le B$ leaves $O(e^{-(B+\epsilon_B)\operatorname{Re}L_i})$ with $\epsilon_B>0$ on smaller quadratic regions, uniformly on smaller parameter polydiscs. All recursively expanded coefficients must be holomorphic on one common tail band and ordinary-parameter neighborhood. For any subset $\operatorname{Re}L_i=c_iU+o(U)$, $c_i>0$, require locally finite grouped weights $\sum c_id_i$ and strictly better exponential remainders after every finite cutoff, on the same complex neighborhoods. If a finite Boolean combination of $P_j>0,P_j=0,P_j<0$ holds along $L_i^{(n)}\to+\infty,z^{(n)}\to z_0$, the claim gives a real-analytic path $u\mapsto(L(u),z(u))$ with those limits and the same sign conditions for all large real $u$. Each coordinate, and any additionally prescribed finite admissible list, is eventually monotone or constant; finite prescribed function limits can also be retained. Every extracted coefficient must itself obey each of the preceding requirements: boundedness, expansions in every remaining length, common holomorphic domains, and grouped-weight remainder estimates. One shrinking of the analytic neighborhoods suffices for the finite original family; constants and length thresholds in estimates may vary with the order of truncation.
