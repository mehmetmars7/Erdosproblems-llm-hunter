\subsection{OpenAI's claim}

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} Let $D$ be bounded, smooth and simply connected, with two distinct marked boundary points, and let triangular-lattice polygons $D_\delta$ have uniformly converging marked boundaries. Sample real vertex heights with normalized Lebesgue measure on the polytope
\[
 h_\delta=\lambda\text{ on }A_{+,\delta},\quad h_\delta=-\lambda\text{ on }A_{-,\delta},\quad
 |h_\delta(x)-h_\delta(y)|\le1\ (x\sim y).
\]
Affine interpolation produces a zero-height chord $\gamma_\delta$ between the marks. There exist universal $A,\sigma>0$ and $\lambda\in(0,1/2]$ such that
\[
 \gamma_\delta\Longrightarrow\mathrm{SLE}_4(D;a,b),\qquad
 (h_\delta-\mathbb Eh_\delta)/\sigma\Longrightarrow\Phi_D.
\]
Curves use uniform distance modulo increasing reparametrization, and fields use distributional convergence with $\operatorname{Cov}\Phi_D=(-\Delta_D)^{-1}$. Writing $v=2/\sqrt3$, the constants satisfy
\[
 \sigma^2=(vA)^{-1},\qquad \lambda^2=\pi/(8vA),\qquad
 m_D(z)=\lambda[\omega_D(z,A_+)-\omega_D(z,A_-)],
\]
where $m_D$ is the limiting mean and $\omega_D$ denotes harmonic measure. The coefficient $A$ is determined implicitly by stationary cell flux, rather than by a claimed elementary value.
