\section*{Primitive-root infinitude for every admissible base}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 029; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that each integer other than $-1$ or a square is primitive modulo at least $c_a x/(\log x)^2$ primes between $x$ and $2x$ for large $x$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
An integer $a$ is admissible when $a\ne-1$ and it is not an integer square. For each such $a$, the claim supplies $c_a>0$, $x_a\ge2$ such that every real $x\ge x_a$ satisfies
\[
 |\{p\text{ prime}:x<p<2x,\ p\nmid a,\ \operatorname{ord}_p(a)=p-1\}|
 \ge c_a x/(\log x)^2.
\]
Here $\operatorname{ord}_p(a)$ is multiplicative order. The estimate yields infinitely many primitive-root primes for each admissible base, without asserting Artin's predicted positive-density asymptotic.

Controlled prime predecessors in fixed progressions are then sieved against complete-splitting obstructions, retaining enough primes on which the base has full multiplicative order.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
For each integer $a$ that is neither $-1$ nor a square, the source gives at least $c_a x/(\log x)^2$ suitable primes in every sufficiently large interval $(x,2x)$. This proves the infinitude requested here and adds a quantitative lower bound. It does not assert the positive prime-relative density required by the separate Artin density conjecture.
