\section*{Finiteness of the little finitistic dimension}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A single finite-dimensional complex algebra is claimed to have finite modules whose finite projective dimensions are unbounded.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} A finite-dimensional unital complex algebra \(A\) is claimed to admit, for every \(m\ge1\), a left module \(N_m\), finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb C\), satisfying
\[
 2m-2\le\operatorname{pd}_AN_m<\infty.
\]
Thus its little finitistic dimension, the supremum of finite projective dimensions of finite-dimensional modules, is infinite. A second complex algebra \(\Lambda\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{findim}\Lambda=\operatorname{Findim}\Lambda=\infty,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{findim}(\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}})
 =\operatorname{Findim}(\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}})=0,
\]
where each displayed dimension is for left modules and the capitalized version allows all modules. The corollary also distinguishes generation of the unbounded derived categories by injectives on the two sides.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
One fixed finitely presented algebra encodes arbitrarily long terminating processes through a twisted derived tensor functor. The subsequent finite-dimensional realization converts these tensor histories into ordinary projective resolutions, retaining unbounded termination lengths without changing the final algebra.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
One finite complex algebra supports modules with finite projective dimension at least $2m-2$ for arbitrarily large integers $m$. Consequently its little finitistic dimension is infinite, giving the requested counterexample within Artin algebras. This is presented as a claimed negative answer.
