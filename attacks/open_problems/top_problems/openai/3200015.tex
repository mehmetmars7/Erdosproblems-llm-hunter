\section*{Exact van der Waerden numbers}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Superexponential van der Waerden growth}: The claimed lower bound is $W_r(k)>k^{ck\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}$ for one absolute positive $c$ once $k$ is large enough. In particular, for each fixed color count at least two, the $k$th root of $W_r(k)$ is unbounded.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} Let $W_r(k)$ be the least $N$ such that every $r$-coloring of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has a monochromatic nonconstant $k$-term arithmetic progression. The claimed lower bound is \[W_r(k)>k^{10^{-5}k\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor}\quad(k\ge K_0,\ r\ge2),\] where $K_0$ is absolute. The quantifier is uniform over all integer color counts $r\ge2$, but the estimate does not give exact van der Waerden numbers.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Superexponential van der Waerden growth}: A cyclic-group coloring uses two circle-coordinate meshes related by an arithmetically chosen dilation. Counting anchored progression patterns gives a balanced-color versus affine-coordinate alternative uniformly in the growing dimension. A norm-dependent perturbation excludes the affine alternative, after which interval and product-color transfers give the stated bounds for all color counts.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Superexponential van der Waerden growth}: The theorem gives a uniform superexponential lower bound for all large progression lengths, but no exact values of W(r,k). It improves extremal bounds within the same family rather than completing the exact-value request.
