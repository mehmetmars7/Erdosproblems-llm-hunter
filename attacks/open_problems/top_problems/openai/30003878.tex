\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 002; 2026-10-03), the argument tracks the discrepancy between analytic periods and arithmetic determinant volumes in integral cohomology. Residual obstruction tests control vertical prime factors, while character specialization and theta-series comparisons remove horizontal factors; Heegner heights treat the rank-one case. For manuscript 2 (family 002; 2026-09-24), a tame deformation gives an upper bound on an Artin-module length. Integral theta-series coefficients and Heegner logarithms produce an incompatible lower bound unless the low-corank converse holds; auxiliary quadratic fields separate the two root-number cases.

For manuscript 3 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the proof compares integral determinant lattices with Siegel-unit periods and central Kummer lattices. Separate residual and character-division arguments establish primitivity at two, and the rank-one comparison uses a squared Heegner logarithm rather than only rational period identities. For manuscript 4 (family 004; 2026-09-24), binary coefficient systems detect central values or Heegner points. Integral interpolation and arithmetic contraction identities isolate one address in a network of twists, transferring a nonvanishing witness back to the original curve.

For manuscript 5 (family 006; 2026-09-23), finite cubes of twists preserve the required local classes. A central determinant or Heegner coordinate is nonzero at the noncentral cube vertices, and bounded integral interpolation recovers the missing vertex; the Selmer distribution then supplies the density assertion.
