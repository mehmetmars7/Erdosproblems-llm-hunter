\section*{Rank-One Equivalences for Elliptic Curves}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 002; 2026-10-03), the asserted result is that a rational elliptic curve satisfying a Selmer-corank condition of zero or one at one prime satisfies the exact BSD leading-coefficient identity, with every primary factor included; In manuscript 2 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that for any prime, a rational elliptic curve of Selmer corank zero or one has the corresponding analytic and rational-point ranks, together with finite Tate--Shafarevich group; the application includes primes congruent to $4$, $7$, or $8$ modulo $9$ as sums of two rational cubes; In manuscript 3 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that the two-adic part of the BSD leading-coefficient identity holds for rational elliptic curves of two-power Selmer corank at most one, and for almost every quadratic twist; In manuscript 4 (family 004; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that a rational elliptic curve with a nonidentity rational point of order two satisfies the low-corank two-adic Selmer converse even at arbitrary reduction at two; In manuscript 5 (family 006; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that among signed squarefree twists of each rational elliptic curve, analytic ranks zero and one each occur with density one half; the paper also claims the low-corank two-Selmer converse.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$ and any prime $q$, write $s_q(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_q}\operatorname{Sel}_{q^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb Q)$, $r(E)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)$ and $a(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$. If $s_q(E)\in\{0,1\}$, the manuscript claims
\[
 r(E)=a(E)=s_q(E),\qquad\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)<\infty,
\]
and, with $r=r(E)$,
\[
 \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
 =\frac{\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_E\,\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)\prod_{\ell\text{ finite}}c_{\ell}(E)}
 {(\#E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors})^2}.
\]
Here $\Omega_E$ is the real N\'eron period, $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ the N\'eron--Tate regulator (one in rank zero) and $c_{\ell}$ the Tamagawa numbers. No reduction, torsion, CM or residual-representation conditions are added. The low-Selmer-corank premise remains essential; this is not a claim of unrestricted BSD.

\paragraph{Manuscript 2.}
For every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$, every prime $p$ and $r\in\{0,1\}$, let $s_p(E)$ be the $\mathbb Z_p$-corank of its $p^{\infty}$ Selmer group. The claimed converse is
\[
 s_p(E)=r\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=r,
 \qquad\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)<\infty.
\]
No additional reduction, CM, residual-representation, rational-torsion or isogeny hypothesis is imposed. Coranks greater than one and an exact BSD leading coefficient are not concluded by this theorem.

\paragraph{Manuscript 3.}
For any elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$, let $s_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb Q)$ and assume $s_2(E)\le1$. The claim is
\[
 r=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=s_2(E),
 \qquad |\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)|<\infty.
\]
With $T_E=E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}$, real N\'eron period $\Omega_E$, N\'eron--Tate regulator $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ and finite-prime Tamagawa numbers $c_{\ell}(E)$, it further claims
\[
 Q_E=\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)|T_E|^2}{r!\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_E\prod_{\ell} c_{\ell}(E)}
 \in\mathbb Q_{>0},\qquad v_2(Q_E)=v_2(|\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)|).
\]
There is no additional restriction on $E[2]$ or reduction at two. The formula determines the two-primary valuation; it does not state equality $Q_E=|\operatorname{Sha}|$ at all primes or address larger Selmer corank.

\paragraph{Manuscript 4.}
For an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$, put $s_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb Q)$. If $E(\mathbb Q)[2]\ne0$ and $s_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$, the manuscript claims
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}E(\mathbb Q)=s_2(E),\qquad
 \#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)<\infty.
\]
Both rational two-torsion and the low-corank condition belong to this theorem; it does not cover arbitrary elliptic curves or arbitrary Selmer corank.

\paragraph{Manuscript 5.}
For $E/\mathbb Q$, write $a(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$, $r(E)=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)$ and $c_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb Q)$. The first claimed theorem is
\[
 c_2(E)\in\{0,1\}\Rightarrow a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E),\quad\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)<\infty.
\]
Let $\mathcal D(X)$ be the nonzero signed squarefree integers with $|d|\le X$ and $E^{(d)}$ the quadratic twist. The analytic Goldfeld assertion is, for each $j=0,1$,
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{|\{d\in\mathcal D(X):a(E^{(d)})=j\}|}{|\mathcal D(X)|}=\tfrac12.
\]
It further claims that a density-one set of twists has equal analytic and Mordell--Weil rank and finite whole Tate--Shafarevich group. This density statement does not assert those properties for every twist.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 002; 2026-10-03), the argument tracks the discrepancy between analytic periods and arithmetic determinant volumes in integral cohomology. Residual obstruction tests control vertical prime factors, while character specialization and theta-series comparisons remove horizontal factors; Heegner heights treat the rank-one case. For manuscript 2 (family 002; 2026-09-24), a tame deformation gives an upper bound on an Artin-module length. Integral theta-series coefficients and Heegner logarithms produce an incompatible lower bound unless the low-corank converse holds; auxiliary quadratic fields separate the two root-number cases.

For manuscript 3 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the proof compares integral determinant lattices with Siegel-unit periods and central Kummer lattices. Separate residual and character-division arguments establish primitivity at two, and the rank-one comparison uses a squared Heegner logarithm rather than only rational period identities. For manuscript 4 (family 004; 2026-09-24), binary coefficient systems detect central values or Heegner points. Integral interpolation and arithmetic contraction identities isolate one address in a network of twists, transferring a nonvanishing witness back to the original curve.

For manuscript 5 (family 006; 2026-09-23), finite cubes of twists preserve the required local classes. A central determinant or Heegner coordinate is nonzero at the noncentral cube vertices, and bounded integral interpolation recovers the missing vertex; the Selmer distribution then supplies the density assertion.
