\section*{Limit Theorems for Random Walks in Random Environments}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents a mathematical result relevant to this question in the selected manuscript. Iid uniformly elliptic nearest-neighbor walks in dimensions $d\geq2$ are claimed to have positive directional speed whenever escape in that direction is almost sure.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Result 1.} On \(\mathbb Z^d\), take iid nearest-neighbor transition rows with a deterministic lower bound \(\kappa>0\) on every transition probability. Let \(P_0\) be the environment-averaged walk law and \(A_\ell=\{X_n\cdot\ell\to+\infty\}\). For \(d\ge2\), the main claim is
\[
 P_0(A_\ell)=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists v\in\mathbb R^d\text{ deterministic}:\
 v\cdot\ell>0,\quad P_0(X_n/n\to v)=1.
\]
For \(d\ge3\), the corollary replaces the premise by \(P_0(A_\ell)>0\). If any such direction exists, then for each fixed \(u\in S^{d-1}\),
\[
 P_0(A_u)=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P_0(A_u)>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ v\cdot u>0.
\]
It does not assert a simultaneous pathwise statement for uncountably many directions.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
Regeneration is split into spatial-radius and temporal estimates. Controlling radii first yields quantitative crossing information without assuming nearby directional transience, and the resulting tail estimates make regeneration duration integrable, producing a deterministic positive speed.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
The main theorem gives an annealed almost-sure deterministic law of large numbers with positive directional speed for uniformly elliptic iid directionally transient environments. It gives neither quenched/annealed central limit theorems nor laws of large numbers in the problem statement full generality. This entry records partial support; complete coverage of the question is not established by this catalogue.
