\section*{GNRS Conjecture}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 089; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that each fixed treewidth class has a uniform $L_1$ distortion bound for arbitrary positive weighted shortest-path metrics; In manuscript 2 (family 089; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that arbitrary positive edge lengths on finite planar graphs admit a common constant-distortion embedding into real $L_1$.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For every integer $k\ge2$, the claim gives a constant $C_k<\infty$ such that any finite connected graph whose tree decomposition has every bag cardinality bounded by $k$, with arbitrary strictly positive edge lengths, has a real $L^1$ embedding $F$ satisfying
\[
 d_{G,\ell}(u,v)\le\|F(u)-F(v)\|_1\le C_kd_{G,\ell}(u,v)
 \qquad(u,v\in V(G)).
\]
The constant depends only on bag size, not on vertex count, decomposition depth or edge-length ratios. Bag size $k$ corresponds to treewidth at most $k-1$.

\paragraph{Manuscript 2.}
Assign arbitrary positive real lengths to the edges of any finite planar graph that is connected. The claim supplies a real $L^1$ embedding and one constant $C<\infty$, common to all these graphs, satisfying
\[
 d_G(x,y)\le\|f(x)-f(y)\|_1\le C d_G(x,y)\quad(x,y\in V(G)).
\]
No dependence on vertex count, particular planar embedding or edge-length ratios occurs in $C$.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 089; 2026-09-23), consistent cut laws are built directly on decomposition bags and glued by a Markov construction. A common-submeasure transport estimate preserves separation along long bag paths; threshold sweeps and protected anchors keep a useful distance scale alive.

For manuscript 2 (family 089; 2026-09-23), measurable labels define cut distances whose upper comparison holds deterministically. Random chart changes, portals, and component ancestry supply a lower separation probability, and packing control permits the cut families to be assembled at all scales.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
Planar graphs and each fixed treewidth class satisfy the claimed embedding bound. The problem includes every proper minor-closed class, so a bound only for those two settings does not establish its universal quantifier.
