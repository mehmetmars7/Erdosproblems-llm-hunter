\section*{Periodic tilings by bounded measurable sets in three dimensions}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Three-dimensional nonperiodic translational tile}: A finite lattice tile in $\mathbb Z^3$ is claimed to tile while admitting no tiling invariant under a finite-index translation group. Its thickening by unit cubes has the same obstruction in Euclidean space, including tilings by real translation vectors.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} The construction asserts a finite nonempty $T\subset\mathbb Z^3$ admitting a disjoint translational tiling $A\oplus T=\mathbb Z^3$, while no such translation set $A$ is invariant under any finite-index subgroup of $\mathbb Z^3$. Its bounded measurable thickening $\Omega=T+[0,1]^3$ tiles $\mathbb R^3$ up to null sets, but no translation set for such a tiling remains unchanged under every translation in any rank-three lattice.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Three-dimensional nonperiodic translational tile}: A Sudoku-type word obstruction is encoded as several dependence and activation tests in a cyclic group. Stacking combines the tests into one tile, and an explicit model supplies a tiling. Marked component shapes rigidify any hypothetical fully periodic Euclidean tiling onto a grid, from which it would descend to a forbidden periodic solution of the encoded tests.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Three-dimensional nonperiodic translational tile}: The manuscript’s result has two distinct conclusions: a finite nonempty Z\textasciicircum{}3 tile with no finite-index-invariant complement, and its bounded positive-volume cube thickening with no full-rank-lattice-invariant real translation set despite an almost-everywhere tiling. The second clause supplies an exact counterexample to this question. The real conclusion explicitly permits arbitrary real translation vectors.

The claimed counterexample lies within the question’s hypotheses and contradicts its universal conclusion.
