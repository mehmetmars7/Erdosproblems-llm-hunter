\section*{Exact trace certificates and a regular counterexample normal form for Caccetta--Haggkvist}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
\textit{Seymour second neighborhoods}: The paper claims that every finite nonempty oriented graph has a vertex whose second out-neighborhood is at least as large as its first. The second neighborhood excludes vertices already reachable in one step.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Mathematical claim 1.} For a nonempty finite oriented graph $D$, with no loops or oppositely directed pair of edges, let $N_1^+(v)$ be the out-neighbors and $N_2^+(v)$ the vertices at directed distance exactly two. The claimed theorem gives \[\exists v\in V(D):\quad|N_1^+(v)|\le|N_2^+(v)|.\] For triangle-free $D$ a corollary yields a vertex with $d^+(v)\le|Z(v)|$ and $n-1\ge2d^+(v)+d^-(v)$, where $Z(v)$ consists of non-neighbors excluding $v$. Hence $\delta^+\ge n/3$ and $\delta^-\ge n/3$ force a directed triangle; a digraph regular in both directions with degree $r$ and girth four has $n\ge3r+1$. These consequences require the specified in-degree conditions and do not give the full out-degree-only Caccetta--H\"aggkvist assertion.

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
\textit{Seymour second neighborhoods}: A smallest counterexample is pruned to yield strict neighborhood inequalities for all proper vertex sets. Replacing vertices by transitive tournaments strengthens these to a discrete concavity inequality. Extremal families of ordered pairs and an augmented bipartite matching are then controlled by two factorizations of one matrix, contradicting that inequality.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
\textit{Seymour second neighborhoods}: The directed-cycle corollary obtains a directed triangle under BOTH minimum indegree and outdegree at least n/3, adding an indegree hypothesis to the recorded outdegree-only assertion.
