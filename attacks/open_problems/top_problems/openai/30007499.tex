\section*{Superpolynomial worst-case trace sample lower bounds}

\subsection{OpenAI's claim}
OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that worst-case exact binary trace reconstruction needs $n^{\Omega(\log\log n)}$ samples at any fixed deletion probability, even with unrestricted computation.

\subsection{Mathematical statement of the claimed result}
\paragraph{Manuscript 1.}
For deletion probability $q\in(0,1)$ and required success probability $s\in(0,1]$, let $T_{q,s}(n)$ be the minimum number of independent ordinary deletion traces sufficient to reconstruct every binary word of length $n$ with success at least $s$. For every fixed $s$ and every fixed
\[
 0<c<\frac1{4\log2},
\]
the claimed lower bound, along any sequence $n\to\infty$ with $q^3\log n\to\infty$, is eventually
\[
 T_{q,s}(n)\ge n^{c\log(q^3\log n)}.
\]
In particular fixed $q,s$ give $T_{q,s}(n)=n^{\Omega(\log\log n)}$ and $T_{q,s}(n)/n^A\to\infty$ for each fixed $A>0$. If $D_q(x)$ is the one-trace probability law, the cited consequence for every fixed $q\in(0,1)$ and $A>0$ is
\[
 n^A\min_{\substack{x\ne y\\x,y\in\{0,1\}^n}}
             d_{\rm TV}(D_q(x),D_q(y))\longrightarrow0.
\]

\subsection{Outline of the argument}
For manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-09-24), nested signed words hide fair orientation bits inside overlapping windows. An auxiliary observation retains side data while projecting to genuine traces; likelihood-score induction forces a large binary subtree for every surviving term and bounds the distance between the two trace laws.

\subsection{Scope relative to this problem}
For each fixed $q\in(0,1)$ and fixed success probability $s>0$, the main theorem gives a lower bound of $n^{\Omega(\log\log n)}$ for all sufficiently large $n$. It allows unlimited computation and requires success on every length-$n$ binary word. These are exactly the statistical conventions and quantifiers in the question.
