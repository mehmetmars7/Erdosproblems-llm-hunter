Count ordered pairs of positive powerful numbers, allowing equal summands. In the bound $n^{o(1)}$, the intended growth assertion is subpower upper growth: for every $\epsilon>0$, the count is at most $n^\epsilon$ eventually up to a constant. It does not assert existence of a representation for every integer. \sref{2397}{1} \sref{2397}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $A$ be the set of powerful numbers (if $p\mid n$ then $p^2\mid n$). Is it true that $ 1_A\ast 1_A(n)=n^{o(1)} $ for every $n$? \sref{2397}{1}

\paragraph{Scope and interpretation.}

The subpower notation is treated as an upper bound, with zero representation counts permitted. A positive two-sided asymptotic would be a different assertion. \sref{2397}{1}
