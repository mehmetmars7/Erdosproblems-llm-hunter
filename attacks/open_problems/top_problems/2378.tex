\section{Distinct Exponents in the Prime Factorization of a Factorial}\label{2378}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2378 · Number Theory}

For a nonnegative integer $n$, $n!=\prod_{j=1}^n j$ with $0!=1$. Factorial arguments and any factors or denominator indices below are integers in the explicitly stated ranges. Distinctness is imposed only where specified. \sref{2378}{1} \sref{2378}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $ n! = \prod_i p_i^{k_i} $ is the factorisation into distinct primes then let $h(n)$ count the number of distinct exponents $k_i$.
Prove that there exists some $c>0$ such that $ h(n) \sim c \left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/2} $ as $n\to \infty$. \sref{2378}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove existence of the normalized limit and, if the heuristic is correct, identify it as sqrt(2 pi). \sref{2378}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Does the number of different prime exponents appearing in a factorial have the proposed square-root-over-logarithm asymptotic with a fixed leading constant?
