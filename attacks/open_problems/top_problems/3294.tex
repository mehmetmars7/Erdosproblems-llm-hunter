\section*{Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture}\label{3294}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3294 · OPG-2108 · Graph Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite family of distinct finite sets, and let
$U=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal F}A$ be its ground set. Assume $U\ne\varnothing$,
so at least one member of $\mathcal F$ is nonempty. The family is
union-closed if
\[
                 A,B\in\mathcal F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                 A\cup B\in\mathcal F.
\]
It need not contain the empty set and need not be closed under
intersections or taking subsets. Finiteness and union closure imply
that $U$ itself belongs to $\mathcal F$.

For $x\in U$, define its frequency
$d_{\mathcal F}(x)=|\{A\in\mathcal F:x\in A\}|$. Frankl's conjecture
asserts that some ground-set element occurs in at least half the
members:
\[
               \exists x\in U:\quad
               d_{\mathcal F}(x)\ge\frac{|\mathcal F|}{2}.
\]
Since frequencies are integers, the threshold is
$\lceil|\mathcal F|/2\rceil$. Every distinct member is counted once;
this is a family of sets rather than a multiset with chosen weights.

Equivalently, choose a member $A$ uniformly from $\mathcal F$. The
assertion is that at least one $x$ has $\mathbb P(x\in A)\ge1/2$.
That element may depend on the whole family. The excluded case
$\mathcal F=\{\varnothing\}$ would have no possible witness. The
coefficient one half cannot be increased in general: for the full
power set of any nonempty finite ground set, each element belongs to
exactly half of its members.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite nontrivial union-closed family have an element belonging to at least half of its sets?
