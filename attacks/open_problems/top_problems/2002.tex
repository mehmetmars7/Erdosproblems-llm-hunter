\section{Long Runs of Widely Separated Consecutive Primes}\label{2002}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2002 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



A run of consecutive primes is a block $p_j,p_{j+1},\ldots,p_{j+r-1}$ from the full increasing prime sequence, with no intervening primes omitted. The pairwise separation requirement is equivalent to $p_{j+t+1}-p_{j+t}>c_2$ for every adjacent pair in the run. The constants $c_1,c_2$ are fixed before the sufficiently large threshold for $x$ is chosen.

$p_j$ denotes the $j$th positive prime in increasing order, starting with $p_1=2$. The prime-counting function is $\pi(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\text{ is prime}\}$. Consecutive primes are consecutive in this full sequence. \sref{2002}{1} \sref{2002}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $c_1,c_2>0$. Is it true that, for any sufficiently large $x$, there exist more than $c_1\log x$ many consecutive primes $\leq x$ such that the difference between any two is $>c_2$? \sref{2002}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Below every sufficiently large bound, can one find more than any prescribed logarithmic number of consecutive primes separated by any prescribed fixed distance?
