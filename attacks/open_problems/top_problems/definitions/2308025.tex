\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 8.25}\label{2308025}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2308025 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. Let $A$ be the boundary restrictions to $\mathbb T$ of the disc algebra: functions continuous on $\overline{\mathbb D}$ and analytic in $\mathbb D$. Let $\psi:\mathbb T\to\mathbb T$ be an orientation-reversing homeomorphism and set \[A_\psi=\{f\in C(\mathbb T):f\in A\text{ and }f\circ\psi\in A\}.\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Characterize the orientation-reversing homeomorphisms $\psi$ for which $A_\psi$ consists exactly of the constant functions. \sref{2308025}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
When can a circle function and its orientation-reversed reparametrization both lie in the disc algebra only if the function is constant?
