\section{Kirby Problem 4.13}\label{2889}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2889 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $B^4$ be the standard closed four-ball and let $K\subset S^3=\partial B^4$ be a smooth knot. Its zero trace $X_K$ is obtained by attaching a two-handle $D^2\times D^2$ to $B^4$ along $K$ with framing zero relative to the Seifert longitude. A smooth $R^4$-homeomorph is a smooth manifold $R$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^4$, allowing the standard smooth structure. It is \emph{large} if it contains a compact subset admitting no smooth embedding into standard $\mathbb R^4$. Define
\[
\mathcal R_K=\{[R]_{\mathrm{diff}}:R\text{ is an }R^4\text{-homeomorph and }X_K\text{ smoothly embeds in }R\}.
\]
The notation $[R]_{\mathrm{diff}}$ denotes a diffeomorphism class. A knot is smoothly slice if it bounds a smoothly embedded proper disk in $B^4$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Answer both questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every large $R^4$-homeomorph $R$, does there exist a knot $K$ that is not smoothly slice such that $[R]_{\mathrm{diff}}\in\mathcal R_K$?
\item[(b)] Do there exist knots $K_1,K_2,\ldots$ such that $\mathcal R_{K_i}\ne\mathcal R_{K_j}$ for every $i\ne j$?
\end{enumerate}
The second assertion requires pairwise different sets of smooth homeomorphs, rather than merely different knots or different embedded traces. \sref{2889}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every large smooth manifold homeomorphic to Euclidean four-space contain the zero trace of a knot that is not smoothly slice, and can infinitely many different trace-embedding sets be distinguished?
