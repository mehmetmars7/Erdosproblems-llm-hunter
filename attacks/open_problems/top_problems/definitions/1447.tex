\section{Casas-Alvero Conjecture}\label{1447}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1447 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $f\in K[x]$ be a monic polynomial of degree $d\ge1$. For $i\ge1$, $f^{(i)}$ denotes the $i$th ordinary formal derivative. The condition that $f$ shares a factor with $f^{(i)}$ means $\deg\gcd(f,f^{(i)})\ge1$; the common factor may depend on $i$. Monicity fixes the normalization implicit in the displayed conclusion: an arbitrary polynomial with leading coefficient $c\ne0$ is normalized to $f/c$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such $K,d,f$, does
\[
 \bigl(\deg\gcd(f,f^{(i)})\ge1\ \text{for each }1\le i\le d-1\bigr)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f(x)=(x-a)^d\ \text{for some }a\in K
\]
hold? In unnormalized form the conclusion is $f(x)=c(x-a)^d$, with $c$ its leading coefficient. Different derivatives need not share the same chosen root with $f$. \sref{1447}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If a characteristic-zero polynomial has a nonconstant common factor with each derivative below its degree, must all its roots coincide, up to its leading scalar?
