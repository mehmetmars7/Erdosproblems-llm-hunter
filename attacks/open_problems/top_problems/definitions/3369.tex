\section*{Lindelöf hypothesis}\label{3369}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3369 · OPG-37255 · Number Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\zeta$ denote the meromorphic continuation of
$\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}$ from $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ to the complex plane.
For real $t$, the point $1/2+it$ lies on the critical line. The
Lindel\"of hypothesis is the pointwise growth assertion
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_\varepsilon>0\ \forall |t|\ge2,
 \qquad |\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|\le C_\varepsilon |t|^\varepsilon.
\]
The constant may depend on $\varepsilon$ and must not depend on $t$.
Replacing $2$ by any fixed positive lower cutoff has no effect after
changing the constant. One may also state the hypothesis as
$\zeta(1/2+it)=o(|t|^\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$:
apply the displayed bound with $\varepsilon/2$ to obtain this form.

The absolute value is the complex modulus, so the bound controls the
whole value of the function. It is required at every sufficiently
large height, rather than merely on average over an interval or
outside an exceptional set. Proving one fixed positive power bound
does not establish the universal family of bounds above. Conversely,
the conjecture does not assert boundedness by a constant or by a
fixed power of $\log |t|$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the size of the zeta function on the critical line grow more slowly than every positive power of the height?
