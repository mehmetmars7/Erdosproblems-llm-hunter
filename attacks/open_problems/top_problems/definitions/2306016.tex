\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.16}\label{2306016}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306016 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$, and $\mathbb D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}$. Let $S$ be the class of analytic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$, $f^{\prime}(0)=1$, with expansion $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_nz^n$ and $a_1=1$. A function in $S$ is starlike if its image is starlike about $0$: $tw\in f(\mathbb D)$ for every $w\in f(\mathbb D)$ and $0\le t\le1$. Write $S^*$ for the starlike subclass and $k(z)=z/(1-z)^2$ for the Koebe function, with $k^{\prime}(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)^3$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $z_0\in\mathbb D$, does the equality of value regions \[\{f^{\prime}(z_0):f\in S^*\}=\{k^{\prime}(z):|z|\le|z_0|\}\] hold? The source update records Hummel’s counterexample to this version of the Marx conjecture. \sref{2306016}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the derivative value region for normalized starlike functions equal the Koebe derivative image of the corresponding closed disk? The source records a counterexample.
