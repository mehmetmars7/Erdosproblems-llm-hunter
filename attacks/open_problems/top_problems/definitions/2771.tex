\section*{Kirby Problem 2.23}\label{2771}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2771 · KP-2.23 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $B$ and $F$ be closed connected oriented surfaces of genera at least two.
A smooth surface bundle over a surface is a smooth locally trivial map
$p:E\to B$ with fiber $F$ and orientation-preserving transition maps. Its total
space $E$ is a closed oriented smooth $4$-manifold.
A real hyperbolic metric on $E$ is a Riemannian metric with sectional curvature
identically $-1$. A metric of strictly negative curvature has
$K_E(\sigma)<0$ for every two-dimensional tangent plane $\sigma$; the curvature
need not be constant. On a closed manifold these metrics are complete.

Does there exist such a bundle whose total space admits a real hyperbolic
metric? More generally, does one exist whose total space admits any metric
of strictly negative sectional curvature?
The genera, bundle, and metric may all be chosen freely. The bundle projection
need not be a Riemannian submersion, and the fibers need not be totally
geodesic. Thus the metric condition is on the total four-dimensional manifold,
not merely on its surface fibers and base. The constant-curvature existence
question implies the variable-curvature one.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a closed surface bundle over a closed surface carry a real hyperbolic metric, or any negatively curved metric?
