\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.39}\label{2548}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2548 · KOU-21.39 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group $G$ is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely
many elements is finite. A subgroup $C\le G$ is characteristic if
$\alpha(C)=C$ for every automorphism $\alpha$ of $G$.
The group is characteristically simple if it is nontrivial and its only
characteristic subgroups are $1$ and $G$; proper normal subgroups are
not excluded unless they are characteristic.

The automorphism group acts on the underlying set of $G$ by evaluation.
Having finitely many automorphism orbits means that there exist
$g_1,\ldots,g_t\in G$ such that every $g\in G$ has the form
$\alpha(g_i)$ for some $i$ and some $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.
These are orbits of all abstract automorphisms, not only conjugation
orbits.

A group is residually finite if each nonidentity $g$ survives in some
finite quotient: there is a homomorphism $\pi:G\to Q$ to a finite group
with $\pi(g)\ne1$. The question asks whether there exists a locally
finite, characteristically simple group with finitely many automorphism
orbits which is not residually finite.

For a proposed witness the last condition means that some fixed
$g\ne1$ is killed by every homomorphism into every finite group.
This must coexist with finiteness of each finitely generated subgroup;
local finiteness alone does not specify quotient separation. No countability,
finite generation, or bound on the sizes of the finite subgroups is
assumed. The requested example must be infinite, since any finite group
separates its elements through its identity map.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a locally finite characteristically simple group have finitely many automorphism orbits while failing residual finiteness?
