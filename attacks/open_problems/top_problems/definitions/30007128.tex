\section*{Gromov width of symmetric polar products}\label{30007128}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $n\geq2$ and origin-symmetric convex body $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ with polar
\[
K^\circ=\{y:\langle x,y\rangle\leq1\text{ for all }x\in K\},
\]
is the Gromov width of $\operatorname{int}(K\times K^\circ)$ in standard symplectic $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ equal to $4$? Equivalently, can every open standard ball of capacity $c<4$ be smoothly symplectically embedded into that interior, while none of capacity $c>4$ can?
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every $n\geq2$ and origin-symmetric convex body $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, with $K^\circ=\{y:\langle x,y\rangle\leq1\text{ for all }x\in K\}$, does $\operatorname{int}(K\times K^\circ)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ with the standard symplectic structure have Gromov width $4$; equivalently, does every open standard ball of capacity $c<4$ admit a smooth symplectic embedding into it, and none of capacity $c>4$?
