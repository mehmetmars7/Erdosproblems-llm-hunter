\section*{Pallet Loading}\label{5500055}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500055 · AMR-054-0055 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The input is five positive integers $A,B,a,b,n$, encoded in binary.
The container is $[0,A]\times[0,B]$. Each of $n$ congruent small
rectangles has side lengths $a,b$, and may be placed with either
orientation $(a,b)$ or $(b,a)$ parallel to the coordinate axes.
Translations are allowed; arbitrary-angle rotations are not.

A feasible packing consists of closed rectangles $R_1,\ldots,R_n$
contained in the container and having pairwise disjoint interiors.
Boundary contact is allowed. Decide whether such a packing exists,
and determine the complexity in terms of
\[
 L=\operatorname{bits}(A)+\operatorname{bits}(B)+
   \operatorname{bits}(a)+\operatorname{bits}(b)+\operatorname{bits}(n).
\]
Positive rational dimensions give the corresponding version with
numerator and denominator lengths counted as input.

The compact binary description is central. An algorithm polynomial
in the numerical value of $n$ is not necessarily polynomial in
$L$, and listing all rectangle positions may itself require
exponentially many entries. Determine, in particular, whether
polynomial-size verifiable certificates exist and whether a
polynomial-time decision algorithm is possible.
The packing is unrestricted beyond axis alignment: it need not
arise by recursive guillotine cuts or by one fixed repeating pattern.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the bit complexity of deciding whether a binary-specified
number of congruent rectangles fits in a larger rectangle, allowing
quarter-turn rotations and arbitrary packing patterns.
