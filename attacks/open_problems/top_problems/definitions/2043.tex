\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there some constant $c>0$ such that for every $n\geq 1$ there exists some $\delta_k\in \{-1,0,1\}$ for $1\leq k\leq n$ with $ 0< \left\lvert \sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\frac{\delta_k}{k}\right\rvert < \frac{c}{2^n}? $ Is it true that for sufficiently large $n$, for any $\delta_k\in \{-1,0,1\}$, $ \left\lvert \sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\frac{\delta_k}{k}\right\rvert > \frac{1}{[1,\ldots,n]} $ whenever the left-hand side is not zero? \sref{2043}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How small can a nonzero signed reciprocal sum be, and can the elementary least-common-multiple lower bound ever remain attainable at large lengths?
