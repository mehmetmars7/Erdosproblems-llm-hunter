\section*{General Unfoldings of Nonconvex Polyhedra}\label{5500043}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500043 · AMR-054-0043 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $P\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a compact connected embedded polyhedral
surface without boundary, equipped with the intrinsic length metric
on its polygonal faces. It may be nonconvex and may have positive
genus. A general cut consists of finitely many polygonal arcs on
the surface; these arcs may cross face interiors and need not lie
on the original edges.

After cutting, take the surface with its separate boundary copies
of the cuts. A general unfolding develops this cut surface into
the plane isometrically on each remaining face piece, producing
one polygonal region with connected interior and no overlap of
distinct interiors. Boundary contacts can be allowed, but separate
pieces merely touching at points do not supply the required
connected interior.

Does every $P$ admit a general unfolding? The cut graph may be
chosen freely, subject to finiteness and retention of one piece.
No upper bound on the number of cuts or the thinness of the
resulting region is imposed.

Allowing cuts through faces distinguishes this question from edge
unfolding. Keeping a single piece distinguishes it from arbitrary
dissection into separately placed triangles. The question concerns
the existence of the final isometric planar layout; it does not
require a collision-free folding animation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every closed polyhedral surface, including nonconvex ones, be
cut along finitely many surface arcs and flattened into one piece
without interior overlap?
