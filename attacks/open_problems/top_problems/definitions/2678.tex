\section*{Kirby Problem 1.19}\label{2678}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2678 · KP-1.19 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $Y=Y_1\#Y_2$ be a connected sum of closed oriented $3$-manifolds with
$Y_i\not\cong S^3$. The separating sphere $S$ has a product neighborhood
$S^2\times[-1,1]$.
A sphere twist $\Phi:Y\to Y$ is the identity off this neighborhood and, within it,
rotates $S^2$ along a loop representing the nonzero element of
$\pi_1(\operatorname{SO}(3))\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The rotations equal the identity
near the two ends, so this defines a diffeomorphism of $Y$.
Assume that $\Phi$ is not isotopic to the identity.

Must there be a smooth embedded circle $K\subset Y$ such that $K$ and $\Phi(K)$
are not ambiently isotopic? Ambient isotopy means a continuous family of
diffeomorphisms $h_t:Y\to Y$ with $h_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $h_1(K)=\Phi(K)$.
The knot need not be null-homologous or null-homotopic.

The given diffeomorphism already identifies the two embedded pairs
$(Y,K)$ and $(Y,\Phi(K))$. The issue is whether some knot detects that this
particular mapping class is nontrivial, through failure of an identification
isotopic to the identity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a nontrivial twist about a connected-sum sphere always be detected by moving some knot to a different isotopy class?
