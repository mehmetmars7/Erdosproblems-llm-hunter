\section{Covering Integers by Late Parts of Prime Residue Classes}\label{2027}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2027 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

Choose representatives $0\leq a_p<p$; otherwise changing a representative changes the cutoff $t\geq k$. The question is about the union of these truncated prime progressions, not merely about their untruncated residue classes. \sref{2027}{1} \sref{2027}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $k\geq 3$. Is there a choice of congruence classes $a_p\pmod{p}$ for every prime $p$ such that all sufficiently large integers can be written as $a_p+tp$ for some prime $p$ and integer $t\geq k$? \sref{2027}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can one residue modulo each prime cover every sufficiently large integer even after omitting a prescribed number of initial terms of each progression?
