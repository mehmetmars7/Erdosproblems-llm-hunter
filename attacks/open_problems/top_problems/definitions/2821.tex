\section*{Kirby Problem 3.23}\label{2821}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2821 · KP-3.23 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Write an orientable complete finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold as
$M=\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$, with $\Gamma$ torsion-free and discrete in
$\operatorname{Isom}^+(\mathbb H^3)$. A number $\varepsilon>0$ is a
Margulis number for $M$ if, for every $x\in\mathbb H^3$ and $a,b\in\Gamma$,
\[
 \max\{d(x,ax),d(x,bx)\}<\varepsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad ab=ba.
\]
Let $\mu(M)$ be the supremum of such $\varepsilon$; equivalently it is
the infimum of the displayed maximum over $x$ and noncommuting pairs $a,b$.
Use displacement lengths, not half-lengths or injectivity radii, throughout.

Define the three-dimensional constant in the class specified by the source by
\[
 \mu_3=\inf_M\mu(M),
\]
where $M$ ranges over all orientable complete finite-volume hyperbolic
three-manifolds. Determine $\mu_3$. More specifically, is it attained uniquely,
up to isometry, by the Weeks manifold $W$, with the source's candidate value
$\mu(W)\approx0.77442$? Here $W$ denotes the closed orientable manifold
known as the Weeks manifold. A numerical value alone does not establish the
universal lower bound or uniqueness, and orbifolds with torsion are not included
in this infimum.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the optimal universal Margulis displacement bound for orientable finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifolds, and decide whether only the Weeks manifold attains it.
