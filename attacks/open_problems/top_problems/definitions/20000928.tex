\section*{Endpoint-deck gluing for one-edge deletion at a 3-uniform Ramsey threshold}\label{20000928}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20000928 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0053 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $n\ge4$, let $R=r_3(n,n)$ be the least integer $N$ such that every
red--blue coloring of the triples of an $N$-element set has an
$n$-element subset whose triples all have the same color.
Fix a triple $e\in\binom{[R]}3$, and form the hypergraph
$H=K_R^{(3)}-e$, whose edge set is
$\binom{[R]}3\setminus\{e\}$. The missing triple receives no color.
A monochromatic $K_n^{(3)}$ in $H$ means an $n$-element subset
$S\subseteq[R]$ such that all its triples belong to $E(H)$ and have
the same color. In particular, $e$ cannot be a subset of $S$.

The question of Dudek and R\"odl is whether every red--blue coloring
of $E(H)$ necessarily contains such a monochromatic complete
hypergraph, for every $n\ge4$. The choice of the deleted edge is
irrelevant up to relabeling, but the coloring is otherwise unrestricted.
The ordinary Ramsey property of $K_R^{(3)}$ does not immediately
answer this: extending a coloring to the missing edge may produce
only complete monochromatic sets that use it.
The nontrivial range is stated explicitly. If $n=3$, then
$r_3(3,3)=3$, deleting the sole edge leaves no $K_3^{(3)}$, and the
unqualified assertion fails. This boundary case is excluded rather
than silently asserted as part of the conjecture.
\subsection{Short English statement}
At the diagonal triple-system Ramsey threshold, does deleting a single triple preserve the Ramsey property?
