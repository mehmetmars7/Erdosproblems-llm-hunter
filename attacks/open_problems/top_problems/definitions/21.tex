\section{Kaplansky's Conjectures}\label{21}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 21 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.


\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $G$ be torsion-free and $K$ a field. The group ring $K[G]$ consists of finite sums of group elements with coefficients in $K$, with multiplication induced by the group law. The zero-divisor assertion asks whether $ab=0$ in $K[G]$ forces $a=0$ or $b=0$. The idempotent assertion asks whether $e^2=e$ forces $e=0$ or $e=1$. The integral unit assertion asks whether every invertible element of $\mathbb Z[G]$ has the form $\pm g$ for $g\in G$. These are three assertions; the last uses integral coefficients.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation references:} \sref{21}{1}, \sref{21}{2}.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do the zero-divisor, idempotent, and integral-unit assertions hold for every torsion-free group?
