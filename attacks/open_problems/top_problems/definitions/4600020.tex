\section*{Equal-entropy SFT covers}\label{4600020}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600020 · AMR-045-0020 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix $d\ge2$. A $\mathbb Z^d$ subshift is a nonempty closed,
translation-invariant set $Y\subset B^{\mathbb Z^d}$ over a finite
alphabet. A shift of finite type (SFT) is defined by excluding finitely
many finite patterns and all their translates. A factor map is a
continuous surjection commuting with every translation; equivalently,
it applies one finite-window local rule at every site. A subshift is
sofic if it is a factor of some $\mathbb Z^d$ SFT.

For a subshift $Z$, let $\mathcal L_{Q_n}(Z)$ be the patterns it realizes
on $Q_n=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^d$. Its entropy per site is
\[
 h(Z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^d}
                         \log|\mathcal L_{Q_n}(Z)|.
\]
Use natural logarithms and the same normalization for all spaces.
The question is whether every such sofic $Y$ admits an SFT $X$ and
a factor map $\pi:X\to Y$ with $h(X)=h(Y)$.

The alphabet and the local constraints of $X$ may be chosen freely,
but its lattice dimension must remain $d$. Equality is exact; an SFT
cover whose entropy is merely arbitrarily close to $h(Y)$ does not
answer the question. No injectivity, finite-to-one condition, or mixing
hypothesis is imposed on the cover. The problem includes zero-entropy
sofic shifts and asks for an equal-entropy finite-type realization in
every dimension greater than one.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every multidimensional sofic shift be covered by a finite-type shift without adding entropy?
