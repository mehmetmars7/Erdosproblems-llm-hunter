\section{Volume Maximizing Convex Shape}\label{5500062}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5500062 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $C$ be a convex planar region of area one, with polygonal or smooth boundary. Choose boundary points $x,y$ whose two intervening boundary arcs have equal length. A perimeter-halving folding identifies these arcs in opposite directions by arclength, gluing the arc from $x$ to $y$ to that from $y$ to $x$. The resulting intrinsic surface has the unique convex realization in $\mathbb R^3$ described in the source, allowing a flat realization of volume zero.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Among all such unit-area convex regions $C$ and all their perimeter-halving foldings, determine the shape that gives the largest enclosed three-dimensional volume and the maximum volume. \sref{5500062}{2}
