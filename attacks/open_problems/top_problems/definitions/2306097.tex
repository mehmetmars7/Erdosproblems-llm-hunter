\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.97}\label{2306097}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306097 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. For a nonconstant holomorphic function $f$ on a domain $D$, its valence is $v_D(f)=\sup_{w\in\mathbb C}n_D(w,f)\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$, where $n_D(w,f)$ counts the zeros of $f-w$ in $D$ with multiplicity. The function is $p$-valent if $v_D(f)\leq p$. Fix an integer $p\geq1$ and a holomorphic $p$-valent function $f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_jz^j$ in $\mathbb D$; thus $f(0)=0$ but no condition $a_1=1$ is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $n>p$, does
\[|a_n|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{2k(n+p)!}{(p+k)!(p-k)!(n-p-1)!(n^2-k^2)}|a_k|\]
hold? Determine whether the bound is sharp with respect to $p,n$ and the initial coefficient magnitudes $|a_1|,\ldots,|a_p|$, as asserted in Goodman's conjecture. \sref{2306097}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Bound every later coefficient of a multivalent function by the stated sharp weighted sum of its first coefficients.
