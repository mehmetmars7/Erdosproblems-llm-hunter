\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $N\geq 1$ and let $k(N)$ denote the smallest $k$ such that there exist $N\leq n_1<\cdots <n_k$ with $ 1=\frac{1}{n_1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n_k}. $ Is it true that $ \lim_{N\to \infty} k(N)-(e-1)N=\infty? $ \sref{2035}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Does the shortest expansion of one using distinct denominators above a threshold exceed its natural linear main term by an amount tending to infinity?
