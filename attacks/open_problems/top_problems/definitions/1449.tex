\section{Crouzeix's Conjecture}\label{1449}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1449 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\ge1$ and $A\in M_n(\mathbb C)$. For vectors use the Euclidean norm, and for matrices use its induced operator norm $\|B\|=\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}\|Bx\|_2$. The numerical range is
\[
 W(A)=\{x^*Ax:x\in\mathbb C^n,\ x^*x=1\},
\]
where $x^*$ is the conjugate transpose. This is a compact convex set containing the spectrum $\sigma(A)$. An analytic function on $W(A)$ means a function $f$ holomorphic on an open neighborhood $U$ of $W(A)$. Its matrix value is defined by holomorphic functional calculus,
\[
 f(A)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma f(z)(zI-A)^{-1}\,dz,
\]
where $\Gamma$ is a positively oriented contour in $U$ enclosing $W(A)$ and contained in a simply connected smaller neighborhood; the value is independent of this choice.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every dimension $n$, every complex matrix $A$, and every such holomorphic $f$, is
\[
 \|f(A)\|\le2\max_{z\in W(A)}|f(z)|
\]
true? The matrix norm is the Euclidean operator norm, and the proposed constant is the universal number $2$. \sref{1449}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the operator norm of every analytic function of a complex matrix at most twice the maximum magnitude of that function on the matrix’s numerical range?
