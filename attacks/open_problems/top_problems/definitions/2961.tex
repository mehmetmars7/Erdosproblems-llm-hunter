\section*{Kirby Problem 4.85}\label{2961}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2961 · KP-4.85 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a closed orientable smooth $4$-manifold. Let
$D=\operatorname{Diff}_0(X)$ be the identity component of its
diffeomorphism group in the $C^\infty$ topology; its elements are
diffeomorphisms smoothly isotopic to the identity.
Use the commutator convention $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
For $f$ in the commutator subgroup $[D,D]$, define its commutator
length by
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_D(f)=\min\{r\ge0:
           f=[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_r,b_r],\ a_i,b_i\in D\}.
\]
The empty product represents the identity.

A group is perfect if it equals its commutator subgroup. It is
uniformly perfect if it is perfect and some finite integer $C$
bounds the commutator length of every element. In the present
setting $D$ is perfect, so the question is whether its commutator
length can be unbounded.

Does there exist such a manifold $X$ for which $D$ is not
uniformly perfect? Equivalently, for each integer $N\ge0$, there
must be $f_N\in D$ which is not a product of $N$ commutators
of elements of $D$.

The commutator factors are arbitrary identity-isotopic diffeomorphisms;
no common support, isotopy length, derivative bound, or fragmentation
restriction is imposed. The desired unboundedness occurs in one
fixed manifold's group. Allowing the manifold itself to vary with
$N$ would not prove this existence assertion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can commutator length be unbounded in the identity component of the diffeomorphism group of a closed orientable smooth four-manifold?
