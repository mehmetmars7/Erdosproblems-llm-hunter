\section*{Serial symmetric exchanges}\label{3000077}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000077 · AMR-029-0077 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a finite matroid and $A,B$ two bases. Recall that
matroid independence is hereditary and satisfies exchange, and
that a basis is a maximal independent set. Fix $X\subseteq A$
with $|X|=m$. A serial symmetric exchange consists of a set
$Y\subseteq B$, $|Y|=m$, and orderings $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ of $X$
and $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ of $Y$ such that, for every $0\le i\le m$,
both
\[
 (A\setminus\{x_1,\ldots,x_i\})\cup\{y_1,\ldots,y_i\},
 \qquad
 (B\setminus\{y_1,\ldots,y_i\})\cup\{x_1,\ldots,x_i\}
\]
are bases.

Must such a choice exist for every matroid, every pair of bases,
and every subset $X$ of the first basis?
The two lists may be ordered in whichever way is most favorable;
the question does not prescribe their order in advance.

The same paired sequence must keep both evolving sets as bases
at every intermediate stage. A simultaneous exchange of the full
sets at the final stage alone is weaker, as is maintaining only
one of the two bases during the process. The bases $A,B$ may
overlap; their elements are treated as actual ground elements,
not automatically as disjoint copies. In particular a common
element may occur in both lists when consistent with the displayed
basis conditions. The empty-subset case requires no exchanges.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can any chosen subset of one matroid basis be exchanged with
an equally sized subset of another, one pair at a time, keeping both sets bases throughout?
