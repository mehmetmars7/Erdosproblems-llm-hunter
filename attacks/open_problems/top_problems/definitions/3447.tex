\section*{Rendezvous on a line}\label{3447}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3447 · OPG-581 · Miscellaneous · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Two players start distance two apart on a line, at global coordinates
$0$ and $2$, at time zero. Neither knows the direction of the other
or has a shared orientation. A strategy is a probability distribution
$\mu$ on continuous paths $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ with $f(0)=0$
and $|f(t)-f(s)|\le|t-s|$; thus speed is at most one. Paths describe
displacement in a player's local coordinates before any meeting.

In the symmetric model both players independently sample $f,g$
from the same distribution $\mu$. Their local orientations
$\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{-1,1\}$ are independent fair signs,
independent of the path samples. Define
\[
 T=\inf\{t\ge0:\epsilon_1 f(t)=2+\epsilon_2g(t)\},
 \qquad R=\inf_\mu\mathbb E[T],
\]
with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$ for a missed meeting. The problem is
to determine $R$ exactly. The infimum is over measurable strategy
distributions for which the hitting-time expectation is defined,
allowing the value infinity.
No shared random bits, preassigned distinct roles, or communication
before meeting is allowed. Both players know the separation and
start simultaneously. The source's additional proposed value is
$R=17/4$ in this normalization. Rescaling initial separation would
rescale time, so the specified distance two is essential.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the smallest expected meeting time for two indistinguishable players distance two apart on an unoriented line, using the same independently randomized strategy?
