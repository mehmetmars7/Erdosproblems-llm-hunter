\section*{Kirby Problem 3.26}\label{2824}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2824 · KP-3.26 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite graph $\Lambda$, its right-angled Artin group is
$A_\Lambda=\langle V(\Lambda)\mid [u,v]=1\text{ for }\{u,v\}\in E(\Lambda)\rangle$.
Let $M$ be a complete finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold, and define
\[
 d(M)=\min\{[\pi_1(M):H]:H\text{ embeds in }A_\Lambda
                    \text{ for some finite }\Lambda\}.
\]
This minimum is finite by virtual specialness. Choose a connected cover
$M^1\to M$ of degree $d(M)$. Among the finite graphs for which
$\pi_1(M^1)\hookrightarrow A_\Lambda$, measure how small $\Lambda$ can be.

Obtain effective quantitative control of this minimum complexity from
explicit data for $M$, for example a triangulation of a compact core. The
source proposes several separate complexity measures: number of vertices,
cohomological dimension of $A_\Lambda$ (the maximum clique size), chromatic
number, or graph diameter. A diameter convention must be specified for
disconnected graphs; the vertex-count version has no such ambiguity.

For example, bound the least possible $|V(\Lambda)|$ for a minimal-degree
cover in terms of its input triangulation. If several minimal-degree covers
exist, one can seek bounds valid for each or optimize over them, explicitly
stating the choice. Estimating $d(M)$ alone does not bound the Artin-group
complexity asked for here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Quantitatively bound the size or other graph complexity of an Artin group containing the fundamental group of a smallest suitable cover of a hyperbolic three-manifold.
