\section*{Kirby Problem 4.23}\label{2899}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2899 · KP-4.23 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Give $S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ and $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$ their standard
smooth structures. Let $i:S^3\hookrightarrow S^4$ be a smooth embedding,
and put $\Sigma=i(S^3)$. The complement has two components; denote
their closures by $W_0$ and $W_1$. Smoothness of the embedded
hypersurface gives each $W_j$ the structure of a compact smooth
$4$-manifold with boundary $\Sigma$.

Must both $W_0$ and $W_1$ be diffeomorphic to the standard closed
ball $B^4=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:\|x\|\le1\}$? This is the smooth
four-dimensional Schoenflies question. The quantifier ranges over all
smooth embeddings $i$, without any bound on a handle decomposition
or on the critical points of a height function on $\Sigma$.

The requested diffeomorphisms are of compact manifolds with boundary,
so they must extend smoothly up to the boundary. No particular map
from $\Sigma$ to the standard $S^3$ is prescribed. Proving standardness
only of the interiors, or obtaining a homeomorphism of the closed
complements, would be a weaker conclusion.

Both sides are part of the assertion. A counterexample would be a
smoothly embedded $3$-sphere for which at least one closed complementary
piece is not a smooth ball. The embedding has codimension one; this
question is different from asking whether every smoothly embedded
$2$-sphere in $S^4$ is unknotted.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are both closed complementary regions of every smoothly embedded three-sphere in the four-sphere standard smooth four-balls?
