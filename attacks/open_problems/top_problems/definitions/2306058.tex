\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.58}\label{2306058}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306058 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\Sigma$ here consist of holomorphic injective exterior functions $f(z)=z+b_0+\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^{-n}$ on $|z|>1$, as in Problem 6.57. The additive constant is unrestricted. A $q$-quasiconformal extension is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the Riemann sphere fixing $\infty$ (equivalently a homeomorphism of the whole plane extended at infinity), locally in $W^{1,2}$ in charts, agreeing with the function on its domain and satisfying $|\partial_{\bar z}F|\le q|\partial_zF|$ almost everywhere for $0\le q<1$.For distinct $z,\zeta$ with $|z|,|\zeta|>1$, put
\[L_f(z,\zeta)=\log\frac{f'(z)f'(\zeta)(z-\zeta)^2}{(f(z)-f(\zeta))^2},\]
using the holomorphic branch that extends to $0$ on the diagonal $z=\zeta$. The quotient is nonzero and equals $1$ on that diagonal. For $0<q<1$, consider the Goluzin condition
\[|L_f(z,\zeta)|\le q\log\frac{|z\overline\zeta-1|^2}{(|z|^2-1)(|\zeta|^2-1)}\qquad(|z|,|\zeta|>1).\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is this condition sufficient for $f$ to admit a quasiconformal extension to the whole plane? Does sufficiency hold at least for every sufficiently small positive $q$? \sref{2306058}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the two-point Goluzin distortion inequality characterize quasiconformal extendibility of exterior univalent functions, at least for small distortion?
