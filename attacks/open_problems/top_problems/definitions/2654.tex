\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.145}\label{2654}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2654 · KOU-21.145 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Thompson's group $F$ consists of orientation-preserving piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of $[0,1]$ with finitely many dyadic rational breakpoints and slopes in $\{2^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, with composition as its operation. It is finitely generated; give it a word metric from a finite generating set. Give the infinite cyclic group $\mathbb Z$ its metric $|m-n|$, and each direct product a word metric, equivalently the sum of the factor word metrics.

A map $f:X\to Y$ between metric spaces is a quasi-isometry if there are constants $\lambda\ge1$, $C\ge0$ such that
\[
 \lambda^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))
 \le\lambda d_X(x,x')+C
\]
for every $x,x'\in X$, and every point of $Y$ is at distance at most $C$ from $f(X)$. Existence of a quasi-isometry between finitely generated groups does not depend on the finite generating sets used.

The two questions are:
\textbf{(a)} Is $F$ quasi-isometric to $F\times\mathbb Z$?
\textbf{(b)} Is $F$ quasi-isometric to $F\times F$?

Each part asks for a map satisfying both the distance inequalities and coarse surjectivity; the maps and constants may differ between the two parts. A quasi-isometry is not required to be a homomorphism, injective, or continuous in the interval-action model. These are therefore comparisons of the large-scale word-metric spaces of the indicated groups.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is Thompson’s group $F$ quasi-isometric to its product with $\mathbb Z$, or to its square?
