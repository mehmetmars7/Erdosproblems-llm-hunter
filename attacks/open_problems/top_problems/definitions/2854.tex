\section{Kirby Problem 3.56}\label{2854}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2854 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $Y$ be a rational homology $3$-sphere. For $\mathfrak s\in\operatorname{Spin}^c(Y)$, $\operatorname{SWF}(Y,\mathfrak s)$ denotes its Seiberg--Witten Floer stable homotopy type, obtained from finite-dimensional approximation and the Conley index of the Chern--Simons--Dirac flow, with the standard grading desuspensions. It has an $S^1$ action; for self-conjugate $\mathfrak s$ there is a $\operatorname{Pin}(2)$ refinement. Here $\operatorname{Pin}(2)=S^1\cup jS^1\subset\mathbb H^\times$, with $j^2=-1$ and $jz=\bar z j$.
Equivalence means stable homotopy equivalence in the specified category, allowing the suspensions in the definition of $\operatorname{SWF}$. Nonequivariantly, a wedge of spheres is a finite wedge of ordinary sphere spectra. In the $S^1$ version of the source's wedge question, the permitted form is a wedge of one representation sphere $S^{n\mathbb C}$ and suspensions of free $S^1$-spheres $S^{2m+1}_+$, with scalar $S^1$ action on the unit sphere in $\mathbb C^{m+1}$ and an added disjoint basepoint. Representation spheres are one-point compactifications, interpreted stably for virtual suspensions.
A monopole Floer L-space has zero reduced monopole Floer homology. It is an $S^1$ homotopy L-space if each spin$^c$ summand of $\operatorname{SWF}$ is a suspension of $S^0$; it is a $\operatorname{Pin}(2)$ homotopy L-space when this also holds in the $\operatorname{Pin}(2)$ category for each self-conjugate summand.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Resolve the following, keeping the equivariant interpretations separate:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is $\operatorname{SWF}(Y,\mathfrak s)$ always a wedge of spheres, nonequivariantly? Is it always of the specified one-representation-sphere/free-spheres form $S^1$-equivariantly?
\item Is every monopole Floer L-space an $S^1$ homotopy L-space, and is it also a $\operatorname{Pin}(2)$ homotopy L-space?
\item Find two rational homology spheres having isomorphic monopole Floer homology but different Seiberg--Witten Floer spectra, separately in the nonequivariant, $S^1$ and $\operatorname{Pin}(2)$ settings.
\item Develop algorithms computing these spectra in each of those three settings.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the possible Seiberg--Witten Floer spectra, compare Floer L-spaces with homotopy L-spaces, find equal-homology manifolds with different spectra, and compute spectra algorithmically, with the ordinary, circle and Pin(2) versions kept distinct.
