\section*{A second-order differential equation}\label{4700009}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4700009 · AMR-046-0009 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix $p>0$, $T>0$, and a real continuous function $f$ on $\mathbb R$ satisfying
$f(t+T)=f(t)$ and $f\not\equiv0$. Seek a twice continuously differentiable
function $x:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)$ such that
\[
 x(t+T)=x(t),\qquad x(t)^p x''(t)=f(t)
 \quad(t\in\mathbb R).
\]
Positivity is pointwise; by periodicity it also gives a positive minimum.
No upper or lower amplitude bound is specified in advance. The problem is
to give necessary and sufficient conditions on $f$, for each fixed $p,T$,
for at least one such positive periodic solution to exist.

The conditions should be stated directly in terms of the forcing and should
apply to arbitrary continuous periodic functions, including those with many
sign changes. Merely restating the existence of a fixed point of a solution
operator is not a characterization of the intended kind.

Two necessary conditions illustrate the issue. Periodicity gives $\int_0^T f(t)x(t)^{-p}\,dt=0$, and integration by parts yields
\[
 \int_0^T f(t)\,dt=-p\int_0^T x(t)^{p-1}x'(t)^2\,dt<0.
\]
The strict inequality follows because nonzero forcing excludes constant
solutions. In particular $f$ must take both positive and negative values.
These restrictions explain why nontrivial sign information is needed; the
question asks for a complete criterion, without assuming that these obvious
necessary conditions are sufficient.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize the periodic forcings for which this singular second-order equation has an everywhere-positive periodic solution.
