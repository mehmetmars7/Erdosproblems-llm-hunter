\section*{Commuting expansive automorphisms}\label{4600014}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600014 · AMR-045-0014 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a two-sided shift of finite type on a finite alphabet, with shift
$\sigma$. Assume it is irreducible: for any two words occurring in $X$,
some finite connecting word makes their concatenation occur in $X$.
Equivalently, a nonempty edge presentation may be chosen with strongly
connected directed graph. Let $F:X\to X$ be an automorphism, meaning a
homeomorphism satisfying $F\sigma=\sigma F$.

Suppose that $F$, considered as a dynamical system in its own right, is
expansive. Thus for a compatible metric $d$ on $X$ there exists
$\delta>0$ such that
\[
 \bigl[d(F^n x,F^n y)<\delta\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb Z\bigr]
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=y.
\]
Must there exist a finite alphabet $B$, a two-sided finite-type shift
$Y\subset B^{\mathbb Z}$, and a homeomorphism $H:X\to Y$ such that
$HF=\sigma_YH$?

The finite-type conclusion concerns the dynamics of $F$, which need not
be the original shift $\sigma$. It asks for some finite forbidden-word
presentation after a conjugacy, rather than requiring the coordinate
description already used for $X$ to serve that purpose. Expansiveness is
two-sided in time, and no positive expansiveness assumption is made.
Irreducibility is retained throughout: allowing arbitrary reducible
finite-type shifts changes the question and admits the counterexamples
discussed in the source.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must an expansive automorphism of an irreducible finite-type shift itself admit a finite-type symbolic presentation?
