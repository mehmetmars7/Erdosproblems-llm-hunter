\section{Suppose k runners having distinct constant speeds start at a common point and run laps on a unit length circular track}\label{3100057}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3100057 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Identify the unit-length circular track with $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. For a real number $u$, write $\|u\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|u-m|$ for its circular distance from zero. Let $k\ge2$ and let $v_1,\ldots,v_k$ be distinct real constant velocities. With all runners starting at the same point at time zero, runner $i$ has position $tv_i$ modulo one at time $t\ge0$.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such choice of velocities and each $i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$, is there a time $t\ge0$ such that
\[\|t(v_j-v_i)\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\ge\frac1k\qquad\text{for every }j\ne i?\]
The time may depend on the runner. For one runner the assertion is vacuous. \sref{3100057}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every runner eventually have circular distance at least the reciprocal of the number of runners from every other runner?
