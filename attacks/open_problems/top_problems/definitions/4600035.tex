\section*{Automorphism groups of full shifts}\label{4600035}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600035 · AMR-045-0035 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $N\ge2$, put $X_N=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^{\mathbb Z}$ with the
product topology, and let $(\sigma_Nx)_j=x_{j+1}$. Define
\[
 \operatorname{Aut}(\sigma_N)=
 \{F:X_N\to X_N:F\text{ is a homeomorphism and }
                         F\sigma_N=\sigma_NF\},
\]
with group operation composition. By the finite-window description
of continuous shift-commuting maps, each element and its inverse
can be expressed by local rules on finitely many coordinates.

Are $\operatorname{Aut}(\sigma_2)$ and
$\operatorname{Aut}(\sigma_3)$ isomorphic as abstract groups?
An affirmative answer requires a bijection $\Psi$ between the
groups satisfying $\Psi(FG)=\Psi(F)\Psi(G)$ for every pair of
automorphisms; a negative answer requires an abstract group
property distinguishing them.

No topology on the automorphism groups is part of the question.
Nor is an isomorphism required to respect local rule radii, to
send the distinguished shift to the other shift, or to be induced
by a homeomorphism between the underlying configuration spaces.
These extra requirements would change the target. The alphabets
are fixed at two and three symbols, and both shifts are two-sided.
Thus the problem compares the complete algebraic symmetry groups
of the two systems, rather than asking whether the shifts
themselves are dynamically conjugate or whether either symmetry
group embeds into the other.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are the abstract automorphism groups of the full binary and ternary shifts isomorphic?
