\section{The Continuum Hypothesis}\label{1135}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1135 · Set Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Work in ZFC, the usual axioms of set theory with the axiom of choice.
For sets \(A,B\), the inequality \(|A|\le|B|\) means that an injection
\(A\to B\) exists, and \(|A|<|B|\) additionally means that no bijection
between them exists.  The countably infinite cardinal is
\(\aleph_0=|\mathbb Z|\); the cardinality of the real numbers is
\(\mathfrak c=|\mathbb R|\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a set \(A\) with
\[
 \aleph_0<|A|<\mathfrak c?
\]
This is the negation of the continuum hypothesis.  As a question over
ZFC alone, it is independent of those axioms, assuming they are
consistent: the cited forcing constructions give models with and
without such an intermediate cardinal. \sref{1135}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a set strictly larger than a countably infinite set and strictly smaller than the real numbers? This particular existence statement is the negation of CH and is independent of ZFC.
