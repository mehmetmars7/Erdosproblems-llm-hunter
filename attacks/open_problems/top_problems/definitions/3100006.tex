\section*{Suppose H is a linear 3-uniform hypergraph, i}\label{3100006}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100006 · AMR-030-0006 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite $3$-uniform hypergraph $H=(V,\mathcal E)$ has three vertices
in each edge. It is linear if distinct edges share at most one
vertex. Say that it has a faithful straight-triangle embedding in
$\mathbb R^3$ if there is an injective placement $p:V\to\mathbb R^3$
such that every edge $e$ spans a nondegenerate closed triangle
$T_e=\operatorname{conv}p(e)$ and
\[
 T_e\cap T_f=\operatorname{conv}p(e\cap f)
                  \qquad(e\ne f),
\]
where the convex hull of the empty set is empty. Thus disjoint edges
give disjoint triangles, and two incident triangles meet only at
their common vertex. Also require $p(v)\notin T_e$ whenever $v\notin e$.

Let $h(n)$ be the maximum number of edges in an $n$-vertex linear
$3$-uniform hypergraph admitting such an embedding. Kalai's question
asks whether
\[
                           h(n)=o(n^2).
\]
Precisely, for every $\varepsilon>0$, must there be $n_0$ such that
every eligible hypergraph with $n\ge n_0$ has at most
$\varepsilon n^2$ edges?

Linearity alone gives only a quadratic upper bound, by counting
pairs of vertices. The question is whether the geometric realization
forces a vanishing quadratic density. Intersections of triangle
interiors, overlaps along unprescribed segments, and a vertex lying
inside a nonincident triangle are excluded by faithfulness; allowing
these would change the geometric restriction substantially.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does faithful realization by triangles in three-dimensional space force every linear 3-uniform hypergraph to have o(n²) edges?
