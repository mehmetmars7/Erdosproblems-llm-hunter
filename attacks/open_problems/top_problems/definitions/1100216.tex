\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.16}\label{1100216}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100216 · AMR-010-0216 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite set $S$ with symmetric labels $m_{st}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$, the Artin group $A$ equates the two alternating words of length $m_{st}$ in $s,t$ whenever that label is finite. No relation is imposed for an infinite label. The associated Coxeter group $W$ is the quotient obtained by adding $s^2=1$ for every $s\in S$. The Artin group has finite, or spherical, type when $W$ is finite; this does not mean that $A$ itself is finite.

A group is a CAT$(0)$ group if it admits an isometric action on a proper geodesic CAT$(0)$ space $X$ which is proper and cocompact. Properness of $X$ means compactness of closed bounded balls. The CAT$(0)$ condition is Euclidean comparison for geodesic triangles. Properness of the action means that only finitely many translates of any compact set meet that set.

Is every finite-type Artin group a CAT$(0)$ group? More broadly, ask the same for all finite defining matrices, as suggested by the source's bracketed finite-type restriction. The space and action may depend on the group; no uniform dimension, fixed cell shape, or prescribed complex is requested. A complete answer must produce a proper cocompact action, rather than merely an action with unbounded orbits or a locally nonpositively curved space whose fundamental group is a different group.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do finite-type Artin groups, and more generally arbitrary Artin groups, admit geometric CAT(0) actions?
