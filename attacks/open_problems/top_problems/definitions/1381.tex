\section{Kalai's 3\textasciicircum{}d Conjecture}\label{1381}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1381 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A convex $d$-polytope $P\subset\mathbb R^d$ is the convex hull of finitely many points with affine dimension $d$. It is centrally symmetric if, after translation of its center to the origin, $P=-P$. Let $f_i(P)$ be the number of its $i$-dimensional faces for $0\le i\le d-1$. Count all nonempty faces, including $P$ itself but excluding the empty face:
\[
 s(P)=1+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}f_i(P).
\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $d\in\mathbb N$ and every centrally symmetric $d$-polytope $P$, is
\[
 s(P)\ge3^d?
\]
\sref{1381}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every centrally symmetric d-dimensional convex polytope have at least 3 to the d nonempty faces, counting the whole polytope?
