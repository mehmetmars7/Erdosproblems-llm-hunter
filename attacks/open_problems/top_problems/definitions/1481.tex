\section{Borel Conjecture}\label{1481}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1481 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A closed topological manifold is a compact manifold without boundary.
A connected manifold \(M\) is aspherical if its universal covering space is
contractible; equivalently, \(\pi_j(M)=0\) for every \(j\ge2\).
Write \(\pi_1(M)\) for its fundamental group. A homeomorphism is a
continuous bijection with continuous inverse. For aspherical manifolds,
an isomorphism of fundamental groups gives a homotopy equivalence;
the classification question concerns whether a homeomorphism also exists.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every pair of connected closed aspherical topological manifolds
\(M,N\), does
\[
 \pi_1(M)\cong\pi_1(N)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\cong N
             \text{ by a homeomorphism}
\]
hold? \sref{1481}{2} \sref{1481}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must any two connected closed aspherical manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups be homeomorphic?
