\section*{Maximum weakly stable matchings in graphs without odd preference cycles}\label{3000052}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000052 · AMR-029-0052 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph. Each vertex has a complete
weak order on its neighbors, permitting ties; every listed neighbor is
preferred to remaining unmatched. Write $u\succ_v w$ when $v$
strictly prefers $u$ to $w$.

For each odd simple cycle $v_0v_1\cdots v_{2r}v_0$, with indices
read cyclically, assume that some vertex strictly prefers its successor
to its predecessor and another strictly prefers its predecessor to its
successor. Ties alone do not witness either requirement.

A matching $M$ is weakly stable if there is no edge $uv\notin M$
such that both endpoints strictly prefer one another to their partners
in $M$, with an unmatched endpoint treating any neighbor as preferable.
Let
\[
 \operatorname{OPT}(G,\succ)=\max\{|M|:M\text{ is weakly stable}\}.
\]
The cycle hypothesis guarantees existence of a weakly stable matching.
Determine how well this maximum can be approximated in polynomial time.
An approximation factor $\alpha\ge1$ means an algorithm always produces
a weakly stable $M$ with $|M|\ge\operatorname{OPT}/\alpha$.

Seek the best factor for this whole class and, separately, its dependence
on a bound on the size of ties in individual lists. The graph need not
be bipartite. Ignoring ties or replacing weak stability by strong or
super-stability changes the feasible set. In particular, producing a
stable matching of arbitrary size does not settle the maximum-size
approximation question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How closely can polynomial-time algorithms approximate the largest weakly stable matching under the stated prohibition on odd preference cycles?
