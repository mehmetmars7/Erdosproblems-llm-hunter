\section*{Weighted bipartite edge colouring}\label{3000088}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000088 · AMR-029-0088 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(U\sqcup W,E)$ be a finite bipartite graph with at least
one edge, and give each edge a weight $w(e)\in[0,1]$.
A weighted edge colouring assigns one colour $f(e)$ to every edge
and is feasible when
\[
                       \sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\f(e)=a}}w(e)\le1
                                 \quad\text{for every vertex }v
                                 \text{ and colour }a.
\]
Adjacent edges may share a colour if their total load remains
within this unit capacity.

For a vertex $v$, let $b_v$ be the minimum number of bins of
capacity one needed to pack all its incident edge weights;
each edge is one indivisible item. Put $b=\max_v b_v$.
An isolated vertex has $b_v=0$, while a nonempty incident list
needs at least one bin even if all its weights are zero.

Is there always a feasible weighted edge colouring using at most
$2b-1$ colours? The empty-edge graph can be handled separately
with zero colours.

The same colour on an edge must be used at both endpoints,
so independent optimal bin packings at the vertices may be
incompatible. Edges may not split their weights among colours.
The lower bound $b$ is a local bin-packing bound, which can
exceed the ceiling of the largest total incident weight; replacing
it by that different quantity would change the conjecture.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every weighted bipartite graph be edge-coloured within unit
load per colour at each vertex using at most twice the local bin-packing bound minus one?
