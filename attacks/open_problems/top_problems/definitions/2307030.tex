\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.30}\label{2307030}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2307030 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and let $u:\mathbb D\to\mathbb R$ be bounded and continuous. Harmonicity means $\Delta u=0$ on $\mathbb D$.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Suppose that for every $z\in\mathbb D$ there is a radius $r(z)$ with $0<r(z)<1-|z|$ such that
\[u(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(z+r(z)e^{i\theta})\,d\theta.\]
Must $u$ be harmonic? The averaging here is along the circumference, rather than over its interior. \sref{2307030}{2}
Update7.30 identifies this with Problem3.8. Update3.8 distinguishes circular from disc averaging and records a negative answer for circumference averaging. \sref{2307030}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a bounded continuous function become harmonic if each point has at least one circle on which its average equals the value at the centre?
