\section*{Kirby Problem 4.119}\label{2995}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2995 · KP-4.119 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A topological $4$-manifold is a Hausdorff second-countable space
locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^4$ or, at boundary points,
to a closed half-space. A CW complex is built inductively from
points by attaching disks along continuous maps of their boundary
spheres to the preceding skeleton. It has the closure-finite weak
topology: each cell closure meets finitely many cells, and closedness
is tested on cell closures.

Is every topological $4$-manifold homeomorphic to a CW complex?
Explicitly, for each such manifold $M$, the question asks for some
CW complex $K$ and a continuous bijection $|K|\to M$ with
continuous inverse. The source's genuinely difficult case includes
closed manifolds without a smooth structure; no smoothability
hypothesis is part of the question.

The requested map is a homeomorphism, not just a homotopy
 equivalence or a simple homotopy equivalence. Thus constructing
a CW model with the same algebraic topology would not suffice.
The cells need not form a regular CW structure: an attaching
map need not be an embedding, and a cell closure need not itself
be an embedded disk.

Likewise, a CW complex need not be a simplicial complex. The
question is therefore distinct from triangulability, which would
require a homeomorphism to the realization of a simplicial complex.
No preferred cell decomposition of the given manifold is supplied;
the task is to find some CW structure realizing its actual topology.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every topological four-manifold homeomorphic to a CW complex, rather than merely homotopy equivalent to one?
