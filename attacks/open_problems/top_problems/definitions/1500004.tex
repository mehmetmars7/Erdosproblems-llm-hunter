\section{Algebraic Stories — Maximal $k$-rank}\label{1500004}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1500004 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $S=\mathbb C[x,y]$, and let $S_e$ denote its homogeneous degree-$e$ forms. For $f\in S_{kd}$, let $\operatorname{rk}_k(f)$ be the smallest number of terms in a decomposition $f=\sum_jg_j^k$ with $g_j\in S_d$. Define $R_{k,d}=\max_{f\in S_{kd}}\operatorname{rk}_k(f)$, the maximal $k$-rank of binary forms.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For all positive integers $k,d$, is $R_{k,d}=k$? In addition, for every pair of non-proportional linear forms $\ell_1,\ell_2\in S_1$, is
\[\operatorname{rk}_k(\ell_1\ell_2^{kd-1})=k?\] \sref{1500004}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are $k$ powers always enough for binary forms, with the stated products of linear forms attaining the maximum?
