\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.50}\label{2559}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2559 · KOU-21.50 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $T\ne1$ be a finite $3$-group, meaning that its order is a positive
power of three. A subgroup $C\le T$ is characteristic if it is invariant
under every automorphism of $T$. For any finite group $G$ containing
$T$, say $T$ is a Sylow $3$-subgroup when $|T|$ is the largest power
of three dividing $|G|$.

For a subgroup $C\le G$, its normalizer is
$N_G(C)=\{g\in G:g^{-1}Cg=C\}$. The derived subgroup $G'$ is generated
by all commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a,b\in G$.
The question asks whether every such $T$ has one nontrivial characteristic
subgroup $C$ with the following universal property:
\[
 \text{for every finite }G\text{ with }T\in\operatorname{Syl}_3(G),
 \qquad T\cap G'=T\cap N_G(C)'.
\]
Thus $C$ is chosen from $T$ before the finite overgroup $G$ is specified.
Its choice cannot depend on the overgroup.

Because $C$ is characteristic in $T$, the group $T$ normalizes $C$ and
lies in $N_G(C)$. The right-hand intersection is therefore meaningful
inside the same copy of $T$, and it is always contained in the left
one. The question asks for the opposite containment uniformly over
all overgroups. The group $T=1$ is excluded explicitly because no
nontrivial subgroup $C$ could then exist. Replacing one characteristic
subgroup by a family is a different conclusion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every nontrivial finite $3$-group have a characteristic subgroup whose normalizer controls its intersection with the derived subgroup in every Sylow overgroup?
