\section*{Kirby Problem 4.69}\label{2945}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2945 · KP-4.69 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth $3$-manifold and $f:M\to M$ a
self-diffeomorphism. A topological pseudo-isotopy from the identity
to $f$ is a homeomorphism
\[
 F:M\times[0,1]\longrightarrow M\times[0,1]
\]
with $F(x,0)=(x,0)$ and $F(x,1)=(f(x),1)$.
A smooth pseudo-isotopy has the same boundary restrictions and is
a diffeomorphism of the product smooth manifolds. Boundary collars
can be included in the standard relative convention.

Does there exist a pair $(M,f)$ for which a topological
pseudo-isotopy exists but no smooth pseudo-isotopy exists? The
manifold $M$ and the diffeomorphism are both to be chosen; the
source imposes no orientability or geometric-structure hypothesis.

A pseudo-isotopy need not preserve the interval coordinate. In
particular its restrictions to intermediate slices need not be
self-maps of those slices. This distinguishes pseudo-isotopy from an
ordinary isotopy $f_t:M\to M$, which would give a level-preserving
product map $(x,t)\mapsto(f_t(x),t)$.

The two endpoint maps are already smooth. The proposed obstruction
concerns extending their prescribed behavior over the $4$-dimensional
cylinder by a diffeomorphism, even though a homeomorphic extension
exists. Showing only that $f$ is not smoothly isotopic to the
identity does not suffice, because a smooth pseudo-isotopy could
still fail to preserve levels. Nor is ordinary homotopy to the
identity a substitute for the required topological extension.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a diffeomorphism of a closed three-manifold be topologically but not smoothly pseudo-isotopic to the identity?
