\section{Lonely Runner Conjecture}\label{1320}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1320 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Consider $k\geq2$ runners starting together at $0$ on the circle $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ of circumference $1$, with pairwise distinct constant real speeds $v_1,\ldots,v_k$. The position of runner $i$ at time $t$ is $tv_i$ modulo $1$. For $x\in\mathbb R$ let $\lVert x\rVert_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|x-m|$, the shorter circular distance from $x$ to $0$. Different runners may have different lonely times.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such $k$ and speed tuple, does each runner $i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ have a time $t\geq0$ such that
\[
\lVert t(v_i-v_j)\rVert_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\geq\frac1k
\qquad\text{for all }j\ne i?
\] \sref{1320}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does each runner eventually become at least one divided by the number of runners away from every other runner on the unit circular track?
