\section{McKay Conjecture}\label{1114}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1114 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite group \(G\), let \(\operatorname{Irr}(G)\) denote the
irreducible complex characters, one per equivalence class of irreducible
finite-dimensional complex representations.  Their degrees are
\(\chi(1)\).  For a prime \(p\), put
\[
 \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(G)
  =\{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G):p\nmid\chi(1)\}.
\]
A Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(P\le G\) has order equal to the largest power
of \(p\) dividing \(|G|\).  Its normalizer is
\(N_G(P)=\{g\in G:gPg^{-1}=P\}\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite group \(G\), every prime \(p\), and every Sylow
\(p\)-subgroup \(P\le G\), is
\[
 |\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(G)|
   =|\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(N_G(P))|?
\]
This compares the numbers of characters of degree prime to \(p\);
it does not require an additional Galois-equivariance property. \sref{1114}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a finite group have as many irreducible complex characters of degree prime to p as the normalizer of a Sylow p-subgroup?
