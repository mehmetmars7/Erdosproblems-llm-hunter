\section{Collinear Triples and Cubic Curves}\label{130}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 130 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


An affine line through two distinct points $x,y\in\mathbb R^2$ is denoted by $\ell(x,y)$. For a finite set $A\subset\mathbb R^2$, let
\[
 T(A)=\bigl|\{(x,y)\in A^2:x\ne y,\ (\ell(x,y)\cap A)\setminus\{x,y\}\ne\varnothing\}\bigr|.
\]
Pairs are ordered. A cubic curve means the zero set of a nonzero real polynomial in two variables of total degree at most three; it may be reducible.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $k\geq2$ and every real $\delta>0$, does there exist $c=c(k,\delta)>0$ such that, for every finite set $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ of cardinality $n\geq1$ with at most $k$ points on any line and $T(A)\geq\delta n^2$, there is a nonzero $F\in\mathbb R[X,Y]$ of total degree at most three with
\[
 |\{a\in A:F(a)=0\}|\geq cn?
\] \sref{130}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a planar point set with bounded line multiplicities and many pairs lying in collinear triples have a positive proportion of its points on one cubic curve?
