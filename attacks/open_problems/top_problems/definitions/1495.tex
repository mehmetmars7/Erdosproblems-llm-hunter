\section{Singmaster's Conjecture}\label{1495}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1495 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For integers \(n\ge0\) and \(0\le j\le n\), the binomial
coefficient is \(\binom nj=n!/[j!(n-j)!]\), with \(0!=1\).
For an integer \(a\ge2\), define its multiplicity in Pascal's triangle by
\[
 M(a)=\#\{(n,j):n\ge0,\ 0\le j\le n,\ \tbinom nj=a\}.
\]
Locations \((n,j)\) and \((n,n-j)\) are counted separately when distinct.
Entries equal to \(1\) are excluded, since they occur on every row's edges.
Each fixed \(a\) has finite multiplicity; the question asks for one
bound valid simultaneously for all \(a\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist an absolute integer \(B\ge1\) such that
\[
                     M(a)\le B\qquad\text{for every integer }a\ge2?
\] \sref{1495}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there one finite bound on how many times any entry greater than one can appear in the whole Pascal triangle?
