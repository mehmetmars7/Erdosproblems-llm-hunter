\section*{Kirby Problem 2.29}\label{2777}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2777 · KP-2.29 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $I$ be a finite set and let $(m_{ij})$ be a symmetric Coxeter matrix:
$m_{ii}=1$ and $m_{ij}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ for $i\ne j$.
The associated Artin group is generated by $s_i$ for $i\in I$, subject to
\[
 \underbrace{s_i s_j s_i\cdots}_{m_{ij}\text{ letters}}
 =\underbrace{s_j s_i s_j\cdots}_{m_{ij}\text{ letters}}
 \qquad(m_{ij}<\infty).
\]
There is no relation for $m_{ij}=\infty$. Its Coxeter group is obtained by
also imposing $s_i^2=1$. The Artin group is of \emph{finite type} when this
Coxeter group is finite; finite type does not mean that the Artin group itself
is finite. Finite type is only a special case of the question below.

Which Artin groups on a finite generating set admit an injective homomorphism into
$\operatorname{Mod}(S)$ for some oriented surface $S$ of finite type?
Here mapping classes are orientation-preserving homeomorphisms modulo isotopy,
with any specified boundary fixed pointwise. The surface may depend on the
Artin group. The task is an existence and classification question: no restriction
is imposed that the displayed generators must map to individual Dehn twists.
A representation having a kernel, or an embedding of only a finite-index
subgroup, does not by itself answer this question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine which finitely generated Artin groups embed faithfully into a surface mapping class group.
