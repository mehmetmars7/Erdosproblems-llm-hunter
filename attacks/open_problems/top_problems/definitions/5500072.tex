\section{Polyhedron with Regular Pentagon Faces}\label{5500072}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5500072 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


$S^2$ is the two-dimensional sphere. Let $M$ be a closed polyhedral surface homeomorphic to $S^2$, with every face a regular pentagon of the same size. Consider an immersion of this surface into Euclidean three-space; self-intersections are permitted. A solid regular dodecahedron has twelve such regular-pentagonal facets.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must every such immersed $M$ be the boundary of a union of solid dodecahedra glued at common facets? The source also asks the same question when the surface is embedded in three-space. \sref{5500072}{2}
