\section{The Erdős–Oler conjecture}\label{7200026}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 7200026 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $m\geq1$, let $L(m)$ be the smallest side length of a closed equilateral triangle that contains $m$ closed disks of radius one with pairwise disjoint interiors. Touching disks and contact with the triangle's boundary are allowed. The radius-one convention fixes the scale. A triangular number is $T_k=k(k+1)/2$. Take $k\geq2$, so both $T_k$ and $T_k-1$ are positive.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $L(T_k-1)=L(T_k)$ for every integer $k\geq2$? In other words, can deleting one disk from an optimal triangular-number packing ever allow a smaller containing equilateral triangle? \sref{7200026}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does packing one fewer equal disk than a positive triangular number still require an equilateral triangle of the same minimum size?
