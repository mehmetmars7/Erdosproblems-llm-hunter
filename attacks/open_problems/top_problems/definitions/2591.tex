\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.82}\label{2591}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2591 · KOU-21.82 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $F_n=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle$ denote the free group on $n$ generators, where $n$ is an integer at least three. A subgroup $N\le F_n$ is characteristic if $\alpha(N)=N$ for every automorphism $\alpha:F_n\to F_n$. Characteristic subgroups are normal, since conjugations are automorphisms, so the quotient $F_n/N$ is defined.

A characteristic quotient of $F_n$ means a quotient by a characteristic subgroup. A finite simple group means a nontrivial finite group with no proper nontrivial normal subgroup; this convention includes cyclic groups of prime order. The conjecture asserts
\[
 \nexists N\le F_n:\quad
 N\text{ characteristic in }F_n
 \quad\text{and}\quad F_n/N\text{ finite simple}.
\]
Equivalently, there should be no surjective homomorphism from $F_n$ to any finite simple group whose kernel is preserved by every automorphism of $F_n$.

A characteristic kernel would allow each automorphism of the free group to induce an automorphism of the finite quotient, compatibly with the quotient map. This is the invariance requirement being tested; mere existence of a finite simple quotient does not impose it. The same assertion is requested separately for each rank $n\ge3$, with no bound on the quotient's size. The trivial quotient is excluded by the definition of simplicity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a free group of rank at least three have a nontrivial finite simple quotient whose kernel is invariant under every automorphism?
