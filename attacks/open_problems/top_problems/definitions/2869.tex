\section*{Kirby Problem 3.71}\label{2869}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2869 · KP-3.71 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $R=\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb F_2$, an $R$-homology sphere is a closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold with the homology of $S^3$ with coefficients in $R$. An $R$-homology cobordism is a compact oriented smooth four-manifold between two such spheres for which both boundary inclusions induce homology isomorphisms with those coefficients. Connected sum gives the group $\Theta^3_R$ of cobordism classes.

Changing coefficients defines a homomorphism
\[
\iota:\Theta^3_{\mathbb Z}\longrightarrow\Theta^3_{\mathbb F_2}.
\]
Is $\ker\iota$ nonzero? Explicitly, is there an integral homology sphere $Y$ which bounds a smooth mod-two homology ball but bounds no smooth integral homology ball? An $R$-homology ball is a compact connected oriented smooth four-manifold $W$ with boundary $Y$ and $H_*(W;R)\cong H_*(B^4;R)$.

Bounding such a ball is equivalent to representing zero in $\Theta^3_R$: remove a small interior ball to obtain a cobordism to $S^3$, or cap that boundary component of a cobordism.

Thus the two filling assertions concern potentially different four-manifolds. The task is to produce a sphere satisfying both, or to prove injectivity of $\iota$. A rational homology ball alone supplies only vanishing with rational coefficients and does not establish mod-two acyclicity. The smooth category and the use of integral homology for the nonvanishing assertion are essential.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an integral homology sphere bound a smooth mod-two homology ball but no smooth integral homology ball?
