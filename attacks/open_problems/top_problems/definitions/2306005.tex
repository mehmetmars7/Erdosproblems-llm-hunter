\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.5}\label{2306005}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306005 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$, and $\mathbb D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}$. Let $\Sigma$ be the class of analytic injective functions on $\mathbb D^*$ with Laurent expansion $F(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_nz^{-n}$. The area theorem gives $\sum_{n\ge1}n|b_n|^2\le1$. The cited estimates give $b_n=O(n^{-1/2-1/300})$, whereas examples give $|b_n|>n^{-1+\delta}$ infinitely often for one fixed $F$ and some absolute $\delta>0$. The update gives the lower example $|b_n|\ge n^{-0.83}$ infinitely often.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the sharp orders of decay of the coefficients $b_n$ over $\Sigma$: find optimal general upper estimates as $n\to\infty$ and corresponding lower examples, with a single fixed $F$ where the source specifies infinitely many indices. In particular determine the optimal decay exponent and any finer factors needed for sharp magnitude estimates. \sref{2306005}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the sharp decay rates of Laurent coefficients of normalized univalent functions outside the disk, including matching examples for a fixed function.
