\section*{Kirby Problem 5.8}\label{3015}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3015 · KP-5.8 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed topological $5$-manifold $M$ is compact, connected and
boundaryless, Hausdorff and second countable, and locally
homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^5$. It is aspherical if its universal
cover is contractible. Equivalently, since a manifold has the
homotopy type of a CW complex, all higher homotopy groups
$\pi_i(M)$ vanish for $i\ge2$.

The manifold is triangulable if it is homeomorphic to the
geometric realization of a simplicial complex. This is a
homeomorphic triangulation of the actual topological space,
not merely a simplicial complex with the same homotopy type.
No requirement that the triangulation give a particular smooth
or piecewise-linear structure is built into this definition.

Does there exist a closed aspherical $5$-manifold which is
not triangulable? Thus a witness must combine a contractible
universal cover with an obstruction to every possible simplicial
triangulation of its underlying topology.

Orientability is not imposed in the question. Neither a finite
fundamental group nor any specific presentation of it is assumed.
The dimension must be exactly five: examples in dimension four
or in dimensions at least six would not resolve this case.
An obstruction only to smoothing, or only to a chosen PL
structure, would be insufficient unless it also ruled out
homeomorphism to every simplicial complex. The assertion asks
for one example, without a classification of all such manifolds.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a closed aspherical five-manifold whose topological space admits no simplicial triangulation?
