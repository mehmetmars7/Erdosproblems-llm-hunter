\section*{Kirby Problem 2.31}\label{2779}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2779 · KP-2.31 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S$ be a closed connected oriented surface of genus at least two, and put
$G=\pi_1(S)$. Write $[G,G]$ for its commutator subgroup and
$[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. For $x\in[G,G]$, define its commutator length by
\[
\operatorname{cl}_G(x)=\min\{k\geq0:x=[a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_k,b_k],\ a_i,b_i\in G\}.
\]
The empty product gives $\operatorname{cl}_G(1)=0$.
Its stable commutator length is
\[
\operatorname{scl}_G(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\operatorname{cl}_G(x^n)}n
=\inf_{n\geq1}\frac{\operatorname{cl}_G(x^n)}n,
\]
where subadditivity ensures existence of the limit.

Prove or disprove that $\operatorname{scl}_G(x)\in\mathbb Q$ for every
$x\in[G,G]$. The commutators in the minimum are taken in the whole surface
group, even if a chosen word for $x$ lies in a smaller subgroup. Each finite
ratio in the formula is rational, but its infimum need not be rational for
that reason alone. The assertion requires exact rationality for every such
element, rather than numerical approximations or bounds.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is stable commutator length always rational for homologically trivial elements of a closed surface group?
