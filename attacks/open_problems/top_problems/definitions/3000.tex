\section{Kirby Problem 4.124}\label{3000}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3000 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A closed hyperbolic four-manifold is a compact boundaryless quotient $M=\mathbb H^4/\Gamma$, where $\mathbb H^4$ has constant sectional curvature $-1$ and $\Gamma$ is a torsion-free cocompact discrete isometry group. It is an integral homology four-sphere if $H_j(M;\mathbb Z)\cong H_j(S^4;\mathbb Z)$ for every $j$, namely $\mathbb Z$ for $j=0,4$ and zero otherwise. An arithmetic hyperbolic four-manifold is one whose lattice is arithmetic: up to commensurability and conjugacy, it arises from integral points of an orthogonal group of a quadratic form over a totally real number field, of signature $(4,1)$ at one real place and positive definite at the others. Commensurable groups have finite-index intersection. The Euler characteristic is $\chi(M)=\sum_j(-1)^j\operatorname{rank}H_j(M;\mathbb Z)$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does a closed hyperbolic integral homology four-sphere exist? Does such a manifold exist with arithmetic lattice? More generally, does any closed hyperbolic four-manifold with $\chi(M)=2$ exist, without requiring its integral homology to equal that of $S^4$? \sref{3000}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a hyperbolic integral homology four-sphere exist, possibly arithmetic; and does any closed hyperbolic four-manifold have Euler characteristic two?
