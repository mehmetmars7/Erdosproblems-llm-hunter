\section*{Transporting causal effects}\label{30007702}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S=1\) denote a source population with a randomized binary treatment \(A\), and \(S=0\) a target population with covariates \(X\) observed but no randomized outcomes. Potential outcomes \(Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\) obey consistency. Source randomization identifies \(\tau_1(x)=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x,S=1]\), and the target estimand is \(\tau_0=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S=0]\). Assume target covariate support is contained in source support. Unobserved moderators and institutional differences may invalidate equality of conditional effects. For a stated nonnegative sensitivity function \(\delta(x)\), impose
\[
|E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x,S=0]-\tau_1(x)|\leq\delta(x).
\]
Together with bounded outcomes, this yields an identified set by integrating admissible conditional effects over the target covariate distribution. Determine which additional target experiments, moderator measurements, institutional restrictions or calibration data justify smaller \(\delta(x)\), and construct sharp bounds and confidence regions under the declared restrictions. If target support differs, include the corresponding unconstrained outcome bounds rather than silently extrapolating. Treatment versions and outcome definitions must agree across populations. The problem is to obtain informative transport under credible added information; unrestricted transport is not identifiable, and known sufficient assumptions are not being presented as unresolved discoveries.
