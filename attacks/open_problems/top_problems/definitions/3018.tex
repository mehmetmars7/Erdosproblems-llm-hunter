\section*{Kirby Problem 5.11}\label{3018}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3018 · KP-5.11 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A $2$-dimensional CW complex is built from vertices, edges,
and $2$-cells, the latter attached by continuous maps of their
boundary circles to the $1$-skeleton. A subcomplex is a union
of cells containing the entire attaching boundary of every cell
it includes. Neither finiteness nor local finiteness is imposed
unless a particular special case is being considered.

A connected CW complex $X$ is aspherical if its universal
cover is contractible, equivalently if $\pi_i(X)=0$ for every
$i\ge2$. A disconnected complex is called aspherical when
each connected component is aspherical.

Whitehead's question asks whether every subcomplex $Y$ of an
aspherical $2$-complex $X$ is itself aspherical. Explicitly,
for every such pair $Y\subseteq X$, must the universal cover
of each component of $Y$ be contractible?

The inclusion is a cellular subcomplex inclusion, not an
arbitrary embedded subset or a map inducing an injection of
fundamental groups. No injectivity assumption on
$\pi_1(Y)\to\pi_1(X)$ is present. An affirmative statement
would therefore follow from the dimensional and asphericity
hypotheses alone.

The question includes finite complexes, the original motivating
setting, as well as the unrestricted CW formulation used by
the source. In dimension two, deciding the vanishing of
$\pi_2$ is enough for asphericity, by applying the Hurewicz
and Whitehead theorems to the simply connected universal
cover; nevertheless the stated conclusion is vanishing in
all higher degrees.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every subcomplex of an aspherical two-dimensional CW complex also aspherical?
