\section{Representing Integers by Initial Divisor Sums}\label{2133}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2133 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural

List every divisor of $n$ greater than one, increasingly, and form all nonempty prefix sums of that list. This defines $D_n$. The integer $f(N)$ minimizes the divisor-list argument over lists having $N$ as a prefix sum. \sref{2133}{1} \sref{2133}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For any $n$ let $D_n$ be the set of sums of the shape $d_1,d_1+d_2,d_1+d_2+d_3,\ldots$ where $1<d_1<d_2<\cdots$ are the divisors of $n$.
What is the size of $D_n\backslash \cup_{m<n}D_m$?
If $f(N)$ is the minimal $n$ such that $N\in D_n$ then is it true that $f(N)=o(N)$? Perhaps just for almost all $N$? \sref{2133}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How many genuinely new values arise from initial sums of divisors, and can every target usually be represented using divisors of a much smaller integer?
