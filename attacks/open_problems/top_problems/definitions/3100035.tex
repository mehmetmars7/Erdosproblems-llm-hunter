\section*{Show that the inversion permutation, i}\label{3100035}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100035 · AMR-030-0035 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a prime $p$, let $\iota_p$ be the permutation of
$\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ defined by multiplicative inversion modulo $p$:
$\iota_p(a)$ is the unique representative in this set satisfying
$a\,\iota_p(a)\equiv1\pmod p$. Its longest increasing subsequence
length is
\[
 L(p)=\max\{\ell:\exists\,1\le a_1<\cdots<a_\ell<p,
           \ \iota_p(a_1)<\cdots<\iota_p(a_\ell)\}.
\]
Determine the growth and finer asymptotic behavior of $L(p)$ as
$p\to\infty$ through primes. In particular, the question seeks
whether Propp's conjectured asymptotic formula
\[
                         L(p)=(2+o(1))\sqrt p
\]
holds, or equivalently whether $L(p)/\sqrt p\to2$ through primes.
The constant is suggested by comparison with uniformly random
permutations, but is conjectural for these inversion permutations.

All comparisons use the ordinary integer representatives
$1,\ldots,p-1$; there is no cyclic ordering of residues in the
definition. Zero is omitted because it is not invertible.
This is a deterministic sequence of permutations indexed by
primes, so probabilistic behavior of uniform random permutations
does not by itself provide an asymptotic formula for $L(p)$.
Upper and lower estimates should hold over the stated sequence of
primes, or explicitly identify any exceptional subsequence.
The selected indices need not be consecutive, and the question
concerns the maximal length, rather than the number of increasing
subsequences of a given length.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the asymptotic length of the longest increasing subsequence of the inversion permutation modulo a prime.
