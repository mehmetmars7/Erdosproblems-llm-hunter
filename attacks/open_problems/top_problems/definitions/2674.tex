\section{Kirby Problem 1.15}\label{2674}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2674 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a closed oriented connected $3$-manifold $Y$, let $s(Y)$ be the minimum number of components of an integrally framed link in $S^3$ whose Dehn surgery gives $Y$. An integral homology sphere has the integral homology of $S^3$; irreducible means every embedded $2$-sphere bounds a ball. For an orientation-preserving smooth cyclic action $\varphi$ of order $n$, let $s_{\mathrm{eq}}(Y,\varphi)$ be the minimum number of components in an integral surgery description equivariant for that action: an invariant framed surgery link and its ambient cyclic action induce the specified action on the surgered manifold.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
(a) Are the values $s(Y)$ unbounded among integral homology spheres, and also among irreducible ones? For nontrivial integral homology spheres $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n$, is $s(Y_1\#\cdots\#Y_n)\geq n$? (b) Are the differences $s_{\mathrm{eq}}(Y,\varphi)-s(Y)$ unbounded over cyclic actions? (c) Is this difference positive for some action of order $2$? \sref{2674}{1}\sref{2674}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine how many surgery components homology spheres require and whether requiring a cyclic symmetry can increase that number, including involutions.
