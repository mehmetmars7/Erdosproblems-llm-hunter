\section*{Kirby Problem 1.9}\label{2668}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2668 · KP-1.9 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a knot $K\subset S^3$, Dehn surgery removes its open tubular neighborhood and
glues in a solid torus, with the meridian of the added torus identified with a
specified essential simple curve on the boundary. A slope $p/q$ means the curve
$p\mu+q\lambda$ for coprime integers $p,q$, where $\mu$ is the meridian and
$\lambda$ the preferred longitude. Surgery is nontrivial when its slope is not
the meridian.
A lens space is a closed $3$-manifold obtained by gluing two solid tori; write
$L(p,q)$ for those with finite cyclic fundamental group of order $|p|$.

A knot is doubly primitive, or a Berge knot, if it can be placed on a genus-two
Heegaard surface $S^3=H_1\cup_\Sigma H_2$ and there are compressing disks
$D_i\subset H_i$ such that $|\partial D_i\cap K|=1$ for $i=1,2$.
The conjecture is that every knot admitting a nontrivial surgery to a lens space
is doubly primitive. The conclusion concerns the knot itself, including its
embedding in $S^3$, rather than merely the homeomorphism type of the resulting
lens space.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every knot with a lens-space surgery lie primitively on both sides of a genus-two Heegaard surface?
