\section{Littlewood Conjecture}\label{1512}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1512 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For \(x\in\mathbb R\), write
\(\|x\|=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|x-m|\) for its distance to
the nearest integer. For \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R\) and
\(n\in\mathbb N\), set
\(F_n(\alpha,\beta)=n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|\ge0\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R\), is
\[
                      \liminf_{n\to\infty}F_n(\alpha,\beta)=0?
\]
Equivalently, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) and every \(N\in\mathbb N\),
does some integer \(n\ge N\) satisfy
\(n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|<\varepsilon\)? \sref{1512}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every two real numbers, can arbitrarily large common integer multiples make the product of their distances to integers, times that multiplier, arbitrarily small?
