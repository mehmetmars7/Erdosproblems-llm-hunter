\section*{Kirby Problem 2.5}\label{2753}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2753 · KP-2.5 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S_g$ be a closed connected oriented surface of genus $g\geq3$.
Its mapping class group acts on $H_1(S_g;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}$,
preserving the algebraic intersection pairing. The Torelli group is the kernel
\[
\mathcal I_g=\ker\bigl(\operatorname{Mod}(S_g)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z)\bigr),
\]
so its elements act trivially on first integral homology.
A finite presentation of a group is a description
$\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\mid r_1,\ldots,r_k\rangle$ with finitely many
generators and relator words, presenting that group exactly.

For each $g\geq3$, find a finite presentation of $\mathcal I_g$, or prove that
no finite presentation exists. A positive answer must prove that the stated
relators generate all relations among the chosen generators. Providing a finite
generating set alone does not answer this question. A negative answer must
exclude every possible finite presentation, rather than just show that a
particular natural presentation has infinitely many relators.
The genus range is part of the question; the low-genus Torelli groups are not
substitutes for the groups specified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the Torelli group finitely presented in each genus at least three, and can such presentations be made explicit?
