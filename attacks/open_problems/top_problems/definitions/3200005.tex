\section{Minimum length of a superpermutation}\label{3200005}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3200005 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb N$, let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A word $w=w_1\cdots w_L$ with $w_i\in[n]$ is a superpermutation if every permutation $(\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(n))$ of $[n]$ occurs as $n$ consecutive letters of $w$; that is, for every $\sigma\in S_n$ there is an integer $j$ with $1\le j\le L-n+1$ such that $w_{j+k-1}=\sigma(k)$ for $1\le k\le n$. Put
\[
s(n)=\min\{L:\text{a superpermutation on }[n]\text{ has length }L\}.
\]
The minimum exists, since concatenating all permutations produces a word with the required property.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $s(n)$ exactly for every integer $n\ge6$. The problem asks for the optimum, rather than the length of a particular construction. Houston's length-$872$ construction at $n=6$ disproves the earlier proposed formula $\sum_{k=1}^{n}k!$, whose value at $n=6$ is $873$. \sref{3200005}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the shortest word containing every ordering of $n$ distinct symbols as a consecutive block, for each $n>5$.
