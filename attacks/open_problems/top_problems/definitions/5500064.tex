\section*{Edge-Unfolding Polycubes}\label{5500064}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500064 · AMR-054-0064 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A polycube is a finite face-connected union of congruent axis-aligned
unit cubes from the cubic lattice. Assume its boundary is an
embedded polyhedral two-sphere, so there are no handles, cavities,
or nonmanifold boundary junctions. Retain the entire tiling of
that boundary by exposed unit-square cube faces.

An allowed cut follows edges of this square tiling, including
edges lying inside larger coplanar faces of the geometric solid.
An edge unfolding cuts some such edges and develops the remaining
surface into one planar piece with connected interior and
pairwise disjoint interiors of its unit squares. Uncut shared
edges retain their length and incidence. No square may be cut
through its interior.

Does every such genus-zero polycube have an edge unfolding, or
does a counterexample exist? The unit grid supplied by the cubes
is available for cutting in full, but it may not be refined by
introducing further subdivisions.

The question is more permissive than cutting only the nonflat
edges of the solid, and more restrictive than an arbitrary
surface dissection. An unfolding of a finer grid does not answer
the specified unit-square version. Only existence of a flat
layout is required, with no prescribed physical unfolding motion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every polycube with spherical boundary be unfolded without
overlap using only the existing unit-square edges on its surface?
