\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.71}\label{2302071}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2302071 · AMR-022-2071 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $H\in\mathbb C[z]$ be a nonconstant polynomial and consider the linear differential equation
\[
y''(z)+H(z)y(z)=0\qquad(z\in\mathbb C).
\]
Its solutions are entire. Characterize those polynomials $H$ for which there exists a nonzero solution $y$ with infinitely many zeros, every one of which lies on the real axis. Multiplicities do not create an ambiguity here: a nontrivial solution of this equation cannot have both $y(z_0)=0$ and $y'(z_0)=0$.
The condition is imposed on one solution. Requiring two linearly independent solutions with only finitely many nonreal zeros gives a much more restrictive situation, mentioned separately in the source. Likewise, a zero-free solution does not qualify, and allowing finitely many nonreal zeros changes the exact classification problem.
One can express the requested class as
\[
\mathcal P=\{H\in\mathbb C[z]\setminus\mathbb C:
\exists y\not\equiv0, y''+Hy=0,
\#y^{-1}(0)=\infty, y^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbb R\}.
\]
Seek necessary and sufficient conditions on the coefficients or associated differential equation for membership in $\mathcal P$. The source supplies linear and certain quartic examples, together with later partial structural results, but those examples are not a proposed exhaustive list.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which polynomial differential equations $y''+Hy=0$ admit a solution with infinitely many zeros and no zeros off the real axis?
