\section*{LP obstructions and cyclic blow-ups for DeVos's distribution conjecture}\label{20001073}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20001073 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0198 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite nonempty directed graph with no directed cycles of lengths one, two, or three.
Parallel arcs can be discarded, since only reachability and vertex sets occur below.
For each vertex $v$, let
\[
 N^+(v)=\{w:v\to w\},\qquad N^-(v)=\{w:w\to v\},
\]
and let $N^-_2(v)$ be the vertices whose shortest directed path to $v$ has length exactly two.
In particular, $N^-_2(v)$ excludes $v$ and all its immediate in-neighbors, even when a two-step walk also exists.
For a probability vector $p:V\to[0,1]$ with $\sum_{v\in V}p(v)=1$, put $p(S)=\sum_{s\in S}p(s)$.

DeVos's question asks whether every such graph admits a single probability vector satisfying simultaneously, at every vertex $v$,
\[
 p(N^+(v))\geq p(N^-(v)),\qquad
 p(N^-_2(v))\geq p(N^-(v)).
\]
The weights are attached to vertices, may be zero, and are not required to be equal.
The same distribution must work for all inequalities; selecting a different distribution for each vertex would weaken the problem.
The formulation is a feasibility assertion for a finite system of linear inequalities determined by the graph.
In particular, it asks for vertexwise inequalities, rather than merely an averaged inequality after summing over $v$.
No assumption of strong connectivity or positive minimum degree is imposed, so sources and sinks are included in the universal quantifier.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every digraph without directed triangles or shorter cycles have a probability distribution satisfying both prescribed neighborhood inequalities at every vertex?
