\section*{Kirby Problem 4.6}\label{2882}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2882 · KP-4.6 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a closed connected orientable smooth $3$-manifold $Y$.
A smooth filling is a compact connected smooth $4$-manifold $W$ with
a boundary identification $\partial W\cong Y$. If orientations are
chosen, use the induced boundary orientation in this identification.
Two fillings $W_0,W_1$ form an absolutely exotic pair if they are
homeomorphic as manifolds with boundary but no diffeomorphism between
them exists, even when arbitrary maps on their boundaries are allowed.

Does every $Y$ admit such a pair with both $W_0$ and $W_1$
orientable? In concrete terms, find for each $Y$ two compact orientable
smooth $4$-manifolds having boundary diffeomorphic to $Y$, a homeomorphism
$W_0\to W_1$, and an obstruction to every diffeomorphism
$W_0\to W_1$.

The requested smooth distinction is absolute. Showing that no
diffeomorphism realizes one preferred identification of the boundary
would prove only a relative distinction, because a different boundary
identification might extend. The homeomorphism is not required to be
the identity under the selected boundary markings.

There is no requirement that a filling be contractible, a homology
ball, simply connected, or symplectic. Imposing any of those would
strengthen the problem. The universal quantifier is on $Y$, and the
topological filling and its two smoothings may depend on $Y$.
Both fillings must be orientable; examples with nonorientable fillings
do not settle the specified question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed orientable three-manifold bound two homeomorphic orientable smooth four-manifolds admitting no diffeomorphism between them?
