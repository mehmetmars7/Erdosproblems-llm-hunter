\section*{Switching reconstruction of digraphs}\label{3129}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3129 · OPG-46952 · Graph Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $D=(V,A)$ be a finite loopless directed graph, with at most one arc for each ordered pair of distinct vertices; the two opposite arcs between a pair may both occur. For $v\in V$, define $D^{(v)}$ by reversing every arc incident with $v$ and leaving all other arcs unchanged. If both opposite arcs occur, they remain a pair after reversal. A vertex switch changes neither the vertex set nor the number of arcs.

The switching deck of $D$ is the multiset
\[
 \mathcal S(D)=\{\!\{[D^{(v)}]:v\in V\}\!\},
\]
where brackets denote directed-graph isomorphism types. Isomorphisms must preserve arc directions. Multiplicity matters: if several vertex switches produce isomorphic graphs, that type appears several times in the deck. Vertex labels and the identity of the switched vertex are discarded from each card.

The question is whether there exist an integer $n\ge12$ and nonisomorphic directed graphs $D,D'$ on $n$ vertices with $\mathcal S(D)=\mathcal S(D')$. Equality means a bijection between their vertex sets can match switching cards by isomorphism; it need not yield one common graph isomorphism or preserve the switched vertices within individual cards.

A negative answer would establish reconstruction from the switching deck for every order at least twelve. A positive answer requires an explicit pair or another existence proof at some such order, with multiplicities verified across the entire deck.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two nonisomorphic directed graphs on at least twelve vertices have exactly the same multiset of graphs obtained by switching one vertex?
