\section*{Extremality of iterated quadratic polynomials}\label{3700031}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3700031 · AMR-036-0031 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a polynomial $p$, define
$I(p)=\int_{|z|<1}|p'(z)|/(1+|p(z)|^2)\,dA(z)$,
where $dA$ is Euclidean area. Let
\[
 \Phi(N)=\sup_{\deg p=N}I(p),\qquad
 \alpha=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\Phi(N)}{\log N}.
\]
This exponent measures the largest possible growth,
in degree, of the integrated spherical derivative.

For $c\in\mathbb C$ put $p_c(z)=z^2+c$.
Call $c$ hyperbolic when the orbit of the critical
point $0$ tends to an attracting periodic orbit
or to infinity. Write $p_c^m$ for composition
iteration, of degree $2^m$, and set
\[
 a(c)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}
                    \frac{\log I(p_c^m)}{m\log2}.
\]
The source asks whether iterated hyperbolic quadratics
are extremal or nearly extremal for $\alpha$.
At the level of growth exponents, a precise form
of this question is
$\alpha=\sup_{c\ \mathrm{hyperbolic}}a(c)$.

Does this equality hold? Equivalently, can every
$\varepsilon>0$ be matched by one fixed hyperbolic
parameter $c$ and arbitrarily large iteration numbers
whose normalized logarithmic integral exceeds
$\alpha-\varepsilon$? If so, determine whether one
parameter attains the exponent or only an approaching
sequence of parameters does.
The integration disk is fixed, and the parameter
in $a(c)$ is held fixed while the iteration number
tends to infinity. No equality of finite-degree
maxima is asserted by this exponent formulation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can iterates of hyperbolic quadratic polynomials approach the optimal polynomial growth exponent for integrated spherical derivatives?
