\section{Configurations with Every Distance Multiplicity Once}\label{1996}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1996 · Combinatorics}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.

Distances are Euclidean. Distinct distances mean distinct numerical values; when a distance is said to occur a certain number of times, unordered pairs of distinct points are counted. Congruence allows any Euclidean isometry, including reflection. \sref{1996}{1} \sref{1996}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For which $n$ are there $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, no three on a line and no four on a circle, which determine $n-1$ distinct distances and so that (in some ordering of the distances) the $i$th distance occurs $i$ times? \sref{1996}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For which sizes can a planar set in general position realize the distance multiplicities one, two, and so on, each exactly once?
