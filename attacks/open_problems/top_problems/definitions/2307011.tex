\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.11}\label{2307011}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2307011 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\geq2$, a quasiconformal map $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ is a homeomorphism in $W^{1,n}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with
\[\|Df(x)\|^n\leq K|\det Df(x)|\quad\text{for almost every }x\]
for some finite $K$; $\|Df\|$ is the Euclidean operator norm. The Sobolev condition means that every coordinate and its first weak derivatives are locally integrable to the nth power. Identify $\mathbb R^n$ with the hyperplane $\{(x,0):x\in\mathbb R^n\}$ in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every quasiconformal $f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ extend to a quasiconformal homeomorphism $F:\mathbb R^{n+1}\to\mathbb R^{n+1}$ satisfying
\[F(x,0)=(f(x),0)\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^n)?\]
The extension may have a different finite dilatation. The source's update states that Tukia and V\"ais\"al\"a established the assertion in all dimensions under consideration. \sref{2307011}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Extend any global quasiconformal map to one additional Euclidean dimension while preserving its restriction to the original hyperplane.
