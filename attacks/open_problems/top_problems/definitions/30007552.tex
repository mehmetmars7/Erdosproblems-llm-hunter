\section*{Realistic equilibrium adjustment}\label{30007552}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider a finite pure-exchange economy with agents \(i=1,\ldots,n\), goods \(g=1,\ldots,m\), positive endowments \(\omega_i\), and strictly increasing, strictly concave, twice continuously differentiable utilities \(u_i\). Assume demands are unique at every strictly positive normalized price. Let \(\Delta_{++}\) be the positive unit price simplex, \(z(p)\) aggregate excess demand, and \(p^*\in\Delta_{++}\) a competitive equilibrium satisfying \(z(p^*)=0\). Fix a delay \(\tau\geq0\), diagonal positive adjustment matrix \(D\), and projection \(\Pi_p\) onto the simplex tangent space. Study the proposed decentralized rule
\[
 \dot p(t)=\Pi_{p(t)}D z(p(t-\tau)),\qquad p(s)=\phi(s)\quad(-\tau\leq s\leq0),
\]
where \(\phi\) is a continuous positive initial price history. Restrict attention to economies and histories for which the resulting trajectory remains in a specified compact subset of \(\Delta_{++}\). Determine economically interpretable necessary or sufficient restrictions on preferences, endowments, \(D\), and \(\tau\) guaranteeing convergence to equilibrium for every admissible history. Derive sharp delay bounds where possible and construct economies demonstrating their failure outside the guaranteed region. Both local stability and genuinely global convergence must be distinguished. This is a proposed delayed-observation extension: the cited 2026 paper resolves substantial stability questions for its own forward-looking model and does not establish this particular formulation as a published open conjecture.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine when the specified delayed excess-demand price-adjustment rule converges for all admissible positive price histories. This is a proposed extension beyond the stability framework addressed by Werning and Lorenzoni, not a verified canonical open conjecture.
