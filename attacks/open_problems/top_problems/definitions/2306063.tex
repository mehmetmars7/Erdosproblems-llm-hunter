\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.63}\label{2306063}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306063 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $H=\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>0,\ 0\le\operatorname{Im}z\le1\}$. For an increasing homeomorphism $\alpha:(0,\infty)\to(a,\infty)$ with $a\ge0$, identify $x$ on the lower edge with $\alpha(x)+i$ on the upper edge. A conformal sewing is such an identification whose quotient carries a Riemann-surface structure extending the complex structure of the strip interior. Its end at infinity is called hyperbolic if a sufficiently far-out annular neighbourhood of that end has finite conformal modulus, with the end represented by a boundary circle rather than a puncture.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If the sewing defined by $\alpha$ has a hyperbolic end at infinity, does there exist a continuous function $\varepsilon:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ such that every conformal sewing defined by a homeomorphism $\beta$ of the same type, satisfying $|\beta(x)-\alpha(x)|<\varepsilon(x)$ for every $x>0$, also has a hyperbolic end at infinity? \sref{2306063}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the hyperbolic type of a sewn half-strip stable under a sufficiently small pointwise perturbation of its sewing map?
