\section{Comparable Elements in Integer Lattices}\label{142}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 142 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $N\geq1$, write $[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Two distinct triples $s,t\in[N]^3$ are comparable in the source sense if at least two coordinates of $s-t$ are strictly positive, or at least two are strictly negative. This is a symmetric sign-pattern relation, rather than the usual coordinatewise partial order. A set is pairwise comparable when every pair of its distinct elements has that property.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist constants $\delta>0$ and $N_0$ such that, for every integer $N\geq N_0$ and every pairwise comparable set $S\subset[N]^3$,
\[
 |S|\leq N^{2-\delta}?
\] \sref{142}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one save a fixed power below the quadratic bound for sets of triples in which every pair has two coordinate differences of the same strict sign?
