\section*{Kirby Problem 3.65}\label{2863}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2863 · KP-3.65 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $Y$ be a closed connected oriented three-manifold, and put $R=\mathbb Z[A,A^{-1}]$. Its Kauffman bracket skein module $S_{2,\infty}(Y)$ is the free $R$-module on isotopy classes of unoriented framed links in $Y$, including the empty link, modulo two local relations:
\[
[L_\times]=A[L_0]+A^{-1}[L_\infty],\qquad
[L\sqcup U]=-(A^2+A^{-2})[L].
\]
In the first relation the three links agree outside a ball, and inside it a framed crossing is replaced by its two planar smoothings in the usual Kauffman bracket convention. In the second, $U$ is an isolated zero-framed unknot bounding a disk disjoint from $L$.

Extend coefficients to the rational-function field $\mathbb Q(A)$. The generic rank is the dimension of
\[
S_{2,\infty}(Y)\otimes_R\mathbb Q(A).
\]
Suppose $Y$ is prime, meaning a connected-sum decomposition of $Y$ has a summand diffeomorphic to $S^3$. If this dimension is one, must $Y$ be diffeomorphic to $S^3$ or $S^1\times S^2$?

The rank condition is over the field of rational functions in an indeterminate. It is not a specialization at one complex value of $A$, and it does not impose finite generation over $R$. In particular, torsion submodules may disappear upon extending coefficients and are not excluded by the hypothesis. The conclusion concerns the underlying manifold without any link chosen in advance.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does generic skein rank one recognize the sphere and the sphere-times-circle among prime three-manifolds?
