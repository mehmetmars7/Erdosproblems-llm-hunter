\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.51}\label{2305051}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305051 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. A Blaschke product is a function \[B(z)=\eta z^m\prod_j\frac{|a_j|}{a_j}\frac{a_j-z}{1-\overline{a_j}z},\qquad |\eta|=1,\quad m\in\{0,1,\ldots\},\quad 0<|a_j|<1,\quad\sum_j(1-|a_j|)<\infty,\] where the product can be finite or infinite and repeated zeros are counted with multiplicity. An analytic function $g$ is Bloch if \[\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}(1-|z|^2)|g^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Give an explicit construction of a Blaschke product $B$ with $B(0)=0$ such that $g=(1+B)/(1-B)$ is Bloch. Equivalently, the construction must satisfy \[\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}\frac{(1-|z|^2)|B^{\prime}(z)|}{|1-B(z)|^2}<\infty.\] \sref{2305051}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Construct explicitly a Blaschke product vanishing at zero whose Cayley transform is a Bloch function.
