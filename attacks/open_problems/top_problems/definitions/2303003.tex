\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 3.3}\label{2303003}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2303003 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A subharmonic function is an upper semicontinuous function with values in $[-\infty,\infty)$, not identically $-\infty$ on a connected component, that satisfies the mean-value inequality on every closed ball in its domain. A real harmonic function is a twice continuously differentiable function $u$ satisfying $\Delta u=0$. Let $H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}$, and fix $K>0$. Let $\mathcal U_K$ consist of subharmonic functions $u<0$ on $H$ satisfying
\[\inf_{|\theta|<\pi/2}u(re^{i\theta})\leq-K\qquad(r>0).\]
The source update gives counterexamples to the constant $1/2$ and asks for the best possible replacement.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must every $u\in\mathcal U_K$ satisfy $u(r)\leq-K/2$ for every $r>0$? More generally, determine the sharp universal constant
\[\alpha_* =\sup\{\alpha\geq0: u(r)<-\alpha K\text{ for every }K>0,\ u\in\mathcal U_K,\ r>0\}.\] \sref{2303003}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does reaching level minus K somewhere on every right-half-plane semicircle force level minus K/2 on the positive axis, and what is the sharp universal constant?
