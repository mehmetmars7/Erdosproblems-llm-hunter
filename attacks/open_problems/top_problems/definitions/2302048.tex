\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.48}\label{2302048}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2302048 · AMR-022-2048 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An entire function is transcendental if it is not a polynomial. First let $A\subset\mathbb R$ and $B\subset\mathbb C$ be arbitrary countable dense sets, dense in their indicated spaces. Must there be a transcendental entire function $f$ satisfying
\[
f(A)=B,\qquad f(\mathbb R\setminus A)\subset\mathbb C\setminus B?
\]
The second requirement excludes extra real preimages of $B$; equivalently $f^{-1}(B)\cap\mathbb R=A$. Surjectivity from $A$ onto $B$ is required, but injectivity on $A$ is not stipulated.
The source also asks a rigidity question with $E,F\subset\mathbb R$ countable and dense. Suppose an entire function $g$ is real-valued and strictly monotone on $\mathbb R$, and
\[
g(E)=F,\qquad g(\mathbb R\setminus E)=\mathbb R\setminus F.
\]
Find substantive additional hypotheses forcing a simple form for $g$. In particular, for $E=F=\mathbb Q$, identify conditions implying $g(x)=ax+b$ with $a\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ and $b\in\mathbb Q$. The known existence of monotone transcendental maps between arbitrary real countable dense sets does not answer this additional rigidity problem or the first mixed real-to-complex question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can arbitrary dense countable real and complex sets be matched by an entire function with exactly the desired real preimages, and when do analogous real maps become rigid?
