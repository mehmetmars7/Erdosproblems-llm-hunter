\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.48}\label{2305048}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2305048 · AMR-022-5048 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb D=\{z:|z|<1\}$. An analytic function with Hadamard gaps is a holomorphic function admitting an expansion
\[
f(z)=a_0+\sum_{k\ge1}a_kz^{n_k},\qquad
0<n_1<n_2<\cdots,\quad
\frac{n_{k+1}}{n_k}\ge q>1,
\]
where the exponents are integers and the displayed series converges in $\mathbb D$. One can omit zero coefficients and disregard finitely many initial terms without changing the gap requirement relevant to unboundedness.
Characterize the subsets $S\subset\mathbb D$ having the following boundedness-determining property:
\[
\sup_{z\in S}|f(z)|<\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}|f(z)|<\infty
\]
for every such Hadamard-gap function $f$. The gap ratio and the exponent sequence may depend on $f$; if a characterization depends on a fixed ratio $q$ or sequence, that dependence should be stated as a separate restricted version.
The bound on $f$ over $S$ may depend on the particular function. The question asks whether boundedness propagates, not whether there is a single universal constant comparing the two suprema. No discreteness, closedness, or radial form is assumed for $S$; a useful answer should explain which geometric or sampling properties of $S$ control all these lacunary analytic functions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which subsets of the disc detect boundedness of every analytic function with geometrically separated Taylor exponents?
