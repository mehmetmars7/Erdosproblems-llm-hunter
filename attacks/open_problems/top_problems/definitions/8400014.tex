\section*{O12 — Finding a quadratic nonresidue}\label{8400014}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 8400014 · AMR-083-0014 · Graph Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, given in binary, and write $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. A quadratic nonresidue is an element $a\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ for which $x^2=a$ has no solution in $\mathbb F_p$. Equivalently, its Legendre symbol is $\left(\frac ap\right)=-1$, or $a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\pmod p$.

Does a deterministic classical algorithm, on every odd prime input $p$, produce an integer $a$ with $1\le a<p$ having this property in time polynomial in $\ell(p)=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor+1$? The exponent and coefficients of the time bound must be independent of $p$. The prime $2$ is excluded because its unit group contains no quadratic nonresidue; this is the necessary nonempty-domain convention for the catalogue question.

The output may be any nonresidue. It need not be the least positive one, nor belong to a prescribed small interval. Primality of the input is a promise; no factorization of $p-1$ or other auxiliary data are supplied. Testing a proposed answer by the displayed modular exponentiation is efficient, but the question concerns finding a successful proposal without random sampling. An exhaustive search through candidates would answer the question only if its worst-case cost is proved polynomial in binary input length, with no unproved hypothesis on character sums or zeroes of zeta functions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one find a quadratic nonresidue modulo every odd prime deterministically in polynomial bit complexity?
