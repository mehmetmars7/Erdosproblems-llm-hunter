\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.55}\label{2302055}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2302055 · AMR-022-2055 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be nonconstant entire functions, and define the complex analytic set
\[
V=\{(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb C^3:
 f_1(z_1)+f_2(z_2)+f_3(z_3)=0\}.
\]
Let $F:\mathbb C^3\to\mathbb C$ be entire, meaning holomorphic in all three complex variables, and assume that its restriction to $V$ is bounded:
\[
\sup_{z\in V}|F(z)|<\infty.
\]
Must there be one constant $c\in\mathbb C$ such that $F(z)=c$ for every $z\in V$?
The conclusion concerns only the restriction of $F$ to the analytic set: $F$ need not be constant throughout $\mathbb C^3$. Neither smoothness nor irreducibility of $V$ is assumed. Consequently an argument valid on one smooth connected piece must still justify a single constant on the complete set. The three separated-variable summands are arbitrary nonconstant entire functions; replacing them all by polynomials changes the problem to a special case.
This is a Liouville-type question for a family of possibly nonalgebraic surfaces. Ordinary Liouville's theorem in the ambient space cannot be applied directly because no bound is assumed away from $V$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every ambient entire function bounded on a separated-variable surface necessarily constant when restricted to that whole surface?
