\section{inverse of an integer matrix}\label{3030}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3030 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $m,n\geq1$ with $m\leq n$, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m)$ with integers $d_i\geq2$, and let $X\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n}$. Set $M=[D\ X]\in\mathbb Z^{m\times(m+n)}$. An integer right inverse is $Y\in\mathbb Z^{(m+n)\times m}$ with $MY=I_m$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Give necessary and sufficient conditions on $D,X$ for an integer right inverse to exist. In particular, is the necessary rowwise condition
\[\gcd(d_i,X_{i1},\ldots,X_{in})=1\quad(1\leq i\leq m)\]
also sufficient? \sref{3030}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize when the indicated integer matrix has an integer right inverse, and decide whether coprime entries in each row suffice.
