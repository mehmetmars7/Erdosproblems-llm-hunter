\section*{Kirby Problem 4.30}\label{2906}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2906 · KP-4.30 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $g\ge0$, let $\Sigma_g$ be the closed connected oriented smooth
surface of genus $g$. Consider smooth embeddings $e:\Sigma_g\hookrightarrow
S^4$, where the ambient sphere has its standard smooth structure.
Their images carry the orientations induced by the embeddings.
Two such surfaces are topologically ambient isotopic if a path of
homeomorphisms $H_t:S^4\to S^4$ starting at the identity sends one
oriented image to the other at $t=1$. They are smoothly ambient
isotopic if the same can be done by a smooth family of diffeomorphisms.

Does there exist a pair which is topologically but not smoothly
ambient isotopic? If a pair exists, does there exist an infinite
family of pairwise smoothly nonisotopic oriented surfaces, all in one
topological ambient-isotopy class? Can the pair or family consist of
$2$-spheres, that is, have genus zero? More generally, can the required
phenomenon occur for each prescribed integer $g\ge0$?

A single topological isotopy class fixes the genus, so an infinite
family must not obtain its distinction merely by changing surface
topology. The requested ambient manifold is exactly $S^4$, and the
surfaces must be orientable. Examples in other $4$-manifolds or using
nonorientable surfaces do not meet those conditions.
The equivalence here concerns oriented embedded images; it does not
require a specified parameterization of the surface to be carried
pointwise along the isotopy.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there orientable surfaces in the four-sphere that are topologically but not smoothly isotopic, including infinite families and every prescribed genus?
