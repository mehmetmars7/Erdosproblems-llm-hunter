\section{Perfect Cuboid}\label{1302}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1302 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A rectangular cuboid has positive edge lengths $a,b,c$, face-diagonal lengths $d,e,f$, and space-diagonal length $g$. A perfect cuboid requires all seven lengths to be positive integers.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist $a,b,c,d,e,f,g\in\mathbb N$ satisfying all four equations
\[
a^2+b^2=d^2,\qquad b^2+c^2=e^2,\qquad c^2+a^2=f^2,\qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=g^2?
\]
No edge length may be zero. \sref{1302}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a rectangular box whose three edges, three face diagonals and space diagonal all have positive integer lengths?
