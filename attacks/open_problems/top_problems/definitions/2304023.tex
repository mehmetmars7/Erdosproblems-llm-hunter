\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.23}\label{2304023}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2304023 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $m\ge2$, $n\ge1$, and let $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb R^m$ be arbitrary points, allowing coincidences. With $|\cdot|$ the Euclidean norm, put
\[E(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^m:\prod_{i=1}^n|x-x_i|\le1\right\},\qquad
 V_{m,n}=\sup_{x_1,\ldots,x_n}\lambda_m(E(x_1,\ldots,x_n)),
\]
where $\lambda_m$ is $m$-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $V_{m,n}$ and the point configurations attaining the maximum. Separately, if $E(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is connected, must there be a centre $a\in\mathbb R^m$ for which
\[E(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\subseteq\{x:|x-a|\le2\}?\]
Both the volume and connected-set covering questions are included; the source records the covering conclusion in dimension two. \sref{2304023}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Maximize the volume of a product-of-distances sublevel set in higher dimensions and decide whether every connected such set fits inside a ball of radius two.
