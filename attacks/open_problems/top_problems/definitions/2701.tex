\section*{Kirby Problem 1.42}\label{2701}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2701 · KP-1.42 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Seifert form is an integral bilinear form $V$ on a free abelian group of even
rank for which $V-V^T$ is unimodular. On a Seifert surface $F$ of a knot it is
given by $V(a,b)=\operatorname{lk}(a^+,b)$ for homology classes represented by
curves, where $a^+$ is a positive normal push-off.
A form on a rank-$2g$ group is metabolic if a rank-$g$ direct summand $M$ has
$V|_{M\times M}=0$. Forms $V_1,V_2$ are algebraically concordant if
$V_1\oplus(-V_2)$ is metabolic.

Do algebraically concordant forms exist such that no pair of smoothly concordant
knots $K_1,K_2$ realizes them as Seifert forms? Ask the same question for
topological locally flat concordance. Concordance means an embedded annulus in
$S^3\times[0,1]$ joining the knots, with the relevant smoothness condition.

The quantifier is over all realizations of the two forms: exhibiting one
nonconcordant pair of knots is insufficient. The problem asks whether prescribed
algebraic data can itself obstruct every concordant realization, even though
the direct-sum form satisfies the usual algebraic concordance condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two algebraically concordant Seifert forms nevertheless be impossible to realize by concordant knots?
