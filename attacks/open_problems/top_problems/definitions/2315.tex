\section{Dense Graphs with Every Four-Cycle Rainbow}\label{2315}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2315 · Graph Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

A graph has a vertex set $V$ and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A subgraph need not be induced unless that word is stated. A proper vertex colouring gives adjacent vertices different colours; $\chi(G)$ is the least number of colours.

$K_t$ is a complete graph and $C_t$ a simple cycle of length $t$. A complete multipartite graph has no edges within parts and all edges between different parts. An independent set has no internal edge; a clique has all its possible internal edges. \sref{2315}{1} \sref{2315}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist some $\epsilon>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$, there exists a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with at least $\epsilon n^2$ many edges such that the edges can be coloured with $n$ colours so that every $C_4$ receives $4$ distinct colours? \sref{2315}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can a positive-density graph use only as many edge colours as vertices while assigning four different colours to every four-cycle?
