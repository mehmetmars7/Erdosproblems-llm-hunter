\section*{Kirby Problem 4.40}\label{2916}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2916 · KP-4.40 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth link of $2$-spheres in $S^4$ is a finite disjoint union
$L=K_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup K_r$ of smoothly embedded copies of $S^2$,
where $r\ge1$. Regard $S^4$ as the boundary of the standard smooth
$5$-ball $B^5$. The link is smoothly slice if there exist disjoint
proper smooth embeddings
\[
 D_j:B^3\hookrightarrow B^5\quad(1\le j\le r)
\]
whose boundary images are $K_j$. Proper means that the inverse image
of $\partial B^5$ is exactly $\partial B^3$, with the usual smooth
boundary-collar condition.

Is every smooth link of $2$-spheres in $S^4$ smoothly slice? Each
component must bound its own $3$-ball, and these balls must be
simultaneously disjoint in the same $B^5$. Individual sliceness of
every component therefore does not alone prove sliceness of the link.

The analogous locally flat question replaces smooth embeddings by
topological embeddings with local Euclidean pair charts, including
half-space charts at the boundary. It asks for the corresponding
locally flat disjoint balls. The source permits studying either
category; the displayed main formulation fixes the smooth one.

The number of components is arbitrary and no unlinking, boundary-link,
or complement-group hypothesis is imposed. The filling dimension is
five: bounding balls inside the original $S^4$ would be a substantially
stronger condition than the sliceness requested here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite link of two-spheres in the four-sphere bound disjoint properly embedded three-balls in the five-ball?
