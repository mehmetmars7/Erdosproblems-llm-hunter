\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.20}\label{2529}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2529 · KOU-21.20 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
All groups and actions are definable in a common sufficiently saturated
first-order structure, and parameters are allowed. The Morley rank of
a nonempty definable set is at least zero; rank at least $k+1$ means
that there are infinitely many disjoint definable subsets of rank at
least $k$. Finite Morley rank means this dimension is finite.
A definable group is connected if it has no proper definable subgroup
of finite index.

Let $M$ be a nontrivial simple group of finite Morley rank with no
involutions. Here simple means no nontrivial proper normal subgroup,
and an involution is an element of order exactly two. Suppose $M$
acts by automorphisms on a connected definable group $S$ of finite
Morley rank. The action map $M\times S\to S$ must be definable.
It is faithful when its kernel is $1$, and irreducible when there is
no definable subgroup $1<H<S$ invariant under every element of $M$.

Prove that every such faithful irreducible action is equivalent to
$M$ acting on itself by conjugation. With a left-action convention,
this means that there is a group isomorphism $\eta:S\to M$ such that
\[
 \eta(m\cdot s)=m\eta(s)m^{-1}
                    \qquad(m\in M,\ s\in S).
\]
Allowing an automorphism of the acting copy of $M$ gives the same
notion of equivalence after adjusting $\eta$.
No nilpotence condition on the proper subgroups of $M$ is assumed.
Connectedness is required of the target, while its simplicity is part
of the proposed conclusion. Irreducibility excludes even disconnected
definable invariant subgroups, not only connected ones.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is conjugation the only faithful irreducible definable action of an involution-free simple group of finite Morley rank on a connected group?
