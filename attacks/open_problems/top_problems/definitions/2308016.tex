\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 8.16}\label{2308016}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2308016 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^n$ equal to the unit polydisk $\mathbb D^n$ or the unit ball $\mathbb B^n$, define the Hardy class $H^p(\Omega)$ by \[\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(r\zeta)|^p\,d\sigma(\zeta)<\infty,\] where $d\sigma$ is normalized product angular measure on the distinguished boundary $\mathbb T^n$ of the polydisk, and normalized surface measure on the sphere for the ball. Products mean single products $gh$, not sums of products.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist $f\in H^1(\mathbb D^2)$ that cannot be expressed as $gh$ with $g,h\in H^2(\mathbb D^2)$? The source update reports Rosay’s affirmative answer for $n=2$. It also asks the analogous question on $\mathbb B^n$ for every $n>1$: is there $f\in H^1(\mathbb B^n)$ that is not a product of two $H^2(\mathbb B^n)$ functions? \sref{2308016}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Record the failure-of-factorization question on the bidisk and its higher-dimensional ball analogue; the source says the bidisk case was settled.
