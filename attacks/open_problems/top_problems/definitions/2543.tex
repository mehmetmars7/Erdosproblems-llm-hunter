\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.34}\label{2543}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2543 · KOU-21.34 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group $G$ has the unique product property if for every pair of
nonempty finite sets $A,B\subseteq G$, some element $c\in AB$ has
exactly one representation $c=ab$ with $(a,b)\in A\times B$.
Equivalently, at least one fibre of the multiplication map
$A\times B\to G$ is a singleton. The property does not require every
product to have a unique representation.

A strict partial order $<$ on $G$ is an irreflexive transitive relation;
comparability of every pair is not required. It is locally invariant
in the sense used here if
\[
 \forall g\in G\quad\forall h\in G\setminus\{1\},\qquad
 g<gh\quad\text{or}\quad g<gh^{-1}.
\]
A group is locally invariant orderable if it admits at least one such
partial order. The chosen alternative may depend on both $g$ and $h$.
No global invariance under all left or right translations is demanded.

Does there exist a group with the unique product property which is not
locally invariant orderable? Thus a positive answer requires proving the
unique product condition for all finite $A,B$, while ruling out every
strict partial order satisfying the displayed local alternatives.

The group is not assumed finite, finitely generated, or finitely
presented. Its order relation is an auxiliary structure that may be
chosen freely, not an order already supplied with the group. The
question separates two existence properties; failing one particular
attempted order does not prove failure of local invariant orderability.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a unique product group admitting no partial order in which every nontrivial right translation moves a point upward in at least one direction?
