\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.11}\label{1100111}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100111 · AMR-010-0111 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finitely generated group $G$ is word-hyperbolic when the geodesic triangles of a Cayley graph are uniformly thin. It is one-ended when removing any finite vertex set from that graph leaves exactly one infinite component. A group is virtually a closed hyperbolic surface group if it has a finite-index subgroup isomorphic to $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$ for some closed orientable surface of genus $g\geq2$; using nonorientable hyperbolic surfaces gives the same virtual class.

Does there exist a one-ended word-hyperbolic group $G$ which is not virtually a closed hyperbolic surface group and nevertheless satisfies
\[
 \forall H\leq G\quad [G:H]=\infty
       \Longrightarrow H\text{ is a free group}?
\]
Here a free group may have a finite or infinite basis, and the trivial subgroup is free of rank zero. The quantifier covers all subgroups, not only finitely generated or quasiconvex ones.

Equivalently, a negative answer would say that every one-ended hyperbolic group outside the virtual surface class has some nonfree infinite-index subgroup. The witnessing subgroup need not have finite presentation or finite rank. The one-ended hypothesis excludes ordinary free groups, while the virtual surface exclusion removes the motivating surface examples. No restriction on the dimension of a classifying space is included. In particular, demonstrating that many finitely generated subgroups are free would not establish the universal subgroup property requested of an example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a one-ended hyperbolic group outside the virtual surface class have only free subgroups of infinite index?
