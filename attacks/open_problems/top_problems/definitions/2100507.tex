\section{Open Problems in Integrable Systems — Argument shift method and Jordan-Kronecker invariants of Lie algebras}\label{2100507}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2100507 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $V=\mathbb R^N$. A quadratic Poisson bracket is given by homogeneous quadratic polynomials $P_{ij}(x)=-P_{ji}(x)$ such that
\[\{f,g\}=\sum_{i,j=1}^N P_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_j}\]
satisfies the Jacobi identity. Let $2r$ be the maximum rank of the skew-symmetric matrix $P(x)$. A complete polynomial integrable system is a collection of $N-r$ polynomial functions with pairwise vanishing brackets and with linearly independent differentials almost everywhere on $V$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every quadratic Poisson bracket on a finite-dimensional vector space admit a complete polynomial integrable system in this sense? \sref{2100507}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every quadratic Poisson structure on a vector space have enough independent commuting polynomial functions for Liouville integrability?
