{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306044 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. Let $S$ consist of holomorphic injective functions $f(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge2}a_nz^n$ on $\mathbb D$; set $a_0=0$, $a_1=1$. A function in $S$ is starlike if its image is starlike about $0$, and convex if its image is a convex domain. For Taylor series $f(z)=\sum a_nz^n$ and $g(z)=\sum b_nz^n$, their Hadamard convolution is $(f*g)(z)=\sum a_nb_nz^n$, and their integrated convolution is $(f\otimes g)(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}(a_nb_n/n)z^n$. These series are holomorphic on $\mathbb D$ when both original functions are.Let $S_R=\{f\in S:a_n\in\mathbb R\text{ for all }n\}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does $f,g\in S_R$ imply $f\otimes g\in S_R$? \sref{2306044}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Historical question: is the integrated convolution of two real-coefficient normalized univalent functions univalent? The source update gives a counterexample.
