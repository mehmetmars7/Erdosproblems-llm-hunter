\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.21}\label{2305021}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305021 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. All Taylor coefficients below are taken at $0$.For $w\in\mathbb D$ use the disc automorphism $\phi_w(z)=(z-w)/(1-\overline w z)$ and write
\[f_w=f\circ\phi_w=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n^{(w)}z^n,\qquad
W_f=\left\{w\in\mathbb D:\sum_{n=0}^\infty|a_n^{(w)}|<\infty\right\}.
\]
Here $f$ is any holomorphic function on $\mathbb D$. Positive measure means positive planar Lebesgue measure; dense means dense in $\mathbb D$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Describe the possible sets $W_f$. Does each of the following assumptions, separately, imply $W_f=\mathbb D$: (i) $W_f$ is dense; (ii) $W_f$ is uncountable; (iii) $W_f$ has positive planar measure? If $f$ is univalent, must $W_f$ be either empty or all of $\mathbb D$? \sref{2305021}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify the centers at which a disc reparametrization has absolutely summable coefficients, including the three largeness criteria and the univalent case.
