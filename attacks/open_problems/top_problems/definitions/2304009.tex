\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.9}\label{2304009}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2304009 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\ge1$ and let $f(z)=\prod_{j=1}^n(z-z_j)$ be a monic polynomial of degree $n$, with zeros repeated according to multiplicity. Its filled unit lemniscate is the compact set
\[E_f=\{z\in\mathbb C:|f(z)|\le1\}.
\]
The monic normalization is the one used in the source's Problem 4.7 and its following lemniscate questions.For a compact set $C\subset\mathbb C$, write $\operatorname{diam}C=\sup\{|z-w|:z,w\in C\}$. Connected components are counted as subsets of $E_f$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each $c>0$, is there a constant $A(c)$ independent of $n$ and $f$ such that the number of components of $E_f$ having diameter greater than $1+c^2$ is at most $A(c)$? The source's Update 4.9 records counterexamples to this uniform-constant conjecture and asks instead whether, for each fixed $c>0$, the number of components of diameter greater than $1+c$ is uniformly $o(n)$, or even $o(n^\varepsilon)$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, as $n\to\infty$. Here uniformly means the supremum over all monic degree-$n$ polynomials. \sref{2304009}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the number of large components of a monic polynomial’s lemniscate be bounded independently of its degree? The source gives counterexamples and asks for smaller-than-linear, or smaller-than-every-power, growth instead.
