\section{Smooth Surfaces Intersecting 2-planes}\label{134}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 134 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\geq3$ and a $C^\infty$ function $f:(-2,2)^{n-2}\to\mathbb R^2$ with $f(0)=0$, define its local graph
\[
 \Gamma_f=\{(x,f(x)):x\in(-1,1)^{n-2}\}\subset\mathbb R^n.
\]
An affine two-plane is a translate of a two-dimensional linear subspace of $\mathbb R^n$. Intersection points are distinct points; neither transversality nor finiteness of the full intersection is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist $n_0$ such that, for every integer $n\geq n_0$ and every such smooth local graph $\Gamma_f$, there is an affine two-plane $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
\[
 |P\cap\Gamma_f|\geq5?
\]
The question concerns the local geometry of a codimension-two smooth surface, rather than global topology. \sref{134}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
In all sufficiently large dimensions, must every smooth local surface of codimension two meet some affine two-plane in at least five points?
