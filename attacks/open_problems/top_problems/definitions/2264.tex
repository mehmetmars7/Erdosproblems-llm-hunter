\section{The Fastest and Slowest Growth of Divisibility Chains of Primes}\label{2264}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2264 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



A prime chain here is an increasing sequence of primes with $p_{j+1}=u_jp_j+1$ for a positive integer $u_j$. The sequence may omit other primes. The limit $p_k^{1/k}\to\infty$ is equivalently $\log p_k/k\to\infty$, while the second question asks for a single chain with the displayed upper growth rate. \sref{2264}{1} \sref{2264}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be a sequence of primes such that $p_{i+1}\equiv 1\pmod{p_i}$. Is it true that $ \lim_k p_k^{1/k}=\infty? $ Does there exist such a sequence with $ p_k \leq \exp(k(\log k)^{1+o(1)})? $ \sref{2264}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove mandatory superexponential growth or construct a near-conjectural slow chain. \sref{2264}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How rapidly must a prime chain grow when each prime divides the next prime minus one, and can the proposed near-minimal growth be achieved?
