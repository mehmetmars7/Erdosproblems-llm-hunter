\section{Kirby Problem 2.13}\label{2761}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2761 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $S$ be an oriented finite-type surface and $\Gamma\subset S$ a finite embedded graph, considered up to ambient isotopy. A graph surgery is permitted when a component $D$ of $S\setminus\Gamma$ is a disk. Choose oriented edges $x,y$ on its boundary and an arc $\lambda$ in $D$ between their interiors. A small regular neighborhood $N$ of $\lambda$ is a rectangle with opposite sides lying in $x,y$. Remove those two subarcs from the graph and replace them by the other two sides of $N$, which run parallel to $\lambda$. This is the source's surgery move. Intermediate complements need not remain disks, and no trivalence, four-valence or unicellular hypothesis is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Give necessary and sufficient conditions on two ambient-isotopy classes of embedded finite graphs $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2\subset S$ for a finite sequence of these surgeries, with isotopies between steps, to transform one into the other. \sref{2761}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize exactly when two embedded graphs on a surface are connected by the source’s disk-face surgery moves, without restricting to unicellular or fixed-valence cases.
