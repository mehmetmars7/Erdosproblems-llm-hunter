\section*{Memory–precision lower bounds for exact Gaussian regression}\label{30007456}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every fixed finite $A>0$, do there exist constants $c_A>0$ and $d_A>=2$ such that the following holds for every $d>=d_A$, integer $0<=M<=Ad^2$, and $0<\epsilon <=1/10$? A jointly measurable one-pass learner has at most $2^M$ persistent states and must stop by a prescribed deterministic finite horizon T. Its independent observations are Gaussian rows $x_t$ with distribution $N(0,I_d)$ and exact labels $<x_t,S>$, where S is an independent uniform unit vector. The data-free shared seed and initial state, and fresh transition randomness, are independent of these data. If its output unit vector estimates S within angle epsilon with probability at least 2/3, must $T>=c_A$ d log(1/epsilon)? Transitions may use the current observation and index but retain only the next state; stopping output uses the terminal state, index and fresh randomness only, and discarded observations cannot be revisited.
