\section*{Stochastic zeta functions}\label{4600010}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600010 · AMR-045-0010 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $P=(p_{ij})$ be a finite stochastic matrix: $p_{ij}\ge0$ and every
row sum is one. Require $P$ to be primitive, meaning some power has all
entries positive. Its stationary Markov measure on bi-infinite admissible
state sequences, together with the shift, is a mixing Markov shift.
For $t\in\mathbb R$, form the matrix $P^{[t]}$ by replacing each positive
entry $p_{ij}$ by $p_{ij}^t$ and leaving zero entries zero.

Using the usual generating-variable normalization, its stochastic zeta
function is
\[
 Z_P(z,t)=\det(I-zP^{[t]})^{-1}
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}
                              \operatorname{tr}((P^{[t]})^r)\right).
\]
The equality holds as a formal power series in $z$, and analytically
for sufficiently small $z$ at each fixed $t$. The trace sums $t$th powers
of weights of closed state paths, where a path's weight is the product
of its transition probabilities.

The problem is to characterize intrinsically all two-variable functions
that occur as $Z_P$ for some finite primitive stochastic $P$ of arbitrary
size. The equivalent source convention uses $\det(wI-P^{[t]})^{-1}$;
the two are related by $w=1/z$ and the evident power of $z$.
A characterization should give checkable structural conditions and a
realization theorem, beyond restating existence of a matrix. The
coefficient functions retain all path weights, so the ordinary unweighted
zeta function or the entropy alone does not supply the requested data.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize exactly which weighted periodic-path zeta functions arise from finite mixing Markov chains.
