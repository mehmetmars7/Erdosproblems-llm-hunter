\section*{Kirby Problem 3.3}\label{2801}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2801 · KP-3.3 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a connected complete noncompact hyperbolic three-manifold of finite
volume. A topological ideal triangulation expresses $M$ as finitely many
tetrahedra with their vertices removed, whose triangular faces are identified
in pairs. The removed vertices correspond to ends of the manifold; repeated
identifications within the quotient are allowed.

A geometric ideal tetrahedron in $\mathbb H^3$ is the convex hull of four
distinct points on the sphere at infinity $\partial_\infty\mathbb H^3=S^2$,
not all lying on one circle. This last condition rules out flat tetrahedra.
A geometric ideal triangulation requires the tetrahedra to have positive
volume and their face identifications to be hyperbolic isometries, with the
resulting metric equal to the given complete metric on $M$.

Does every such $M$ admit a geometric ideal triangulation? The tetrahedron
shapes and the combinatorial triangulation may both be chosen. In the
orientable case all tetrahedra must have positive orientation relative to the
orientation of $M$. A decomposition using flat or negatively oriented
tetrahedra does not satisfy this requirement. Neither does a geometric
decomposition into more general ideal polyhedra, unless it can be subdivided
into tetrahedra compatibly across every glued face.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every cusped hyperbolic three-manifold be assembled from genuine positive-volume ideal tetrahedra with its complete hyperbolic metric?
