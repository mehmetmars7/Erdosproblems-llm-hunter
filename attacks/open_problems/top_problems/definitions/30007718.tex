\section*{Biodiversity and economic substitutability}\label{30007718}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider one agricultural basin with species abundance vector \(b\in\mathbb R_+^J\), reproducible capital \(k\geq0\), and agricultural output \(F(k,b,x)\), where \(J\) is the number of measured functional groups and \(x\) records land, labor, and climate held fixed in the comparison. Let \(b(s)\) be a stated biodiversity-loss path indexed by severity \(s\in[0,1]\), and let baseline output be \(y_0=F(k_0,b(0),x)\). Define the replacement-cost frontier
\[R(s)=\inf\{k-k_0:k\geq k_0,\ F(k,b(s),x)\geq y_0\},\]
with \(R(s)=+\infty\) if no feasible capital level preserves output. Identify whether \(R(s)\) stays finite, rises sharply, or becomes infeasible within the basin’s empirically relevant loss range. Allow ecosystem feedbacks and lagged effects, and specify the time horizon before treating measured abundance as a productive input. Statistical correlations between biodiversity and productivity need an ecological and economic identification strategy to recover this frontier. Evaluate bounds under alternative production restrictions rather than presume a constant elasticity of substitution.
