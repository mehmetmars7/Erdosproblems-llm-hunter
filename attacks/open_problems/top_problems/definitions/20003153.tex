\section*{Finite-window largeness of the O'Neill tensor}\label{20003153}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20003153 · AIM-TOPOLOGY-0241 · Topology · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\pi:(M^{b+f},g)\to(B^b,h)$ be a surjective Riemannian submersion between closed connected smooth Riemannian manifolds, with $b,f\geq1$.
This means that $d\pi$ is surjective and restricts to an isometry from the horizontal space
$\mathcal H_x=(\ker d\pi_x)^\perp$ onto $T_{\pi(x)}B$.
The vertical space $\mathcal V_x=\ker d\pi_x$ has dimension $f$, the fiber dimension.
Assume all sectional curvatures of $g$ are strictly positive.

Must every such submersion satisfy
\[
 f<b?
\]
The workshop also asks the weaker question whether there exists a function $F:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ such that $f\leq F(b)$ for every submersion in this class, with no dependence on the metric, the total dimension, or the topology of the base.
Positive-dimensional base and fiber exclude the constant-map and identity-map degeneracies.
Neither totally geodesic fibers nor homogeneity of the total space is assumed.

The suggested differential-geometric approach concerns O'Neill's tensor:
for horizontal vector fields $X,Y$, define $A_XY=\tfrac12[X,Y]^{\mathcal V}$.
At a point it determines a linear map
$\Lambda^2\mathcal H_x\to\mathcal V_x$.
The accompanying structural question is what lower bounds on its image dimension positive curvature can force at some point.
No unquantified assertion that this image is ``large'' is treated as an additional hypothesis; the two dimension inequalities above are the precise primary targets.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a Riemannian submersion from a positively curved closed manifold have smaller-dimensional fibers than base, or at least uniformly bounded fiber dimension?
