\section{Strongly maximal matchings}\label{3000084}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3000084 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A hypergraph is $H=(V,\mathcal E)$, with an arbitrary possibly infinite vertex set and a set of hyperedges. A matching $M\subseteq\mathcal E$ has pairwise disjoint edges. It is strongly maximal if, for every other matching $N$, \[|N\setminus M|\le |M\setminus N|,\] where the comparison is between cardinalities, including infinite ones. Bounded rank means that some fixed $k\in\mathbb N$ satisfies $|e|\le k$ for every $e\in\mathcal E$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must every bounded-rank hypergraph have a strongly maximal matching? \sref{3000084}{2}
This is the historical matching part of Conjecture 2.1 in \sref{3000084}{2}. Theorem 1.2 of \sref{3000084}{3} gives a 3-uniform counterexample, so that question has a negative answer.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a uniform finite bound on hyperedge size force a matching that cannot be improved by any competing matching, even through infinitely many changes?
