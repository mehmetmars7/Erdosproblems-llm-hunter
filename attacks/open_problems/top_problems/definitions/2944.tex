\section*{Kirby Problem 4.68}\label{2944}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2944 · KP-4.68 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a closed smooth $4$-manifold $X$, a self-diffeomorphism
$f:X\to X$ is exotic if it is topologically isotopic to the identity
but not smoothly isotopic to it. The topological condition means a
continuous family of homeomorphisms $H_t$, with $H_0=\mathrm{id}$
and $H_1=f$. A smooth isotopy would be a smooth family of
diffeomorphisms with those endpoints. Both are isotopies of the
whole manifold, without a boundary condition.

Does every closed smooth $4$-manifold admit an exotic diffeomorphism?
Two highlighted cases ask: (a) does any definite closed smooth
$4$-manifold admit one; and (b) does the standard $S^2\times S^2$
or the standard K3 surface admit one?
For an oriented manifold, definite means that its real intersection
form on $H_2(X;\mathbb R)$ is positive definite or negative definite.
The standard K3 smooth manifold may be represented by a nonsingular
quartic complex surface in $\mathbb{CP}^3$.

An exotic diffeomorphism is a nontrivial smooth mapping class which
becomes trivial in the topological mapping class group. Failure to
be equal to the identity map is far weaker; even a nontrivial
rotation can be smoothly isotopic to it. Likewise a homotopy of maps
is weaker than the required topological isotopy.
The universal question and each named special case concern the
specified smooth manifold itself, without first changing its smoothing
or stabilizing it by connected sum.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed smooth four-manifold admit a diffeomorphism topologically but not smoothly isotopic to the identity, including definite examples, S²×S² and K3?
