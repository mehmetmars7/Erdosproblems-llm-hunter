\section{Square packing in a square}\label{7200031}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 7200031 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a real $x\geq1$, let $N(x)$ be the maximum number of closed unit squares contained in $[0,x]^2$ with pairwise disjoint interiors. Each small square may be translated and rotated independently. Define the wasted area by $W(x)=x^2-N(x)$. In particular, $W(m)=0$ for every positive integer $m$, since an integer-sided square admits the usual grid tiling.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the asymptotic behavior of $W(x)$ as the real side length $x\to\infty$, including its dependence on side lengths between integers. Any asymptotic description must account for $W(m)=0$ at integer $m$. \sref{7200031}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How does the area left unused by the best packing of unit squares in a large square grow as the side length increases, including the fluctuations between exact integer tilings?
