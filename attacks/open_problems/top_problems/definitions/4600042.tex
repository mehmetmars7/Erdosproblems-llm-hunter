\section*{Jointly periodic points in one dimension}\label{4600042}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600042 · AMR-045-0042 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $A$ be a finite alphabet, $X=A^{\mathbb Z}$, and
$(\sigma x)_j=x_{j+1}$. A cellular automaton is specified by a
radius $r\ge0$ and a rule $\varphi:A^{2r+1}\to A$ through
$(Fx)_j=\varphi(x_{j-r},\ldots,x_{j+r})$. Assume that
$F:X\to X$ is onto. The full shift has the product topology,
whose basic neighborhoods prescribe finitely many coordinates.

A configuration $x$ is jointly periodic if positive integers
$p,q$ exist with $\sigma^p x=x$ and $F^q x=x$.
The conjecture asserts that these configurations form a dense
subset of $X$. Explicitly, for every finite word
$w\in A^{\{0,\ldots,L-1\}}$ there should exist $x\in X$ and
$p,q\ge1$ such that
\[
        x_j=w_j\ (0\le j<L),\qquad
        \sigma^p x=x,\qquad F^q x=x.
\]
Neither period is prescribed or required to equal the word length.

For a fixed $p$, the finite set $\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^p)$
is mapped into itself by $F$. Every trajectory on that finite
set eventually enters a cycle, but some configurations can lie
strictly before the cycle. The problem asks that actual cycle
points, over all spatial periods, realize every finite pattern.
Global surjectivity on $A^{\mathbb Z}$ does not simply assert
that each of these finite restrictions is a permutation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every finite word occur inside a configuration periodic both in space and in time for an onto one-dimensional cellular automaton?
