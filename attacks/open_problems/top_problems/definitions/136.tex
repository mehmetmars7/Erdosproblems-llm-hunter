\section{Balanced Ham Sandwich Line}\label{136}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 136 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an affine line $\ell\subset\mathbb R^2$, write $H_\ell^+$ and $H_\ell^-$ for its two open half-planes, in either order. Points on $\ell$ are counted in neither half-plane. For a finite set $X$, its imbalance at $\ell$ is
\[
 b_X(\ell)=\bigl||X\cap H_\ell^+|-|X\cap H_\ell^-|\bigr|.
\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $n\geq2$ and every $n$-point set $X\subset\mathbb R^2$, does there exist an affine line $\ell$ with $|X\cap\ell|\geq2$ and $b_X(\ell)\leq100$? No general-position assumption is made, and the line may contain more than two points. \sref{136}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite planar point set admit a line through two of its points with at most one hundred points of imbalance between the two sides?
