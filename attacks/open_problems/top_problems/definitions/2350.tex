Let $r_A^{\rm un}(m)=\#\{a\leq b:\ a,b\in A,\ a+b=m\}$. At most one value $m$ may satisfy $r_A^{\rm un}(m)>1$; all other represented sums must be unique. The exceptional value's multiplicity is not bounded. \sref{2350}{1} \sref{2350}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots N\}$ be a set such that there exists at most one $n$ with more than one solution to $n=a+b$ (with $a\leq b\in A$). Estimate the maximal possible size of $\lvert A\rvert$ - in particular, is it true that $ \lvert A\rvert \leq (1+o(1))\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}N^{1/2}? $ \sref{2350}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove or disprove the 2/sqrt(3) upper constant. \sref{2350}{1}

If only one sum may have multiple unordered representations, is the size of an interval subset bounded by the proposed improved square-root constant?
