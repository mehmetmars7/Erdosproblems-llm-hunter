\section*{Classification of spherical quadrilaterals}\label{3700011}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3700011 · AMR-036-0011 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A spherical quadrilateral is an oriented surface homeomorphic
to a closed disk, with four distinct boundary vertices
$v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4$ in cyclic order, equipped with a metric of
Gaussian curvature $1$ away from the vertices. Each boundary
arc between successive vertices is geodesic, and the interior
angle at $v_j$ is $\pi\alpha_j$, with $\alpha_j>0$.
The metric is smooth in the interior and along the open sides;
the vertices have the prescribed corner singularities.

Two such quadrilaterals are identified by an isometry preserving
the ordered vertices. The underlying conformal quadrilateral
has modulus $m>0$: it is conformally equivalent to a
Euclidean rectangle of width $1$ and height $m$, with
vertices matched in the specified cyclic order.

Classify spherical quadrilaterals for arbitrary positive angle
data. Determine which quadruples $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4)$
are realizable, which moduli $m$ occur for each realizable
quadruple, and the isometry classes for fixed angles and
modulus, including their possible multiplicity.
The area identity
\[
           \operatorname{Area}=\pi
                         (\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4-2)
\]
gives a necessary positivity condition, but the problem
requires the full restrictions.
Angles larger than $\pi$ are allowed. The metric need not
come from a simply embedded region of the standard sphere;
its developing map may overlap. Restricting to convex
spherical quadrilaterals would omit the main general cases.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify curvature-one quadrilaterals by their angles, conformal modulus, and possible nonuniqueness.
