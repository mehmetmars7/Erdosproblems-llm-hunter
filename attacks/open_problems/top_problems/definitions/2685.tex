\section*{Kirby Problem 1.26}\label{2685}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2685 · KP-1.26 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset S^3$ be a knot, and let $J_n(K;q)$ denote its zero-framed
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ colored Jones polynomial in the standard variable $q$, colored
by the $n$-dimensional representation. Use the unreduced normalization
$J_n(U;q)=(q^{n/2}-q^{-n/2})/(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})$.
Let $d_+(n)$ and $d_-(n)$ be the largest and smallest powers of $q$ occurring.
The set of Jones slopes is
\[
js(K)=\operatorname{Clust}_{n\to\infty}\frac{4d_+(n)}{n^2}
\ \cup\ \operatorname{Clust}_{n\to\infty}\frac{4d_-(n)}{n^2},
\]
where cluster points are limits along infinite subsequences.

A boundary slope $p/q$ is realized by a properly embedded orientable surface
in $X=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K$, with nonempty essential boundary
curves of class $p\mu+q\lambda$, that is incompressible, boundary-incompressible,
and not boundary parallel. Incompressibility rules out compressing disks;
boundary-incompressibility rules out disks giving compressions along essential
arcs on the surface.

The Slope Conjecture asks whether every member of $js(K)$ is a boundary slope
of such a surface, for every knot $K$. The inclusion in the boundary-slope set
is the conjectural target supplied by the source notes after their definition
of $js(K)$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every limiting quadratic degree of the colored Jones polynomials arise from an essential surface in the knot exterior?
