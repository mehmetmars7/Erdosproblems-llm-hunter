\section*{Non-equilibrium learning attractors}\label{30007566}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let a finite two-player game have rational payoff matrices \(A,B\in\mathbb Q^{r\times s}\), mixed-strategy space \(X=\Delta_r\times\Delta_s\), and replicator flow \(\varphi_t\) defined by
\[
 \dot x_a=x_a((Ay)_a-x^TAy),\qquad
 \dot y_b=y_b((x^TB)_b-x^TBy).
\]
Here \(\Delta_k\) is the probability simplex on \(k\) actions. The preference graph has pure profiles as vertices and an arc for each unilateral weak payoff improvement. Let \(H\) be a strongly connected component with no outgoing arcs. Its content \(K_H\) consists of mixed profiles whose product distribution is supported entirely on \(H\). A compact invariant set \(S\subseteq X\) is attracting if some positively invariant neighborhood \(U\) of \(S\) satisfies \(\sup_{z\in U}\operatorname{dist}(\varphi_t(z),S)\to0\); it is an attractor if it has no proper compact invariant attracting subset. Apply these definitions to \(S=K_H\). A subgame \(Y\) is a product of faces of the two simplexes. A local source of \(H\) is a point \(z\in K_H\cap Y\), with \(Y\not\subseteq K_H\), that is a quasi-strict Nash equilibrium of the negated-payoff game restricted to \(Y\): its best responses are exactly its supported actions. Determine whether every sink component whose content fails to be an attractor necessarily has such a local source in some subgame. Prove the implication for all finite bimatrix games in the stated domain, or supply a counterexample and identify the alternative obstruction. The converse witness question is explicitly posed after the paper disproves earlier one-to-one sink–attractor conjectures; those disproved conjectures are not the target here.

\par\textbf{Formulation and scope.} A scoped instance of the residual witness question in §4, item1, restricted here to rational bimatrix games rather than all finite normal-form games. Attraction is defined for arbitrary compact invariant sets before specializing to sink content, and the source’s uniform-attraction convention is retained. \subsection{Short English statement}
For a finite rational bimatrix game, must every sink component whose content is not a replicator attractor contain a local-source witness in some subgame? This is the residual converse question, not the older sink–attractor correspondence conjectures that have been disproved.
