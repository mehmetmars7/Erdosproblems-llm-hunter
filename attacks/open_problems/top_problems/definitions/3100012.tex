\section{Is it true that every graph whose vertices have odd degree greater than one contains a cycle of length 2\textasciicircum{}(n) for some n}\label{3100012}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3100012 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph, with $V\ne\varnothing$. Its vertex degrees are $\deg_G(v)=|\{w:\{v,w\}\in E\}|$. A cycle of length $\ell\geq3$ consists of distinct vertices $v_1,\ldots,v_\ell$ with edges $v_iv_{i+1}$, interpreting $v_{\ell+1}=v_1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If every $\deg_G(v)$ is an odd integer greater than one, must $G$ contain a cycle of length $2^r$ for some integer $r\geq2$? This is the recorded odd-degree instance of the Erd\H{o}s--Gy\'arf\'as power-of-two cycle problem; the cited source states the broader minimum-degree-at-least-three conjecture. \sref{3100012}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every finite graph with all vertex degrees odd and at least three contain a cycle whose length is a power of two?
