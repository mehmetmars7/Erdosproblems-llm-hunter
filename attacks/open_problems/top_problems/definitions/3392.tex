\section*{Perfect cuboid}\label{3392}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3392 · OPG-37221 · Number Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A cuboid is a rectangular box with positive edge lengths $a,b,c$.
Its three face diagonals have lengths
$\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2+c^2}$ and $\sqrt{b^2+c^2}$, and
its space diagonal has length $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$. A perfect cuboid
has all seven lengths integral. Thus the existence question is
whether the system
\[
 a^2+b^2=d^2,\quad a^2+c^2=e^2,\quad b^2+c^2=f^2,
 \qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=g^2
\]
has a solution in strictly positive integers $a,b,c,d,e,f,g$.
All four diagonal equations are required simultaneously. A box
with only the face-diagonal conditions does not yet meet the target.

Permuting the edges changes no existence condition, so one may
assume $a\le b\le c$. Since the equations are homogeneous,
existence over positive rational lengths is equivalent to existence
over positive integers, by clearing denominators. Scaling also
allows restriction to primitive solutions with $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$.
Zero edges are excluded because they collapse the box into lower
dimension. The problem asks for one certified perfect cuboid, or
a proof that none exists over the entire positive integer range;
a search with bounded edges supplies only a finite exclusion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a rectangular box have integer edges, all three integer face diagonals, and an integer space diagonal?
