\section{10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Matrix version of 6 deviations suffice}\label{2800403}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2800403 · Probability}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a real matrix \(M\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), its operator norm is
\[\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\|v\|_2=1}\|Mv\|_2.\]
Let \(H_1,\ldots,H_n\) be real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrices with \(\|H_k\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\) for every \(k\). A signing is a vector \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there an absolute constant \(C>0\), independent of \(n\) and of the matrices, such that for every integer \(n\ge1\) and every such family one can choose a signing with
\[\left\|\sum_{k=1}^n\varepsilon_k H_k\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt n?\] \sref{2800403}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can signs always be chosen so that the operator norm of the signed sum of n symmetric n-by-n contractions is at most a universal constant times the square root of n?
