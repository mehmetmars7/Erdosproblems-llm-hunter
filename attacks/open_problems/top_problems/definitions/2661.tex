\section*{Kirby Problem 1.2}\label{2661}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2661 · KP-1.2 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $V=S^1\times D^2$ be a solid torus and let a pattern $P\subset\operatorname{int}V$
be a smooth knot. For a knot $K\subset S^3$, identify $V$ with a tubular neighborhood
of $K$, sending the preferred longitude of $V$ to the zero-linking longitude of
$K$. The image of $P$ is the satellite $K_P$.
Write $c(J)$ for the minimum number of crossings among diagrams of a knot $J$.
The geometric winding number is
\[
w(P)=\min_D |P\cap D|,
\]
where $D$ ranges over properly embedded meridian disks of $V$ transverse to $P$.
These are unsigned intersection counts, so $w(P)$ can differ from the absolute
value of the integer represented by $[P]\in H_1(V;\mathbb Z)$.

The two proposed inequalities are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a genuine satellite with nontrivial companion $K$ and essential companion
torus, $c(K_P)\geq c(K)$.
\item More generally, for every pattern and companion,
$c(K_P)\geq w(P)^2c(K)$.
\end{enumerate}
Here an essential companion torus is incompressible and not boundary parallel in
the satellite exterior. The second inequality uses the geometric winding number
even for patterns with algebraic winding number zero.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a satellite have fewer crossings than its companion, or fewer than the companion's crossing number multiplied by the square of the pattern's geometric winding?
