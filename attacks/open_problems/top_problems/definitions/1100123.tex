\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.23}\label{1100123}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100123 · AMR-010-0123 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a connected finite simplicial complex. A local coefficient system $\mathcal M$ on $X$ assigns an abelian group to each point and coherent transport isomorphisms along paths; equivalently, after a basepoint choice it is a module with an action of $\pi_1(X)$. A map $f:Y\to X$ pulls it back to a system $f^*\mathcal M$ on $Y$.

Does there always exist a connected locally CAT($-1$) polyhedral complex $Y$ and a map $f:Y\to X$ such that
\[
 f_*:\pi_1(Y)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(X),\qquad
 f_*:H_j(Y;f^*\mathcal M)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_j(X;\mathcal M)
\]
for every integer $j\geq0$ and every local coefficient system $\mathcal M$ on $X$? Locally CAT($-1$) means that each point has a neighborhood with a geodesic metric satisfying comparison with hyperbolic triangles of curvature $-1$; the polyhedral metrics agree along glued faces.

This asks for a negatively curved version of the Kan--Thurston realization theorem. Its group-theoretic variant asks for a homology realization by an aspherical space whose fundamental group is word-hyperbolic. The requested local-coefficient isomorphisms are stronger than equality of ordinary integral homology. In particular, the fundamental-group map need not be injective or an isomorphism. Merely placing the fundamental group inside a hyperbolic group does not supply the map or the homology equivalences required here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every finite complex have all its local-coefficient homology realized by a negatively curved aspherical complex?
