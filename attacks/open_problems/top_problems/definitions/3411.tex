\section*{Smooth 4-dimensional Poincare conjecture}\label{3411}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3411 · OPG-37125 · Topology · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S^4=\{x\in\mathbb R^5:\|x\|=1\}$ with its standard smooth
structure. A smooth homotopy four-sphere is a closed, connected,
smooth four-dimensional manifold $M$ homotopy equivalent to $S^4$.
Closed means compact without boundary. Homotopy equivalence means
there are continuous maps $f:M\to S^4$ and $g:S^4\to M$ with
$g\circ f$ and $f\circ g$ homotopic to the respective identities.

The smooth four-dimensional Poincar\'e conjecture is
\[
 M\simeq S^4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                    M\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}S^4
\]
for every such $M$. A diffeomorphism is a bijective smooth map with
smooth inverse. No particular homotopy equivalence is required to
become the diffeomorphism, and no orientation is prescribed.

The smooth category and dimension four are part of the target.
A homeomorphism alone does not identify differentiable structures.
The statement also cannot be tested using noncompact manifolds or
manifolds with boundary, since neither is in the domain of the
quantifier. A negative answer must supply a closed smooth homotopy
four-sphere and an obstruction to every diffeomorphism to the
standard sphere. Merely a nonstandard presentation, handle picture,
or triangulation does not establish such an obstruction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every closed smooth four-manifold with the homotopy type of the four-sphere diffeomorphic to the standard four-sphere?
