\section*{Exact colorings of graphs}\label{3114}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3114 · OPG-37229 · Graph Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer $c$, an exact $c$-coloring of the complete graph on $\mathbb N$ is a surjective function $\varphi:[\mathbb N]^2\to\{1,\ldots,c\}$, where $[X]^2$ denotes the unordered two-element subsets of a set $X$. Exactness concerns the whole infinite graph: every color must occur on at least one edge, but a color need not occur infinitely often.

For positive integers $c,m$, let $P(c,m)$ mean that every exact $c$-coloring admits an infinite subset $W\subseteq\mathbb N$ for which precisely $m$ colors occur among its edges:
\[
 \left|\{\varphi(e):e\in[W]^2\}\right|=m.
\]
The proposed classification is
\[
 P(c,m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\in\{1,2,c\},
 \qquad 1\le m\le c.
\]
Repeated entries in this set are harmless for $c=1$ or $2$. The restriction $m\le c$ is explicit, since more colors cannot appear on a subgraph than in the whole graph.

The positive direction requires an infinite vertex set for every admissible coloring, and may use different sets for different values of $m$. The negative direction asks, for each pair $3\le m<c$, for an exact $c$-coloring such that no infinite vertex set realizes exactly $m$ colors. Finite complete subgraphs realizing $m$ colors do not suffice. No density, regularity, or effectiveness restriction is placed on either the coloring or the infinite subset.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which exact numbers of edge colors must appear on some infinite complete subgraph of every finite exact coloring?
