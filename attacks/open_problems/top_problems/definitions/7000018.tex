\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 4.2}\label{7000018}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000018 · AMR-069-0018 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a compact convex body with nonempty
interior. Its support function is
$h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle$ for $u\in S^2$.
The body has constant width $w>0$ when
\[
                         h_K(u)+h_K(-u)=w\quad(u\in S^2).
\]
Thus every pair of parallel supporting planes is separated by
the same distance. Write $A(K)$ for the area of its boundary
and $V(K)$ for its three-dimensional volume.

For a prescribed $A_0>0$, determine
\[
       \inf\{V(K):K\text{ has constant width},\ A(K)=A_0\},
\]
and characterize the bodies attaining the sharp bound, up to
rigid motion. The numerical width is allowed to vary between
competitors: the fixed quantity in this catalogue entry is
surface area.

Equivalently, determine the minimum possible scale-invariant
ratio $V(K)/A(K)^{3/2}$ among constant-width bodies. Nonsmooth
convex boundaries are permitted, so minimizers are not ruled
out merely because they contain seams or curvature jumps.
The planar analogue optimizes area of constant-width curves;
it does not itself settle this three-dimensional surface-area
normalization.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which three-dimensional body of constant width encloses the least
volume when its surface area is prescribed?
