\section{Köthe Conjecture}\label{1455}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1455 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $R$ be any associative ring; neither commutativity nor a multiplicative identity is assumed. A left ideal is an additive subgroup $J\le(R,+)$ with $RJ\subseteq J$; a right ideal has $JR\subseteq J$. A two-sided ideal satisfies both conditions. An element $x$ is nilpotent if $x^m=0$ for some integer $m\ge1$. An ideal is nil if every one of its elements is nilpotent; the exponent may depend on the element, so a nil ideal need not be a nilpotent ideal. \sref{1455}{2}
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every associative ring $R$, is the following implication true?
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &R\text{ has no nonzero nil two-sided ideal}\\
 &\qquad\Longrightarrow
 R\text{ has no nonzero nil left ideal and no nonzero nil right ideal}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The conclusion concerns both left and right ideals; a nil ideal has elementwise nilpotence, without a common bound on the exponents. \sref{1455}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a ring with no nonzero nil two-sided ideal also have no nonzero nil one-sided ideal? The question concerns arbitrary associative rings, including rings without an identity.
