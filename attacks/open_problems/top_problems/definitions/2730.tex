\section*{Kirby Problem 1.71}\label{2730}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2730 · KP-1.71 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a closed $C^{1,1}$ embedded curve $C\subset\mathbb R^3$, its thickness is
its reach,
\[
\tau(C)=\sup\{r>0:\text{the nearest-point projection }U_r(C)\to C
\text{ is single-valued}\}.
\]
Here $U_r(C)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,C)<r\}$. Fix a length $\ell\geq2\pi$. A physical unknot is an unknotted curve of length
$\ell$ and thickness at least one. A physical isotopy is a continuous path of
embedded curves of that same length, all satisfying the same thickness bound.
Allow reparametrization of the curves; the constraints concern their images.

Does there exist a physical unknot $C$ for which no physical isotopy reaches a
round circle of length $\ell$? Such an example is called a Gordian unknot.
More generally, two ordinary isotopic curves of the same fixed length that lie
in distinct components of this constrained configuration space form a Gordian
pair.

The round comparison circle has radius $\ell/(2\pi)\geq1$, so it is itself an
admissible configuration. The obstruction sought must come from the global
length-and-thickness constraint, even though an ambient isotopy without those
metric restrictions unknots the curve.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a thick unknotted loop be trapped away from a round circle when its length and minimum thickness must be preserved?
