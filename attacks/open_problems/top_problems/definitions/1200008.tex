\section*{Some Questions — Question 8}\label{1200008}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200008 · AMR-011-0008 · Graph Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple vertex-transitive graph, with maximum degree at most $d$. Its edge-expansion constant is the minimum of $|E(A,V\setminus A)|/|A|$ over nonempty sets $A$ of size at most $|V|/2$. For a permutation $\sigma$ of $V$, define its adjacency error and distance to the automorphism group by
\[
 e_G(\sigma)=\frac{|\sigma(E)\mathbin{\triangle}E|}{|V|},\qquad
 a_G(\sigma)=\min_{\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)}
            \frac{|\{v:\sigma(v)\ne\tau(v)\}|}{|V|}.
\]
Here $\sigma(E)$ is the set of unordered pairs obtained by applying $\sigma$ to both endpoints of each edge.

For fixed $d$ and $h>0$, is the following uniform stability statement true? For every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that, for every such graph with expansion at least $h$,
\[
 e_G(\sigma)<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_G(\sigma)<\varepsilon.
\]
Equivalently, along any bounded-degree vertex-transitive expander family, permutations making a vanishing fraction of edge errors are a vanishing normalized Hamming distance from genuine automorphisms.

The approximating automorphism may vary with the permutation and graph, but the error threshold cannot depend on graph order. Counting a few incorrect edges alone does not require a permutation to be close pointwise to the identity; it may approximate any automorphism. Expansion and vertex transitivity are both hypotheses of the intended question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must an almost adjacency-preserving permutation of a vertex-transitive expander be close to a genuine automorphism?
