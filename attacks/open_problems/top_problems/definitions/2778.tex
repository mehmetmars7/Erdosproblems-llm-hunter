\section*{Kirby Problem 2.30}\label{2778}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2778 · KP-2.30 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Given a finite simple graph $\Gamma=(V,E)$, form the right-angled Artin group
\[
A(\Gamma)=\langle v\ (v\in V)\mid vw=wv\text{ for }\{v,w\}\in E\rangle.
\]
For a closed orientable surface $S_g$ of genus $g\geq2$, its fundamental group
has presentation
\[
\pi_1(S_g)=\left\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\ \middle|
\prod_{i=1}^g[a_i,b_i]=1\right\rangle.
\]
Such groups are called closed hyperbolic surface groups.

Characterize the graphs $\Gamma$ for which there is some $g\geq2$ and an
injective homomorphism $\pi_1(S_g)\hookrightarrow A(\Gamma)$.
In addition, is there a terminating algorithm deciding this property from
the finite adjacency data of $\Gamma$, or a necessary-and-sufficient
graph-theoretic criterion?
The genus is existentially quantified and is not part of the input. Realizing
the surface relator by elements of $A(\Gamma)$ is not enough: the resulting
homomorphism must have trivial kernel. Allowing nonorientable closed hyperbolic
surfaces would not change the existence question, since their orientable double
covers give subgroups of the displayed orientable type.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which right-angled Artin groups contain a closed hyperbolic surface group, and can this be decided from the defining graph?
