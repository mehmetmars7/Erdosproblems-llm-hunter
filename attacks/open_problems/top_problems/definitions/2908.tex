\section*{Kirby Problem 4.32}\label{2908}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2908 · KP-4.32 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Sigma$ be a closed connected surface, with no orientability
assumption. A topological embedding $f:\Sigma\to S^4$ is locally
flat if near each point of its image the pair $(S^4,f(\Sigma))$ is
homeomorphic to $(\mathbb R^4,\mathbb R^2\times\{0\})$ near the
origin. This excludes wild local behavior but imposes no differentiability
on $f$.

Call the embedding smoothable by ambient isotopy if there is a
continuous path of homeomorphisms $H_t:S^4\to S^4$, beginning at
the identity, such that $H_1(f(\Sigma))$ is the image of a smooth
embedding of the surface into the standard smooth $S^4$.
A smooth structure on the abstract surface may be used when describing
this final smooth embedding.

Does there exist a locally flat embedding which is not smoothable
in this sense? In particular, can such an example have $\Sigma=S^2$?
The negative requirement rules out every topological ambient isotopy
to every smooth representative, not just the possibility of smoothing
the original parameterization in place.

The ambient smooth structure stays the standard one. Local flatness
is required everywhere; a wild embedding would not give the intended
example. The general question allows all closed surface types, whereas
the sphere case is a separate, stronger existence request. Obstructions
available for a surface in some other smooth $4$-manifold would need
to be realized in $S^4$ to answer this problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a locally flat closed surface in the standard four-sphere fail to be topologically ambient isotopic to every smooth surface, even when it is a sphere?
