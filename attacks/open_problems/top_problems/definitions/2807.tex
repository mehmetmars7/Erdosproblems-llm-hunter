\section*{Kirby Problem 3.9}\label{2807}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2807 · KP-3.9 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For compact connected orientable irreducible three-manifolds, the Haken
condition means the existence of a properly embedded two-sided incompressible
surface which is not a sphere or boundary-parallel. Irreducibility means that
every embedded sphere bounds a ball; incompressibility here requires
$\pi_1$-injectivity and, when the surface has boundary, the customary
boundary-incompressibility condition. In the closed case the witnessing
surface is closed of positive genus.

For $G=\pi_1(M)$ let $\widehat G=\varprojlim G/N$, with $N$ ranging over
finite-index normal subgroups. Ask whether the Haken condition is a
\emph{profinite invariant}: if two manifolds in the standard irreducible
three-manifold setting have isomorphic profinite completions and one is
Haken, must the other be Haken?

The core unresolved case identified by the source is particularly precise.
Let $M,N$ be closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifolds with
$H_1(M;\mathbb Q)=H_1(N;\mathbb Q)=0$. If
$\widehat{\pi_1(M)}\cong\widehat{\pi_1(N)}$ and $M$ contains a closed
embedded two-sided $\pi_1$-injective surface of positive genus, must $N$
contain one? The witnessing surfaces need not correspond under a given
completion isomorphism; their existence is the proposed invariant.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can finite quotients detect whether a three-manifold contains an incompressible surface, especially for closed hyperbolic rational homology spheres?
