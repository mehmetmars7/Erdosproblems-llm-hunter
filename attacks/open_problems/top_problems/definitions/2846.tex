\section{Kirby Problem 3.48}\label{2846}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2846 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb F_2=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. For a closed oriented $3$-manifold $Y$, $\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y;k)$ denotes hat Heegaard Floer homology with coefficients $k$: it is the homology of $\widehat{\operatorname{CF}}=\operatorname{CF}^-/U\operatorname{CF}^-$, where $\operatorname{CF}^-$ is the Heegaard Floer chain complex over $k[U]$. All spin$^c$ summands are included unless one is specified. A rational homology sphere has $H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(S^3;\mathbb Q)$. Its first integral homology is finite. An L-space satisfies $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y;\mathbb F_2)=|H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)|$. A manifold is prime if a connected-sum decomposition has an $S^3$ summand. Following the source, a left-orderable group must be nontrivial and have a strict total order $<$ with $a<b\Rightarrow ga<gb$ for all $g$. A coorientable taut foliation is a codimension-one foliation with a consistent transverse orientation such that each leaf meets some closed transverse curve. 
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every prime rational homology $3$-sphere $Y$, are the following three conditions equivalent?
\[
\pi_1(Y)\text{ is left-orderable};\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y;\mathbb F_2)>|H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)|;\qquad
Y\text{ has a coorientable taut foliation}.
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
For prime rational homology spheres, are left-orderability, failure to be an L-space, and existence of a coorientable taut foliation exactly equivalent?
