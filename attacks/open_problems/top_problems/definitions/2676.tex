\section*{Kirby Problem 1.17}\label{2676}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2676 · KP-1.17 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A link in $S^3$ is a finite disjoint union of smoothly embedded circles, considered
up to ambient isotopy. It is alternating if it has a regular projection with
crossing information in which overcrossings and undercrossings alternate as each
component is traversed. A nonalternating link is one admitting no alternating
diagram; possessing a single nonalternating diagram is not enough.

For a link $L$, let $\Sigma_2(L)$ be its double branched cover: away from $L$ the
cover corresponds to the homomorphism of the link group to $\mathbb Z/2$ sending
every meridian to the nonzero element, and along $L$ it is completed by the local
branched model $(z,t)\mapsto(z^2,t)$.

Do there exist an alternating link $A$ and a nonalternating link $N$ with
\[
\Sigma_2(A)\cong\Sigma_2(N)?
\]
The homeomorphism is between the underlying $3$-manifolds and need not intertwine
the two covering involutions. Equivalently, does the existence of an alternating
branch-set presentation force every branch-set presentation over $S^3$ of that
same manifold to be alternating? Allowing orientation reversal does not affect
the question, since mirroring preserves alternation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one closed $3$-manifold be the double branched cover of both an alternating and a nonalternating link?
