\section*{Kirby Problem 1.39}\label{2698}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2698 · KP-1.39 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An algebraic knot is the oriented link $f^{-1}(0)\cap S^3_\epsilon$ of an
irreducible complex plane curve singularity at $0\in\mathbb C^2$, for a sufficiently
small sphere transverse to the curve. Use the orientation induced by the complex
curve, and take distinct nontrivial oriented knot types as generators.
Two knots are smoothly concordant if they cobound a smoothly embedded annulus
in $S^3\times[0,1]$. Requiring instead a locally flat topological annulus defines
topological concordance. Under connected sum the resulting groups are
$\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{TOP}}$; the inverse is the mirror with
reversed orientation.

For each of these two groups, is the homomorphism
\[
\bigoplus_{K\text{ algebraic}}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathcal C
\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal C^{\mathrm{TOP}},\qquad e_K\longmapsto[K],
\]
injective? Explicitly, if distinct algebraic knots $K_1,\ldots,K_m$ and integers
$a_1,\ldots,a_m$ satisfy $\sum a_i[K_i]=0$, must all $a_i$ be zero?
The relation is finite, and negative coefficients mean taking concordance
inverses. The smooth and topological assertions are separate questions, since
the permitted annuli and slice disks differ.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are the distinct algebraic knots linearly independent in smooth concordance, and also in topological concordance?
