\section{Kirby Problem 5.13}\label{3020}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3020 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite-volume hyperbolic $n$-manifold is a connected complete Riemannian $n$-manifold of sectional curvature $-1$ with finite total volume; it may be closed or cusped. A finite cover here is a connected covering of finite degree. Its first Betti number is $b_1=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_1(-;\mathbb Q)$. In the source's usage, a cubulated group is the fundamental group of a compact nonpositively curved cube complex, equivalently it acts freely and cocompactly on that complex's CAT(0) universal cover. Fibering over $S^1$ means a locally trivial manifold bundle over the circle.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite-volume hyperbolic $n$-manifold $M$, resolve the following four assertions (the finite cover may differ between parts):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Some finite cover has $b_1>0$.
\item[(b)] Some finite cover has fundamental group admitting a surjection onto a nonabelian free group.
\item[(c)] Some finite cover has cubulated fundamental group.
\item[(d)] If $n$ is odd, some finite cover fibers over $S^1$.
\end{enumerate} \sref{3020}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds virtually have positive first Betti number, a free-group quotient, a cubulation, and, in odd dimension, a fibration over the circle?
