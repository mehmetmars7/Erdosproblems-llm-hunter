\section*{Fixed-polynomial semi-algebraic Ramsey bounds\\ and unbounded-degree universality}\label{20000938}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20000938 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0063 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $k\geq2$ and a polynomial $f\in\mathbb R[x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_k,y_k]$.
For an ordered sequence $P=(v_1,\ldots,v_N)$ of distinct points in $\mathbb R^2$, define a $k$-uniform hypergraph $H_f(P)$ on $[N]$ by
\[
 \{i_1,\ldots,i_k\}\in E(H_f(P))
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f(v_{i_1},\ldots,v_{i_k})\geq0,
 \qquad i_1<\cdots<i_k.
\]
The order specifies how a potentially nonsymmetric polynomial is evaluated; zero values are edges.
A vertex subset is homogeneous if all its $k$-element subsets are edges, or all are nonedges.
Write $\omega(H)$ and $\alpha(H)$ for the largest sizes of these two kinds of subsets.

Define the guaranteed homogeneous-set size
\[
 h_f(N)=\min_P\max\{\omega(H_f(P)),\alpha(H_f(P))\},
\]
where the minimum ranges over all ordered configurations of $N$ distinct planar points.
The question is to determine sharp lower bounds for $h_f(N)$, and their dependence on $k$ and the defining polynomial, together with configurations showing sharpness.
The polynomial is fixed as $N$ grows: letting its degree or coefficients be chosen anew for every configuration would change the problem.
The source's unrestricted Ramsey lower bound is only a starting point; the objective is to exploit the planar algebraic description.
A complete answer may distinguish polynomials whose induced hypergraphs have very different homogeneous-set growth.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How large a clique or independent set is forced when a planar point hypergraph is defined by one fixed polynomial sign condition?
