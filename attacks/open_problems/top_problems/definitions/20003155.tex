\section*{The maximal symmetry-rank conjecture and a sharp product defect formula}\label{20003155}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20003155 · AIM-TOPOLOGY-0243 · Topology · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(M^n,g)$ be a closed connected simply connected Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\geq2$ with nonnegative sectional curvature:
$\sec_g(\sigma)\geq0$ for every tangent two-plane $\sigma$.
Its isometry group $\operatorname{Isom}(M,g)$ is a compact Lie group.
Define its symmetry rank to be the dimension of a maximal torus in the identity component of this group.
Equivalently, it is the largest integer $r$ for which the torus $T^r=(S^1)^r$ acts effectively and isometrically on $(M,g)$.
Effective means that no nonidentity group element fixes every point of the manifold.

The proposed bound asks whether every such manifold satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Isom}(M,g)
          \leq\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor.
\]
The integer floor is simply the integral form of the source inequality $r\leq2n/3$.
The metric is arbitrary within the nonnegative-curvature class, and the bound is to depend only on dimension.
No assumption that the torus action is free, has a fixed point, or has a specified orbit type is imposed.
The problem concerns the maximal dimension of a torus subgroup, not the dimension of the entire isometry group, which is a different invariant.
Simple connectivity is a hypothesis on the underlying manifold and cannot be dropped merely because some orbits are connected.
A counterexample would consist of a single such metric with an effective isometric torus action exceeding the displayed rank.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the symmetry rank of a simply connected, nonnegatively curved closed manifold at most two thirds of its dimension?
