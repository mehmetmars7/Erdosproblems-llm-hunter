\section*{Diameter of Mandelbrot limbs}\label{5300040}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5300040 · AMR-052-0040 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $P_c(z)=z^2+c$, let
$M=\{c\in\mathbb C:(P_c^n(0))_{n\ge0}\text{ is bounded}\}$
be the Mandelbrot set. Its principal cardioid parameterizes attracting
fixed points and has boundary
$c(\mu)=\mu/2-\mu^2/4$ for $|\mu|=1$.
For relatively prime integers $0<p<q$, put
\[
 c_{p/q}=\tfrac12e^{2\pi ip/q}-\tfrac14e^{4\pi ip/q}.
\]
The closed $p/q$-limb $M(p/q)$ is the part of $M$ attached to the
principal cardioid at $c_{p/q}$ on the side away from that cardioid,
including its attachment point. Equivalently it is the intersection
of $M$ with the closed parameter wake bounded by the two characteristic
external parameter rays landing at $c_{p/q}$.

Does there exist an absolute constant $K>0$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}M(p/q)\le K/q^2
 \quad\text{for every reduced }0<p/q<1?
\]
If this stronger estimate fails, does the weaker uniform estimate
$\operatorname{diam}M(p/q)\le K\log(q)/q^2$ hold?
Diameter is Euclidean distance in the parameter plane, not angular
width or hyperbolic diameter. The constant must be independent of
both numerator and denominator. The limb contains all decorations
in its wake, not merely the period-$q$ hyperbolic component attached
to the cardioid. Bounds for the visible bulb alone therefore do not
give the stated estimate for the entire limb. The denominator is taken
after reducing the rational angle, so artificial repetitions of a fraction
cannot improve the proposed bound.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do all principal Mandelbrot limbs have Euclidean diameter $O(q^{-2})$, or at least $O(q^{-2}\log q)$?
