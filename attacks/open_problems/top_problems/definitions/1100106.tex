\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.6}\label{1100106}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100106 · AMR-010-0106 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of a group $G$, its Cayley graph has vertices $G$ and unit-length edges from $g$ to $gs$ for $s\in S$. The associated path metric is the word metric. A finitely generated group is word-hyperbolic if some $\delta\geq0$ makes every geodesic triangle in this graph $\delta$-thin: each side lies in the $\delta$-neighborhood of the other two. This property is independent of the finite generating set.

The group is one-ended if its Cayley graph is infinite and deleting any finite set of vertices leaves exactly one infinite connected component. Let $\Sigma_g$ denote the closed connected orientable surface of genus $g$. Its fundamental group has presentation
\[
 \pi_1(\Sigma_g)=\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\mid
                  [a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g]=1\rangle,
 \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
\]
Does every one-ended word-hyperbolic group $G$ admit an injective homomorphism $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)\to G$ for some integer $g\geq2$?

The genus may depend on $G$, and no bound on it is requested. The surface subgroup is not required to have finite index or to be quasiconvex. Allowing nonorientable closed hyperbolic surfaces gives the same existence question, since their orientation double covers supply orientable surface subgroups. The assumption of one end excludes free groups, whose subgroups are free and therefore cannot be closed hyperbolic surface groups. This is a subgroup-existence question, rather than the construction of a quotient onto a surface group.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every one-ended hyperbolic group contain the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus at least two?
