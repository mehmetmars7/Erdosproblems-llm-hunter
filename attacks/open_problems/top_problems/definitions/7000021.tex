\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 5.2}\label{7000021}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000021 · AMR-069-0021 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed rectifiable space curve is a continuous map
$\gamma:S^1\to\mathbb R^3$ of finite positive length. Length
counts the traversal with multiplicity, so retracing an arc
consumes length again. Let $K_\gamma$ be the compact convex hull
of its image and let $V(K_\gamma)$ be its ordinary
three-dimensional volume.

For $L>0$, determine
\[
 V_*(L)=\sup\{V(K_\gamma):\gamma\text{ is closed and rectifiable},
                              \ \operatorname{length}(\gamma)=L\}.
\]
Find the sharp value and characterize maximizing curves, or
maximizing limiting configurations if the supremum is not
attained in a more restrictive smooth class.

Scaling a curve by a factor $t$ multiplies length by $t$ and
volume by $t^3$, so the main numerical target is the dimensionless
constant $V_*(1)$, with $V_*(L)=L^3V_*(1)$. The curve may be
nonplanar and need not lie on the boundary of its convex hull.

The closure condition is part of the admissible class. Results
for open arcs, centrally symmetric curves, or curves with a
specified knot type do not by themselves determine this general
supremum. The volume being optimized belongs to the convex hull,
not to a choice of spanning surface.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How much volume can the convex hull of a closed space curve enclose
when the curve has a fixed total length? Find the sharp constant
and extremal shapes.
