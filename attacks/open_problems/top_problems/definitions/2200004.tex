\section*{Problems Around Polynomials — Problem 1}\label{2200004}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2200004 · AMR-021-0004 · Algebra · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $k\geq1$. Consider the constant-coefficient differential equation
\[
 y^{(k)}+a_1y^{(k-1)}+\cdots+a_ky=0,\qquad a_j\in\mathbb C,
\]
and its characteristic polynomial
$\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^k+a_1\lambda^{k-1}+\cdots+a_k$.
Let $\Omega_k$ consist of those equations for which any two distinct
characteristic roots have different real parts. Repeated roots are permitted:
the restriction concerns distinct values, rather than their multiplicities.

Every solution extends to an entire function and can be written
$y(z)=\sum_{\lambda}P_\lambda(z)e^{\lambda z}$, with
$\deg P_\lambda$ smaller than the multiplicity of $\lambda$ in $\chi$.
A real zero means a real number $x$ at which this complex-valued function
vanishes, so that both its real and imaginary parts vanish. Exclude the
identically zero solution.

Does there exist a finite integer $B(k)$, depending only on the order $k$,
such that every nonzero solution of every equation in $\Omega_k$ has at
most $B(k)$ real zeros? One may count these zeros with their analytic
multiplicities. Existence of a uniform bound is equivalent for distinct
zeros and for multiplicity counting, since a nonzero solution of an
order-$k$ equation cannot have a zero of multiplicity $k$.
The bound must be independent of all coefficients, imaginary parts of
the characteristic roots, separations between their real parts, and
initial values. In particular, a bound for each individual equation
does not settle the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the order alone bound the real zeros of every solution when distinct characteristic roots have distinct real parts?
