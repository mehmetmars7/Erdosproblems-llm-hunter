\section{Scalar Curvature Question [?34]: Bounds on Width and on the Macroscopic Dimension}\label{6700033}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 6700033 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.


\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every dimension $n$, consider complete Riemannian $n$-manifolds $X$ with scalar curvature bounded below by $\sigma>0$. Must the macroscopic dimension satisfy $\dim_{\mathrm{mc}}X\leq n-2$? Is there a dimension-dependent constant bounding the stated $(n-2)$-dimensional width by $C_n\sigma^{-1/2}$? The question also asks for the weaker $(n-1)$-dimensional width bound at this same scale. Width measures the size of fibers of a map to a lower-dimensional polyhedron.
