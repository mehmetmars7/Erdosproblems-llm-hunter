\section*{Sum of prime and semiprime conjecture}\label{3359}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3359 · OPG-37404 · Number Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A semiprime is a positive integer with exactly two prime factors
counted with multiplicity. Thus it has the form $qr$ with primes
$q,r$, and $q=r$ is allowed. An odd semiprime has both prime factors
odd. The assertion asks whether
\[
 \forall N\in2\mathbb Z,\ N>10,\quad
 \exists\text{ odd primes }p,q,r\quad N=p+qr.
\]
No distinctness among $p,q,r$ is imposed. For example, the first
admissible integer has the representation $12=3+3\cdot3$.
The strict lower cutoff excludes $10$, which cannot be expressed
using an odd prime at least three and an odd semiprime at least nine.

Every even integer above this cutoff is covered, rather than only
all sufficiently large integers or almost all even integers. Replacing
the semiprime by a number with at most two prime factors changes the
problem, since that would additionally permit a prime in its place.
The displayed formula requires exactly two factors counted with
multiplicity. A negative answer consists of an even integer $N>10$
for which every odd prime $p<N$ leaves a difference $N-p$ that is
not an odd semiprime; a positive answer must cover the entire range.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every even integer greater than ten the sum of an odd prime and a product of two odd primes, allowing repeated factors?
