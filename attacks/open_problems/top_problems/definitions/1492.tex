\section{Ramsey Number R(5,5)}\label{1492}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1492 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write \(K_N\) for the complete simple graph on \(N\) vertices.
A red/blue edge coloring assigns one of these two colors to each
unordered pair of distinct vertices. A monochromatic \(K_5\)
is a set of five vertices whose ten mutual edges have the same color.
Define
\[
 R(5,5)=\min\{N\in\mathbb N:\text{every red/blue coloring of }K_N
                          \text{ has a monochromatic }K_5\}.
\]
Equivalently every simple graph on \(R(5,5)\) vertices has a
five-vertex clique or a five-vertex independent set.
The cited result gives \(43\le R(5,5)\le46\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the exact integer \(R(5,5)\); equivalently, decide
which of \(43,44,45,46\) satisfies the defining minimum. \sref{1492}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the smallest number of vertices that forces a monochromatic complete graph on five vertices in every red/blue edge coloring?
