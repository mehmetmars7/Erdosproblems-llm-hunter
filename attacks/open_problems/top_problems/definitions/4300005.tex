\section*{Entropy values and Lehmer's problem}\label{4300005}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4300005 · AMR-042-0005 · Number Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a monic polynomial $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of positive degree $d$, write
$f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^d(x-\alpha_j)$, listing complex roots with multiplicity.
Its logarithmic Mahler measure is
\[
 m(f)=\sum_{j=1}^d\log\max\{1,|\alpha_j|\},
 \qquad M(f)=\exp m(f).
\]
All logarithms here are natural. Roots on the unit circle, and roots equal to
zero, contribute zero. The degree is allowed to vary with the polynomial.
The problem asks whether
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\quad\exists\text{ monic }f\in\mathbb Z[x]
 \quad 0<m(f)<\varepsilon.
\]
Equivalently, does the set of positive logarithmic Mahler measures of monic
integer polynomials have infimum zero? A negative answer would give a single
constant $c>0$, independent of both degree and coefficients, such that every
such polynomial has either $m(f)=0$ or $m(f)\ge c$.

The strict inequality at zero is essential: powers of $x$ and products of
cyclotomic polynomials have zero measure and do not answer the question.
Factorization into monic irreducible factors does not change the existence
question, since Mahler measure is additive under multiplication. Thus one may
equivalently seek algebraic integers of arbitrarily small positive logarithmic
Mahler measure, counting all conjugates. Ward's question is the small-positive-value
form of Lehmer's problem; the usual gap conjecture predicts a negative answer.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can positive Mahler measures of monic integer polynomials approach zero, with no bound on their degrees?
