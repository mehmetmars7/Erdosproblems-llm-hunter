\section*{Stationary distributions in higher dimensions}\label{9600002}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 9600002 · AMR-095-0002 · Probability · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider exclusion on $\mathbb Z^2$: a particle at $x$ attempts jumps to $x+e_i$ at rate $p_i$ and to $x-e_i$ at rate $q_i$, for $i=1,2$, and a jump is suppressed when its destination is occupied. Assume $p_i>q_i>0$ and $p_1+q_1+p_2+q_2=1$. Write $m=(p_1-q_1,p_2-q_2)$ for the mean jump vector.

Fix a nonzero integer vector $v\in\mathbb Z^2$ with $v\cdot m>0$. Does there exist a probability measure $\mu$ on $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z^2}$ which is stationary for this process, is invariant under every lattice translation $z$ satisfying $z\cdot v=0$, and obeys
\[
\lim_{n\to-\infty}\mu(\eta(nv)=1)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(\eta(nv)=1)=1?
\]
Here stationarity means that the law remains $\mu$ at every nonnegative time when the initial configuration has law $\mu$. Translation invariance is only required along the interface, in directions perpendicular to $v$; no independence between occupation variables is assumed.

The integer-vector convention spells out the rational-direction formulation explicitly mentioned in the source: one clears denominators before evaluating the configuration at $nv$. The strict scalar-product inequality is equivalent to the angle between the proposed density-gradient direction and the drift being less than $\pi/2$. The desired conclusion is a family of stationary measures for every admissible direction and every irreducible nearest-neighbor kernel, not merely for a direction parallel to a coordinate axis or a direction permitting an explicit product measure.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a stationary two-dimensional exclusion interface point in every rational direction having positive projection onto the particle drift?
