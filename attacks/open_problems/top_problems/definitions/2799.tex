\section*{Kirby Problem 3.1}\label{2799}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2799 · KP-3.1 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A hyperbolic three-manifold here is a connected complete Riemannian
three-manifold of constant sectional curvature $-1$ and finite volume.
Its cusps are ends isometric outside compact sets to quotients of horoballs.
A cusp cross-section is a closed flat surface: a torus for an orientable
cusp and a Klein bottle for a nonorientable cusp. An orientable manifold
has only torus cusps, but a nonorientable manifold can also have torus cusps.

For each of the following classes $\mathcal A$, determine
$v(\mathcal A)=\inf\{\operatorname{vol}(M):M\in\mathcal A\}$ and identify
all manifolds attaining it, up to isometry:
\begin{enumerate}
\item closed nonorientable hyperbolic three-manifolds;
\item manifolds with exactly $n$ cusps, without an orientability restriction,
for each $n\geq3$;
\item manifolds with exactly $n$ orientable cusps, allowing nonorientable
ambient manifolds and any additional nonorientable cusps;
\item orientable manifolds with exactly $n$ cusps, for every $n\geq1$.
\end{enumerate}
For (c), this reads the source's ``$n$ orientable cusps'' as counting torus
cusps only. The alternative reading requires exactly $n$ cusps in total,
all tori; its minimum should be distinguished if additional cusps are excluded.
Neither reading requires ambient orientability. In each family the curvature normalization is fixed, since otherwise
rescaling the metric would make the volume comparison meaningless.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the least hyperbolic volume and every minimizing manifold in the specified closed, cusped, and orientability classes.
