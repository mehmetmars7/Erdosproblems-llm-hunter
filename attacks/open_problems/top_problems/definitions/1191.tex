\section{The Inverse Galois Problem}\label{1191}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1191 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite Galois extension \(L/\mathbb Q\) is a finite-degree field
extension that is normal and separable.  Its Galois group
\(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) consists of the field automorphisms
of \(L\) fixing every rational number, with composition as the group
operation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite group \(G\), does there exist a finite Galois
extension \(L/\mathbb Q\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\cong G?
\] \sref{1191}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every finite group occur as the Galois group of a finite extension of the rational numbers?
