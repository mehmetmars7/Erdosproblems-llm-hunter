\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.40}\label{2549}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2549 · KOU-21.40 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer $n$, let $\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Q)$ be the
group of invertible $n$ by $n$ matrices with rational entries, under
matrix multiplication. Let $G$ be any subgroup, not assumed finitely
generated. Its abstract automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ acts
on its elements by $\alpha\cdot g=\alpha(g)$.

Suppose this action has finitely many orbits. In explicit terms, some
finite list $g_1,\ldots,g_t$ has the property that for every $g\in G$
there are $i$ and $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with $g=\alpha(g_i)$.
The automorphisms in this condition need not be induced by conjugation
inside $\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Q)$, and the number $t$ is not
prescribed uniformly.

A group $L$ is soluble if its derived series $L^{(0)}=L$,
$L^{(j+1)}=[L^{(j)},L^{(j)}]$ reaches $1$ after finitely many steps.
A group is virtually soluble if it contains a soluble subgroup of
finite index. The question asks whether every $G$ as above is virtually
soluble:
\[
 |G/\operatorname{Aut}(G)|<\infty
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists L\le G:\ [G:L]<\infty,\ L\text{ soluble},
\]
where the quotient notation on the left denotes the set of automorphism
orbits, not a quotient group. The possible dimension, index and derived
length may vary with $G$. The conclusion concerns one soluble subgroup
of finite index in the whole group, not just solubility of selected
finitely generated subgroups.

The editors mark this entry with an unverified AI solution, which their
preface expressly distinguishes from a confirmed solution. The mathematical
question is therefore retained without endorsing that proposed argument.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every rational linear group with finitely many orbits of elements under its abstract automorphisms virtually soluble?
