\section{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.88}\label{2597}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2597 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite group $G$, conjugacy is the relation $x\sim y$ if $y=g^{-1}xg$ for some $g\in G$. Let $k(G)$ be the number of its conjugacy classes, including the identity class. Odd order means that $|G|$ is odd; nonabelian means that some pair of elements fails to commute.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a finite nonabelian group $G$ of odd order satisfying
\[
 \frac{k(G)}{|G|}=\frac1{17}?
\]
The ratio is the exact rational number $1/17$, rather than an asymptotic density or approximation. \sref{2597}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a finite nonabelian odd-order group whose conjugacy-class count is exactly one seventeenth of its order?
