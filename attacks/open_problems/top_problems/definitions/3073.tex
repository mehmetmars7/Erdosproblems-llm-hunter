\section{A conjecture on iterated circumcentres}\label{3073}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3073 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $d\geq1$ be an integer, and let $(p_i)_{i\geq1}$ be points in $\mathbb R^d$. A sphere is a set $\{x:\|x-c\|_2=R\}$ with centre $c\in\mathbb R^d$ and radius $R>0$. Suppose that for each $i\geq d+2$ the preceding $d+1$ points $p_{i-1},\ldots,p_{i-d-1}$ are distinct and lie on a unique sphere, with centre $p_i$. The least period of a periodic sequence is the least $T\geq1$ such that $p_{i+T}=p_i$ for every $i\geq1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If the sequence is periodic, is its least period necessarily $2d+4$? \sref{3073}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If repeatedly taking the circumcentre of the previous d+1 points eventually returns to the original sequence, must its least period be 2d+4?
