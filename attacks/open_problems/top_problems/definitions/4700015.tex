\section*{Reversible quadratic systems}\label{4700015}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4700015 · AMR-046-0015 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For real parameters $D,F$, consider the reversible quadratic system
\[
 \dot x=-y+xy,\qquad \dot y=x+Dx^2+Fy^2.
\]
The equilibrium at the origin is a center. Let $\mathcal A_{D,F}$ be its
period annulus, the maximal surrounding family of nonconstant periodic
orbits. Choose a smooth transversal meeting each such orbit once and use
a regular coordinate $s$ that increases outward. Write $T_{D,F}(s)$ for
the least positive time needed to traverse that orbit.

A critical period is an isolated zero of $T'_{D,F}(s)$ at an interior
point of the annulus. The problem asks whether
\[
 \sup_{D,F\in\mathbb R}
  \#\{\text{critical periods in }\mathcal A_{D,F}\}=2.
\]
Thus one seeks a universal upper bound of two and parameter choices with
two distinct stationary periods. Changes of regular transverse coordinate
preserve the count. Changes in the speed along trajectories generally do
not preserve the period function and are not allowed.

The claim concerns the entire annulus, including stationary periods far
from the center. The center itself and any boundary polycycle are not
interior critical periods. Parameters for which the period function is
constant are treated as isochronous cases; their continuum of stationary
orbits is not counted as a collection of isolated critical periods. This
is the global critical-period question for the normalized Loud family,
rather than solely the local bifurcation count near an isochronous center.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is two the sharp global maximum number of isolated critical periods in the reversible quadratic Loud family?
