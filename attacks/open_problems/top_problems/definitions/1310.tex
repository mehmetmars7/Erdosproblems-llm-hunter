\section{Herzog–Schönheim Conjecture}\label{1310}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1310 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be any group. A nontrivial finite left-coset partition is a family $\{g_iH_i:1\leq i\leq k\}$ with $k\geq2$, $g_i\in G$, and subgroups $H_i\leq G$ of finite index, such that the cosets are pairwise disjoint and their union is $G$. The index $[G:H_i]$ is the number of left cosets of $H_i$ in $G$. The subgroups themselves need not be distinct.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
In every such partition, must there exist distinct indices $i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ with
\[
[G:H_i]=[G:H_j]?
\]
Equivalently, can a nontrivial finite coset partition ever have all its subgroup indices pairwise distinct? \sref{1310}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Must two subgroup indices repeat whenever finitely many nontrivial left cosets partition a group?
