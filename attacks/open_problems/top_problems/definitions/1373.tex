\section{Carathéodory Conjecture}\label{1373}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1373 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A closed $C^2$ convex surface $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ is the compact embedded boundary, without boundary of its own, of a convex body with nonempty interior. Choose a unit normal field $\nu$; its shape operator is $S_p=-d\nu_p:T_p\Sigma\to T_p\Sigma$. Its two real eigenvalues are the principal curvatures. A point $p$ is umbilical if $S_p$ is a scalar multiple of the identity, equivalently the principal curvatures agree. This condition is independent of the normal orientation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every closed $C^2$ convex surface $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ have at least two distinct umbilical points?
\[
 \#\{p\in\Sigma:\ S_p=\kappa\,\operatorname{id}_{T_p\Sigma}\text{ for some }\kappa\in\mathbb R\}\ge2.
\]
The question includes surfaces with nonisolated umbilics. \sref{1373}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the two principal curvatures agree at two or more points on every twice continuously differentiable closed convex surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space?
