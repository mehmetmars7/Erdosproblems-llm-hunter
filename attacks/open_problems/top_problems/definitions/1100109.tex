\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.9}\label{1100109}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100109 · AMR-010-0109 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X_G$ be a connected finite two-dimensional CW complex with fundamental group $G$, equipped with a piecewise Euclidean length metric after a finite subdivision. Let $H\leq G$ be finitely presented, and let $X_H\to X_G$ be the connected covering corresponding to $H$, with the lifted metric. Write $\widetilde X$ for the universal cover. For $x\in X_H$, choose a lift $\widetilde x$ and define
\[
 I(x)=\frac12\inf\{d(\widetilde x,h\widetilde x):h\in H\setminus\{1\}\},
\]
with infimum $+\infty$ if $H$ is trivial. This is the covering injectivity radius, equivalently half the infimal length of an essential loop based at $x$.

Assume $I(x)$ tends to infinity as $x$ leaves every compact subset of $X_H$: for each $R$ there is a compact $K_R$ outside which $I(x)>R$. Must the subgroup inclusion be a quasi-isometric embedding? Explicitly, for word metrics from finite generating sets, must constants $A\geq1$, $B\geq0$ exist such that
\[
 A^{-1}|h|_H-B\leq |h|_G\leq A|h|_H+B\qquad(h\in H)?
\]
The upper inequality follows from finite generation; the lower inequality is the substantive conclusion. The original question concerns finite presentations generally, with the word-hyperbolic ambient case singled out as particularly significant. Divergence is required uniformly outside compact sets, not just along a chosen ray.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the disappearance of short essential loops far out in a subgroup cover force the subgroup to be undistorted?
