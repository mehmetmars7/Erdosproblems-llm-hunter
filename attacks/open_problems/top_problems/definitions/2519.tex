\section{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.10}\label{2519}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2519 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite set $X$, let $F(X)$ be the free group on $X$. A presentation $\langle X\mid R\rangle$ denotes $F(X)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$, where $R$ is a finite set of relator words and double angle brackets denote normal closure. In the source's terminology a presentation is finite when its presented group is finite, and it is just finite if deleting any one specified relator produces an infinite group.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every finite group $G$ admit a presentation $\langle X\mid R\rangle\cong G$ such that
\[
 \bigl|F(X)/\langle\!\langle R\setminus\{r\}\rangle\!\rangle\bigr|=\infty
 \qquad\text{for every }r\in R?
\]
The words in the other relators and the generating set are kept fixed when a relator is removed. \sref{2519}{2} \sref{2519}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every finite group be presented so that removal of any single relation makes the resulting group infinite?
