\section*{Kirby Problem 4.88}\label{2964}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2964 · KP-4.88 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\tau:S^4\to S^4$ be a smooth free involution: $\tau^2=\operatorname{id}$ and $\tau(x)\ne x$ for every point. Its quotient $Q=S^4/\langle\tau\rangle$ is a smooth four-manifold covered by $S^4$ with deck group $\mathbb Z/2$. The involution reverses orientation, and $Q$ has the homotopy type of $\mathbb{RP}^4$.

Must there be a smoothly embedded sphere $S\cong S^2$ with $\tau(S)=S$? The restriction is a free involution of the sphere, so its quotient is an embedded $\mathbb{RP}^2$ in $Q$. Equivalently, ask for an embedded projective plane $P\subset Q$ representing the nonzero class in $H_2(Q;\mathbb Z/2)$. This condition ensures the essential projective-plane lift, rather than a null-homologous local embedding.

The analogous topological question uses a free topological involution and a locally flat embedded projective plane with a normal disk bundle. It must be considered separately from the smooth question. In this category the quotient need not be identified with the standard projective four-space; the statement is naturally made directly in $Q$.

The invariant sphere need not be a standard equatorial sphere or unknotted, and no conjugacy of the entire involution to a linear action is requested. Conversely, merely finding some embedded projective plane in the quotient without checking its mod-two class does not establish the desired equivalent formulation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every free involution of the four-sphere preserve an embedded two-sphere?
