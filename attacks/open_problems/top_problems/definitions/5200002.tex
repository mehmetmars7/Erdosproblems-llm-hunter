\section{Open Problems on Billiards and Geometric Optics}\label{5200002}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5200002 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


An outer billiard point is outside the domain enclosed by $\gamma$. Choose one of the two tangent branches consistently with the orientation of $\gamma$; reflect the point in its point of tangency. This defines $T$ near $\gamma$. An invariant curve is a curve preserved by $T$; integrability here concerns a foliation by such curves. A homothetic ellipse is a scaled copy with the same center.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\gamma$ be a smooth closed strictly convex plane curve. The outer billiard map $T$ sends a point $A$ near $\gamma$ to the point $T(A)$ for which $[A,T(A)]$ is tangent to $\gamma$ at its midpoint. Outer billiards about ellipses are integrable, with phase space foliated by homothetic invariant ellipses. Are there other integrable outer billiards? \sref{5200002}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether a smooth strictly convex outer billiard other than an ellipse can be integrable.
