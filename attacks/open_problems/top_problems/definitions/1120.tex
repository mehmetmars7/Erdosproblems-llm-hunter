\section{Are There Infinitely Many Mersenne Primes?}\label{1120}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1120 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A prime is a positive integer greater than one whose only positive
divisors are one and itself.  A Mersenne number is \(M_p=2^p-1\).
When \(M_p\) is prime, its exponent \(p\) must be prime; the converse
does not hold.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the set
\[
 \{\,p\in\mathbb N:p\text{ is prime and }2^p-1\text{ is prime}\,\}
\]
infinite?  Equivalently, for every \(B\in\mathbb N\), is there a prime
\(p>B\) for which \(2^p-1\) is also prime? \sref{1120}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many Mersenne primes?
