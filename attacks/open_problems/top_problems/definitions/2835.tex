\section*{Kirby Problem 3.37}\label{2835}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2835 · KP-3.37 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A three-manifold group is a group isomorphic to $\pi_1(M)$ for a connected
three-manifold $M$. Consider those which are finitely generated. A group
$G$ is linear over a characteristic-zero field if there are a field $k$
with $\operatorname{char}(k)=0$, an integer $d\geq1$, and an injective
homomorphism
\[
 \rho:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_d(k).
\]
Faithfulness means that the only element represented by the identity matrix
is the identity of $G$; an arbitrary nontrivial matrix representation is
not sufficient.

(a) Is every finitely generated three-manifold group linear over some
characteristic-zero field? Both the field and dimension may depend on $G$.

(b) If so, what bounds can be given for the minimum possible dimension
$d_0(G)$? One can seek a universal bound for all such groups, or an effective
bound in terms of specified finite manifold data, and these versions must
be distinguished. A bound obtained for a single geometric piece is not by
itself a bound for the full manifold group after gluing its pieces.

The characteristic-zero qualification is part of the problem. Representations
over fields of positive characteristic alone do not establish (a), and the
injectivity requirement concerns all elements, not just a chosen generating set.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all finitely generated three-manifold groups faithful matrix groups in characteristic zero, and how large a matrix dimension is necessary?
