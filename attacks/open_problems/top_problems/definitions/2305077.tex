\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.77}\label{2305077}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305077 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. The disc algebra $A(\overline{\mathbb D})$ consists of the functions continuous on $\overline{\mathbb D}$ and analytic in $\mathbb D$. A univalent function means an injective analytic function. Let $m:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb C$ be an arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable function.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every such $m$ occur as \[\lim_{r\uparrow1}f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})=m(\theta)\quad\text{for almost every }\theta\] for some $f\in A(\overline{\mathbb D})$ that is univalent on $\mathbb D$? \sref{2305077}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every measurable complex boundary function be realized almost everywhere as the radial limit of the derivative of a univalent disc-algebra function?
