\section{Large Least Preimages of Totient Values}\label{1903}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1903 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



A totient value is an integer in the image of $\phi:\N\to\N$. For such $a$, $n_a=\min\{n\geq1:\phi(n)=a\}$. The requested divergence is along an infinite sequence of distinct attainable values tending to infinity, not over all integers.

Euler\textquotesingle s totient is $\phi(n)=\#\{1\leq a\leq n:\gcd(a,n)=1\}$. Distinct totient values are counted once unless the question explicitly asks for their preimage multiplicity. \sref{1903}{1} \sref{1903}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there an infinite set $A\subset \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $a\in A$ there is an integer $n$ such that $\phi(n)=a$, and yet if $n_a$ is the smallest such integer then $n_a/a\to \infty$ as $a\to\infty$? \sref{1903}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can attainable totient values have their smallest preimages exceed those values by a ratio tending to infinity?
