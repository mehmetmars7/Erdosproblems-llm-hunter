{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306050 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\Sigma$ be the source's class of holomorphic injective exterior functions $f(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^{-n}$ on $|z|>1$.For $0\le\alpha\le1$, define the Laurent germ near $\infty$ by
\[F(z)=z\exp\left((1-\alpha)\log(f(z)/z)+\alpha\log(g(z)/z)\right)=z+\sum_{n\ge0}A_nz^{-n},\]
with the logarithms tending to $0$ at $\infty$. This fixes all coefficients even if fractional powers cannot be continued across exterior zeros.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $f,g\in\Sigma$ and $0\le\alpha\le1$, is $\sum_{n\ge1}n|A_n|^2\le1$? \sref{2306050}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the coefficient area inequality survive a weighted geometric product of two exterior univalent maps?
