\section*{Kirby Problem 3.32}\label{2830}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2830 · KP-3.32 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Give a tame closed embedded surface in $\mathbb R^3$ by a finite triangulated
polyhedral surface whose vertices have rational coordinates, with faces
meeting only along their common subfaces. Smooth surfaces may instead be
represented by equivalent finite triangulation data. Wild embeddings and
arbitrary unencoded continuous maps are not inputs to the algorithmic problem.

Two such surfaces $S_0,S_1$ are ambiently isotopic if there is a continuous
family of homeomorphisms $H_t:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$, $0\leq t\leq1$,
with $H_0=\operatorname{id}$ and $H_1(S_0)=S_1$. Work in the corresponding
tame PL or smooth category. The surfaces are compared as embedded subsets,
without a prescribed parametrization of their points.

Is there an algorithm that takes two finite descriptions and decides whether
the surfaces are ambiently isotopic, halting in every case? The source's
principal case is that both surfaces are connected, with arbitrary genus.
Disconnected inputs additionally involve their relative placement.

Matching abstract surface genus is insufficient, since the embedding can
be knotted. Even homeomorphism information for complementary regions must
be made compatible along their boundary surfaces to yield the required
ambient isotopy. The requested conclusion is decidability, without a
polynomial-time requirement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is ambient isotopy of closed tame surfaces in three-dimensional Euclidean space algorithmically decidable from finite descriptions?
