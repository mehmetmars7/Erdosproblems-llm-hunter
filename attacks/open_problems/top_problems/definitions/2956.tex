\section*{Kirby Problem 4.80}\label{2956}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2956 · KP-4.80 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a closed simply connected smooth $4$-manifold.
The groups $\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(X)$ and
$\pi_0\operatorname{Homeo}(X)$ consist respectively of smooth
and topological isotopy classes of self-maps, with composition as
the group operation. Use the $C^\infty$ and compact-open
topologies to define their path components. Set
\[
 K_X=\ker\bigl(\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(X)
           \longrightarrow\pi_0\operatorname{Homeo}(X)\bigr).
\]
Its elements are smooth mapping classes represented by diffeomorphisms
topologically isotopic to the identity.

Do there exist an integer $n>2$, such a manifold $X$, and an
element of order exactly $n$ in $K_X$? In representative terms,
there should be a diffeomorphism $f$ topologically isotopic to the
identity for which $f^n$ is smoothly isotopic to the identity,
while $f^j$ is not for $1\le j<n$.
More generally, does $K_X$ contain a finite subgroup of order
$n>2$ for some $X$? That subgroup need not be cyclic.

Finite order here belongs to the mapping class, not necessarily
to the diffeomorphism as an actual map. Thus the question does
not require $f^n=\mathrm{id}$ pointwise or realization of the
whole finite subgroup by an action. The existence of infinite-order
elements alone would not answer either finite-order request.
The order requested is exact, rather than merely a divisor of a
chosen integer; the two formulations distinguish cyclic torsion
from more general finite subgroups.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the kernel from smooth to topological mapping classes of a simply connected closed four-manifold contain torsion of order greater than two, or a finite subgroup of such order?
