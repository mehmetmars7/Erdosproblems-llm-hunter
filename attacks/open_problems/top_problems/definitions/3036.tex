\section{Something like Picard for 1-forms}\label{3036}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3036 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $D^*=D\setminus\{0\}$. Let $U_1,\ldots,U_n$ be open subsets of $D^*$ covering $D^*$, where $n\geq1$ is finite; connectedness is not assumed. Suppose each $f_j:U_j\to\mathbb C$ is holomorphic and injective, and $f'_j=f'_k$ on every overlap. These derivatives define a holomorphic function $g$ on $D^*$ and a one-form $\omega=g(z)\,dz$. A meromorphic extension at zero means that $g$ has at most a pole there: some $z^N g(z)$ extends holomorphically to $D$, for an integer $N\geq0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must $\omega$ extend meromorphically to all of $D$ under these hypotheses? \sref{3036}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do finitely many injective holomorphic local primitives with agreeing derivatives force the glued differential to have at most a pole at the missing origin?
