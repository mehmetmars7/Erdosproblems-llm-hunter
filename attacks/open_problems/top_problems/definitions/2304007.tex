\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.7}\label{2304007}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2304007 · AMR-022-4007 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $n\geq1$ and let
$P(z)=z^n+a_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n$ be a monic complex polynomial.
Its unit lemniscate is the compact set
\[
 E_P=\{z\in\mathbb C:|P(z)|\leq1\}.
\]
For a finite family of closed Euclidean disks
$D_j=\{z:|z-c_j|\leq r_j\}$, with arbitrary centers $c_j\in\mathbb C$
and radii $r_j\geq0$, define the cost of the covering to be
$\sum_j r_j$. Radii, rather than diameters or disk areas, are being
summed; overlaps between disks are allowed.

Does every such $P$, in every degree, admit a disk covering of
$E_P$ of cost at most $2$? Explicitly, must there exist an integer
$s\geq1$ and choices $c_1,\ldots,c_s,r_1,\ldots,r_s$ such that
\[
 E_P\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}D_j,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^{s}r_j\leq2?
\]
The number of disks is not prescribed, and the claim must hold
without any connectedness assumption on the lemniscate.
Monicity fixes the natural scale: for a general leading coefficient
$a\ne0$ and a positive level $t$, the corresponding radius scale is
$(t/|a|)^{1/n}$. Thus the displayed normalized statement asks for a
constant independent of degree, coefficients, and the number or
geometry of components. Covering only the polynomial's zeros does
not suffice; the entire closed sublevel set must be covered.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every monic polynomial's unit lemniscate be covered by disks whose radii sum to at most two?
