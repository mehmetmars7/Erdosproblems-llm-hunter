\section*{Kirby Problem 4.5}\label{2881}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2881 · KP-4.5 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Here an open $4$-manifold $M$ is a topological manifold without boundary
which is noncompact; assume it is connected, Hausdorff and second
countable. A smoothing is an atlas of local coordinates in $\mathbb R^4$
with smooth transition maps inducing its given topology. Two smoothings
are considered the same if there is some diffeomorphism between the
resulting smooth manifolds, regardless of whether that map is isotopic
to the identity on the underlying topological manifold.

Does every such $M$ support uncountably many pairwise nondiffeomorphic
smoothings? Explicitly, the desired conclusion is an uncountable index
set $A$ and smooth manifolds $M_\alpha$, each homeomorphic to $M$,
with $M_\alpha\not\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}M_\beta$ whenever
$\alpha\ne\beta$.

The topology and end structure of $M$ remain fixed throughout the
family. No finite-generation condition on homology or fundamental group,
no finiteness of the number of ends, and no orientability hypothesis
is part of the question. The smooth structures may behave differently
near infinity, and a distinguishing diffeomorphism is allowed to move
points arbitrarily far out the ends.

The equivalence relation is essential: uncountably many marked atlases,
or even many smoothings inequivalent under homeomorphisms isotopic to
the identity, need not give uncountably many diffeomorphism types.
The question asks for the latter, for every connected open manifold,
not only for Euclidean space or punctured closed manifolds.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every connected noncompact boundaryless four-manifold have uncountably many smoothings up to diffeomorphism?
