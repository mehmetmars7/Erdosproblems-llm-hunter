\section{Weinstein Conjecture}\label{1339}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1339 · Partial Differential Equations}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $(M^{2n},\omega)$ be a smooth symplectic manifold: $d\omega=0$ and $\omega$ is nondegenerate. For a smooth Hamiltonian $H:M\to\mathbb R$, let $X_H$ be defined by $\omega(X_H,\cdot)=dH$. A regular level $Y=H^{-1}(a)$ has $dH\ne0$ along it. It is of contact type if there is a one-form $\lambda$ on $Y$ such that $d\lambda=\omega|_Y$ and $\lambda$ is nonzero on the characteristic line $\ker(\omega|_{TY})$ at every point. Equivalently $\lambda\wedge(d\lambda)^{n-1}$ is nowhere zero. Its Reeb field $R_\lambda$ is characterized by $\lambda(R_\lambda)=1$ and $d\lambda(R_\lambda,\cdot)=0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every dimension $2n$, does each nonempty compact regular contact-type level $Y$ carry a nonconstant periodic Hamiltonian orbit: a map $\gamma:\mathbb R/T\mathbb Z\to Y$ for some $T>0$ with $\dot\gamma=X_H\circ\gamma$? Equivalently, for every nonempty closed contact manifold $(Y^{2n-1},\lambda)$, does $R_\lambda$ have a closed orbit? Here closed means compact without boundary. \sref{1339}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every compact regular Hamiltonian energy surface of contact type, in any dimension, have a periodic trajectory; equivalently, must every closed contact manifold’s Reeb field have a closed orbit?
