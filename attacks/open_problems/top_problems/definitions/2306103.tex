\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.103}\label{2306103}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306103 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. Let $S_H$ consist of injective, sense-preserving harmonic functions on $\mathbb D$ with the expansion
\[f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nz^n+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{-n}\overline z^{\,n},\qquad a_1=1.\]
Harmonicity means that the real and imaginary parts satisfy the planar Laplace equation. Writing $f=h+\overline g$ with $h,g$ holomorphic, sense preservation is $|h'(z)|^2-|g'(z)|^2>0$ throughout $\mathbb D$. The subclass $S_H^0$ is defined by $a_{-1}=0$.
Let
\[k(z)=2\operatorname{Re}\frac{z+z^3/3}{(1-z)^3}
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n^2+1}{3}(z^n+\overline z^{\,n}).\]
The closure $\overline{S_H}$ means locally uniform limits on $\mathbb D$ of maps in $S_H$; the source places $k$ in this closure.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $k$ extremal for the coefficient bounds in $S_H$? Precisely, for every $n\geq2$, are the sharp upper bounds
\[\sup_{f\in S_H}|a_n|=\sup_{f\in S_H}|a_{-n}|=\frac{2n^2+1}{3},\]
with the displayed limit map furnishing the extremal coefficients? \sref{2306103}{2} \sref{2306103}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are the quadratic-in-degree coefficients of the displayed limiting harmonic map the sharp coefficient bounds for all normalized univalent harmonic maps?
