\section*{Kirby Problem 2.3}\label{2751}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2751 · KP-2.3 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S$ be a connected orientable surface of finite type. The mapping class
group $\operatorname{Mod}(S)$ is the group of isotopy classes of
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of $S$, fixing its boundary pointwise and
preserving its finite puncture set. Composition induces the group operation.
A complex linear group is a group admitting an injective homomorphism into
$\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb C)$ for some finite positive integer $N$.

Is $\operatorname{Mod}(S)$ linear for every finite-type surface $S$? Thus, for
each fixed $S$, does there exist a finite $N=N(S)$ and a representation
\[
\rho_S:\operatorname{Mod}(S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{N(S)}(\mathbb C)
\quad\text{with}\quad\ker\rho_S=\{1\}?
\]
There is no requirement that $N$ be independent of genus, punctures, or boundary
components. Faithfulness means that every nontrivial mapping class acts by a
nonidentity matrix. Actions on ordinary homology are examples of linear
representations, but their kernels must be checked and cannot simply be
discarded. The target is an ordinary finite-dimensional representation over
$\mathbb C$, as specified by the source's convention for linearity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does each finite-type surface's mapping class group have a faithful finite-dimensional complex matrix representation?
