\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.21}\label{2306021}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306021 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$.A holomorphic function is typically real if $f(z)\in\mathbb R$ exactly when $z\in\mathbb R$, for $z\in\mathbb D$. Consider normalized typically-real functions $f(0)=0$, $f'(0)=1$, written $f(z)=zP(z)/(1-z^2)$, with $P$ holomorphic, $\operatorname{Re}P(z)>0$ and $P(0)=1$. Since $f$ is typically real, $P(\overline z)=\overline{P(z)}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Characterize the additional conditions on $P$ necessary and sufficient for $f(z)=zP(z)/(1-z^2)$ to be univalent on $\mathbb D$. \sref{2306021}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which positive-real-part factors in the typically-real representation yield a univalent function?
