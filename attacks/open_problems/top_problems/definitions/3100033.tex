\section*{Given two permutations $\sigma$ and $\tau$, what is the expected number of copies of $\sigma$ in a permutation chosen uniformly at random f}\label{3100033}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100033 · AMR-030-0033 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix permutations $\sigma\in S_k$ and $\tau\in S_m$, with $m\ge2$.
A classical occurrence of a pattern $\rho\in S_s$ in
$\pi\in S_n$ is a choice of positions $i_1<\cdots<i_s$ whose
values have the same relative order as $\rho$. Let
$\operatorname{occ}_\rho(\pi)$ be the number of such choices and
\[
 \operatorname{Av}_n(\tau)=
       \{\pi\in S_n:\operatorname{occ}_\tau(\pi)=0\}.
\]
Choose $\Pi_n$ uniformly from this finite avoidance class, and define
\[
 \mu_{\sigma,\tau}(n)
   =\mathbb E[\operatorname{occ}_\sigma(\Pi_n)]
   =\frac{\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau)}
                       \operatorname{occ}_\sigma(\pi)}
          {|\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau)|}.
\]
Determine these expected occurrence counts for prescribed patterns,
by exact formulas, generating functions, or sharp asymptotic
expressions as $n\to\infty$.

Avoidance and occurrence both refer to arbitrary subsequences of
positions, not only adjacent positions. Overlapping occurrences
are all counted. For $m\ge2$ the avoidance class is nonempty, so
the uniform distribution is well defined; avoiding the unique
length-one pattern would give no permutations of positive length.
Some pairs force zero occurrences, while others produce different
growth rates. The task is to explain this dependence on both
patterns, rather than average over an unrestricted uniformly
random permutation, for which symmetry gives a much simpler count.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the expected number of occurrences of one permutation pattern in a uniformly chosen permutation avoiding another fixed pattern.
