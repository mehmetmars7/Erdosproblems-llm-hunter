\section*{Kirby Problem 1.6}\label{2665}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2665 · KP-1.6 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Seifert matrix is an integral square matrix $V$ with $V-V^T$ unimodular. For a
Seifert surface $F$ and a basis $a_i$ of $H_1(F;\mathbb Z)$, it is realized by
$V_{ij}=\operatorname{lk}(a_i^+,a_j)$, where $a_i^+$ is pushed in the positive
normal direction to $F$.
Two such matrices are $S$-equivalent if related by integral unimodular congruences
and elementary enlargements or their inverses. An enlargement adjoins two basis
vectors $e,f$, with $f$ having zero pairings with the old module and with itself,
and $(V(e,f),V(f,e))=(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$; the other new pairings are arbitrary.

Given any two $S$-equivalent matrices $V_1,V_2$, must there be one knot $K$ with
Seifert surfaces $F_1,F_2$ realizing these matrices in suitable bases? The surfaces
are not required to be disjoint or to have equal genus.
The source also asks the stronger uniform realization question: for each entire
$S$-equivalence class, does some single knot realize every form in that class?
That assertion has a different quantifier order from the specified-pair question;
both formulations keep the boundary knot fixed while the surface varies.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two algebraically equivalent Seifert forms be realized by surfaces bounding the same knot?
