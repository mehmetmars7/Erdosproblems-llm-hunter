\section{Kirby Problem 5.2}\label{3009}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3009 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a homeomorphism $h$ of $\mathbb R^n$, the orbit of $x$ is $\mathcal O_h(x)=\{h^m(x):m\in\mathbb Z\}$, using positive and negative iterates. Compact-open convergence of maps $h^{m_j}$ to the identity means uniform convergence to the identity on every compact subset of $\mathbb R^n$. The closed ball is $B^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|\leq1\}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $n\geq1$, if a homeomorphism (or smooth diffeomorphism) $h:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfies
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R^n}\operatorname{diam}\mathcal O_h(x)\leq1,
 \qquad h^{m_j}\longrightarrow\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R^n}
 \text{ in the compact-open topology for some }m_j\to\infty,
\]
must $h$ be the identity? Also resolve the ball version: if $h:B^n\to B^n$ is a homeomorphism equal to the identity on $\partial B^n$ and some positive powers $h^{m_j}$ with $m_j\to\infty$ converge to the identity, must $h=\operatorname{id}_{B^n}$? \sref{3009}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a recurrent homeomorphism with uniformly bounded orbits be trivial, and must a recurrent homeomorphism of a ball fixing its boundary be trivial?
