\section{Unit Distance Problem}\label{1383}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1383 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ of distinct points, let
\[
 u(P)=\#\{\{x,y\}\subseteq P:\ x\ne y,\ \|x-y\|_2=1\}.
\]
Pairs are unordered and the distance is Euclidean. Define $u(n)=\max_{|P|=n}u(P)$, with the maximum over all planar $n$-point sets.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the maximum $u(n)$ for general $n\in\mathbb N$, and its sharp asymptotic order as $n\to\infty$. No lattice, general-position, or algebraicity restriction is imposed on $P$. \sref{1383}{1}\sref{1383}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Among n distinct points in the Euclidean plane, what is the greatest possible number of pairs exactly one unit apart, and how does this maximum grow?
