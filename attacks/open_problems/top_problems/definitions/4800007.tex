\section*{Multiple ergodic averages — Problem 7}\label{4800007}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4800007 · AMR-047-0007 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer sequence $a(n)$, call two-fold recurrence
of powers the following property: for every probability
space $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$, invertible measure-preserving
map $R$, nonzero integers $r,s$, and measurable set $A$
of positive measure, there is $n\ge1$ with $a(n)\ne0$ and
\[
                    \mu(A\cap R^{r a(n)}A
                              \cap R^{s a(n)}A)>0.
\]
The set $R^kA$ denotes the image under the corresponding
invertible iterate. Recurrence for commuting pairs instead
requires that for every commuting pair $T,S$ and every
positive-measure $A$, some such $n$ satisfy
$\mu(A\cap T^{a(n)}A\cap S^{a(n)}A)>0$.

Does a sequence exist which has two-fold recurrence of
powers but fails two-fold recurrence for commuting pairs?
A positive answer must supply an integer sequence satisfying
the universal first property and also one probability-preserving
commuting system and positive-measure set $A$ for which
\[
        \mu(A\cap T^{a(n)}A\cap S^{a(n)}A)=0
        \quad\text{whenever }a(n)\ne0.
\]
A negative answer would prove the implication for every
integer sequence. Repeated terms and negative values are
allowed; the condition $a(n)\ne0$ prevents a zero term
from giving a vacuous recurrence. Neither property assumes
ergodicity, and the powers hypothesis includes every nonzero
integer pair, not only $1,2$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can recurrence along every pair of powers of one transformation fail for a general commuting pair?
