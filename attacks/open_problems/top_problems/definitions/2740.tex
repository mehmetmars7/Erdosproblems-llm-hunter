\section{Kirby Problem 1.81}\label{2740}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2740 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Here a knot is any topological embedding $k:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$, including wild embeddings. An arc of embeddings is a continuous family $k_t:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$, $t\in[0,1]$, in the compact-open topology. A knot is trivial in this question if such a family joins it to a standard round unknot. This is the weak isotopy convention of the source; it does not require an ambient isotopy of $S^3$. Let $B:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ denote the Bing sling, the wild knot specified by the construction and illustration in the cited problem and its Bing reference.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is every topologically embedded circle in $S^3$ trivial under this arc-of-embeddings equivalence? In particular, the named conjecture asks whether the Bing sling is nontrivial: is there no arc of embeddings joining $B$ to a round unknot? \sref{2740}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Using paths of embeddings, including wild knots, ask whether every knot can become the unknot and whether the Bing sling supplies a counterexample.
