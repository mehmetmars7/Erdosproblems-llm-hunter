\section*{Quasipolynomial trace samples for arbitrary symbols}\label{30007459}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $n\ge2$ and $0<p\le1$. A deletion trace retains each symbol of a length-$n$ string independently with probability $p$, reports retained symbols exactly and preserves their order. The decoder knows $n,p$ and receives independent traces. Does there exist an absolute $C>0$ and a decoder recovering every input string with probability at least $2/3$ from at most
\[
 \left\lceil\exp\bigl(Cp^{-1}(\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6\bigr)\right\rceil
\]
traces? The symbols need not be binary. No restriction is placed on the decoder's computational cost.

\subsection{Short English statement}
For every length $n\ge2$ and known retention probability, can exactly observed symbols be reconstructed from the stated quasipolynomial number of traces, without a time bound?
