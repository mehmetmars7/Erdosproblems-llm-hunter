\section*{Some Questions — Question 48}\label{1200048}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200048 · AMR-011-0048 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(\xi_m)_{m\in\mathbb Z}$ and $(\eta_m)_{m\in\mathbb Z}$ be independent families of independent Bernoulli random variables, each taking zero and one with probability $1/2$. Define the random sets
\[
 A=\{m:\xi_m=1\},\qquad B=\{m:\eta_m=1\}.
\]
Give both sets the metric inherited from $\mathbb Z$, so $d(a,a')=|a-a'|$, rather than the path metric of the possibly disconnected induced graph.

Are $A$ and $B$ almost surely quasi-isometric? Explicitly, with probability one, do there exist a map $f:A\to B$ and constants $L\geq1$, $C\geq0$ such that
\[
 L^{-1}|a-a'|-C\leq |f(a)-f(a')|\leq L|a-a'|+C
\]
for all $a,a'\in A$, and every $b\in B$ has distance at most $C$ from $f(A)$?

The map and constants may depend on the two realized sets. The source does not require the map to be order-preserving, bijective, translation-equivariant or measurably selected as a function of the samples. Those additional constraints give different questions. Both random subsets are unbounded in both directions almost surely, but their random gaps have no deterministic upper bound. The same problem can be posed for independent Bernoulli subsets of other Cayley graphs, in particular a Cayley graph of $\operatorname{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)$, using their inherited word metrics.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are two independent random subsets of the integers almost surely equivalent at large scales?
