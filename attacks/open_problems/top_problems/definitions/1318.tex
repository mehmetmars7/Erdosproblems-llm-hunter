\section{Navier-Stokes Regularity}\label{1318}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1318 · Partial Differential Equations}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix a viscosity $\nu>0$. For $x\in\mathbb R^3$ and $t\geq0$, seek a velocity $u(x,t)\in\mathbb R^3$ and pressure $p(x,t)\in\mathbb R$ satisfying
\[
\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u=\nu\Delta u-\nabla p+f,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad u(x,0)=u^0(x).
\]
Here $\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^3\partial_{x_j}^2$ acts componentwise and $u^0$ is smooth and divergence-free. For a multi-index $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^3$, write $\partial_x^\alpha=\prod_j\partial_{x_j}^{\alpha_j}$. Euclidean admissible data satisfy, for all $\alpha$ and nonnegative integers $m,K$,
\[
|\partial_x^\alpha u^0(x)|\leq C_{\alpha K}(1+|x|)^{-K},\qquad
|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^mf(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha mK}(1+|x|+t)^{-K}.
\]
A Euclidean admissible solution has $u,p\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[0,\infty))$ and $\sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\mathbb R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty$. In the periodic case, $u^0,f,u,p$ are $\mathbb Z^3$-periodic in $x$, so the spatial domain is $\mathbb T^3=\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3$. Periodic admissible data instead require smooth $u^0$ and
\[
|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^mf(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha mK}(1+t)^{-K}
\]
for every $\alpha,m,K$. A periodic admissible solution requires smooth $u,p$ on $\mathbb T^3\times[0,\infty)$; the uniform-in-time Euclidean energy condition is not imposed on this alternative. Constants may depend on the displayed indices but not on $x,t$. The periodicity requirement for $p$ includes the official erratum.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Resolve the three-dimensional global regularity question by proving at least one of the following official alternatives, with viscosity $\nu>0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] Every Euclidean admissible initial velocity, with $f\equiv0$, admits a Euclidean admissible solution for all $t\geq0$.
\item[(B)] Every periodic admissible initial velocity, with $f\equiv0$, admits a periodic admissible solution for all $t\geq0$.
\item[(C)] There exist Euclidean admissible $u^0$ and smooth forcing $f$ for which no Euclidean admissible global solution exists.
\item[(D)] There exist periodic admissible $u^0$ and smooth forcing $f$ for which no periodic admissible global solution exists.
\end{enumerate}
The forced breakdown alternatives and the unforced existence alternatives are distinct parts of this formulation. \sref{1318}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Do smooth three-dimensional incompressible fluid data always give global smooth solutions, or can admissible data and forcing prevent this? The official problem allows an answer either in Euclidean space or on the periodic three-torus.
