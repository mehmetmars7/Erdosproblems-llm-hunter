\section{Odd cycles and low oddness}\label{3109}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3109 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite graph is cubic if every vertex has degree three, and bridgeless if deleting any one edge does not increase its number of connected components. A $2$-factor is a spanning subgraph in which every vertex has degree two, hence a union of disjoint cycles. For a bridgeless cubic graph define
\[\omega(G)=\min\{\text{number of odd-length cycles in }F:F\text{ is a }2\text{-factor of }G\}.\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If every cycle in every $2$-factor of a bridgeless cubic graph $G$ has odd length, must $\omega(G)\leq2$? \sref{3109}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every spanning cycle cover of a bridgeless cubic graph consists entirely of odd cycles, must some such cover have at most two cycles?
