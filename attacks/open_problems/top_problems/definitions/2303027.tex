\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 3.27}\label{2303027}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2303027 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix $n\ge2$ and an unbounded domain $D\subset\mathbb R^n$, where a domain is nonempty, open and connected. A real-valued harmonic function $u$ on $D$ satisfies $u\in C^2(D)$ and $\Delta u=0$. The Euclidean norm is denoted by $|x|$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist, for every such $D$, a continuous function $\varepsilon_D:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ such that
\[\bigl(|u(x)|<\varepsilon_D(|x|)\text{ for every }x\in D\bigr)\ \Longrightarrow\ u\equiv0
\]
for every harmonic $u$ on $D$? The function may depend on $D$ and $n$, but not on $u$. \sref{2303027}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each unbounded domain, can one choose a positive radial size bound that no nonzero harmonic function can satisfy everywhere?
