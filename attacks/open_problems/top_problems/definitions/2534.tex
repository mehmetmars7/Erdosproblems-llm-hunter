\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.25}\label{2534}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2534 · KOU-21.25 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite group $G$ is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal
subgroups are $1$ and $G$. For a prime $p$ dividing $|G|$, write
$|G|=p^a m$ with $p\nmid m$. A Sylow $p$-subgroup is a subgroup of
order $p^a$. For subgroups $U,V\le G$, the notation $\langle U,V\rangle$
denotes the smallest subgroup containing both, consisting of finite
products of elements from $U\cup V$ and their inverses.

The question is whether for every finite simple group $G$ and every
pair of prime divisors $p_1,p_2$ of $|G|$, one can choose Sylow
$p_i$-subgroups $H_i$ with
\[
 \langle H_1,H_2\rangle=G.
\]
The primes are allowed to coincide. In that case the two Sylow subgroups
may be different conjugates; the assertion does not restrict them to be
the same subgroup. When the primes are different, no compatibility
between the initial Sylow choices is prescribed.

The desired conclusion is generation by the union of the two subgroups,
not the stronger set-product equality $G=H_1H_2$. The choices may depend
on both primes and on $G$, and the assertion does not claim that every
pair of Sylow subgroups works. Cyclic simple groups of prime order are
included and give immediate cases; the substantive question also covers
all nonabelian finite simple groups. A negative answer needs one group
and prime pair for which every eligible choice generates a proper subgroup.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every finite simple group be generated by suitably chosen Sylow subgroups for any two prescribed prime divisors of its order, including equal primes?
