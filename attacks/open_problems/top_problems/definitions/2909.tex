\section*{Kirby Problem 4.33}\label{2909}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2909 · KP-4.33 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S_1,S_2$ be smoothly embedded closed surfaces in a closed oriented smooth four-manifold $X$. Assume they are topologically ambiently isotopic: a path of self-homeomorphisms beginning at the identity carries $S_1$ to $S_2$. Smooth ambient isotopy requires such a path through diffeomorphisms.

An external stabilization replaces each pair $(X,S_i)$ by $(X,S_i)\#(S^2\times S^2,\varnothing)$, taking the connected sum away from the surface. A standard internal stabilization replaces it by $(X,S_i)\#(S^4,T)$, where $T$ is the standard unknotted torus. It adds a trivial local tube to the surface without changing $X$. A general internal stabilization adds an embedded tube along an arc joining two disks on the surface; the arc need not lie in a small ball. Make compatible choices so that the resulting surfaces have the same abstract type.

Under what hypotheses do $S_1$ and $S_2$ become smoothly isotopic after one external stabilization, after one standard internal stabilization, or after one general internal stabilization? More generally, for each of these operations, is there a finite upper bound depending only on $X$ for the number needed for arbitrary such pairs?

The ambient manifold stays fixed in the internal versions and changes in the external version. A uniform bound for fixed $X$ must be independent of the surfaces and their complexity. Where even eventual isotopy is not known, existence of a finite number of stabilizations is itself part of the problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
When does stabilization remove exotic smooth knotting of topologically isotopic surfaces, and can the number depend only on the ambient manifold?
