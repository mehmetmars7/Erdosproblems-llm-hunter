\section{Ehrhart's Volume Conjecture}\label{1219}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1219 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\geq1$ and let $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ be compact and convex with nonempty interior. Its Euclidean $n$-dimensional volume is $\operatorname{vol}_n(K)$ and its barycenter is
\[
b(K)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_n(K)}\int_Kx\,dx.
\]
The lattice is the standard lattice $\mathbb Z^n$. No lattice-vertex or polytope assumption is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If there exists $z\in\mathbb Z^n$ such that
\[
\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{z\},\qquad b(K)=z,
\]
must
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(K)\leq\frac{(n+1)^n}{n!}
\]
hold for every such $K$ and every $n$? \sref{1219}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the volume of a convex body bounded by the Ehrhart constant when its unique interior lattice point is its center of mass?
