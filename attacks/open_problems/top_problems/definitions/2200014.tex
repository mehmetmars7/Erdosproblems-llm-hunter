\section{Problems Around Polynomials — Conjecture 9}\label{2200014}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2200014 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\geq1$ and $f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jz^j$ with all $a_j>0$, and put $a_{-1}=a_{n+1}=0$. For an increasing index list $0=k_1<\cdots<k_m=n$, define its parity-change count by
\[v=\bigl|\{j:1\leq j<m,\ k_j\not\equiv k_{j+1}\pmod2\}\bigr|.\]
Real roots are counted with multiplicity.\par Put $\widetilde c_j=(j+1)a_j^2-ja_{j-1}a_{j+1}$, and let $k_1,\ldots,k_m$ be exactly the indices for which $\widetilde c_j>0$, in increasing order. The endpoint indices belong to the list since $\widetilde c_0>0$ and $\widetilde c_n>0$. Write $v_9(f)$ for its parity-change count.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the number of real zeros of $f$ at most $v_9(f)$ for every such $f$? \sref{2200014}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the parity-change count among indices with positive shifted coefficient differences bound the number of real roots?
