\section*{Kirby Problem 3.41}\label{2839}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2839 · KP-3.41 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth knot and $X=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K$
its exterior. Choose the meridian $\mu$ and the preferred longitude $\lambda$
on $\partial X$, with $\lambda$ null-homologous in $X$. For a reduced rational
number $r=p/q$, $q>0$, write $S^3_r(K)$ for the oriented Dehn filling
obtained by attaching a solid torus so that its meridian is identified with
the slope $p\mu+q\lambda$. This fixes the surgery coefficient convention.

A positive cooriented contact structure on an oriented three-manifold is
$\xi=\ker\alpha$ with $\alpha\wedge d\alpha>0$. It is tight when it has
no embedded overtwisted disk, meaning a disk with Legendrian boundary whose
contact framing agrees with the disk framing. The structure is allowed to
depend on the surgery coefficient.

The high surgery conjecture asks whether
\[
 \forall K\subset S^3\quad\exists n_K\in\mathbb Z\quad
 \forall r\in\mathbb Q,
 \quad r\geq n_K\Longrightarrow
 S^3_r(K)\text{ admits a tight contact structure}.
\]
The threshold depends on $K$, but all sufficiently large rational slopes,
not just integers or one subsequence, must qualify. No hyperbolicity or
fiberedness assumption is imposed on the knot.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every knot, do all sufficiently large positive rational surgeries admit tight contact structures?
