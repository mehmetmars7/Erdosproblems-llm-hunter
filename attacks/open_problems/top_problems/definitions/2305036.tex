\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.36}\label{2305036}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305036 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. All Taylor coefficients below are taken at $0$.Let $1\le n_0<n_1<\cdots$ be integers and let $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_kz^{n_k}$ be holomorphic on $\mathbb D$, with $\sum_{k\ge0}|c_k|=\infty$. For real $\alpha<\beta$ with $0<\beta-\alpha\le2\pi$, let $V_{\alpha,\beta}=\{re^{i\theta}:0<r<1,\ \alpha<\theta<\beta\}$ be an open angular sector.Assume Hadamard gaps: there is a fixed $q>1$ with $n_{k+1}>q n_k$ for every $k\ge0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite value $a\in\mathbb C$, must (i) $f(z)=a$ have at least one solution in $\mathbb D$; (ii) it have infinitely many distinct solutions in $\mathbb D$; and (iii) it have at least one solution in every sector $V_{\alpha,\beta}$? These are three separate assertions. \sref{2305036}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a Hadamard-gap power series whose coefficients are not absolutely summable, ask separately whether it takes every finite value once, infinitely often, and in every angular sector.
