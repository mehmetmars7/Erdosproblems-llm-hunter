\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.90}\label{2599}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2599 · KOU-21.90 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finite connected simple graph of diameter three, on $v$
vertices. Put $A_i(x,y)=1$ if the distance from $x$ to $y$ is $i$, and zero
otherwise, for $0\leq i\leq3$. Thus $A_0=I$ and $A_1$ is the adjacency matrix.
The graph is distance-regular if the number of vertices $z$ with
$d(x,z)=i$ and $d(y,z)=j$ depends only on $i,j,d(x,y)$.
Its distance matrices span a commutative algebra with mutually orthogonal
primitive idempotents $E_0,E_1,E_2,E_3$, where $E_0=J/v$, $J$ is the all-ones
matrix, $E_iE_j=0$ for $i\ne j$, and $\sum E_i=I$.

It is $Q$-polynomial if these idempotents can be ordered so that, for the
entrywise matrix product $\circ$,
\[
E_1\circ E_i=\frac1v\sum_{j=0}^3 q_{1i}^{j}E_j,
\]
the coefficient matrix $(q_{1i}^{j})_{i,j=0}^3$ is irreducible tridiagonal:
entries with $|i-j|>1$ vanish and every immediately adjacent off-diagonal
entry is positive.

Let $\Gamma_i$ have adjacency matrix $A_i$. Does such a $Q$-polynomial
distance-regular $\Gamma$ exist for which both $\Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_3$ are
strongly regular? A strongly regular graph is regular of valency $k$ with
$0<k<v-1$, and has constants $\lambda,\mu$ such that adjacent vertex pairs
have $\lambda$ common neighbors and distinct nonadjacent pairs have $\mu$.
The two distance graphs may have different parameters.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a $Q$-polynomial distance-regular graph of diameter three have both its distance-two and distance-three graphs strongly regular?
