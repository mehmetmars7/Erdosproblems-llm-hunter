\section{Algebraic Stories — Hilbert series of numerical semigroup rings}\label{1500017}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1500017 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,\ldots\}$, and let $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb N_0$ be an additive submonoid containing zero with finite complement. With minimal generators $s_1,\ldots,s_e$, its semigroup ring over a field $K$ is $K[\mathcal S]=K[t^{s_1},\ldots,t^{s_e}]$. Set
\[P_{\mathcal S}(z)=(1-z)\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}z^s.
\]
The finite complement makes $P_{\mathcal S}$ a polynomial. Call $\mathcal S$ cyclotomic if this polynomial factors over $\mathbb Q$ into cyclotomic polynomials (equivalently, all its complex roots lie on the unit circle). The ring is a complete intersection if the kernel of $K[y_1,\ldots,y_e]\to K[t]$, $y_i\mapsto t^{s_i}$, is generated by a regular sequence of length $e-1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $\mathcal S$ cyclotomic if and only if $K[\mathcal S]$ is a complete intersection? \sref{1500017}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are cyclotomic numerical semigroups exactly those whose defining semigroup rings are complete intersections?
