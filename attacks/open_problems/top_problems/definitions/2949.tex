\section{Kirby Problem 4.73}\label{2949}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2949 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(D^4)$ be the topological group of smooth diffeomorphisms of the closed disk that are the identity on a neighborhood of its boundary, with the smooth topology; $B\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(D^4)$ is its classifying space. Let $\operatorname{Top}(4)$ be the topological group of homeomorphisms of $\mathbb R^4$ fixing the origin, with the compact-open topology, and let $O(4)$ be the orthogonal group. Use
\[
F_4=\operatorname{hofib}\bigl(BO(4)\longrightarrow B\operatorname{Top}(4)\bigr)
\]
for the based homotopy fiber. This is the space denoted $\operatorname{Top}(4)/O(4)$ in smoothing theory. Write $\Omega^4_0F_4$ for the path component of the constant map in the based fourfold loop space $\operatorname{Map}_*(S^4,F_4)$. A weak homotopy equivalence induces a bijection on path components and isomorphisms of all based homotopy groups.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does the four-dimensional analogue of Morlet's equivalence hold:
\[
 B\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(D^4)\simeq\Omega^4_0\bigl(\operatorname{Top}(4)/O(4)\bigr)
\]
as a weak homotopy equivalence? The disk dimension is four and the loop-space component is the component of the constant map. \sref{2949}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does Morlet’s comparison between smooth disk diffeomorphisms and topological smoothing data remain a weak equivalence for the four-disk?
