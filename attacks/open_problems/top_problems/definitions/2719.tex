\section*{Kirby Problem 1.60}\label{2719}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2719 · KP-1.60 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $R=\mathbb Z$ or $R=\mathbb Z/2$, an $R$-homology four-ball is a compact
connected oriented smooth $4$-manifold $W$ with
$H_*(W;R)\cong H_*(B^4;R)$. In this problem require additionally an
orientation-preserving identification $\partial W\cong S^3$.
A knot $K\subset S^3$ is smoothly slice in $W$ if, under this identification,
it bounds a properly embedded smooth disk $D\subset W$.
Its smooth four-genus $g_4(K)$ is the minimum genus of smooth connected
oriented bounding surfaces in the standard $B^4$.

Does there exist a knot $K$ with $g_4(K)>0$ that is smoothly slice in an
integer homology four-ball? Ask the same existence question for a
$\mathbb Z/2$-homology four-ball.
The manifold $W$ and disk may depend on the example and need not be simply
connected. Requiring $\partial W=S^3$ ensures the same ordinary knot is being
compared in both settings. A disk in a rational homology ball alone does not
meet either stated homology condition; integral and mod-two acyclicity must be
checked with their specified coefficient rings.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a knot fail to be smoothly slice in the standard ball yet bound a smooth disk in a ball with the same integral or mod-two homology?
