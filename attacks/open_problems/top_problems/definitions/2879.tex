\section{Kirby Problem 4.3}\label{2879}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2879 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an oriented smooth closed $4$-manifold $X$, an exotic smooth structure is a smooth manifold orientation-preservingly homeomorphic to $X$ but not diffeomorphic to its standard smooth structure. Write $\#_kX$ for the connected sum of $k$ copies, with $\#_0X=S^4$ when a zero summand occurs. Let $\mathbb{CP}^2$ have its complex orientation and $\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$ the opposite orientation. A standard K3 surface is a simply connected compact complex surface with trivial canonical bundle, with its usual complex-oriented smooth structure. Use the usual product smooth structure on $S^2\times S^2$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine which of the following closed simply connected manifolds admit exotic smooth structures:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(a)}&\#_k\mathbb{CP}^2,\quad k\ge1;\\[2pt]
\text{(b)}&\#_m(\mathbb{CP}^2\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}),\quad1\le m\le8,
\quad\text{and }\#_n(S^2\times S^2),\quad1\le n\le10;\\[2pt]
\text{(c)}&(\#_p\mathrm{K3})\#(\#_q(S^2\times S^2)),\quad q\ge0,\quad p\ge q+5.
\end{array}
\]
If such structures exist, determine the smallest possible $k,m,n$ in the indicated families, and the largest possible $p,q$ in (c), or whether those parameters are unbounded. The standard connected-sum smooth structures are the structures against which exoticness is measured. \sref{2879}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do exotic smooth structures exist on positive-definite sums of complex projective planes, small zero-signature connected sums, or K3 sums in the specified spin range, and what are the smallest or largest parameters admitting them?
