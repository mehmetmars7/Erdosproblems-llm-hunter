\section*{Vertex-Unfolding Polyhedra}\label{5500042}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500042 · AMR-054-0042 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $P$ be a compact connected polyhedral surface without boundary in
$\mathbb R^3$. Its faces are simple planar polygons, each homeomorphic
to a disk; every edge is incident to two faces. Convexity is not
assumed. The prescribed faces are kept intact.

A vertex unfolding cuts along original edges, possibly along every
edge, while retaining selected identifications at original vertices.
Place each face in the plane by a rigid motion so that retained
identifications agree. The resulting union must be connected and
distinct face interiors must be disjoint. Faces are allowed to
remain joined only at a vertex, so the interior of the planar union
need not be connected. Accidental geometric contacts do not replace
the required retained connections in the original surface.

Does every $P$ admit such an unfolding? An important restricted
question asks the same for convex polyhedra. The source reports an
affirmative result when every face is triangular, leaving general
polygonal faces as the issue.

Subdividing faces into triangles changes the prescribed face
structure and would evade the question. Likewise, an ordinary
edge unfolding requires a stronger form of connection through
uncut edge intervals. The task asks for a flat nonoverlapping
realization, not a collision-free continuous physical motion to it.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can any closed polyhedral surface be flattened without overlapping
face interiors, if cuts follow edges and faces may remain connected
only at vertices?
