\section{Uniform convergence to an irrationally indifferent fixed point}\label{5300068}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5300068 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A holomorphic germ $\varphi$ at $z_0\in\mathbb C$ is a holomorphic map defined near $z_0$, with two representatives identified when they agree on a smaller neighborhood. Assume
\[\varphi(z_0)=z_0,\qquad \varphi'(z_0)=e^{2\pi i\alpha},\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q.\]
Iterates are considered wherever a representative and its analytic continuations make them defined. The domain sought below need not be invariant under $\varphi$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can $\varphi^n(z)$ converge uniformly to $z_0$ on some nonempty open connected domain $D$ on which all these iterates are defined? Investigate also the additional hypotheses that $\varphi$ extends to an entire function, or that it is a germ of an algebraic function. \sref{5300068}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an irrationally indifferent holomorphic germ have iterates converging uniformly to its fixed point on a domain? Also consider germs extending to entire functions and algebraic germs.
