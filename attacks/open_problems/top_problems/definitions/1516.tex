\section{Sphere Packing Problem Higher Dimensions}\label{1516}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1516 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For \(d\ge1\), equip \(\mathbb R^d\) with Euclidean distance
\(|\cdot|\) and Lebesgue volume \(\operatorname{vol}_d\).
Let \(B_d(x,r)=\{y:|y-x|<r\}\).
A packing of equal unit balls is
\[
                P_X=\bigcup_{x\in X}B_d(x,1),
\]
where \(X\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is discrete and
\(|x-y|\ge2\) whenever \(x\ne y\) in \(X\).
Define its upper density and the optimal sphere-packing density by
\[
 \delta(P_X)=\limsup_{R\to\infty}
     \frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(P_X\cap B_d(0,R))}
          {\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d(0,R))},
 \qquad \Delta_d=\sup_X\delta(P_X).
\]
The supremum ranges over all such sets of centers, including nonlattice
and nonperiodic packings. Scaling all balls and centers by the same factor
does not affect the density.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the exact value of \(\Delta_d\) for every integer \(d>3\).
For each remaining dimension, identify a packing with optimal density
and prove an upper bound that applies to every packing. \sref{1516}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the greatest fraction of Euclidean space that equal nonoverlapping balls can occupy in each dimension greater than three?
