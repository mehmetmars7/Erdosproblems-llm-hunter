\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.62}\label{2307062}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2307062 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\omega_1$ be the first uncountable ordinal; $\alpha<\omega_1$ means that $\alpha$ is countable. For each $\alpha$ seek a nonnegative nonincreasing sequence $(x_{n,\alpha})_{n\ge1}$ with finite sum. Every asserted quotient below is required to be defined for all sufficiently large $n$, so that a denominator in that assertion is eventually positive. No monotonicity is imposed on the test sequence $(x_n)$ in the second condition.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Without assuming the Continuum Hypothesis, can one construct such a family satisfying both
\[\alpha<\beta<\omega_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n,\alpha}}{x_{n,\beta}}=0,
\]
and
\[x_n>0,\quad\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n<\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists\alpha<\omega_1:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_n}{x_{n,\alpha}}=0?
\]
The construction is required in the usual axioms of set theory with choice, without the extra continuum assumption. \sref{2307062}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a countable-ordinal hierarchy of convergent decreasing series grow successively more slowly and eventually dominate every positive summable sequence, without assuming the Continuum Hypothesis?
