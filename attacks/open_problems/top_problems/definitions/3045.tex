\section{Shuffle-Exchange Conjecture}\label{3045}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3045 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $A$ be an alphabet of $k\geq2$ letters and $E=A^n$ the words of length $n\geq2$. Write $\mathfrak S(E)$ for all permutations of $E$, acting on the right, so $x(\alpha\beta)=(x\alpha)\beta$. The shuffle is $(x_1\cdots x_n)\sigma=x_2\cdots x_nx_1$. The exchange subgroup $G$ consists of all permutations preserving the first $n-1$ letters of every word. For subsets $P,Q$ of this permutation group, $PQ=\{pq:p\in P,q\in Q\}$. Define
\[
 d(k,n)=\min\{d\geq2:(\sigma G)^d=\mathfrak S(E)\},
\]
where the power uses exactly $d$ factors, not products of at most $d$ factors.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $d(k,n)$ for every pair of integers $k,n\geq2$. In particular, is the recorded shuffle-exchange conjecture
\[
 d(k,n)=2n-1\qquad(k,n\geq2)
\]
true? \sref{3045}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the exact number of shuffle-and-exchange stages needed to realize every permutation of words, including the proposed value two times the word length minus one.
