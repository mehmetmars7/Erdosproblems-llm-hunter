\section*{Kirby Problem 4.11}\label{2887}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2887 · KP-4.11 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a smooth $4$-manifold and $f:S^2\hookrightarrow M$ a
smooth embedding with trivial normal bundle. Thus a tubular neighborhood
$\nu f$ is diffeomorphic to $S^2\times D^2$.
The Gluck-twisted manifold $M_f$ is formed by cutting out its interior
and regluing by the boundary self-map
$\tau(x,e^{i\theta})=(R_\theta x,e^{i\theta})$, where $R_\theta$
rotates $S^2$ once as $\theta$ traverses the circle. Compose $\tau$
with the original attaching map. Any existing boundary of $M$ remains
outside this interior operation.

Part (a) asks for an orientable $M$ and such an $f$ for which $M_f$
and $M$ are homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic.
Part (b) asks for an orientable $M$ and smooth embeddings
$f,g:S^2\hookrightarrow M$, both with trivial normal bundle, which
are homotopic as maps, but for which $M_f$ and $M_g$ are homeomorphic
and not diffeomorphic.

A homotopy in (b) is a continuous map $S^2\times[0,1]\to M$ connecting
the embeddings; its intermediate maps need not be embeddings. Thus
homotopy is weaker than isotopy or concordance. In both parts, the
smooth distinction is between entire resulting manifolds, with arbitrary
diffeomorphisms allowed, rather than between marked surgery descriptions.
No closedness or simple-connectivity assumption is added to the source's
orientable ambient-manifold question. Merely changing homeomorphism type
by a Gluck twist would not answer either part.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can Gluck twisting change the smooth type while preserving the homeomorphism type in an orientable four-manifold, including twists along homotopic spheres?
