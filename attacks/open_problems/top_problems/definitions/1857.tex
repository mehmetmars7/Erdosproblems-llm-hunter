\section*{\texorpdfstring{Erdős \$3000 Conjecture on Arithmetic Progressions}{Erdős \$3000 Conjecture on Arithmetic Progressions}}\label{1857}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$, divergence of its reciprocal sum means $\sum_{a\in A}1/a=\infty$. A nonconstant arithmetic progression of length $k$ is $a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d$ with integers $a,d\ge1$. The question is whether
\[
 \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \forall k\ge1\ \exists a,d\ge1:\ \{a+jd:0\le j<k\}\subseteq A.
\]
Positive density is not assumed, and the common difference can depend on the requested length.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jlms.70483}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does divergence of a set's reciprocal sum force it to contain arithmetic progressions of every finite length?
