\section{Failures of Chromatic Reflection at Uncountable Cardinals}\label{2381}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2381 · Graph Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



The symbols $\aleph_\xi$ denote infinite cardinalities. Chromatic number is the least cardinality of a proper vertex-colour set. The assertions quantify over all subgraphs of the specified smaller vertex cardinality, not just some favourable restrictions.

A graph has a vertex set $V$ and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A subgraph need not be induced unless that word is stated. A proper vertex colouring gives adjacent vertices different colours; $\chi(G)$ is the least number of colours. \sref{2381}{1} \sref{2381}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a graph with $\aleph_2$ vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_2$ such that every subgraph on $\aleph_1$ vertices has chromatic number $\leq\aleph_0$?
Is there a graph with $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_1$ such that every subgraph on $\aleph_\omega$ vertices has chromatic number $\leq\aleph_0$? \sref{2381}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Produce the two exact graphs in ZFC, refute their existence, or state and verify precise additional set-theoretic hypotheses under which each exact cardinal instance follows. \sref{2381}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can graphs have the specified uncountable chromatic number while every subgraph of a prescribed smaller cardinality remains countably colourable?
