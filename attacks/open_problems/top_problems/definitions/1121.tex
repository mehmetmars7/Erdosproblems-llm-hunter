\section{Sphere Packing Problem in Higher Dimensions}\label{1121}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1121 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix \(n\ge1\), and write \(\operatorname{vol}_n\) for Lebesgue
measure on Euclidean \(\mathbb R^n\).  Let \(B_n(x,r)\) be the open ball
of radius \(r\) about \(x\).  A packing of unit balls is a union
\[
 \mathcal P=\bigcup_{x\in X}B_n(x,1)
 \quad\text{with}\quad \|x-y\|\ge2\text{ for }x\ne y.
\]
The set \(X\) may be any set of centers satisfying this separation
condition; a lattice is not required.  Its upper density and the
optimal packing constant are
\[
 \overline\delta(\mathcal P)
  =\limsup_{R\to\infty}
    \frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(\mathcal P\cap B_n(0,R))}
         {\operatorname{vol}_n(B_n(0,R))},
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=\sup_{\mathcal P}\overline\delta(\mathcal P).
\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the exact value of \(\Delta_n\) and an arrangement attaining
optimal density in each dimension \(4\le n\le23\); more generally,
determine \(\Delta_n\) for arbitrary positive integer \(n\).
The problem concerns all equal-sphere packings, including nonlattice
packings, with the upper-density convention above. \sref{1121}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the greatest fraction of Euclidean space that can be occupied by nonoverlapping equal balls, particularly in dimensions 4 through 23 and, more generally, in every dimension.
