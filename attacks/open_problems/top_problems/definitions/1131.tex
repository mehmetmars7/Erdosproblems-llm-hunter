\section{The Union-Closed Sets Conjecture}\label{1131}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1131 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let \(\mathcal F\) be a finite, nonempty family of finite sets,
with \(\bigcup\mathcal F\ne\varnothing\).
The family is union-closed if \(S\cup T\in\mathcal F\) whenever
\(S,T\in\mathcal F\).  For \(x\in\bigcup\mathcal F\), define its frequency
by \(f_{\mathcal F}(x)=|\{S\in\mathcal F:x\in S\}|\).
The nontriviality requirement excludes the family consisting only of
the empty set.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such finite union-closed family \(\mathcal F\), is there
an element \(x\in\bigcup\mathcal F\) such that
\[
 f_{\mathcal F}(x)\ge\frac{|\mathcal F|}{2}?
\] \sref{1131}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must some element occur in at least half the sets of every nontrivial finite family closed under unions?
