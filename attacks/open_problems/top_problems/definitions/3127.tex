\section{Turán number of a finite family.}\label{3127}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3127 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite nonempty family $\mathcal F$ of finite simple graphs, let $\operatorname{ex}(n,\mathcal F)$ be the maximum number of edges of an $n$-vertex graph containing no member of $\mathcal F$ as a subgraph; write $\operatorname{ex}(n,F)=\operatorname{ex}(n,\{F\})$. Let $C_j$ be the cycle of length $j$ and $e(G)=|E(G)|$. A graph is $F$-free if it contains no subgraph isomorphic to $F$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such $\mathcal F$, is there a member $F\in\mathcal F$ and constants $C>0,n_0$ such that \[\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\le C\operatorname{ex}(n,\mathcal F)\qquad(n\ge n_0)?\] The source also asks the following stronger even-cycle question: for each integer $\ell\ge3$, does there exist $c(\ell)>0$ such that for every integer $2\le k<\ell$ and every $C_{2\ell}$-free graph $G$, some $C_{2k}$-free subgraph $H\subseteq G$ satisfies $e(H)\ge e(G)/c(\ell)$? \sref{3127}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one forbidden graph in a finite family determine its extremal edge count up to a constant factor? The accompanying stronger question asks whether deleting a bounded fraction of edges avoids any shorter prescribed even cycle.
