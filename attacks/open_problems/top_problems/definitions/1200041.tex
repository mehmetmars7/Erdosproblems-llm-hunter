\section*{Some Questions — Question 41}\label{1200041}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200041 · AMR-011-0041 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let a countable discrete group $\Gamma$ act by measure-preserving Borel transformations on a standard atomless probability space $(X,\mu)$. The action is essentially free if almost every point has trivial stabilizer. Its orbit equivalence relation is $R=\{(x,\gamma x):x\in X,\gamma\in\Gamma\}$, considered modulo null sets.

A graphing of $R$ is a countable family of measure-preserving partial Borel bijections $\phi_i:A_i\to B_i$ whose graphs lie in $R$ and whose generated equivalence relation equals $R$ almost everywhere. Its cost is $\sum_i\mu(A_i)$, possibly infinite. Define
\[
 \operatorname{cost}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X)
   =\inf_{\Phi\text{ graphing }R}\sum_i\mu(A_i).
\]
The domain measures count each chosen partial bijection once, rather than separately counting its inverse.

Does every countable group have fixed price? That is, for any two essentially free probability-measure-preserving actions of the same group, must their costs agree? The common value is allowed to be infinite and may depend on the group.

The two actions can occur on different probability spaces, and neither is assumed ergodic or equivalent to the other. The condition of essential freeness is part of the question: arbitrary actions with stabilizers need not have the same orbit-relation cost. The infimum defining each cost ranges over all graphings of that orbit relation, not merely the transformations arising from one preferred finite generating set of the group.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do all essentially free probability-measure-preserving actions of a countable group have the same cost?
