\section*{Kirby Problem 1.5}\label{2664}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2664 · KP-1.5 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Seifert surface for an oriented knot $K\subset S^3$ is a compact, connected,
oriented embedded surface whose oriented boundary is $K$. Its least possible genus
is the Seifert genus $g(K)$. The unknotting number $u(K)$ minimizes the number of
crossing changes turning $K$ into the unknot, over all diagrams.
A knot is fibered if its exterior $S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K$ is a bundle
over $S^1$ with fiber a Seifert surface. It is positive if it admits an oriented
diagram in which every crossing has positive sign under the usual right-hand
convention. It is alternating if it admits a diagram whose overpasses and
underpasses alternate along the knot.

Two questions impose different diagrammatic hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does every positive fibered knot satisfy $g(K)=u(K)$?
\item Does every alternating fibered knot satisfy $g(K)\geq u(K)$?
\end{enumerate}
In each part the minimum defining $u(K)$ is unrestricted: the diagrams used in an
unknotting sequence need not be positive, alternating, or diagrams of a fibered
knot. Genus here means Seifert genus in $S^3$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Compare genus and unknotting number for positive fibered knots and for alternating fibered knots.
