\section{Real roots of the flow polynomial}\label{3221}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3221 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Choose an orientation of a finite graph $G$. A nowhere-zero $q$-flow assigns to every edge a nonzero element of $\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, with the outgoing sum equal to the incoming sum at each vertex. The flow polynomial $Q_G(x)$ is the polynomial whose value at each positive integer $q$ counts these flows, independently of the orientation. Graphs with bridges have the zero flow polynomial.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
The main recorded conjecture asks whether every real zero $x$ of every nonzero flow polynomial satisfies $x\le4$. The source's later discussion separately proposes the bounds $x\le5$ and $x\le6$ in place of $4$. \sref{3221}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How large can a real root of a nonzero graph flow polynomial be? The source records the historical bounds four, five and six.
