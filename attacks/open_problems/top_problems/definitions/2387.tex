\section{Equal Prime Supports of Two Disjoint Consecutive Products}\label{2387}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2387 · Number Theory}

The lengths $k_1,k_2$ are fixed first. The two nonnegative starts may vary subject to disjointness. Equality concerns sets of distinct prime divisors, not equality of exponents or of the two products. \sref{2387}{1} \sref{2387}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $k_1\geq k_2\geq 3$. Are there only finitely many $n_2\geq n_1+k_1$ such that $ \prod_{1\leq i\leq k_1}(n_1+i)\textrm{ and }\prod_{1\leq j\leq k_2}(n_2+j) $ have the same prime factors? \sref{2387}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For two fixed block lengths at least three, can only finitely many disjoint integer blocks have products involving exactly the same primes?
