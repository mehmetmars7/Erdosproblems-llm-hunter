\section{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.68}\label{2577}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2577 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite group $G$ is semi-abelian in the source's sense if there are subgroups
$1=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_m=G$ such that, for each $i<m$, some abelian group $A_i$ and action of $G_i$ on $A_i$ give a semidirect product $A_i\rtimes G_i$ admitting a surjective homomorphism onto a group isomorphic to $G_{i+1}$. A finite group is monomial if every irreducible complex character $\chi$ is induced from a one-dimensional character $\lambda$ of some subgroup $H\leq G$: $\chi=\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\lambda$. Here irreducible characters are traces of irreducible finite-dimensional complex representations and a one-dimensional character is a homomorphism $H\to\mathbb C^\times$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is every finite semi-abelian group monomial? That is, does each irreducible complex character of every group built by the specified subgroup sequence occur as induction from a linear character of a subgroup? \sref{2577}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all finite groups constructed through the source’s abelian semidirect-product quotient process monomial?
