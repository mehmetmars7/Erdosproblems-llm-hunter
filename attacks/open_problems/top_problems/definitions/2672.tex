\section*{Kirby Problem 1.13}\label{2672}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2672 · KP-1.13 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $Y$ be a closed oriented $3$-manifold and $K\subset Y$ a null-homotopic knot.
Let $X=Y\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K$ and let $F\subset X$ be an oriented
Seifert surface. Zero surgery $Y_0(K)$ fills $X$ along the longitude $\partial F$;
capping $F$ with a meridian disk of the filling gives a closed surface $\widehat F$.
For a surface $A=\bigsqcup A_i$ put $\chi_-(A)=\sum_i\max(0,-\chi(A_i))$.
The Thurston norm of a relative homology class is the minimum of $\chi_-$ over
embedded oriented representatives. As in the source, a connected surface is taut
if it minimizes this norm in a nonzero class, or if it is a sphere.

The two assertions to establish are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $Y_0(K)$ fibers over $S^1$ with fiber class $[\widehat F]$, then $X$
fibers over $S^1$ with fiber class $[F]\in H_2(X,\partial X;\mathbb Z)$.
\item If $F$ is taut in $X$, then $\widehat F$ is taut in $Y_0(K)$.
\end{enumerate}
The prescribed fiber classes in the first assertion are part of the hypothesis
and conclusion; the existence of an unrelated fibration does not suffice.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does zero surgery preserve the tautness of a Seifert surface and detect a knot fibration in its specified homology class?
