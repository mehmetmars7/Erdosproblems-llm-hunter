\section{Cycle Double Cover Conjecture}\label{1327}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1327 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A graph here is a finite undirected multigraph; parallel edges and loops are allowed, and a loop contributes two to its vertex's degree. A bridge is an edge whose deletion increases the number of connected components. A cycle is a connected 2-regular subgraph (including a loop or a pair of parallel edges when applicable). A cycle double cover is a finite list $(C_1,\ldots,C_m)$ of cycles; repetitions in the list are allowed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite undirected graph $G$ with no bridges, does there exist a list of cycles $C_1,\ldots,C_m$ such that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf 1_{\{e\in E(C_j)\}}=2\qquad(e\in E(G))?
\]
For an edgeless graph the empty list is permitted. \sref{1327}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every edge of a finite bridgeless graph be covered exactly twice by a list of cycles?
