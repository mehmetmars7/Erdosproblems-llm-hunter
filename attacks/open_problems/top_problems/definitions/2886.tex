\section*{Kirby Problem 4.10}\label{2886}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2886 · KP-4.10 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth homotopy $4$-ball is a compact smooth $4$-manifold $X$
homotopy equivalent to a point; here its boundary is additionally
required to be diffeomorphic to $S^3$. Let $K:S^2\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{int}(B^4)$ be a smooth embedding. Its tubular neighborhood
is $S^2\times D^2$. The Gluck operation replaces this neighborhood
using the boundary map
\[
 (x,e^{i\theta})\longmapsto(R_\theta x,e^{i\theta}),
\]
where $R_\theta$ makes one complete rotation about an axis in $S^2$.
Equivalently this is the loop generating $\pi_1(SO(3))$.
The original outer boundary $\partial B^4=S^3$ is untouched.

Part (a) asks whether every smooth homotopy $4$-ball with boundary
$S^3$ is diffeomorphic to a manifold obtained by one such Gluck twist
inside $B^4$. The embedding may depend on the ball, and may be
knotted. The comparison is a diffeomorphism of manifolds with boundary;
no preassigned boundary parameterization is required in the statement.

Part (b) asks whether every $X$ obtained by that construction satisfies
$X\times[0,1]\cong B^5$ smoothly, after rounding the corners of the
product. The question is about a $5$-dimensional smooth ball, not just
a contractible product. It does not require the diffeomorphism to
preserve the product coordinate. Even if the product is standard,
that conclusion alone is weaker than asserting that $X$ itself is a
standard smooth $4$-ball.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all smooth homotopy four-balls with sphere boundary single Gluck twists of the standard ball, and do all such twists become a standard five-ball after taking a product with an interval?
