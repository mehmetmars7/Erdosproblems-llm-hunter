\section{Upper bound on the divisorial gonality of a graph}\label{3000087}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3000087 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite connected unweighted multigraph without loops. A divisor is $D=\sum_{v\in V}D(v)v$ with $D(v)\in\mathbb Z$; it is effective if $D(v)\geq0$ for every $v$, and $\deg D=\sum_vD(v)$. For $f:V\to\mathbb Z$ define $\Delta f(v)=\sum_{e=vw}(f(v)-f(w))$, counting parallel edges separately. Divisors are linearly equivalent when their difference is $\Delta f$ for some $f$. Say $D$ has rank at least one if, for every $v\in V$, $D-v$ is equivalent to an effective divisor. The divisorial gonality $\operatorname{gon}(G)$ is the least degree of such a divisor. Set $g=|E|-|V|+1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every such $G$ satisfy
\[\operatorname{gon}(G)\leq\frac{|E|-|V|}{2}+2=\frac{g+3}{2},\]
equivalently $\operatorname{gon}(G)\leq\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor$? \sref{3000087}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite connected graph admit a divisor of rank at least one whose degree is at most $\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor$, where $g$ is its genus?
