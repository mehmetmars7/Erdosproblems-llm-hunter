\section{Imbalance Conjecture}\label{1438}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1438 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite simple undirected graph $G$, write $d_G(v)$ for the number of neighbors of a vertex $v$. The imbalance of an edge $e=uv$ is $i_G(e)=|d_G(u)-d_G(v)|$. Its imbalance multiset is $M_G=\{\!\{i_G(e):e\in E(G)\}\!\}$, where repeated values are counted once for each edge. A finite multiset is graphic when it is the multiset of vertex degrees of some finite simple graph.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every finite simple graph $G$ such that $i_G(e)\ge1$ for every edge $e$, is $M_G$ graphic? Equivalently, writing $m=|E(G)|$, must there exist a simple graph $H$ with $m$ vertices and a bijection $\psi:E(G)\to V(H)$ such that
\[
 d_H(\psi(e))=i_G(e)\qquad(e\in E(G))?
\]
If $G$ has no edges, the empty graph $H$ realizes the empty multiset. \sref{1438}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every edge joins vertices of different degrees, must the edge degree-differences be the vertex degrees of some simple graph?
