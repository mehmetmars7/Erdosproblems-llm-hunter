\section{The Bounded Burnside Problem}\label{1188}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1188 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For \(m\ge1\), let \(F_m\) be the free group on \(m\) generators.
For \(n\ge1\), let \(N_{m,n}\) be the normal subgroup generated by
\(\{w^n:w\in F_m\}\), and put
\[
 B(m,n)=F_m/N_{m,n}.
\]
This is the free \(m\)-generator group satisfying \(g^n=1\) for every
element \(g\); every \(m\)-generated group satisfying that identity is a quotient.
Finiteness here concerns this entire group, rather than just its finite quotients.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine all pairs \((m,n)\in\mathbb N^2\) for which
\(B(m,n)\) is finite.  In particular, is the free two-generator
group of exponent five, \(B(2,5)\), finite? \sref{1188}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which numbers of generators and exponents give a finite free Burnside group? The question includes, specifically, the exponent-five group on two generators.
