\section{Uniform Boundedness Conjecture for Rational Points}\label{1470}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1470 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A number field $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$, with degree $d=[K:\mathbb Q]\ge1$. A curve here means a smooth projective geometrically connected one-dimensional variety $C$ over $K$. Its genus is $g=\dim_K H^0(C,\Omega_C^1)$, the dimension of its regular differential forms. The set $C(K)$ consists of its $K$-rational points.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every pair of integers $g\ge2$ and $d\ge1$, does there exist an integer $N(g,d)$ such that, for every number field $K$ of degree $d$ and every such curve $C/K$ of genus $g$,
\[
 \#C(K)\le N(g,d)?
\]
The bound may depend only on $g$ and $d$, not on the particular field, curve, coefficients, or rank of its Jacobian. \sref{1470}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the number of rational points on every smooth curve of genus at least two be bounded using only its genus and the degree of its number field?
