\section*{Unique natural-number witnesses for every enumerable set}\label{30007204}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $n\geq 1$ and recursively enumerable $S\subset N^{n}$, do there exist $m\geq 1$ and $P\in Z[A_{1},\dots ,A_{n},W_{1},\dots ,W_m]$ such that each $a\in S$ has exactly one tuple $w\in N^m$ with $P(a,w)=0$ and each $a\notin S$ has none?
