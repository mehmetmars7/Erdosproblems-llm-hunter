\section{Rectangling a Rectangle}\label{5500078}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5500078 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A rectangular partition has finitely many rectangular pieces with disjoint interiors and union equal to the outer rectangle. Each piece has positive edge lengths; its area is the product of its edge lengths and its perimeter is twice their sum.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist rectangular partitions into $n\ge2$ pieces of equal area with pairwise different perimeters? The source reports a seven-piece construction with real edge lengths and asks whether such a partition is possible with all edge lengths rational, conjecturing that it is not. The source also asks for the analogous equal-volume, distinct-boundary-measure partition into boxes in $\mathbb R^d$ for $d\ge3$, with a conjectured negative answer. \sref{5500078}{2}
