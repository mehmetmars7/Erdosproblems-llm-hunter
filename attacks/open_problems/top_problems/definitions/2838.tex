\section*{Kirby Problem 3.40}\label{2838}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2838 · KP-3.40 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a closed connected oriented hyperbolic three-manifold. A positive
cooriented contact structure is a smooth two-plane field
$\xi=\ker\alpha\subset TM$, where $\alpha$ is a globally defined one-form
and $\alpha\wedge d\alpha$ is everywhere positive for the orientation of $M$.
Multiplying $\alpha$ by a positive function preserves the contact structure.

A knot is Legendrian when its tangent line lies in $\xi$. An overtwisted
disk is an embedded disk whose boundary is Legendrian and whose contact
framing along that boundary agrees with the disk framing; equivalently its
boundary has Thurston--Bennequin number zero relative to the disk.
A contact structure is tight if it contains no overtwisted disk.

Does every closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold admit a tight contact
structure? One may choose the plane field freely; the question does not
ask that a given contact structure be tight. No compatibility of the contact
planes with the hyperbolic metric is imposed. In particular, the desired
structure need not arise from a chosen geometric flow or foliation.

The target is tightness itself. Existence of an overtwisted contact structure
does not suffice, while requiring a symplectic filling would add a stronger
condition which is not part of this problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed oriented hyperbolic three-manifold support a contact plane field with no overtwisted disk?
