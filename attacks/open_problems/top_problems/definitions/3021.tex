\section*{Kirby Problem 5.14}\label{3021}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3021 · KP-5.14 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite-volume hyperbolic $n$-manifold is a connected complete Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature $-1$ whose total Riemannian volume is finite. In the noncompact case, each end is a cusp: outside a compact set it is modeled on $F\times[0,\infty)$ with metric $dt^2+e^{-2t}g_F$, where $(F,g_F)$ is a closed flat $(n-1)$-manifold. Equivalently the manifold is a quotient $\mathbb H^n/\Gamma$ by a torsion-free lattice.

For each integer $n\ge5$, does there exist such a manifold with exactly one cusp? The cusp condition counts ends, not boundary components of an arbitrarily chosen compact subset. Orientability is not required in the stated question; an orientable version would be a further restriction.

The source's phrase ``for any $n\ge5$'' can also motivate the weaker existence question: is there even one dimension at least five admitting a one-cusped example? Record the dimension-by-dimension question and this weaker test separately, rather than conflating their quantifiers.

The group need not be arithmetic, and no fixed flat cross-section is prescribed. An orbifold with torsion in its lattice does not meet the manifold requirement. A construction with several cusps likewise does not suffice, even if those cusps are isometric or permuted transitively by symmetries. Passing to a finite cover is allowed as a method, but the resulting number of ends must still be exactly one.
\subsection{Short English statement}
In which dimensions at least five do complete finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds with exactly one cusp exist?
