\section*{Kirby Problem 4.2}\label{2878}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2878 · KP-4.2 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed smooth $4$-manifold is compact and has empty boundary.
A smooth manifold $X'$ is an exotic copy of a given smooth manifold
$X$ if there exists a homeomorphism $X'\to X$ but no diffeomorphism
$X'\to X$. Thus the topological manifold is unchanged while the smooth
structures are inequivalent up to diffeomorphism. A homeomorphism and
a diffeomorphism are both bijections with continuous inverse; the latter
and its inverse must additionally be smooth in the specified atlases.

Does every closed smooth $4$-manifold $X$ have an exotic copy? More
strongly, for every such $X$, does there exist a sequence
$(X_j)_{j\ge1}$ of smooth manifolds, each homeomorphic to $X$, such
that the $X_j$ are pairwise nondiffeomorphic and none is diffeomorphic
to $X$?

The first assertion requests at least one different diffeomorphism
type for each underlying topological manifold; the second requests
infinitely many. An infinite collection of atlases related by
self-diffeomorphisms would not suffice. The comparison does not mark
points or a preferred identification with $X$, and no relative-boundary
condition occurs because the manifolds are closed.
No simple-connectivity, orientability, symplectic structure, or restriction
on the intersection form is imposed in the stated question. One may
first study connected manifolds; treating arbitrary finite disjoint
unions does not replace the universal connected cases. Any proposed
family must establish both the common homeomorphism type and its
pairwise smooth distinctions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed smooth four-manifold admit another smooth structure, and in fact infinitely many, up to diffeomorphism?
