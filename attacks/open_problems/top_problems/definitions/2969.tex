\section*{Kirby Problem 4.93}\label{2969}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2969 · KP-4.93 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be the standard smooth K3 surface, represented for example
by any nonsingular quartic complex hypersurface in
$\mathbb{CP}^3$, with its underlying smooth $4$-manifold.
A symplectic form on $X$ is a smooth real $2$-form $\omega$
with $d\omega=0$ and with $\omega_x$ nondegenerate on every
tangent space. A symplectomorphism from $(X,\omega)$ to
$(X,\eta)$ is a diffeomorphism $f$ with $f^*\eta=\omega$.

A K\"ahler form means a symplectic form $\eta$ compatible with
an integrable complex structure $J$ on $X$: it satisfies
$\eta(Ju,Jv)=\eta(u,v)$ and $\eta(u,Ju)>0$ for all nonzero
$u$. Integrability means $J$ arises from complex coordinate
charts with holomorphic transition maps.

Is every symplectic form $\omega$ on the standard K3 smooth
manifold symplectomorphic to a K\"ahler form? Thus both $J$
and its K\"ahler form $\eta$ may be chosen in response to
$\omega$, as may the diffeomorphism $f$.

No fixed complex structure is prescribed in the conclusion.
On the other hand, equality of cohomology classes, deformation
through symplectic forms with varying classes, or agreement of
characteristic classes alone is weaker than the requested
symplectomorphism. The underlying differentiable manifold remains
the standard K3 throughout; replacing it by an exotic manifold
homeomorphic to K3 would be a different question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every symplectic form on the standard K3 smooth manifold carried by a diffeomorphism to a Kähler form for some complex structure?
