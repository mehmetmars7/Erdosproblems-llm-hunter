\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 8.9}\label{2308009}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2308009 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. For $p>0$, the Bergman space is $A^p(\mathbb D)=\{f\text{ analytic}:\|f\|_{A^p}=(\int_{\mathbb D}|f|^p\,dA)^{1/p}<\infty\}$, where $dA=dx\,dy$ is ordinary area measure. On $A^1$ define the complex linear functional \[\Lambda(f)=\int_{\mathbb D}f(z)\operatorname{sgn}(\operatorname{Re}z)\,dA(z),\] where the sign is $1$, $-1$ or $0$ according as the real part is positive, negative or zero. Its norm is \[\|\Lambda\|=\sup_{\|f\|_{A^1}\le1}|\Lambda(f)|.\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the exact value of $\|\Lambda\|$. The source records that it is strictly below $1$ and that the supremum is attained. \sref{2308009}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Compute the exact norm of the Bergman linear functional obtained by integrating against the sign of the real part.
