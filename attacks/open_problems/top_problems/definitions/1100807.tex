\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 8.7}\label{1100807}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100807 · AMR-010-0807 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a prime $p$. The Prüfer group is the direct limit
\[
 C_{p^\infty}=\bigcup_{j\geq1}C_{p^j},
\]
where the inclusion sends a generator of order $p^j$ to the $p$th power of a generator of order $p^{j+1}$. Equivalently, it is the multiplicative group of all complex roots of unity of $p$-power order, regarded as a discrete group.

A group $G$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_n$ over $\mathbb Z$ if the trivial module $\mathbb Z$ has a projective $\mathbb ZG$-resolution whose terms in degrees $0,\ldots,n$ are finitely generated. Type $\mathrm{FP}_\infty$ requires this in every degree. Type $F_n$ means that $G$ has a classifying CW complex $K(G,1)$ with finite $n$-skeleton; its universal cover is contractible.

Can $C_{p^\infty}$ embed in a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_\infty$? Can it embed in a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_3$? Ask these for each prime $p$, allowing the target to depend on $p$.

The source also asks whether, for every integer $n\geq2$, every group of type $F_n$ embeds in some group of type $F_{n+1}$. All embeddings are injective abstract group homomorphisms. The ambient groups need not share the original group's dimension or torsion properties. Homological finiteness $\mathrm{FP}_n$ and geometric finiteness $F_n$ are explicitly different conditions; neither is silently substituted for the other in these three questions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can Prüfer groups embed in stronger homological finiteness classes, and can every $F_n$ group embed in an $F_{n+1}$ group?
