\section*{Kirby Problem 1.50}\label{2709}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2709 · KP-1.50 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Work smoothly in the standard ball $B^4$ with boundary $S^3$.
A knot is slice if it bounds a properly embedded disk in $B^4$. An
$m$-component link is slice if its components bound $m$ pairwise disjoint such
disks. A properly embedded surface is ribbon if, after isotopy relative to the
boundary, the radial function on its interior is Morse with no local maxima.
Equivalently it has a presentation by disks and bands, with only index-zero and
index-one critical points. A ribbon knot or link bounds disks with this property.
For a knot $K$, its smooth four-genus $g_4(K)$ is the least genus of a connected
oriented smooth surface in $B^4$ bounded by $K$.

The questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does every slice knot bound a ribbon disk?
\item Does every slice link bound a disjoint collection of ribbon disks?
\item Does every knot bound a connected oriented ribbon surface of genus
exactly $g_4(K)$?
\end{enumerate}
In (b), the disks must be disjoint simultaneously; sliceness of each component
considered by itself is a weaker hypothesis. Part (c) specializes to (a) when
the four-genus is zero.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can slice disks always be chosen ribbon, and can every minimal-genus surface in the four-ball be replaced by a ribbon surface of the same genus?
