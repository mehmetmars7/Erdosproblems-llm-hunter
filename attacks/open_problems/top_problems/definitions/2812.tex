\section*{Kirby Problem 3.14}\label{2812}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2812 · KP-3.14 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth knot and suppose $M=S^3\setminus K$ carries
its complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric, normalized to sectional curvature
$-1$. A surface $S\subset M$ is closed if it is compact without boundary,
and embedded if its inclusion is injective and locally a smooth submanifold.
It is totally geodesic if its second fundamental form vanishes identically;
equivalently, every geodesic of its induced metric is locally an ambient
hyperbolic geodesic.

Can there exist such a pair $(K,S)$ with $S$ a closed embedded totally
geodesic surface? The Menasco--Reid conjecture gives the negative answer:
\[
 \text{No hyperbolic knot complement in }S^3\text{ contains a closed
 embedded totally geodesic surface.}
\]
All three adjectives matter. A punctured or spanning totally geodesic surface
is not closed; an immersed one need not be embedded; a surface with arbitrarily
small but nonzero principal curvatures is not totally geodesic. The knot must
have one component and lie in $S^3$, so examples in multicomponent link
complements or in other ambient three-manifolds do not decide this question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the complement of a hyperbolic knot in the three-sphere contain a compact embedded surface that is exactly totally geodesic?
