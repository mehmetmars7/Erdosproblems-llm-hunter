\section*{Net gains from place-based policies}\label{30007749}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider a national system of regions with mobile households and firms. Let \(a\in\{0,1\}\) denote a specified place-based subsidy in one economically distressed region, funded by a national tax rule announced with the subsidy. Production includes explicitly parameterized agglomeration spillovers; wages, rents, firm location, and household migration adjust in equilibrium. Define \(v_i(a)\) as household lifetime monetary-equivalent utility, \(\Pi_f(a)\) firm profits net of real costs, and \(K(a)\) administration and tax-distortion costs. The national welfare target is
\[\Delta W=E\!\left[\sum_i\{v_i(1)-v_i(0)\}+\sum_f\{\Pi_f(1)-\Pi_f(0)\}\right]-\{K(1)-K(0)\}.\]
Household monetary equivalents exclude profit distributions already entered in the separate profit term. Include households and firms outside the subsidized region, and use consistent accounting so transfers and land rents are not counted twice. Identify whether local employment gains represent additional national output, relocation, or changes in resident composition. Estimate or bound spillovers and migration responses using credible policy variation, and report rankings under multiple equilibrium selections when the model permits them. The cited spatial review provides relevant theoretical context, but a verified primary passage establishing this precise welfare comparison as an open problem was not found. This is a proposed quantitative design question.
