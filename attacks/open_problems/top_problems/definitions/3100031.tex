\section{Is it true that the sum of the k largest Laplacian eigenvalues of a graph with m edges is at most k(k+1)/2+m}\label{3100031}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3100031 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be a finite simple undirected graph on $n\geq1$ vertices with $m=|E(G)|$. Its adjacency matrix $A$ has $A_{ij}=1$ exactly when $i,j$ are adjacent, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(\deg_G(1),\ldots,\deg_G(n))$. The combinatorial Laplacian is $L=D-A$, whose real eigenvalues, with multiplicity, are ordered $\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_n$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $G$ and every integer $1\leq k\leq n$, is
\[\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\leq m+\frac{k(k+1)}2?\] \sref{3100031}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the sum of the largest k Laplacian eigenvalues always at most the number of edges plus k(k+1)/2?
