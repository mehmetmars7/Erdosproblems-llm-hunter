\section*{Kirby Problem 2.25}\label{2773}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2773 · KP-2.25 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $p:E\to B$ be a smooth oriented bundle with fiber $F$, where $B$ and $F$
are closed connected surfaces of genus at least two. Transport around loops in
$B$ gives its monodromy homomorphism
$\rho:\pi_1(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(F)$, defined up to conjugacy, where
$\operatorname{Mod}(F)$ is the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms
of $F$ modulo isotopy. Assume that $\rho$ is injective.

An unbranched multisection of degree $k\geq1$ is an embedded surface
$C\subset E$ such that $p|_C:C\to B$ is a $k$-sheeted covering map. The surface
$C$ need not be connected. Equivalently, there is a continuously varying
unordered configuration of $k$ distinct points in every fiber, with no collisions.
In configuration-space language it is a section of the associated bundle
whose fiber is
\[
 \operatorname{UConf}_k(F)=
 \{(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\in F^k:x_i\ne x_j\ (i\ne j)\}/\mathfrak S_k.
\]
Does every such bundle admit an unbranched multisection for some finite $k$?
The degree may depend on the bundle. A branched multisection, where points can
merge over exceptional base points, does not meet the requirement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every surface bundle with injective monodromy admit a finite, continuously varying set of distinct points in each fiber?
