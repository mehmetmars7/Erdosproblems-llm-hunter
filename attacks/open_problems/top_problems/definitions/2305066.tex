\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.66}\label{2305066}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305066 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. A Blaschke product is a function \[B(z)=\eta z^m\prod_j\frac{|a_j|}{a_j}\frac{a_j-z}{1-\overline{a_j}z},\qquad |\eta|=1,\quad m\in\{0,1,\ldots\},\quad 0<|a_j|<1,\quad\sum_j(1-|a_j|)<\infty,\] where the product can be finite or infinite and repeated zeros are counted with multiplicity. Call the product infinite when its zero sequence has infinitely many terms. For $w\in\mathbb C$, the fibre is $B^{-1}(\{w\})=\{z\in\mathbb D:B(z)=w\}$, counted as distinct points.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every infinite Blaschke product $B$, is there a number $\delta_B>0$ such that $B^{-1}(\{w\})$ is infinite for every $|w|<\delta_B$? The source update reports Stephenson’s negative answer. \sref{2305066}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every infinite Blaschke product take every sufficiently small value infinitely many times? The source records a counterexample.
