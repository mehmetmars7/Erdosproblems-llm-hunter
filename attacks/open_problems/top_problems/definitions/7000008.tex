\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.8}\label{7000008}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000008 · AMR-069-0008 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(M,g)$ be any smooth two-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
with no boundary unless separately specified. Both the manifold
and metric are $C^\infty$; compactness and completeness are not
assumed. A smooth isometric embedding into $\mathbb R^4$ is an
injective smooth immersion $f$ that is a homeomorphism onto its
image and satisfies
\[
           g_u(v,w)=\langle df_u(v),df_u(w)\rangle_{\mathbb R^4}
           \quad(u\in M,\ v,w\in T_uM).
\]
Does every such $(M,g)$ admit a $C^\infty$ isometric embedding
$f:M\to\mathbb R^4$?

The assertion is global: local embeddings on separate coordinate
patches must arise from one injective map on all of $M$. The
metric equality preserves lengths of curves and the induced
intrinsic distance, rather than claiming that intrinsic distance
equals the ambient straight-line distance between image points.
For noncompact $M$, properness is not included in the definition
unless imposed as an additional condition.

Increasing the ambient dimension, dropping injectivity to obtain
an immersion, or lowering the regularity to $C^1$ changes the
question. The fixed target dimension four and smooth regularity
must therefore be retained in either a construction or an
obstruction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every smooth two-dimensional Riemannian manifold be embedded
globally and smoothly in four-dimensional Euclidean space with
its metric exactly preserved?
