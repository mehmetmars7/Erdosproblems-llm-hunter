\section*{Most Circular Partition of a Square}\label{5500059}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500059 · AMR-054-0059 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a compact convex plane region $K$ with nonempty interior, let
$r(K)$ be the radius of its largest contained disk and $R(K)$ the
radius of its smallest enclosing disk. Their centers may differ.
Define its circularity ratio by $c(K)=R(K)/r(K)$.

Let $Q=[0,1]^2$. A finite convex partition is a collection
$\mathcal P=\{K_1,\ldots,K_m\}$ of convex regions with nonempty
interiors, pairwise disjoint interiors, and union $Q$. Its quality
is the largest ratio among its pieces. Determine
\[
 c_* = \inf_{\mathcal P}\ \max_{1\le i\le m}c(K_i),
\]
where the number $m$ of pieces is not fixed. Also determine whether
any finite partition attains $c_*$, and describe optimal partitions
or sequences approaching the infimum.

The circles in the definition are Euclidean, and both radii are
optimized separately for each piece. The objective minimizes the
worst piece, not the average ratio or a ratio formed from total
areas. The distinction between a minimum and an infimum matters:
an improving sequence with increasing numbers of pieces need not
have a finite optimal member. Rescaling the original square does
not affect any circularity ratio.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How nearly circular can every piece of a convex partition of a
square be? Find the best worst-piece ratio and decide whether
finitely many pieces can achieve it.
