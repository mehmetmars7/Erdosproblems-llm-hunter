\section{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.147}\label{2656}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2656 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A right order on $G$ is a total order $<$ satisfying $x<y\Rightarrow xg<yg$ for all $g\in G$. A subgroup $H$ is convex for this order if $h_1<x<h_2$, with $h_1,h_2\in H$, implies $x\in H$. Let $\mathrm{RC}(G)$ consist of subgroups convex for at least one right order; different subgroups may use different orders. Order $\mathrm{RC}(G)$ by inclusion. In the full subgroup lattice, the meet is intersection and the join of $H,K$ is $\langle H\cup K\rangle$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every right-orderable $G$, is $\mathrm{RC}(G)$ a sublattice of the subgroup lattice: do $H\cap K$ and $\langle H\cup K\rangle$ both belong to $\mathrm{RC}(G)$ whenever $H,K$ do? \sref{2656}{1}\sref{2656}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Ask whether intersections and generated joins of right-relatively convex subgroups remain right-relatively convex.
