\section*{Locality of connective constants}\label{10000024}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 10000024 · AMR-099-0024 · Probability · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(G_n,o_n)$ and $(G,o)$ be infinite connected locally finite vertex-transitive graphs with distinguished roots. Say that $G_n$ converges locally to $G$ if, for every integer radius $R\ge0$, the induced rooted balls $(B_{G_n}(o_n,R),o_n)$ and $(B_G(o,R),o)$ are isomorphic for all sufficiently large $n$. The threshold index may depend on $R$.

For such a graph $H$ with root $v$, let $c_m(H)$ be the number of nearest-neighbor paths $(v_0,\ldots,v_m)$ with $v_0=v$ and all $m+1$ vertices distinct. Its connective constant is
\[
\mu(H)=\lim_{m\to\infty}c_m(H)^{1/m}
       =\inf_{m\ge1}c_m(H)^{1/m}.
\]
The limit exists because vertex transitivity gives $c_{m+k}(H)\le c_m(H)c_k(H)$; its value does not depend on the root.

Prove that local convergence implies $\mu(G_n)\to\mu(G)$. No common group presentation, compatible generating set, or graph height function is included in the hypothesis. Local convergence already forces the degrees to agree eventually, so a separate eventual degree bound is unnecessary.

For each fixed $m$, local convergence identifies the walks of length $m$ and gives $c_m(G_n)=c_m(G)$ eventually. This supplies the upper bound $\limsup_n\mu(G_n)\le\mu(G)$. The substantive remaining demand is the reverse lower bound for the asymptotic growth rate, which involves arbitrarily long walks and is not decided by one fixed neighborhood.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does agreement of increasingly large neighborhoods force convergence of self-avoiding-walk connective constants?
