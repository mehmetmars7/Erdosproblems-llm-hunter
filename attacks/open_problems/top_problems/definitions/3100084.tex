\section*{What is the probability that a random nXn matrix over Z\_(p) has zero permanent as n goes to infinity}\label{3100084}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100084 · AMR-030-0084 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an odd prime $p$. Let $A_n=(a_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le n}$ be a
random matrix over $\mathbb F_p$ whose $n^2$ entries are independent
and uniformly distributed in the field, including zero.
Its permanent is the field element
\[
           \operatorname{per}(A_n)
               =\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_{i=1}^n a_{i,\sigma(i)}.
\]
Unlike the determinant, this sum has no permutation signs.
Determine the limiting probability of a zero permanent.
The proposed answer is
\[
             \lim_{n\to\infty}
                    \Pr(\operatorname{per}(A_n)=0)=\frac1p.
\]
The prime is fixed throughout the limit; uniform estimates with
$p$ growing are a stronger, separate question.

This model is not a random $0$--$1$ or random sign matrix reduced
modulo $p$, and the matrix is not conditioned to be invertible.
The conjecture asks for equidistribution at zero, rather than
asserting an exact equality for each finite $n$. Characteristic
two is excluded because permanent and determinant then coincide,
leading to a different limiting singularity probability.
Recent work on permanents over finite fields supplies partial
distributional information; such information should not be
identified with the displayed asymptotic formula unless its
hypotheses and limiting conclusion establish this specific law.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a fixed odd prime, does a uniformly random square matrix over that field have zero permanent with limiting probability1/p?
