\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.8}\label{2305008}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305008 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. All Taylor coefficients below are taken at $0$. A normalized holomorphic function has $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$.For such a function, let
\[b(f)=\sup\{\rho>0: f|_U:U\longrightarrow B(w,\rho)\text{ is biholomorphic for some open }U\subset\mathbb D\text{ and }w\in\mathbb C\}.\]
A biholomorphic map is a holomorphic bijection with holomorphic inverse. Define Bloch's constant by $B=\inf_f b(f)$, the infimum over all normalized holomorphic functions on $\mathbb D$. Thus a disc counted in $b(f)$ must admit one univalent inverse branch. The original collection records the historical bounds $\sqrt3/4\le B<0.472$ and attributes its upper estimate to Ahlfors and Grunsky.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the exact value of $B$, the sharp universal lower bound for the radii $b(f)$. \sref{2305008}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the sharp universal radius of a disc covered by a single univalent branch of a normalized holomorphic function.
