\section*{Markov random fields and Bernoulli shifts}\label{4600028}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600028 · AMR-045-0028 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $A$ be a finite alphabet and let $X\subseteq A^{\mathbb Z^d}$ be a
closed shift-invariant space. A probability measure $\mu$ on $X$ is a
Markov random field if, for each finite set $V\subset\mathbb Z^d$, the
conditional distribution of the coordinates in $V$, given all coordinates
outside $V$, depends only on the coordinates in the nearest-neighbor
boundary of $V$. Fix a consistent collection $\gamma$ of these conditional
probability kernels on admissible boundary configurations.

Assume $\mu$ is translation invariant and is the unique probability
measure satisfying the kernels $\gamma$. Uniqueness is among all such
measures on $X$, including those that are not translation invariant.
The question is whether $(X,\mu,\sigma)$ must be measurably isomorphic
to a Bernoulli $\mathbb Z^d$ shift.

Explicitly, a Bernoulli shift is an independent product process
$(B^{\mathbb Z^d},\nu^{\mathbb Z^d},\sigma)$ for a probability space
$(B,\nu)$. An isomorphism is a measurable bijection between invariant
full-measure subsets, preserving probabilities and commuting with every
coordinate shift, with measurable inverse.

The statement asks for an isomorphism of the entire lattice action,
not just of a single generating transformation. The uniqueness
hypothesis is stronger than uniqueness within the translation-invariant
class and cannot be weakened silently. Conversely no uniform mixing
rate or exponential decay of conditional influences is assumed; imposing
such an additional condition would address only a special case. The
specified kernels make precise which competing measures are compared.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does uniqueness of a Markov random field for its local conditional laws force the resulting lattice action to be Bernoulli?
