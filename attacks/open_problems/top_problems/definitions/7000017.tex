\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 4.1}\label{7000017}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000017 · AMR-069-0017 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S$ be the boundary of a compact convex body in $\mathbb R^3$,
with its intrinsic length metric $d_S$. Here $d_S(x,y)$ is the
infimum of lengths of curves joining $x$ to $y$ on the surface,
not their Euclidean chord length. Write
\[
        D=\sup_{x,y\in S}d_S(x,y),\qquad A=\operatorname{area}(S).
\]
The extremal question asks whether every such surface satisfies
$A\le\pi D^2/2$, with the sharp extremal metric given by a
doubled Euclidean disk.

The doubled disk of radius $r$ is the intrinsic surface obtained
by taking two copies of a flat closed disk and gluing their
boundary circles pointwise. It has area $2\pi r^2$ and intrinsic
diameter $2r$, explaining the proposed constant. It is an
allowed degenerate convex-surface limit, not the ordinary
single disk counted once.

If only boundaries of bodies with nonempty three-dimensional
interior are admitted, interpret the question as the corresponding
sharp supremum, approached by increasingly flattened bodies.
A complete result should determine the equality or limiting
case as well as the inequality. The question includes nonsmooth
convex surfaces; restricting to a symmetric subclass gives only
a special case.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Among convex surfaces of a fixed intrinsic diameter, is a doubled
flat disk the area maximizer, giving the sharp bound
$A\le\pi D^2/2$?
