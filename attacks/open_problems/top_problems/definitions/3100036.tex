\section*{What is the length of the shortest sequence in [n]* containing, as a (consecutive) subword, each permutation of [n]}\label{3100036}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100036 · AMR-030-0036 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer $n\ge1$, a superpermutation is a finite word
$w=w_1\cdots w_L$ over the alphabet $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such
that every permutation $\sigma\in S_n$ occurs as a consecutive
length-$n$ block. Precisely, for each $\sigma$ there is an index
$1\le j\le L-n+1$ with
\[
                  (w_j,\ldots,w_{j+n-1})
                        =(\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(n)).
\]
The word may repeat symbols and may contain blocks that are not
permutations. Different required blocks may overlap.

Let $s(n)$ be the least possible length $L$ of such a word.
Determine $s(n)$, or establish asymptotically sharp bounds for it
as the alphabet size grows. Concatenating all permutations gives
the elementary upper bound $s(n)\le n\,n!$, whereas counting
available starting positions gives $s(n)\ge n!+n-1$.

The word is linear: an occurrence cannot wrap from its final
letter back to its first. This convention differs from cyclic
superpermutations. It also differs from superpatterns, where only
relative order in a possibly nonconsecutive subsequence is
required. Here every letter and its position in the block must
agree exactly with the prescribed permutation. The optimization
is over all words, without imposing a recursive construction,
symmetry, or a particular order in which permutations first appear.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the shortest length of a word containing every permutation of n symbols as a consecutive block.
