\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.67}\label{2306067}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306067 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. For locally injective holomorphic $f$, put $P_f=f''/f'$ and $S_f=P_f'-\tfrac12P_f^2$, its pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives. A quasiconformal map is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism locally in $W^{1,2}$ whose distributional derivatives satisfy $|F_{\bar z}|\le k|F_z|$ almost everywhere for some $k<1$. Let $f$ be holomorphic and injective on $\mathbb D$ and assume $f(\mathbb D)$ is a Jordan domain. A quasiconformal extension across $\mathbb T$ extends $f$ to a quasiconformal homeomorphism on a neighbourhood of $\overline{\mathbb D}$. The limits below range over all points approaching the boundary, not only one radius.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is each of the following conditions, separately, sufficient for such an extension? \[\limsup_{|z|\to1}(1-|z|^2)|P_f(z)|<2;\qquad \limsup_{|z|\to1}(1-|z|^2)^2|S_f(z)|<2.\] The source notes that the two conditions together suffice and that the constant $2$ would be sharp in each separate assertion. \sref{2306067}{2}
Update 6.67 reports that Gehring and Pommerenke answered the first, pre-Schwarzian question affirmatively. \sref{2306067}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do either of two sharp boundary derivative bounds independently guarantee a quasiconformal extension of a Jordan conformal map?
