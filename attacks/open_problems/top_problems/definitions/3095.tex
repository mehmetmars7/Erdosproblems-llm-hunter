\section{Jacobian Conjecture}\label{3095}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3095 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, let $n\geq1$, and let $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ with $f_i\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. A polynomial automorphism means a polynomial map with a polynomial inverse over $k$. Its Jacobian determinant is
\[J_F=\det\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\right)_{i,j=1}^n\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n].\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such $k,n,F$, is
\[F\text{ a polynomial automorphism}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad J_F\in k\setminus\{0\}?\] \sref{3095}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a polynomial map over any characteristic-zero field have a polynomial inverse exactly when its Jacobian determinant is a nonzero constant?
