\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.58}\label{2305058}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2305058 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. Let $f$ be analytic, injective and nowhere zero on $\mathbb D$. Write $H^{\infty}(\mathbb D)=\{h\text{ analytic}:\sup_{\mathbb D}|h|<\infty\}$. An analytic $F$ with $\operatorname{Re}F>0$ admits the canonical logarithm on the right half-plane, so $F^{1/p}=\exp(p^{-1}\log F)$ is well defined. The known factorization for $0<p<1/2$ is $f=B_pF_p^{1/p}$ with $B_p,1/B_p\in H^{\infty}$ and $\operatorname{Re}F_p>0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does the same factorization exist at $p=1/2$; that is, for every such $f$, are there $B,1/B\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb D)$ and analytic $F$ with $\operatorname{Re}F>0$ such that $f=BF^2$? The square of the right half-plane maps onto a slit plane, giving the source’s equivalent bounded-factor/slit-plane formulation. The update distinguishes this question from a stronger conjecture that has a counterexample. \sref{2305058}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every zero-free univalent disk function admit the endpoint factorization into an invertible bounded analytic factor and the square of a positive-real-part function?
