\section*{Kirby Problem 1.48}\label{2707}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2707 · KP-1.48 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A knot $K\subset S^3$ is smoothly doubly slice if an unknotted smooth sphere
$S^2\subset S^4$ intersects an equatorial $S^3$ transversely in $K$. Unknotted
means ambiently isotopic to the standard sphere. The topological version uses
locally flat embeddings and topological isotopy. A smoothly slice knot bounds
a smooth disk in $B^4$. Write $\#$ for connected sum of knots or of oriented
manifolds, as appropriate.

The questions concern cancellation and torsion phenomena:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $K$ and $K\#J$ are smoothly doubly slice, must $J$ be smoothly doubly slice?
\item Is there a knot $K$ that is smoothly slice and topologically doubly slice,
but not smoothly doubly slice, while $K\#K$ is smoothly doubly slice?
\item If closed connected $3$-manifolds $M$ and $M\#N$ admit smooth embeddings
in $S^4$, must $N$ also admit a smooth embedding in $S^4$?
\end{enumerate}
Ask (a) and (c) separately also in the locally flat topological category.
Double sliceness requires the two slice disks on opposite sides of the equator
to form a single unknotted sphere. Separate sliceness of each half, or ordinary
concordance to a doubly slice knot, does not supply that condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does double sliceness cancel under connected sum, can doubling remove its smooth obstruction, and does an analogous cancellation hold for $3$-manifolds in $S^4$?
