\section{Erdős-Straus Conjecture}\label{1158}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1158 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A unit fraction is \(1/x\) with \(x\in\mathbb N\).
The three denominators below need not be distinct; no order or
coprimality condition is imposed on them.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer \(n\ge2\), do there exist positive integers
\(x,y,z\) such that
\[
 \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z?
\] \sref{1158}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can four divided by every integer at least two be written as a sum of three positive unit fractions, with repeated denominators allowed?
