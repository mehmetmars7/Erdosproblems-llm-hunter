\section*{Problems Around Polynomials — Problem 7}\label{2200017}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2200017 · AMR-021-0017 · Algebra · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix $n\geq1$ and consider real polynomials $P$ of degree $n$ having
$n$ distinct real zeros. Each derivative $P^{(j)}$, for
$0\leq j<n$, then has exactly $n-j$ simple real zeros. To form an
unambiguous word, restrict to polynomials for which zeros belonging
to different derivative orders do not coincide.

Arrange all $\binom{n+1}{2}$ zeros on the real line, and replace a
zero of $P^{(j)}$ by the symbol $j$. The resulting word has length
$n(n+1)/2$, with exactly $n-j$ occurrences of $j$. Rolle's theorem
requires the subword using just $j,j+1$ to alternate, starting and
ending with $j$. Equivalently, between successive occurrences of
$j$ there is exactly one occurrence of $j+1$.

Which words satisfying these necessary conditions occur for a
polynomial $P$? Give a characterization valid for arbitrary $n$,
with a polynomial realization of each admitted word and an
obstruction for every excluded word. Only the total order of the
zeros is prescribed, not their spacings; thus this is different from
prescribing numerical locations for every derivative zero.
Polynomials differing by a positive affine change of the variable
or a nonzero scalar have the same word. Reflection of the variable
reverses the word and preserves realizability. Degenerate
configurations with coincident zeros from different derivative
orders are boundary cases, rather than words in this strict-order
formulation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize the possible orderings of all zeros of a real-rooted polynomial and its derivatives.
