\section*{Rental regulation and insurance}\label{30007745}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider renters and landlords in one metropolitan rental market over ten years. Let \(d\in\{0,1,2\}\) denote existing regulation, a specified cap on incumbent rent increases, or a means-tested rental insurance payment financed by a stated tax. Landlords may adjust maintenance, conversion, entry, and tenant screening; households may move. Let \(v_i(d)\) be household monetary-equivalent utility under rent and income risk, \(\Pi_j(d)\) landlord profit net of real operating costs, and \(K(d)\) administrative and financing distortion costs. Define
\[W(d)=E\!\left[\sum_i v_i(d)+\sum_j\Pi_j(d)\right]-K(d).\]
Household monetary equivalents exclude profit distributions already entered in the separate profit term. Identify or bound \(W(1)-W(2)\) while retaining incumbent renters, prospective entrants, and displaced households. Benefits of insurance against rent shocks must be valued together with supply and allocation responses; changes in observed incumbent rents alone do not identify net welfare. Specify preferences, risk exposure, supply adjustment, eligibility rules, and treatment of tax transfers. Use policy variation or explicit structural restrictions and compare sensitivity to mobility and risk aversion. The original empirical review concerns rent-control effects, but a readable explicit open statement of this particular regulation-versus-insurance comparison was not verified. This is a proposed local policy-design problem.
