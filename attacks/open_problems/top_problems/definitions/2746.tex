\section*{Kirby Problem 1.87}\label{2746}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2746 · KP-1.87 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A link $L\subset S^3$ is fibered if its exterior fibers over $S^1$ with oriented
surface fibers whose boundary is the preferred parallel copy of $L$.
Let $f=(f_1,f_2):\mathbb R^4\to\mathbb R^2$ be a polynomial map with $f(0)=0$.
An isolated singular point at the origin means that $Df(0)$ has rank less than
two, while $Df(x)$ has rank two for every nonzero $x$ in some neighborhood of
the origin. For sufficiently small transverse spheres, define its link by
\[
L_f=\epsilon^{-1}\bigl(f^{-1}(0)\cap S^3_\epsilon\bigr)\subset S^3.
\]
The small-sphere link type is independent of the sufficiently small radius.

Does every fibered link in $S^3$ occur as $L_f$ for such a real polynomial map?
The rank condition is required on a whole punctured neighborhood, including
points outside the zero fiber. Requiring it only on
$f^{-1}(0)\setminus\{0\}$ is the weaker notion of a weakly isolated singularity
and gives a different realization problem. The polynomials are real; they need
not arise from a holomorphic map $\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every fibered link the link of an isolated critical point of a real polynomial map from four-space to two-space?
