\section*{Integer decomposition of smooth polytopes}\label{3000047}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000047 · AMR-029-0047 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A lattice polytope $P\subset\mathbb R^d$ is the convex hull of finitely
many points of $\mathbb Z^d$. Assume it is full-dimensional.
At a vertex $v$, each incident edge has a primitive outgoing integer
direction: divide its displacement vector by the greatest common
divisor of its integer coordinates. The polytope is smooth if exactly
$d$ edges meet at every vertex and their primitive outgoing directions
form a basis of the lattice $\mathbb Z^d$.

For an integer $m\ge1$, write $mP=\{mx:x\in P\}$.
The integer decomposition property is the assertion that
\[
                  mP\cap\mathbb Z^d
                   =\underbrace{(P\cap\mathbb Z^d)+\cdots+
                                  (P\cap\mathbb Z^d)}_{m\text{ summands}}
                                      \quad\text{for every }m\ge1.
\]
Does every smooth lattice polytope have this property, in every dimension?

Repeated summands are allowed. The inclusion from right to left follows
from convexity; the problem is to express each lattice point of every
dilate as a sum of lattice points of the original polytope. Smoothness
is a local integral condition at each vertex, whereas the conclusion
concerns all lattice points globally. Requiring only real summands
would be automatic, and checking a few small dilates does not establish
the universal quantifier over $m$ without an additional argument.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the local lattice smoothness of a polytope ensure that every
lattice point in every integer dilate decomposes into lattice points of the original polytope?
