\section{Matching Prime Supports across Three Consecutive Integers}\label{2339}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2339 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.

For a positive integer $u$, its prime support is $\operatorname{supp}_p(u)=\{p:p\mid u,\ p\text{ prime}\}$, with empty support for one. Equality of prime supports ignores exponents. The question requires $\operatorname{supp}_p(x+j)=\operatorname{supp}_p(y+j)$ simultaneously for $j=0,1,2$, with $x\ne y$. \sref{2339}{1} \sref{2339}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can there exist two distinct integers $x$ and $y$ such that $x,y$ have the same prime factors, $x+1,y+1$ have the same prime factors, and $x+2,y+2$ also have the same prime factors? \sref{2339}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove unconditional nonexistence or exhibit x,y. \sref{2339}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can two distinct three-term consecutive blocks match term by term in their sets of distinct prime factors?
