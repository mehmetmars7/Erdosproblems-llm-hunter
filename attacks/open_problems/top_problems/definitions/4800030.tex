\section{Multiple ergodic averages — Problem 30}\label{4800030}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 4800030 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb P$ be the positive prime numbers, $\ell\in\mathbb N$, and $c,c_1,\ldots,c_\ell>0$ be arbitrary real numbers. Floors $\lfloor t\rfloor$ are integer parts; the exponents need not be distinct or nonintegral.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do the primes contain both types of patterns \[\{m,m+\lfloor n^c\rfloor,m+2\lfloor n^c\rfloor,\ldots,m+\ell\lfloor n^c\rfloor\},\qquad \{m,m+\lfloor n^{c_1}\rfloor,\ldots,m+\lfloor n^{c_\ell}\rfloor\}?\] Each type is required to occur for infinitely many $n\in\mathbb N$, with a suitable starting value $m\in\mathbb N$ allowed to depend on $n$ and the type. \sref{4800030}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do primes contain arbitrarily long progressions with power-floor differences and all specified mixed-power-floor patterns, for infinitely many input integers?
