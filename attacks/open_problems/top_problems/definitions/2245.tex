\section{Quadratic Diameter from Separation of All Pair Distances}\label{2245}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2245 · Combinatorics}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



The separation applies to every two different unordered pairs: their distance values must be different and at least one apart. This is stronger than allowing repeated distance values and separating only unequal values. The diameter is the largest pair distance.

Distances are Euclidean. Distinct distances mean distinct numerical values; when a distance is said to occur a certain number of times, unordered pairs of distinct points are counted. Congruence allows any Euclidean isometry, including reflection. \sref{2245}{1} \sref{2245}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be a set of $n$ points such that all pairwise distances differ by at least $1$. Is the diameter of $A$ at least $(1+o(1))n^2$? \sref{2245}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Clarify the source quantifier order and, under the fixed-dimension reading, prove or refute the conjecture already for d=2. \sref{2245}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

When the distances of every two different pairs are separated by at least one, must the diameter reach the proposed quadratic lower scale?
