\section{Subdivision of a transitive tournament in digraphs with large outdegree.}\label{3268}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3268 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $k\ge1$, the transitive tournament $T_k$ has vertices $1,\ldots,k$ and arcs $i\to j$ whenever $i<j$. A subdivision replaces every arc by a directed path with the same endpoints, all internal vertices of these paths distinct and disjoint from the tournament's branch vertices. Write $\delta^+(D)$ for the minimum outdegree of a finite simple digraph $D$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a function $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ such that, for every $k$ and every $D$ with $\delta^+(D)\ge f(k)$, the digraph $D$ contains a subdivision of $T_k$? The source singles out existence of $f(5)$ as a test case. \sref{3268}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does sufficiently large minimum outdegree force a subdivision of any prescribed transitive tournament?
