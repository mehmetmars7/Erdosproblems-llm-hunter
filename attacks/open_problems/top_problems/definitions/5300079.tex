\section{Wandering stable components for complex Hénon maps}\label{5300079}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5300079 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $f:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^2$ be a polynomial diffeomorphism, meaning a polynomial automorphism with polynomial inverse, with nonzero constant complex Jacobian determinant $\delta$. Set
\[K^+(f)=\{z\in\mathbb C^2:\{f^n(z):n\geq0\}\text{ is bounded}\}.\]
Assume $\operatorname{int}K^+(f)\ne\varnothing$, let $U$ be one of its connected components, and let $g:U\to\mathbb C^2$ be a limit of a subsequence of iterates, uniformly on compact subsets. Rank is complex differential rank. The component is wandering if its forward images are pairwise distinct. Volumes mean four-dimensional Euclidean volume in $\mathbb C^2$. For a periodic component replace $f$ by an iterate so that $f(U)=U$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can $U$ wander? If it does, necessarily $|\delta|<1$ in the source setting. Can the limit map $g$ have rank zero or rank one? Can a wandering $U$ be bounded, unbounded of finite volume, or unbounded of infinite volume? \sref{5300079}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a polynomial automorphism of the complex plane of dimension two have a wandering stable component? Determine both possible limit-map ranks and all three boundedness and volume alternatives.
