\section{Normality of Pi}\label{1258}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1258 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write the unique nonterminating decimal expansion of $\pi$ as $\pi=3+\sum_{j\geq1}d_j10^{-j}$, with digits $d_j\in\{0,1,\ldots,9\}$. For a block $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_k)$ of length $k\geq1$, overlapping occurrences are counted by
\[
A_w(N)=\#\{1\leq j\leq N:(d_j,d_{j+1},\ldots,d_{j+k-1})=w\}.
\]
Normality concerns every finite block, not merely equal frequencies of single digits.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $k\geq1$ and every block $w\in\{0,1,\ldots,9\}^k$, is
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{A_w(N)}N=10^{-k}?
\]
Equivalently, is $\pi$ normal in base $10$? \sref{1258}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite string of decimal digits occur in the expansion of pi with its expected uniform limiting frequency?
