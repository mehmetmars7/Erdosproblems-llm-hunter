\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.19}\label{2306019}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306019 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$, and $\mathbb D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}$. Let $S$ be the class of analytic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$, $f^{\prime}(0)=1$, with expansion $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_nz^n$ and $a_1=1$. If $\sum_{n\ge1}|a_n|<\infty$, the Taylor series converges uniformly on $\overline{\mathbb D}$ and defines a continuous boundary function. For the more general alternative let $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n$ be analytic with the same absolute summability, without requiring the normalization or global injectivity of $S$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can the compact boundary image $f(\mathbb T)$ have positive two-dimensional Lebesgue measure (a) when $f\in S$, or (b) under the weaker requirement $f^{\prime}(z)\ne0$ for every $z\in\mathbb D$? \sref{2306019}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an absolutely summable analytic Taylor series have a boundary image of positive area, either for a normalized univalent function or merely a locally univalent one?
