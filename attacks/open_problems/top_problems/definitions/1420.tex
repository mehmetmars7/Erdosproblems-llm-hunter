\section{Lovász Conjecture}\label{1420}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1420 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ is connected when every pair of vertices is joined by a path. It is vertex-transitive if, for every $u,v\in V$, there is a bijection $\phi:V\to V$ preserving adjacency with $\phi(u)=v$. A Hamiltonian path is an ordering $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of all $n=|V|$ vertices such that $v_iv_{i+1}\in E$ for $1\le i<n$. A single vertex is a Hamiltonian path.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every nonempty finite connected vertex-transitive graph have a Hamiltonian path? The path is required to visit every vertex exactly once, but need not close to a cycle; the assertion is not restricted to Cayley graphs. \sref{1420}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can all vertices of every finite connected vertex-transitive graph be visited exactly once along one path?
