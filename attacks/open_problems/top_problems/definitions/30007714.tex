\section*{Nonmarket climate losses}\label{30007714}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider coastal households in one prespecified country facing two feasible climate-policy paths over thirty years. Let \(p\in\{0,1\}\) index the paths, with \(1\) reducing warming relative to \(0\). For household \(i\), define consumption \(c_{it}(p)>0\), health \(h_{it}(p)\), accessible ecosystem services \(e_{it}(p)\), and an indicator \(d_{it}(p)\) for involuntary relocation in year \(t\). Let lifetime utility be
\[U_i(p)=E\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{30}\beta^t u_i(c_{it}(p),h_{it}(p),e_{it}(p),d_{it}(p))\right],\]
where \(\beta\in(0,1)\), preferences are stable over the comparison, and the expectation covers specified climate and income risks. Fix a transfer schedule \(\ell_t\geq0\) with \(\sum_{t=0}^{30}\beta^t\ell_t=1\). Define compensating variation \(v_i\) by replacing \(c_{it}(0)\) with \(c_{it}(0)+v_i\ell_t\) until utility equals \(U_i(1)\), when a unique feasible solution exists. The schedule fixes the timing of this present-value transfer. The research task is to identify population-weighted \(E[v_i]\) and its distribution without counting health, displacement, or ecosystem losses twice through market expenditure measures. Specify revealed-preference or stated-preference assumptions, treatment of cultural losses that cannot be traded, and sensitivity to adaptation and income-dependent valuation. Existing valuations inform this exercise; the proposed combined estimand is not a canonical unsolved theorem.
