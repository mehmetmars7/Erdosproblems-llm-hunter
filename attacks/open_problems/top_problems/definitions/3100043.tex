\section*{("Diamond-Free Posets Problem") What is the size of the largest subset of the Boolean lattice B\_(n) which includes no B\_}\label{3100043}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100043 · AMR-030-0043 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and order its power set $2^{[n]}$ by
inclusion. A family $\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}$ is diamond-free
in the weak-poset sense if it contains no four distinct sets
$A,B,C,D$ with
\[
                 A\subsetneq B\subsetneq D,
              \qquad A\subsetneq C\subsetneq D.
\]
No extra incomparability condition on $B$ and $C$ is imposed.
Define
\[
 \operatorname{La}(n,B_2)
   =\max\{|\mathcal F|:\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}
                              \text{ is diamond-free}\}.
\]
Determine this maximum, especially its sharp asymptotic value
relative to the central binomial coefficient. The familiar
asymptotic conjecture is
\[
      \operatorname{La}(n,B_2)
              =(2+o(1))\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]
Two middle levels of the Boolean lattice supply the corresponding
asymptotic lower bound, since a required configuration needs
three distinct ranks along each displayed chain.

Families need not be downward closed, symmetric, or concentrated
in a bounded number of levels. Distinctness of all four sets is
essential. This is ordinary forbidden-poset containment, where
required order relations must be preserved but additional
relations are allowed. Requiring $B$ and $C$ to be incomparable
would instead give the induced-diamond variant, with a different
admissible class of set families.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the largest weak-diamond-free family of subsets of an n-element set, in particular whether its asymptotic size is twice the middle level.
