\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.35}\label{2302035}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2302035 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an entire function $f$, write
\[
 M(r,f)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|,\qquad m_0(r,f)=\min_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.
\]
For nonconstant $f$, its order is $\rho(f)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log\log M(r,f)/\log r$;
the lower order is defined using $\liminf$ instead. Every constant entire function, including the zero function, has order and lower order zero. In logarithmic modulus ratios, use the extended-real convention $\log0=-\infty$.
Let $f$ be nonconstant entire and let $\Gamma\subset\mathbb C$ be an unbounded continuum, meaning a closed connected unbounded subset of the plane. Write
\[L(f,\Gamma)=\limsup_{\substack{z\in\Gamma\\|z|\to\infty}}
\frac{\log|f(z)|}{\log M(|z|,f)}.\]
The limit is along points of the continuum going to infinity, not a separate radial parameter.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
The source gives an absolute constant $A$ with $L(f,\Gamma)\ge -A$ for all such $f,\Gamma$. Can $A=1$ always be used? If not, find a good numerical estimate for the optimal universal $A$. \sref{2302035}{2}
Update 2.35 records Hayman and Kjellberg's affirmative answer to the $A=1$ question. \sref{2302035}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How small can an entire function remain along an unbounded continuum compared with its maximum size on the same circles? Is the universal logarithmic factor one?
