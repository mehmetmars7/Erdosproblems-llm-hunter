\section*{Multiple ergodic averages — Problem 28}\label{4800028}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4800028 · AMR-047-0028 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For positive integers $n$, put $a(n)=\lfloor n\sin n\rfloor$,
where the angle is measured in radians and the floor
is the greatest integer at most its argument.
The sequence has both positive and negative values.
Let $T$ be an invertible measure-preserving map on
an arbitrary probability space $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$.

Prove both of the following assertions for every
integer $\ell\ge1$. For all integers
$k_1,\ldots,k_\ell$ and all functions
$f_i\in L^\infty(\mu)$, the averages
\[
             \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\prod_{i=1}^\ell
                             f_i\circ T^{k_i a(n)}
\]
converge in $L^2(\mu)$. For all nonzero integers
$k_1,\ldots,k_\ell$ and every measurable $A$
with $\mu(A)>0$, some $n\ge1$ with $a(n)\ne0$
satisfies
\[
            \mu\bigl(A\cap T^{k_1a(n)}A\cap\cdots
                              \cap T^{k_\ell a(n)}A\bigr)>0.
\]
These are, respectively, multiple convergence and
multiple recurrence of powers. Negative exponents
are interpreted using $T^{-1}$, and the nonzero
condition in recurrence excludes a vacuous return.
The system need not be ergodic and the sequence
is fixed independently of it. No pointwise
convergence or particular formula for the norm
limit is requested. The oscillating integer
sequence itself is the target; replacing it by
$n$, $|a(n)|$, or a monotone rearrangement would
be a different problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Show that the oscillating sequence floor(n sin n) supports every finite-order recurrence and mean-convergence problem for powers of one transformation.
