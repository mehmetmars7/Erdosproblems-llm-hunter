\section*{Maximum weight bounded fractional matching}\label{3000053}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000053 · AMR-029-0053 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite undirected graph. Its perfect-matching
polytope is
\[
       P(G)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_M:M\text{ is a perfect matching of }G\}.
\]
An empty family gives an empty feasible polytope. Given nonnegative
rational edge weights $w_e$ and capacities $u_e$, solve
\[
                  \max\Bigl\{\sum_{e\in E}w_ex_e:
                                    x\in P(G),\ 0\le x\le u\Bigr\},
\]
or correctly report infeasibility.

The requested algorithm is combinatorial and strongly polynomial.
Its number of arithmetic operations and comparisons must be bounded
by a polynomial in $|V|+|E|$, independent of numerical magnitudes,
with intermediate encoding lengths polynomial in the input size.
The source further specifies linear dependence of arithmetic on the
capacity data: additions, comparisons and multiplication by constants
independent of those capacities, rather than products of varying
capacity quantities. This requirement distinguishes the target from
general-purpose rational linear programming.

The vector $x$ is allowed to be fractional, but it must lie in the
convex hull of actual perfect matchings. The simpler constraints
$x\ge0$ and $x(\delta(v))=1$ alone do not describe this polytope in
general nonbipartite graphs. Nor is $x$ required to be one capacity-
respecting perfect matching; convex combinations can be feasible
even when no individual perfect matching obeys the same capacities.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can capacity-constrained optimization over the perfect-matching
polytope be solved by a combinatorial strongly polynomial algorithm linear in the capacity data?
