\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.25}\label{2305025}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2305025 · AMR-022-5025 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The analytic Wiener algebra is
\[
W^+=\left\{f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n:\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|<\infty\right\},
\qquad\|f\|_{W^+}=\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|.
\]
Each member is holomorphic in $\mathbb D$ and continuous on its closure, with multiplication given by pointwise products of functions. A function $f\in W^+$ generates this unital Banach algebra if
\[
\overline{\{P\circ f:P\in\mathbb C[z]\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{W^+}}=W^+.
\]
Characterize these generators. In particular, is injectivity of $f$ on the entire closed disc $\overline{\mathbb D}$ sufficient? Injectivity there is necessary because evaluations at two points with the same $f$-value agree on every polynomial in $f$.
Density is required in the sum-of-absolute-coefficients norm. Uniform approximation on the closed disc is weaker and does not give the required conclusion. The source records sufficient hypotheses supplementing univalence, including $f'\in H^1$, where $H^1$ means bounded circular integral means of $|f'|$. The main question removes such extra smoothness assumptions and asks whether closed-disc injectivity alone already forces generation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every function in the analytic Wiener algebra that is injective on the closed disc generate the algebra by polynomial approximation in coefficient norm?
