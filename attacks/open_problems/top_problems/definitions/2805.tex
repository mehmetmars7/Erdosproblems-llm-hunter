\section*{Kirby Problem 3.7}\label{2805}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2805 · KP-3.7 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a connected complete finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold.
A finite-sheeted cover $\widetilde M\to M$ fibers over the circle if
$\widetilde M$ is the mapping torus of a homeomorphism
$\phi:F\to F$ of a finite-type surface:
\[
 M_\phi=(F\times[0,1])/((x,1)\sim(\phi(x),0)).
\]
For a cusped manifold the fiber can be punctured, or equivalently one can
use compact cores with boundary. A pseudo-Anosov monodromy has invariant
transverse measured singular foliations $\mathcal F^s,\mathcal F^u$, whose
transverse measures are multiplied by $\lambda^{-1}$ and $\lambda$ for
some $\lambda>1$.

In this question \emph{orientable pseudo-Anosov} means that both invariant
foliations are transversely orientable: away from their singularities, normal
directions can be chosen continuously and consistently around every loop.
It does not merely mean that $\phi$ preserves the orientation of $F$.

Does every finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold have a finite-sheeted
cover admitting such a fibration with orientable pseudo-Anosov monodromy?
Both the cover and the fibered structure may be chosen. Virtual fibering by
itself provides pseudo-Anosov monodromy but does not impose the transverse
orientability required here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
After passing to a finite cover, can every hyperbolic three-manifold fiber over the circle with transversely orientable invariant foliations for its monodromy?
