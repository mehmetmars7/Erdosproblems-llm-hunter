\section{Smoothness of exponential-family parameter hairs}\label{5300062}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5300062 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A transcendental entire map is a holomorphic function $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ that is not a polynomial. Its dynamics means the iterates $f^n$, their orbit behaviour, and the stable (Fatou) and Julia sets: the Fatou set is the largest open set where the iterates form a normal family, and its complement is $J(f)$. Write $E_\lambda(z)=\lambda e^z$, $\lambda\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$. The hairs in this question are the parameter-plane curves on which $J(E_\lambda)=\mathbb C$, described in the source; regularity concerns real curves, with $C^\infty$ meaning smooth of every order and analytic meaning real analytic.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are these exponential-family parameter hairs $C^\infty$? Are they analytic? Where and how do they terminate? \sref{5300062}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are the parameter hairs smooth to every order or analytic, and where and in what way do they end?
