\section{Happy Ending Problem}\label{1379}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1379 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ is in general position if no three of its points are collinear. A subset $Q\subseteq P$ is in convex position if each of its points is a vertex of $\operatorname{conv}(Q)$, its convex hull. Thus the points of $Q$, when $|Q|=n\ge3$, form a convex $n$-gon; other points of $P$ are allowed inside that polygon.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $n\ge3$, determine the least integer $g(n)$ such that every $g(n)$-point set $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ in general position contains a subset $Q\subseteq P$ with $|Q|=n$ in convex position. Equivalently, every point of $Q$ must be a vertex of $\operatorname{conv}(Q)$. \sref{1379}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many points with no three collinear guarantee that some n of them are the vertices of a convex polygon?
