\section{Pisot substitution conjecture}\label{4600053}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 4600053 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $A$ be a finite alphabet. A substitution $\sigma:A\to A^+$ maps every letter to a nonempty finite word and extends by concatenation. Its incidence matrix is $M_{ab}=|\sigma(b)|_a$, the number of occurrences of $a$ in $\sigma(b)$. It is an irreducible Pisot substitution if the characteristic polynomial of $M$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ and its Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue $\beta$ is a Pisot number: an algebraic integer $\beta>1$ all of whose other algebraic conjugates have modulus less than one. Such a substitution is primitive. Let $X_\sigma\subset A^{\mathbb Z}$ consist of the bi-infinite sequences whose finite words occur in iterates $\sigma^j(a)$. With the left shift $S$, it has a unique invariant probability measure $\mu_\sigma$. Pure discrete spectrum means that $L^2(X_\sigma,\mu_\sigma)$ is the closed linear span of eigenfunctions of the unitary operator $Uf=f\circ S$; an eigenfunction satisfies $Uf=\lambda f$ for some $|\lambda|=1$. No algebraic-unit or two-letter restriction is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does $(X_\sigma,\mu_\sigma,S)$ have pure discrete spectrum for every irreducible Pisot substitution $\sigma$? \sref{4600053}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every irreducible Pisot substitution generate a shift whose measure-theoretic spectrum is entirely discrete?
