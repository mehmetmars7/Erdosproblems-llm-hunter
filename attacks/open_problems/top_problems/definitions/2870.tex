\section*{Kirby Problem 3.72}\label{2870}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2870 · KP-3.72 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Theta^3_R$ denote the smooth oriented homology cobordism group of $R$-homology three-spheres, for $R=\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Q$. A cobordism identifies two classes when its boundary inclusions induce isomorphisms on homology with those coefficients. The group operation is connected sum and the inverse reverses orientation. Changing coefficients gives
\[
\Phi:\Theta^3_{\mathbb Z}\longrightarrow\Theta^3_{\mathbb Q},
\qquad K=\ker\Phi.
\]
Elements of $K$ are represented by integral homology spheres bounding smooth rational homology four-balls.

(a) Does $K$ contain a subgroup isomorphic to $\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb Z$? Concretely, seek spheres $Y_j$ representing elements of $K$ such that every finite relation $\sum_j a_j[Y_j]=0$ in integral homology cobordism has all integer coefficients $a_j=0$.

(b) Can such a subgroup $A$ be chosen as a direct summand of $K$? This asks for a decomposition $K\cong A\oplus B$, equivalently a retraction $K\to A$ restricting to the identity on $A$. A splitting in the entire group $\Theta^3_{\mathbb Z}$ would be a stronger conclusion and should be specified separately.

Here $\mathbb Z^\infty$ means the direct sum, whose elements have finite support. An infinite collection of distinct spheres, or infinitely many multiples of a single infinite-order class, does not establish the required independent subgroup. The stronger splitting assertion also requires more than proving independence.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the integral-to-rational cobordism kernel contain an infinite free abelian subgroup, and can it split off?
