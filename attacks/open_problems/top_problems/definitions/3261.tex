\section{Antidirected trees in digraphs}\label{3261}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3261 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite simple digraph has no loops and at most one arc for each ordered pair of distinct vertices; opposite arcs may both occur. An antidirected tree is an orientation of a tree in which each vertex has indegree zero or outdegree zero. Its order is its number of vertices. Containment means an injective map preserving arcs, not necessarily an induced copy.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $k\ge2$, does every digraph $D$ with \[|A(D)|>(k-2)|V(D)|\] contain every antidirected tree of order $k$? The same source records the undirected Erd\H{o}s--S\'os special case: does every simple graph $G$ with $|E(G)|>(k-2)|V(G)|/2$ contain every tree of order $k$? \sref{3261}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the sharp arc-density condition force every antidirected tree, including the corresponding undirected tree conjecture?
