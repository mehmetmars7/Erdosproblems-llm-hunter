\section*{Policy effects beyond IV compliers}\label{30007706}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider a population with covariates \(X\), binary treatment \(D\), bounded potential outcomes \(Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\), and a possibly multivalued instrument \(Z\). Latent vector \(V\) describes treatment choices and effect heterogeneity. Assume consistency, instrument exclusion, and conditional independence of \(Z\) from potential outcomes and potential treatment choices given \(X\); impose monotonicity only where justified. For each model \(M\) satisfying these restrictions, let \(D_M^q(V,X)\) denote treatment under a specified new assignment policy \(q\), and \(D_M^b(V,X)\) treatment under the baseline policy \(b\). The policy effect is
\[
\psi(M)=E_M[Y(D_M^q(V,X))-Y(D_M^b(V,X))].
\]
Define \(\mathcal M(P,\mathcal R)\) as models reproducing observed data law \(P\) and obeying additional restrictions \(\mathcal R\), and define the identified set \(\{\psi(M):M\in\mathcal M(P,\mathcal R)\}\). Which credible structural restrictions, new instruments, randomized policy pilots or outcome bounds narrow this set enough to evaluate \(q\)? Clearly specify how each model maps the proposed policy into treatment choices, rather than equating a complier effect with the policy effect. Existing IV and partial-identification methods already solve important cases; this proposed problem asks which added information is credible in an application with multidimensional heterogeneity.
