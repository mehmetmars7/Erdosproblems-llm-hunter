\section{Kirby Problem 1.63}\label{2722}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2722 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


In standard contact $\mathbb R^3$, $\xi_{\mathrm{std}}=\ker(dz-y\,dx)$. A Legendrian knot is tangent to $\xi_{\mathrm{std}}$. Its Thurston--Bennequin number $\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)$ is the linking number with a contact-framing push-off. Positive and negative stabilization insert a zigzag in the front projection and decrease $\operatorname{tb}$ by one. A representative is non-destabilizable if it is not Legendrian isotopic to either stabilization of another representative. A smooth knot type $K$ is fixed throughout the quantifier below.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a single smooth knot type $K$ such that for every $N\in\mathbb N$ there is a non-destabilizable Legendrian representative $\Lambda_N$ of $K$ satisfying $\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda_N)\le -N$? \sref{2722}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one fixed knot type have non-destabilizable Legendrian representatives with arbitrarily negative Thurston--Bennequin number?
