\section*{Kirby Problem 1.24}\label{2683}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2683 · KP-1.24 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $U$ be the unknot and let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth knot. Normalize the Jones
polynomial by $V_U(t)=1$ and the oriented skein relation
\[
t^{-1}V_{L_+}(t)-tV_{L_-}(t)
=(t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})V_{L_0}(t).
\]
The three links agree outside a ball containing a positive crossing, a negative
crossing, or their oriented smoothing. Normalize the HOMFLYPT polynomial by
$P_U(a,z)=1$ and $aP_{L_+}-a^{-1}P_{L_-}=zP_{L_0}$.
Let $J_n(K;q)$ be the zero-framed $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ colored Jones invariant,
with the knot colored by the $n$-dimensional irreducible representation and
normalized to $J_n(U;q)=1$.

Does a nontrivial knot exist satisfying, respectively,
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V_K(t)=1$;
\item $J_n(K;q)=1$ for every integer $n\geq1$;
\item $P_K(a,z)=1$?
\end{enumerate}
These are polynomial identities in formal variables, not equalities at a selected
numerical evaluation. In part (b) a single knot must satisfy all colors
simultaneously. Each negative answer would say that the indicated invariant
detects the unknot among knots; the restriction to one component is essential.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a nontrivial knot have the unknot's Jones polynomial, every colored Jones polynomial, or HOMFLYPT polynomial?
