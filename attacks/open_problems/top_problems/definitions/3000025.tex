\section*{Deciding kernel-perfectness}\label{3000025}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000025 · AMR-029-0025 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $D=(V,A)$ be a finite loopless digraph; opposite arcs are allowed.
A kernel is a subset $K\subseteq V$ that is stable and absorbing:
no arc has both endpoints in $K$, and every $v\in V\setminus K$
has an arc $v\to u$ with $u\in K$. For $U\subseteq V$, let $D[U]$
be the induced subdigraph retaining exactly the arcs with both endpoints
in $U$.

The digraph is kernel-perfect when
\[
                        \text{every }D[U],\ U\subseteq V,
                        \text{ has a kernel}.
\]
Determine the computational complexity of recognizing kernel-perfect
digraphs, both in general and for structurally specified classes.
In particular, seek efficient recognition criteria for natural classes
where the universal induced-subgraph condition can be tested without
enumerating every subset, and hardness classifications where it cannot.

The input is the digraph itself, rather than a supplied kernel for each
induced subgraph. Having a kernel in $D$ alone is strictly less demanding
than kernel-perfectness. The empty induced subgraph has the empty kernel.
With the absorption convention above, reversing all arcs also reverses
the direction used in the kernel condition; using out-dominating stable
sets without that reversal would describe a different property of the
given orientation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the complexity of recognizing digraphs in which every
induced subdigraph has an absorbing stable set.
