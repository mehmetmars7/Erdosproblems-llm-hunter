\section{The Second Moment of Consecutive Prime Gaps}\label{1999}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1999 · Number Theory}

$p_j$ denotes the $j$th positive prime in increasing order, starting with $p_1=2$. The prime-counting function is $\pi(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\text{ is prime}\}$. Consecutive primes are consecutive in this full sequence. \sref{1999}{1} \sref{1999}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$, where $p_n$ is the $n$th prime. Prove that $ \sum_{1\leq n\leq N}d_n^2 \ll N(\log N)^2. $ \sref{1999}{1}

{\small\emph{Transcription note.} Only unambiguous escape/formatting damage and nonmathematical serialization debris were repaired in the displayed source statement.}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Is the average square of the first prime gaps bounded by a constant times the square of the logarithm of their number?
