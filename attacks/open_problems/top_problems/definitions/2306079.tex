\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.79}\label{2306079}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306079 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. The class $S$ consists of holomorphic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$, so $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_nz^n$. A quasiconformal map is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism locally in $W^{1,2}$ whose distributional derivatives satisfy $|F_{\bar z}|\le k|F_z|$ almost everywhere for some $k<1$. For a fixed $0<k<1$, let $S_k(\infty)$ contain those $f\in S$ admitting a whole-plane quasiconformal extension $F$ with $|F_{\bar z}|\le k|F_z|$ almost everywhere and $F(\infty)=\infty$ on the sphere. Here $k$ bounds the Beltrami coefficient, rather than the dilatation $(1+k)/(1-k)$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $f\in S_k(\infty)$ and $0<r<1$, must the normalized dilation $f_r(z)=f(rz)/r$ also belong to $S_k(\infty)$, with the same bound $k$? \sref{2306079}{2}
Update 6.79 attributes an affirmative answer to Krushkal. \sref{2306079}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does restricting and rescaling a normalized univalent map preserve its exact quasiconformal extension bound?
