\section{The Worst-Case Length of an Egyptian Fraction}\label{2038}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2038 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

A unit fraction is the reciprocal $1/m$ of a positive integer. An expansion with distinct denominators uses each denominator at most once; reordering its terms does not produce a different underlying denominator set. \sref{2038}{1} \sref{2038}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For integers $1\leq a<b$ let $N(a,b)$ denote the minimal $k$ such that there exist integers $1<n_1<\cdots<n_k$ with $ \frac{a}{b}=\frac{1}{n_1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n_k}. $ Estimate $N(b)=\max_{1\leq a<b}N(a,b)$. Is it true that $N(b) \ll \log\log b$? \sref{2038}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For a prescribed denominator, how many distinct unit fractions can be required in the shortest expansion of a rational number below one?
