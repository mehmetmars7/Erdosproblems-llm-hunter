\section{Some Open Problems in Elasticity — Uniqueness of equilibrium}\label{2000008}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2000008 · Partial Differential Equations}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Write $\partial\Omega=\Gamma_D\cup\Gamma_N\cup N$, where $\Gamma_D,\Gamma_N$ are disjoint relatively open subsets, $\mathcal H^2(N)=0$, and $\mathcal H^2(\Gamma_D)>0$. Here $\mathcal H^2$ is two-dimensional Hausdorff measure. A deformation $y:\Omega\to\mathbb R^3$ belongs to the Sobolev space $W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb R^3)$, so its weak derivative $Dy$ is defined almost everywhere. Prescribe $y=\bar y$ on $\Gamma_D$ in the trace sense and zero applied traction on $\Gamma_N$.
Let $\mathbb M_+^{3\times3}=\{A\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}:\det A>0\}$. The homogeneous stored-energy density $W:\mathbb M_+^{3\times3}\to[0,\infty)$ is $C^1$, satisfies frame indifference $W(RA)=W(A)$ for $R\in\operatorname{SO}(3)$, and satisfies
\[
 W(A)\longrightarrow+\infty\quad\text{as}\quad\det A\longrightarrow0^+.
\]
Extend $W$ by $+\infty$ when $\det A\le0$. Put
\[
 I(y)=\int_\Omega W(Dy(x))\,dx,\qquad
 \mathcal A=\{y\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb R^3):I(y)<\infty,\ y|_{\Gamma_D}=\bar y\}.
\]
Assume $\mathcal A\ne\varnothing$. For a matrix $A$, $\operatorname{cof}A$ denotes its cofactor matrix and $|A|$ its Frobenius norm.
For the pure-displacement problem let $\Gamma_D=\partial\Omega$ and $\Gamma_N=\varnothing$, and suppose that $\overline\Omega$ is homeomorphic to a closed three-dimensional ball. A strictly polyconvex density has a representation
\[W(A)=g(A,\operatorname{cof}A,\det A)\]
with $g$ strictly convex. A sufficiently smooth, orientation-preserving equilibrium deformation solves
\[
 \operatorname{div}D_AW(Dy)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,
 \qquad y=\bar y\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,
 \qquad\det Dy>0.
\]
Equivalently its stress satisfies $\int_\Omega D_AW(Dy):D\varphi\,dx=0$ for test fields $\varphi$ vanishing on the boundary; $A:B=\operatorname{tr}(A^TB)$. Smoothness here is sufficient to interpret these equilibrium equations classically; the question concerns smooth solutions, rather than singular cavitating deformations.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For homogeneous bodies homeomorphic to a ball and strictly polyconvex $W$, are sufficiently smooth solutions of the pure-displacement equilibrium problem unique for the given boundary deformation, or can one construct two distinct such solutions? \sref{2000008}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does strict polyconvexity force uniqueness of smooth equilibria with all boundary positions prescribed when the body has the topology of a ball?
