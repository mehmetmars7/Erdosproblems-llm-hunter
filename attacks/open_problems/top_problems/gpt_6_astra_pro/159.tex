\section{\texorpdfstring{Manins Conjecture on the Asymptotic Density of Rational Points on Fano Varieties}{Manins Conjecture on the Asymptotic Density of Rational Points on Fano Varieties}}\label{159}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth projective variety $X/K$ is Fano when $-K_X$ is ample. Fix an adelic metric on this line bundle, its multiplicative height $H$, and let $\rho(X)$ denote the rank of the Picard group over $K$. For a nonempty Zariski open $U\subseteq X$, put
\[
 N(U,B)=\#\{x\in U(K):H(x)\le B\}.
\]
The catalogue requests $N(U,B)\sim cB(\log B)^{\rho(X)-1}$ on a suitable open subset, with $c>0$ when that asymptotic is intended. \textbf{Formulation warning:} this unrestricted closed-exceptional-set version has counterexamples; a primary discussion is \eref{159}{1}, Introduction and Proposition 1.4. Modern formulations allow a thin exceptional set and additional hypotheses. Those are different statements, and are not silently substituted here. The original target is retained for traceability, not certified as an open universal conjecture.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \sref{159}{1}, \eref{159}{1}.
\cataloguescope{Prove that for any smooth Fano variety X over a number field K with ample anticanonical bundle, the number of rational points of anticanonical height <= B on a Zariski open subset satisfies N(U, B) \textasciitilde{} c B (log B)\textasciicircum{}\{rho(X)-1\}.}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does deleting a proper algebraic exceptional subset make rational-point counts on every Fano variety follow the proposed height asymptotic? The unrestricted version printed in the catalogue has counterexamples.

\subsection{Research attempt}

\paragraph{Current frontier.}
\textbf{Editorial status note.} The universal assertion actually printed in
this section is false. This is not a new discovery: Batyrev--Tschinkel's
cubic bundles already contradict it, and Gao's Proposition 1.4 explicitly
records the failure of every proper closed exceptional set
\eref{159}{1}, \eref{159}{2}. The modern thin-set conjecture is a different
problem. Gao's Theorem 1.8 identifies the geometric exceptional maps for
this example; it does not prove the proposed counting asymptotic after
removing them \eref{159}{1}. Below the established surface-counting input
is separated from a direct verification that it defeats the exact
quantifiers of the supplied target.

\paragraph{Attack route.}
There are two possible ways to refute the printed assertion: absence of
rational points, or excessive accumulation despite Zariski density. The
second is more informative and does not exploit the missing rational-point
hypothesis. One might try to sum estimates over infinitely many exceptional
fibres, but that creates an unnecessary uniformity problem. The route here
uses a single fibre, chosen after the open set but before the height bound.
Its logarithmic growth already exceeds the ambient prediction.

\paragraph{Attempt: a fibre-amplification lemma.}
Let $q:V\to T$ be a dominant morphism of geometrically integral varieties
over a number field, and let $H$ be a height on $V$. Suppose there is a
Zariski-dense set $D\subset T(K)$ with the following property: for every
$t\in D$ and every nonempty open $W\subset V_t$, there are constants
$c_{t,W}>0$ and $B_{t,W}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{159:fibre-bound}
 \#\{z\in W(K):H(z)\le B\}\ge c_{t,W}B^a(\log B)^b
 \qquad(B\ge B_{t,W}).
\end{equation}
Assume additionally that every nonempty open $U\subset V$ has image
containing a nonempty open subset of $T$. Then for every such $U$,
\begin{equation}\label{159:amplification}
 N(U,B)\gg_U B^a(\log B)^b.
\end{equation}
Indeed, choose $t\in D\cap q(U)$ and use the nonempty open
$W=U\cap V_t$. Its points are distinct points of $U$, so
\eqref{159:fibre-bound} supplies \eqref{159:amplification}. There is no
interchange of an infinite sum and a limit, and no uniformity in $t$ is
asserted or needed. The image condition holds, for example, for a smooth
surjective family; more generally one can restrict to a nonempty open
where the dominant family is smooth.

Consequently, if $b>\rho(V)-1$ and $a=1$, no open $U$ can have the
asymptotic required by this section. This lemma is an elementary
abstraction of the accumulation mechanism, not a claimed new counting
theorem.

\paragraph{Attempt: verifying the ambient geometry.}
Put $K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and
\begin{equation}\label{159:variety}
 X=\left\{([x],[y])\in\PP^3_K\times\PP^3_K:
           \sum_{i=0}^3x_i y_i^3=0\right\}.
\end{equation}
The four derivatives with respect to the $x_i$ are $y_i^3$. They cannot
vanish simultaneously at a projective point. Thus the hypersurface is
smooth. More intrinsically, projection to $\PP^3_y$ identifies $X$ with
the projective bundle of one-dimensional subspaces of the rank-three
kernel of the everywhere-surjective map
\[
 \mathcal O_{\PP^3_y}^{\oplus4}\longrightarrow
 \mathcal O_{\PP^3_y}(3),\qquad
 (u_i)\longmapsto\sum_i u_i y_i^3.
\]
This also proves geometric integrality and gives
$\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\Z^2$, with generators the restrictions of
$\mathcal O(1,0)$ and $\mathcal O(0,1)$. In particular $\rho(X)=2$ over
this fixed field, not just after extending it. Adjunction gives
\begin{equation}\label{159:anticanonical}
 -K_X=\mathcal O_X(3,1).
\end{equation}
It is ample as the restriction of an ample bundle on the product. Hence
$X$ meets the smooth projective Fano hypotheses and the proposed ambient
order is $B\log B$.

Choose the product metric with height
$H([x],[y])=H_3([x])^3H_3([y])$, where the projective heights use the
product-formula normalizations of the formulation and of
\eref{159}{2}, Section 2. The same argument works for any continuous
adelic metric on this fixed bundle: on the projective variety the ratio
of two such heights is bounded above and below by positive constants.
This comparison is used only for a fixed variety and a fixed metric, not
uniformly in a family.

\paragraph{Attempt: selecting a split cubic fibre.}
Over the open set $T=\{x_0x_1x_2x_3\ne0\}\subset\PP^3_x$, projection
$q:X\to\PP^3_x$ is a smooth cubic-surface family. The set
\[
 D=\{[a_0^3:a_1^3:a_2^3:a_3^3]:a_i\in K^\times\}
\]
is Zariski dense in $T$: it is the image of the dense set of $K$-points
of the torus under the dominant coordinatewise cubing map. For $t\in D$,
the change $z_i=a_i y_i$ identifies $X_t$ with the Fermat cubic surface
$\sum z_i^3=0$. All its lines are defined over $K$. The precise
established input is Batyrev--Tschinkel, Corollary 2.4: for every nonempty
open $W$ on such a surface, its anticanonical height count is
$\gg_W B(\log B)^3$ \eref{159}{2}. Only this lower bound is used; a full
Manin asymptotic for an arbitrary smooth cubic surface is not presumed.

For completeness, the reason the surface theorem applies to the ambient
height is an equality of line bundles, not an analogy:
\[
 (-K_X)|_{X_t}=\mathcal O_{X_t}(1)=-K_{X_t}.
\]
The $x$-height is a fixed positive multiplier on this fibre, and the
coordinate change modifies the $y$-height by bounded factors. Thus the
surface lower bound is exactly of the form \eqref{159:fibre-bound} with
$a=1$ and $b=3$.

Now let $U\subset X$ be \emph{any} nonempty Zariski open subset. It meets
$q^{-1}(T)$, and the image of that intersection is nonempty open in $T$
because the restricted morphism is smooth. Choose $t\in D$ in this
image. The lemma proves
\begin{equation}\label{159:contradiction}
 N(U,B)\ge c_U B(\log B)^3,
 \qquad
 \frac{N(U,B)}{B\log B}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{equation}
The second assertion follows from the lower bound, since $c_U>0$ is fixed.
It excludes every finite proposed constant $c$, even before the requirement
$c>0$ is considered.

\paragraph{Stress test.}
An open set may remove many special fibres, but cannot remove all fibres
above the dense set $D$: the smooth-image argument checks precisely this
point. Conversely, density of $D$ alone would not imply density of an
arbitrary selection of one point per fibre; no such unsupported implication
is used. The surface estimate applies after removing \emph{any} proper
closed subset of the chosen surface, so removing its 27 lines does not
repair the argument for the conjecture. All height-comparison constants
are fixed before $B\to\infty$.

The height on a cubic fibre is not the height induced from a power of its
anticanonical bundle: \eqref{159:anticanonical} verifies that the power is
one. Confusing this point would change the exponent of $B$. Also, this
proof says nothing against a thin exceptional set. The cubing map itself
illustrates why a dense collection of exceptional arithmetic fibres need
not survive a thin-set deletion. Finally, the construction is known; the
research contribution of this entry is its quantifier and normalization
audit, together with the isolated amplification lemma.

\paragraph{Outcome.}
\textbf{Outcome: Counterexample candidate.}

Equation \eqref{159:variety}, over the single fixed field
$\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, defeats the literal statement for every nonempty open
$U$, by \eqref{159:contradiction}. Here the prescribed candidate label
refers to the reconstruction in this notebook: the underlying
Batyrev--Tschinkel counterexample is an established result, not a new
unverified disproof. No replacement conjecture has been substituted for
the target, and no solution of its modern thin-set refinement is claimed.
