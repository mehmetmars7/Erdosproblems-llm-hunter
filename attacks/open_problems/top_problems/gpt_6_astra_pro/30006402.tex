\section*{\texorpdfstring{The Log-Rank Conjecture}{The Log-Rank Conjecture}}\label{30006402}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $f:X\times Y\to\{-1,1\}$, Alice knows $x$, Bob knows $y$, and $D(f)$ is the minimum worst-case number of communicated bits in a deterministic protocol computing $f(x,y)$. Its sign matrix is $M_f=(f(x,y))$. The conjecture is
\[
 \exists C\ge1\ \forall f:\qquad
 D(f)\le C\bigl(\log_2\max\{2,\operatorname{rank}_{\R}M_f\}\bigr)^C.
\]
The same constant works for all nonempty finite $X,Y$. The rank is over $\R$; it is not Boolean rank or nonnegative rank, and the communication is deterministic.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \sref{30006402}{1}.
\cataloguescope{Prove that there is a universal constant C >= 1 such that, for every Boolean function f:X x Y -> \{-1,1\} on finite nonempty sets X,Y, D(f) <= C[log\_2 max\{2,rank\_R(M\_f)\}]\textasciicircum{}C, where M\_f(x,y)=f(x,y) is its sign communication matrix and D(f) is deterministic two-party communication complexity in bits.}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is deterministic communication complexity always bounded by a fixed power of the logarithm of the sign matrix's real rank?

\subsection{Research attempt}

\paragraph{Current frontier.}
The rank is real rank and communication is deterministic. The general bound
has improved from the $O(\sqrt r\log r)$ bound of the supplied source to
$O(\sqrt r)$ in Sudakov--Tomon. \sref{30006402}{1}\eref{30006402}{1}
Hambardzumyan--Lovett--Shirley give a signed sum of $O(r\log(2r))$ rectangle
indicators for a rank-$r$ $0/1$ matrix. Removing cancellations with
quasipolynomial overhead remains conjectural, not part of their theorem.
\eref{30006402}{2} We therefore investigate how to organize a rectangle
representation rather than simply find a low-rank factorization.

\paragraph{Attack route.}
Put $B=(M_f+\mathbf1)/2$, so the two ranks differ by at most one and the
communication costs agree up to an output convention. Write
\begin{equation}\label{30006402:signed}
 B(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^t\epsilon_i1_{A_i}(x)1_{B_i}(y),
 \qquad\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{equation}
One possible missing step is to organize one party's sets into few laminar
families; another is to extract a large rectangle with very small error.
The first produces an explicit protocol, but the universal one-sided version
will fail even for a low-communication matrix. That failure identifies a
need for adaptive two-sided structure, not a log-rank counterexample.

\paragraph{Attempt: a laminar protocol and its limitation.}
A family is laminar if any two sets are nested or disjoint. Suppose the
$A_i$ in \eqref{30006402:signed} split into $k$ laminar families. Identify duplicate
sets. In each family Alice sends the index of the smallest set containing
$x$, or a symbol for none. The sets containing $x$ form a chain; another
set contains $x$ exactly when it contains that smallest set. Bob reconstructs
all membership bits and evaluates the signed sum. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{30006402:protocol}
 D(f)\le k\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil+1,
\end{equation}
including an optional final answer bit. Signs and cancellations cause no
problem once the full membership pattern is known. The same argument works
with the parties exchanged.

Each family has at most $t+1$ membership patterns, hence $k$ such families
produce at most $(t+1)^k$ distinct rows. This bound holds for any signed
representation, not just a chosen decomposition. Define $I_s$ with rows
all subsets $S\subseteq[s]$, columns $j\in[s]$, and entries $1_{j\in S}$,
and form
\[
 B_s=\begin{pmatrix}I_s&0\\0&I_s^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Singleton rows show $\operatorname{rank}I_s=s$, so
$\operatorname{rank}B_s=2s$. There are at least $2^s$ distinct rows and at
least $2^s$ distinct columns. Any one-sided laminarization of any signed
rectangle representation, on either side, therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{30006402:obstruction}
 k\log_2(t+1)\ge s.
\end{equation}
Thus quasipolynomial $t$ and polylogarithmic $k$ cannot both hold universally.
The sign matrix $2B_s-\mathbf1$ has rank at most $2s+1$.

Nevertheless $B_s$ has a protocol using at most
$\lceil\log_2s\rceil+3$ bits: each party announces its block; off-diagonal
blocks output zero. On the first diagonal block Bob sends $j$, and on the
second Alice sends $j$; the other party returns the membership bit.
Thus the failed strengthening is global \emph{one-sided} compression,
not the desired communication upper bound. The example even has a positive
rectangle partition; cancellations are not its obstruction.

\paragraph{A second route: eliminate a sufficiently small minority.}
Here is a direct low-error extraction argument of the type in the source's
Claim 4.1; no novelty is asserted. \sref{30006402}{1} Let a sign matrix of rank
at most $r\ge1$ have negative fraction $\eta\le1/(8r)$. At least half its
rows have negative density at most $2\eta$. Choose a basis of their row
space consisting of $q\le r$ such good rows, and retain columns where all
basis rows are $+1$. A union bound retains at least
$1-2q\eta\ge3/4$ of the columns. Every good row is a linear combination
of the basis rows and hence constant on the retained columns. It cannot be
$-1$ there: its negative fraction is at most $2\eta\le1/4$, whereas at
least $3/4$ of the columns were retained. Thus there is an all-positive
rectangle with at least $3/8$ of the original area.

Consequently this additional assertion would suffice: every rank-$r$ sign
matrix contains a rectangle of relative area at least
$2^{-(\log(r+2))^c}$ on which the minority sign has density at most $1/(8r)$,
for one fixed $c$. Extraction gives a monochromatic rectangle of comparable
area. The Nisan--Wigderson reduction, stated in the source's Theorem 1.4
and proved in Section 4.1, then gives a polylogarithmic deterministic
protocol. \sref{30006402}{1} The condition must apply uniformly at all ranks and
all submatrices; one favorable rectangle in the original matrix does not
justify recursive use. The signed decomposition theorem alone has not
provided this bias estimate.

\paragraph{Stress test.}
A positive term in a signed sum is not a nondeterministic acceptance
certificate, since other terms can cancel it. Randomized approximation of
the sum does not establish deterministic communication. Equation
\eqref{30006402:obstruction} applies even after replacing the rectangles, so a
new decomposition does not repair the one-sided proposal. But the explicit
short protocol shows that proposal was stronger than log-rank in a
structurally misleading way.

The script checks exact ranks, membership counts and the displayed protocol
for $1\le s\le5$, the low-error extraction on all $3\times3$ sign matrices
meeting its hypothesis, and additional planted examples. Those checks audit
finite identities and the extraction proof, not the missing universal
polylogarithmic estimate.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Outcome.}
\textbf{Outcome: Exploratory approach with unresolved gap.}
A signed-rectangle protocol criterion was proved, and an explicit family
refutes its natural universal one-sided strengthening while itself having
low communication complexity. A separate low-error extraction lemma isolates
a sufficient bias estimate, still unproved. Adaptive two-sided compression
or a stronger bias argument remains necessary.
