\section*{\texorpdfstring{Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)}{Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)}}\label{30006704}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each fixed $k$, let
\[
 s_k=\inf\{s\ge0:k\text{-SAT on }n\text{ variables is decidable in }
 O(2^{sn}\operatorname{poly}(|F|))\text{ deterministic time}\}.
\]
The strong exponential time hypothesis is $\lim_{k\to\infty}s_k=1$. Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ some fixed clause width $k$ rules out a deterministic $O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\operatorname{poly}(|F|))$ algorithm. The algorithm may depend on $k$; the claim is not that every fixed-width SAT problem has exponent one.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \sref{30006704}{1}.
\cataloguescope{Prove or disprove that the optimal exponent s\_k for k-SAT satisfies lim\_\{k to infinity\} s\_k = 1.}
\subsection{Short English statement}
As clause width grows, must the best deterministic SAT exponent approach exhaustive-search exponent one?

\subsection{Research attempt}

\paragraph{Current frontier.}
The target takes the limit of optimal deterministic exponents after fixing the clause width \sref{30006704}{1}. An exponent below one for each $k$ does not refute SETH if the saving tends to zero. Efremenko--Itsykson prove a strong lower bound for bounded-depth resolution over parities, not for every deterministic SAT algorithm \eref{30006704}{1}. The recent conditional applications in the catalogue likewise do not prove their SETH premise. We attempt a width-independent algorithmic saving and test its proposed representation of eliminated variables.

\paragraph{Attack route.}
A lower bound for every algorithm must go beyond a chosen branching or refutation model. An algorithmic route could eliminate a linear-size variable block while summarizing its influence on the remaining variables. The missing condition is not merely the ability to project constraints, but a sufficiently small total cost of building and using the projection. We derive that cost exactly and test a compact-CNF repair.

\paragraph{Attempt: the easy fixed-width gain has the wrong limit.}
For a $k$-CNF on $n\ge k$ variables, choose a nonempty clause and extend its variable set to $k$ variables. At least one of the $2^k$ assignments falsifies the clause. Branch on the other assignments, simplify, and stop at empty clauses or an empty conjunction. With polynomial work at each node, the recurrence has the form
\[
 T_k(n)\le(2^k-1)T_k(n-k)+\operatorname{poly}(|F|).
\]
Charging work to the search-tree nodes, rather than multiplying a new polynomial at every recursive level, gives
\begin{equation}\label{r65:branch}
 T_k(n)=O\!\left((2^k-1)^{n/k}\operatorname{poly}(|F|)\right),\qquad
 \alpha_k=\frac{\log_2(2^k-1)}{k}<1.
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha_k=1+k^{-1}\log_2(1-2^{-k})\to1$. The $k=1$ case is directly polynomial. Thus this complete deterministic algorithm is consistent with SETH.

\paragraph{A block-elimination bound with all boundary costs counted.}
Choose a block $B$ of $t$ variables. Let $C$ consist of outside variables in clauses meeting $B$, and let $b=|C|$. Write $F_B$ for those clauses and $F_0$ for the others. Define
\[
 R_B(c)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \exists u\in\{0,1\}^{B}:F_B(u,c)=1.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{r65:projection}
 F\text{ satisfiable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \exists x\in\{0,1\}^{V\setminus B}:F_0(x)=1\text{ and }R_B(x|_C)=1.
\end{equation}
A standard multitape implementation can stream through all $c$. For each, compute $R_B(c)$ by enumerating the $2^t$ internal assignments; when it is true, enumerate assignments of the remaining $n-t-b$ variables and test $F_0$. Thus no free random-access table lookup is assumed. The total time is
\begin{equation}\label{r65:cost}
 \operatorname{poly}(|F|)\bigl(2^{t+b}+2^{n-t}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the enumerations themselves need only polynomial work space.

\begin{notebookproposition}\label{r65:block}
Suppose an absolute $\delta>0$ has the following property. For every fixed $k$, a polynomial-time procedure finds in each $k$-CNF a block with
\[
 t\ge\delta n,\qquad t+b\le(1-\delta)n,
\]
except on instances already decidable in time $O(2^{(1-\delta)n}\operatorname{poly}(|F|))$. Then SETH is false.
\end{notebookproposition}
\begin{proof}
Both exponential terms in~\eqref{r65:cost} are at most $2^{(1-\delta)n}$. The procedure and polynomial factor may depend on fixed $k$, but $\delta$ does not. Therefore $s_k\le1-\delta$ for all $k$.
\end{proof}

No such decomposition theorem is proved. Dense primal graphs defeat a purely graph-based argument: when every pair of variables occurs in a clause, every nonempty proper block has $t+b=n$. Even easy positive 2-CNF examples have this graph. Thus the criterion is sufficient but not necessary; a proof cannot simply assume that hard instances have small boundaries.

\paragraph{Attempted repair: exact projection as a short CNF.}
Maybe a large boundary could still be summarized by a short CNF. Take boundary bits $x_1,\ldots,x_b$ and auxiliary bits $z_2,\ldots,z_b$. Enforce
\[
 z_2=x_1\oplus x_2,\qquad z_j=z_{j-1}\oplus x_j\ (3\le j\le b),
 \qquad z_b=0.
\]
The constraint $w=u\oplus v$ is exactly the conjunction
\[
 (u\vee v\vee\neg w),\quad(u\vee\neg v\vee w),\quad
 (\neg u\vee v\vee w),\quad(\neg u\vee\neg v\vee\neg w).
\]
This gives $O(b)$ clauses of width at most three. Existentially eliminating all auxiliary variables produces precisely even parity on the $b$ boundary bits.

\begin{notebooklemma}\label{r65:parity}
Every CNF on just the boundary variables that computes even parity has at least $2^{b-1}$ non-tautological clauses.
\end{notebooklemma}
\begin{proof}
Delete duplicate literals and tautologies. A clause missing a variable can be falsified by a partial assignment that extends to an even-parity assignment, using the missing variable to choose parity. Such a clause cannot be an implicate. Every remaining clause must mention all $b$ variables, and therefore excludes exactly one assignment. Rejecting all $2^{b-1}$ odd-parity assignments requires at least that many clauses.
\end{proof}

So exact auxiliary-variable elimination can turn a linear-size 3-CNF into an exponentially large CNF on its boundary. This disproves the hoped-for general compact-CNF projection lemma. It does not prohibit a compact circuit or parity representation of the same relation; parity itself is easy. Nor does it show that every input defeating the block criterion is hard for SAT.

\paragraph{Stress test.}
The boundary variables are included in the construction cost, and the multitape implementation streams rather than assuming unit-cost access to an exponentially large array. A saving $\delta_k\to0$ would not contradict SETH. The parity lower bound concerns formulas after removing all auxiliaries; retaining auxiliary variables would invalidate its hypothesis. The verification script checks the four XOR clauses and all boundary/auxiliary assignments for $2\le b\le7$, and compares the projected truth table with even parity. The universal exponential lower bound follows from the proof, not from these finite checks.

A uniform exponent improvement would refute SETH but would not automatically give $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$. Conversely, failure of this elimination strategy or a lower bound in the resolution system of \eref{30006704}{1} would not establish SETH. The deterministic and unrestricted quantifiers in the target remain unchanged.

\paragraph{Outcome.}
\textbf{Outcome: Exploratory approach with unresolved gap.}

The attempt supplies a width-uniform sufficient block criterion for a SETH refutation and an exact exponential obstruction to one proposed projection representation. The missing step is a different representation with a uniform construction-and-evaluation bound, or another mechanism giving an absolute exponent saving. Neither a universal speedup nor a lower bound for all deterministic algorithms is established.
