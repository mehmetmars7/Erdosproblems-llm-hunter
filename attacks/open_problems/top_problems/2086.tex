\section{Smooth Blocks Beginning at Prime Squares}\label{2086}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2086 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural

For $u>1$ let $P^+(u)$ be its greatest prime divisor. Since $p^2$ occurs in the product, its greatest prime divisor is at least $p$. The required equality therefore says that every integer $p^2,p^2+1,\ldots,p^2+k$ has all prime divisors at most $p$. The block length $k+1$ is fixed before seeking infinitely many primes $p$. \sref{2086}{1} \sref{2086}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is it true that for every $k$ there are infinitely many primes $p$ such that the largest prime divisor of $ \prod_{0\leq i\leq k}(p^2+i) $ is $p$? \sref{2086}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For any fixed block length, do infinitely many prime squares begin a block whose product has no prime factor larger than that prime?
