\section*{Euler-Mascheroni constant}\label{3370}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3370 · OPG-37329 · Number Theory · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The Euler--Mascheroni constant is the real number
\[
 \gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac1j-\log n\right),
\]
where $\log$ is the natural logarithm. The limit fixes a particular
constant; it is not Euler's number $e$ or a value of the gamma function.
A real number is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$ if it is a zero of a
nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients, and is transcendental
otherwise. The source asks whether $\gamma$ is transcendental:
\[
 \forall d\ge0\ \forall(a_0,\ldots,a_d)\in\mathbb Z^{d+1}
       \setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\},\qquad
                   \sum_{j=0}^{d}a_j\gamma^j\ne0.
\]
Clearing denominators gives the same condition for polynomials with
rational coefficients. A positive answer must exclude polynomials of
every degree, with no bound on their coefficients. Irrationality
would exclude only nonzero linear polynomials and would be a weaker
conclusion. A negative answer must produce a nonzero polynomial
annihilating $\gamma$ with a rigorous identity, rather than a numerical
near-equality. This is an exact arithmetic question; any finite decimal
approximation alone cannot distinguish an algebraic constant from a
transcendental one.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the Euler--Mascheroni constant the root of no nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients?
