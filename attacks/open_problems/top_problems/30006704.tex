\section*{\texorpdfstring{Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)}{Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH)}}\label{30006704}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each fixed $k$, let
\[
 s_k=\inf\{s\ge0:k\text{-SAT on }n\text{ variables is decidable in }
 O(2^{sn}\operatorname{poly}(|F|))\text{ deterministic time}\}.
\]
The strong exponential time hypothesis is $\lim_{k\to\infty}s_k=1$. Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ some fixed clause width $k$ rules out a deterministic $O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\operatorname{poly}(|F|))$ algorithm. The algorithm may depend on $k$; the claim is not that every fixed-width SAT problem has exponent one.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2007.07.007}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
As clause width grows, must the best deterministic SAT exponent approach exhaustive-search exponent one?
