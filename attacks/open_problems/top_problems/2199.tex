\section{Finite Hypergraphs Unavoidable at Uncountable Chromatic Number}\label{2199}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2199 · Graph Theory}

fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural

A 3-uniform hypergraph has edges that are three-element vertex sets. Its chromatic number is the least number of vertex colours with no monochromatic edge. This is weak hypergraph colouring, not a requirement that all three vertices have different colours. \sref{2199}{1} \sref{2199}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Characterize those finite 3-uniform hypergraphs which appear in every 3-uniform hypergraph of chromatic number $>\aleph_0$. \sref{2199}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Characterize the forced finite 3-uniform hypergraphs. \sref{2199}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Which finite three-uniform hypergraphs must occur in every three-uniform hypergraph that cannot be properly coloured with countably many colours?
