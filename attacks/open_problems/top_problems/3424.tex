\section*{Hilbert-Smith conjecture}\label{3424}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3424 · OPG-37297 · Topology · OpenGarden}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a connected finite-dimensional topological manifold:
a Hausdorff second-countable space locally homeomorphic to
$\mathbb R^n$, with no boundary. Let $G$ be a locally compact
Hausdorff topological group. A continuous action is a jointly
continuous map $\alpha:G\times M\to M$ satisfying
$\alpha(1,x)=x$ and $\alpha(gh,x)=\alpha(g,\alpha(h,x))$.
Each group element then acts by a homeomorphism. The action is
faithful, or effective, if the only element fixing every point of
$M$ is the identity.

The Hilbert--Smith conjecture asserts
\[
 G\text{ has such a faithful action on }M
             \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ is a Lie group}.
\]
A Lie group here has a compatible finite-dimensional smooth
manifold structure for which multiplication and inversion are smooth;
compatibility includes the original group topology. In this conclusion,
no countability restriction is placed on the set of connected components
of the Lie group.
Connectedness of $M$ is part of the standard formulation, made
explicit to avoid actions on unrelated components. No differentiable,
Lipschitz, or measure-preserving regularity is required of the action.
Faithfulness does not mean that each nonidentity element has no
fixed points, only that none fixes the entire manifold. The target
covers all finite dimensions and all locally compact groups satisfying
these hypotheses, without assuming that $G$ itself is connected.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a locally compact group acting continuously and faithfully on a connected finite-dimensional manifold be a Lie group?
