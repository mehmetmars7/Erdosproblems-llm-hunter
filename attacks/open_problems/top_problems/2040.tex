\section{The Closest Nonzero Reciprocal Subsum Error}\label{2040}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2040 · Graph Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

Only nonzero approximation errors enter the minimum; exact representations of one are discarded. The finite ground set makes the remaining set of errors finite. The proposed constant is equivalently the limit of $-N^{-1}\log\delta(N)$. \sref{2040}{1} \sref{2040}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\delta(N)$ be the minimal non-zero value of $\lvert 1-\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}\rvert$ as $A$ ranges over all subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Is it true that $ \delta(N)=e^{-(c+o(1))N} $ for some constant $c\in (0,1)$? \sref{2040}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How close, without being equal, can a reciprocal subset sum from a finite interval come to one, and is the best error governed by one exponential rate?
