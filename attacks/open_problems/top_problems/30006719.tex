\section*{\texorpdfstring{Campana's Conjecture Characterizing Special Varieties by Potential Density of Rational Points}{Campana's Conjecture Characterizing Special Varieties by Potential Density of Rational Points}}\label{30006719}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a rank-one coherent subsheaf $\mathcal F\subseteq\Omega^p_{X_{\bar K}}$, its Kodaira dimension measures the growth of sections of its reflexive tensor powers, with value $-\infty$ if none occur. Campana specialness requires $\kappa(\mathcal F)<p$ for all $1\le p\le\dim X$ and all such $\mathcal F$. Potential density means density after some finite field extension. The conjecture is the biconditional
\[
 X_{\bar K}\text{ special}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists L/K\text{ finite}:\overline{X(L)}^{\rm Zar}=X.
\]
Both directions are required, for every smooth projective geometrically integral number-field variety. Weak specialness and integral points on open varieties are different notions.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701105}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Are precisely Campana's special projective varieties those whose rational points become dense after a finite field extension?
