\section{Ulam's Worst-Packing Convex Body Problem}\label{1226}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1226 · Geometry}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,

$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;

For a compact convex body $K\subset\R^3$ with nonempty interior, let $\delta(K)$ be the supremal upper density of packings by congruent copies of $K$ with pairwise disjoint interiors. Rotations and translations of copies are allowed. For the Euclidean ball $B$, the conjectured comparison is $\delta(K)\geq\delta(B)$ for every such $K$. This is not restricted to lattice or translative packings. \sref{1226}{1} \sref{1226}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the sphere the worst-packing convex solid? \sref{1226}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Extend the local symmetric result to arbitrary convex bodies and global shape space, or exhibit a convex solid whose optimal packing density is below that of the ball. \sref{1226}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Among all three-dimensional convex bodies, is a ball the shape whose densest congruent-copy packing occupies the smallest fraction of space?
