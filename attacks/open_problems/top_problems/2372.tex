\section{A Doubling Ratio for Divisor Counts of Mersenne Numbers}\label{2372}

{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2372 · Number Theory}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.

The divisor-count function is $\tau(n)=\#\{d\in\N:d\mid n\}$. Divisors are positive, including one and $n$; the function is unrelated to a graph transversal number using the same letter elsewhere. \sref{2372}{1} \sref{2372}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $\tau(n)$ counts the divisors of $n$ then let $ f(n)=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\tau(2^k-1). $ Does $f(2n)/f(n)$ tend to a limit? \sref{2372}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove or refute f(2n)/f(n) tends to infinity. \sref{2372}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Does the cumulative divisor count of numbers one below powers of two have a limiting ratio when the index range is doubled?
